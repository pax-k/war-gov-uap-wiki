# Sighting — Japan multi-station glow phenomenon (25 Jan 1949)

A multi-station unidentified-glow event over central Japan on the night of **25 January 1949** at approximately **1946–1950 JST**, observed independently by six **Japanese Central Meteorological Observatory (CMO/Tokyo)** weather stations. Forwarded to AMC and MATS via the **2143D Air Weather Wing (APO 925)**, signed Headquarters 2143D AWW dated **3 February 1949**, with six inclosures (three station reports + chart of observation path + two surface-synoptic charts at 250600Z and 251200Z).

## Reporting stations and observations

The American forwarding letter notes: "Inclosures 1, 2 and 3 are reports received by the Japanese Central Meteorological Observatory in Tokyo. The observations indicated in these reports were taken by **Japanese Weather personnel** at the respective stations." ^[extracted]

| Station | Coordinates | Local time | Observation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fukushima** | 37°45'N 140°28'E | ~1930 JST | "Pale glow flying NW → SE, fell with a roar like thunder. Spot of descent seems to be Nakagori Village, Tamura County, east of Koriyama (37°24'N 140°23'E). Probably a meteor." |
| **Iida** | central Japan | 1946:30 JST | Pale glow, NE at 35° altitude, moved through N → W, then disappeared. **1.5× lunar diameter**, considerable brightness. At ~1950, "a heavy sound was heard and doors trembled." |
| **Suwa** | central Japan | 1950 JST | Pale glow with sound; appeared a few degrees NNE of Betelgeuse (Orion); moved to SE; reached **maximum brightness at 60° altitude, illuminating ground objects momentarily**; turned yellowish, then yellow-red, descended to 40° SW, disappeared. ~3–4 sec total. ~1 minute after disappearance: a sound "resembling a distant thunder" lasting ~15 seconds. |
| **Nagano** | central Japan | ~1950 JST | "Phenomenon, probably a meteor, with a sound like that of explosion." Glow appeared ESE at medium altitude, moved WSW with palish white light, disappeared below horizon; observation duration ~5 minutes; ~3 minutes later, **explosion-like sound** from WSW. |
| **Takayama** | 36°09'N 137°15'E | ~1940 JST | "Remarkable glow" SE of station, moved SW, disappeared, then sound "like thunder after a few minutes." Loud near Osaka Town. From Miya Village, glow "almost overhead", then thunder-like sound from south. |
| **Gifu** | central Japan | ~1940 JST | Glow seen NNE. (Nagoya District CMO observed nothing.) |

## JCMO evaluation

The Japanese stations' consensus interpretation: **"this phenomenon might be the explosion of a meteor."** ^[extracted]

## Object signature (synthesized)

- **Brightness**: sufficient to **illuminate ground objects** at peak (Suwa).
- **Color sequence**: pale → palish white → yellowish → yellow-red → faded.
- **Path**: trajectories cluster around a **west-of-Tokyo-and-NE arc** — Fukushima (east coast, NE Honshu), passing through Iida / Suwa / Nagano (central), terminating around Takayama / Gifu / Osaka in the SW. Implied path: NE → SW across central Honshu, ~700 km arc. ^[inferred]
- **Sound delay**: 1–3 minutes after visual disappearance, consistent with **acoustic propagation from an upper-atmosphere event**.
- **Duration of light**: 3–5 seconds at most stations; 5 minutes at Nagano (likely an outlier or a different sub-phase). ^[ambiguous]
- **Apparent diameter at peak**: 1.5× lunar diameter (Iida).

## Significance

- **Earliest non-Western-witness UAP report in the corpus.** The wiki's prior UAP sources are all British / US / Allied-aircrew. The Japanese CMO stations are an independent civilian-meteorological observation chain reporting the same morphology class as the contemporaneous American "ball of fire" sightings (e.g., [[references/sighting-kodiak-alaska-1949-04|Kodiak]] just 10 weeks later).
- **Six-station independent multi-vantage corroboration with synchronized timing** (~1946–1950 JST) is among the **strongest geometric solutions** in the entire corpus — the path can in principle be triangulated from the station reports (and the file includes a chart-of-path inclosure that the OCR did not preserve). ^[inferred]
- **Conventional candidate explanation**: an upper-atmosphere bolide (large meteor) — accepted by the Japanese stations themselves. The 2143D AWW's American forwarding letter does **not** offer an alternative; it forwards the JCMO conclusion to AMC for evaluation. The institutional posture is to **route, not adjudicate** — consistent with the Cabell directive policy. ^[inferred]
- **Routing significance**: this is the only report in the 1949 file that originates from a **foreign civilian government meteorological agency** rather than a US or UK military / civil-aviation organization. It demonstrates the [[concepts/fsr-200-4-reporting-regulation|Cabell / FSR 200-4 intake apparatus]] reaching into **occupied-Japan civilian observation networks** through the 2143D AWW. ^[inferred]
