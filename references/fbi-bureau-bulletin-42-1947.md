# Bureau Bulletin No. 42, Series 1947 — Section (B) "Flying Discs"

A **two-paragraph operational directive** issued by FBI Director [[entities/fbi|J. Edgar Hoover]] on **30 July 1947** as Section (B) of Bureau Bulletin No. 42, Series 1947, distributed to all FBI field offices. ^[extracted] The bulletin codifies the Bureau's role in disc investigations under the new [[concepts/fbi-aaf-saucer-cooperation-1947|FBI-AAF cooperation arrangement]] negotiated with [[entities/george-f-schulgen|Brig. Gen. George F. Schulgen]], Chief of the Air Forces' Requirements Intelligence Branch, by [[entities/s-w-reynolds|SA S. W. Reynolds]] of the FBI Liaison Section. ^[extracted]

## Verbatim text (Section B)

The bulletin opens with the cooperation framing: ^[extracted]

> **(B) FLYING DISCS** — The Bureau, at the request of the Army Air Forces Intelligence, has agreed to cooperate in the investigation of flying discs. The Air Forces have confidentially advised that it is possible to release three or more discs in odd numbers, attached together by a wire, from an airplane in high altitudes and that these discs would obtain tremendous speed in their descent and would depend to the earth in an arc. The Army Air Forces Intelligence has also indicated some concern that the reports sightings might have been made by subversive individuals for the purpose of treating a mass hysteria.

The instructions to field offices: ^[extracted]

> You should investigate each instance which is brought to your attention of a sighting of a flying disc in order to ascertain whether or not it is a bona fide sighting, an imaginary one or a prank. You should also bear in mind that individuals might report seeing flying discs for various reasons. It is conceivable that an individual might be desirous of seeking personal publicity, causing hysteria or playing a prank.
>
> The Bureau should be notified immediately by teletype of all reported sightings and the results of your inquiries. In instances where the report appears to have merit, the teletype should be followed by a letter to the Bureau containing in detail the results of your inquiries. The Army Air Forces have assured the Bureau complete cooperating in these matters and in any instances where they fail to make information available to you or make the recovered discs available for your examination, it should promptly be brought to the attention of the Bureau.
>
> Any information you develop in connection with these discs should be promptly brought to the attention of the Army through your usual liaison channels.

## What the bulletin actually does

Three operational consequences embedded in the text: ^[inferred]

| Provision | What it does | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **"investigate each instance"** | Establishes the FBI as a **first-witness sincerity tribunal**, not a physical investigator | Inverts the normal evidentiary frame: discs are *people* problems, not *aerial* problems |
| **"three or more discs in odd numbers, attached by a wire"** | Smuggles a *prejudicial cover-story* into the Bureau's operational baseline — i.e. plants the AAF's preferred prosaic explanation directly into field-office heads | Field offices arrive at every site with the wire-released-disc hypothesis already pre-loaded — a structural framing bias ^[inferred] |
| **"reports might have been made by subversive individuals for the purpose of [creating] mass hysteria"** | Smuggles the **counterintelligence frame** into UAP investigation — **disc reporters are intelligence subjects** | Establishes the channel later expanded to formal AFOSI / Air Police political-loyalty checks on saucer-witnesses; institutionally upstream of the [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-serial-449\|Oct 1966 LA SAC AFSCA-magazine memo]] |

The bulletin closes with the **disc-access-or-escalation clause**: if the AAF *"fail to make information available to you or make the recovered discs available for your examination"*, field offices must escalate to HQ. ^[extracted] This clause encodes Hoover's earlier marginal note (page 126 of section 1) that *"in the La. case the Army grabbed it and would not let us have it for cursory examination"* — the founding insistence on **full FBI access** to recovered discs as the precondition for cooperation. ^[extracted]

## Position in FBI corpus

Bureau Bulletin No. 42 is the **single most institutionally consequential document in section 1**. ^[inferred] Subsequent FBI field-office correspondence in the file (e.g. Milwaukee SAC's 12 Aug 1947 memo on Schaefer/Hanson) explicitly cites "Bureau Bulletin No. 42, Series 1947, dated July 30, 1947, Section (B)" as the operative authority. ^[extracted] It governed FBI field-office disc-investigations for the duration of 1947, until [[concepts/flying-disc-reporting-protocol|Cabell's AIRMEM #4]] (15 Feb 1949) reorganized the inter-agency framework. ^[inferred]

The "wire-released disc" cover-story is **structurally analogous** to later prosaic-explanation cover-stories: the 1947 Roswell weather-balloon framing (serial 18 → [[references/sighting-roswell-1947-07-08]]), the 1949 Project Mogul reframing, the 1950s temperature-inversion / swamp-gas defaults of [[concepts/project-sign|Project Sign]] / GRUDGE / BLUE BOOK era. The wire-released-disc hypothesis appears in **no other source in the wiki corpus** — it is the most aggressive prosaic explanation that the AAF privately offered the Bureau in this era. ^[inferred]
