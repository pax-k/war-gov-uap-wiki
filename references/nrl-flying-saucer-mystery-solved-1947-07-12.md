# Flying Saucer Mystery Solved (12 July 1947)

A **7-page typewritten memo** dated **12 July 1947**, anonymous in authorship, filed at FBI HQ as part of [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-serial-130|62-HQ-83894 serial 130]] OCR pp. 55-61. ^[extracted] The memo proposes that **all flying-disc sightings are sun-reflection artifacts** — on automobile ventilation-wing windows for ground observers, and on electric-light-bulb reflections in cockpit windows for airborne observers. ^[extracted]

This is the **earliest formal optical-skepticism framework** in the wiki corpus. ^[inferred] It **predates AFBIR-CO's 30 Jul 1947 analytical document by 18 days**, predates the [[references/fbi-bureau-bulletin-42-1947|Bureau Bulletin No. 42]] (30 Jul 1947) by 18 days, and predates [[concepts/project-sign|Project SIGN's]] Jan 1948 stand-up by ~6 months. ^[inferred] The memo is structurally **distinct from** the [[concepts/saucer-as-hoax-default|hoax-default frame]] — which assumes deliberate prankster fabrication of physical objects — by proposing that **no physical disc artifact need exist at all**: the discs are **pure optical artifacts**. ^[inferred]

## The reflection theory

### Mechanism for ground observers (automobiles)

The memo's central claim: ^[extracted]

> These flying discs or saucers can be observed by any motorist traveling along the highways when the sun is shining on most any afternoon or evening. **During the daylight most of these so-called flying discs are reflections from the sun shining upon some bright object in or on the automobile driven by the observer.** For instance, if you were driving in a north-westerly direction with the sun shining on the handle of the front or rear door on the left side of the car, you may notice a shiny object appear in the ventilation wing of the window on the drivers left, if it is opened to about a 45° angle.

The mechanism: ^[extracted]

1. The **car's ventilation-wing window** (open at ~45°) acts as a **partial mirror**.
2. The **sun reflects off bright objects on the car** (door handles, chrome trim, bumpers, etc.).
3. The **angle and motion of the car** make the reflections **appear in the sky** through the wing-window glass.
4. **Multiple reflections** (from different bright objects) account for **multiple-disc reports**.
5. The **car's motion** makes the reflections **appear to fly at high speeds** relative to ground references.
6. The **angle of the wing-window** determines apparent flight direction (the memo asserts: "**The flying saucer appears to be traveling in the same general direction as the automobile but sometimes a little to the right which makes it appear that it will eventually cross the drivers path. The apparent flight direction of the saucer is due to the angle of the ventilation wing glass in its relation to the direction in which the car is traveling.**"). ^[extracted]
7. Reflections from **approaching vehicles' bright objects** behind the observer can also project into the wing-window — accounting for "discs from behind". ^[extracted]

### Mechanism for airborne observers (cockpit electric lights)

The memo extends the reflection theory to **aviator reports**: ^[extracted]

> [If a cockpit-ventilation window's reflection of] electric light bulbs, [you may] see as many as three flying discs from one electric light bulb, which may have more or less of a bell shape. These discs many times appear as inverted saucers, and if you w[atch] [continues]

The pilot variant: cockpit-internal **electric light bulbs** reflect off cockpit-side windows or ventilation wings, producing **bell-shaped or inverted-saucer** apparent objects.

### Other explanations the memo allows

The memo concedes some sightings are **not** optical artifacts: ^[extracted]

> Some reports stemmed from pure imagination or hysteria, while others were deliberate hoaxes. **Any other reports can be explained if all the surrounding facts are known.**

This residual-category framing is **structurally consistent with the [[concepts/saucer-as-hoax-default|hoax-default disposition]]**: ^[inferred] all reports fall into one of three categories — **(a) optical reflections, (b) hoaxes, (c) imagination/hysteria** — none of which require a real flying object.

## Why this memo matters

### Earliest formal optical-skepticism framework in the corpus

The 12 Jul 1947 reflection-theory memo is **18 days before AFBIR-CO's 30 Jul 1947 analytical breakdown**. ^[inferred] If the AFBIR-CO author had access to the reflection-theory memo, they would have had to **rule out the reflection hypothesis case-by-case** for each of the 18 selected sightings. ^[inferred] The AFBIR-CO breakdown's **field set does not include "Reflection Hypothesis Eliminated"** ^[extracted] — i.e. AFBIR-CO did not formally apply the reflection-theory test to its sample. **The two analytical instruments coexist without cross-reference**, leaving the reflection theory uncontested in the AAF analytical record until at least the 23 Sep 1947 Twining memo. ^[inferred]

The reflection-theory memo's existence in serial 130 — alongside AFBIR-CO's "Something is really flying around" finding — captures the **AAF's 1947 analytical bifurcation**: ^[inferred]

| Position | Document | Date |
|---|---|---|
| **Optical artifact** (reflection theory) | 12 Jul 1947 anonymous memo | 12 Jul 1947 |
| **Real but unidentified** (AFBIR-CO + Newfoundland tranche) | OCR p. 35 6-claim summary; AFBIR-CO 21-field breakdown | ~25-30 Jul 1947 |

The wave's analytical interpretation **fractures within the same 18-day window** in the same packet retained at FBI HQ — and the reflection-theory frame is preserved alongside the real-but-unidentified frame **without resolution of the contradiction**. ^[inferred] This is **paradigm-defining for the corpus**: the AAF's 1947 reception of the disc wave is **internally inconsistent at the analytical level**, and serial 130 is the **single ingest artifact that captures the bifurcation in one place**. ^[inferred]

### Distinct from the hoax-default frame

The reflection-theory memo is **structurally distinct from** [[concepts/saucer-as-hoax-default|the hoax-default frame]]: ^[inferred]

| Frame | Hoax-default | Reflection-theory |
|---|---|---|
| **Assumes a physical artifact?** | Yes — a fabricated disc | No — pure optical illusion |
| **Investigative disposition** | Recover the artifact, characterize the prank | Educate observers about reflections; no investigation |
| **Witness implication** | Witness fooled by deliberate fabrication | Witness fooled by their own perception |
| **Field-office application** | FBI Bureau Bulletin No. 42 paragraph B | None — the memo does not propose institutional handling |
| **Active in the corpus** | Strongly active 1947-48 (≥9 confirmed prank-disc events) | Single-document datapoint; no derivative wiki pages |

The two frames converge structurally — **both deny the existence of a genuine flying object** — but **mechanistically diverge** on what produces the witness experience. ^[inferred]

### Anonymous authorship

The memo is **unsigned** in the OCR record, with **no apparent letterhead, originating command, or addressee block**. ^[extracted] The page-3 footer carries "**(2) / 47**" — suggesting the memo is **page 2 of a 7-page document numbered 47** in the serial-130 page sequence. ^[inferred] The **OCAMA Tinker Field 24 Jul 1947 + 30 Jul 1947 memos** ([[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-serial-130|serial 130]] OCR pp. 53-54) immediately precede the memo in the packet — possibly indicating OCAMA Tinker Field as the originating organization. ^[ambiguous] The corpus does not yet confirm authorship.

## Open threads

- **Identify the memo's author** — possibly an OCAMA Tinker Field analyst, possibly an AAF HQ public-affairs officer, possibly an outside engineer (the reflection-theory mechanism is consistent with **automotive engineering / glass-window optics expertise**). ^[open]
- **Determine whether the memo was distributed** beyond FBI HQ — the reflection theory does not appear in the [[concepts/project-sign|Project SIGN]] incident summaries; if SIGN evaluators applied the reflection theory case-by-case, that should appear in their reports. ^[open]
- **Cross-reference the reflection-theory frame** to **post-1947 mainstream public-press skepticism** — the memo's automotive-ventilation-wing-mirror argument may have **leaked into press explanations** or **influenced AFBIR-CO's analytical-bias-toward-real-object position**. ^[inferred]
- **Locate the corpus's earliest meteorological-balloon frame** as a comparable optical-skepticism alternative — the OCR p. 35 Newfoundland-tranche 6-claim summary names "**meteorological balloon**" as a candidate (Element a-Report 16 Elmendorf "Resembled balloon"); the relationship between the reflection-theory frame and the balloon-frame in 1947 AAF analytical practice is open. ^[open]
