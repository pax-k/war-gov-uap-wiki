# Sighting — Durango, Mexico (18 March 1950) — Robles Jr. flying-saucer photographs

A **photographically-evidenced UAP observation over the streets of Durango, Mexico**, by Mexican civil-engineering student **[[entities/german-horacio-robles-jr|Germán Horacio Robles Jr.]]** (Escuela Nacional de Ingeniería ^[inferred]). Reported in a Mexican newspaper masthead-dated **Thursday 18 March 1950** ^[ambiguous] (OCR'd as *"JUEVES 18 DE MARZO DE 1880"*; corrected to 1950 by surrounding evidence — see below). The newspaper clipping was enclosed in [[entities/miguel-angel-garcia-macias|Miguel Ángel García Macías]]'s 19 Mar 1950 unsolicited letter to the *"President of the Commission of Scientific Investigation of the United States of North America"* and arrived at the FBI New York office on **APR 7 1950** stamp, where it entered FBI HQ administrative file **62-HQ-83894** as **serial 220**. ^[extracted]

## Reported morphology

Robles Jr.'s description of the object, as relayed by the newspaper: ^[extracted]

- **Geometry**: *"a **double truncated cone, joined at its bases**"* — two cone frusta joined at their wider ends, producing a biconvex / lens silhouette.
- **Propulsion**: *"moves by something **like jet propulsion**"*.
- **Maneuvering**: *"displaces itself by **changing position** in order to use its motive force in the direction desired"*.
- **Aspect dependence**: *"For which reason it can be seen under apparently **different forms**"* — i.e., the apparent silhouette varies with viewing aspect.

## Newspaper-clipping content (translated by Mrs. Sophia Saliba for the FBI) ^[extracted]

> *"Mr. Germán Horacio Robles Jr., student at the National School of Engineering, is one of the thousands of persons who have seen 'with their own eyes' the flying saucers or disks which have become the general topic of commentaries and have given rise to the most varied hypotheses. But at the same time the young man himself is the first to have photographed those strange apparatuses. He observed one of them and was able to take its picture against the skies of the city of Durango. (1) Picture of the 'flying saucer' at an altitude of 8000 to 9000 feet above the streets of Durango. The apparatus seems to be a double truncated cone, joined at its bases which moves by something like jet propulsion and which displaces itself by changing position in order to use its motive force in the direction desired, for which reason it can be seen under apparently different forms. (2) Conception of Mr. Robles Jr., concerning the real form of the strange mechanical objects drawn by him. (3) Another aspect of the flying saucer. (4) The future civil engineer, Horacio Robles Jr. sketched in our editorial offices after having reported his observations on the flying disk and after having handed over the pictures of this apparatus which he had obtained."*

## Source caveats — what the FBI artifact preserves and what it doesn't

- The artifact preserves the **Mistral-OCR'd printed newspaper page**, including 5 image regions (img-3 through img-5 are the saucer photographs; img-6 / img-7 / img-8 are detail/sketch panels; img-0 / img-1 / img-2 / img-9 are García Macías's hand-drawn diagrams of *"global-conic and semi-global stability"* and *"wings and propellers"*). ^[extracted]
- The original Robles **photographs themselves** are **not preserved in the artifact** — only the **printed-newspaper reproductions** of them. The photograph custody chain after publication is unknown. ^[inferred]
- A **second OCR pass** of the same newspaper page (artifact page index 5) is degraded — Robles's name OCRs as *"George Horacio Rodes Jr."* and altitude OCRs as *"3000 to 5000 feet"* (vs. the cleaner pass's 8,000–9,000 ft). The wiki uses the **cleaner OCR pass and the Spanish-original**. ^[ambiguous]
- The newspaper's identity is **not preserved** in the masthead OCR — the visible header reads *"FEDERACIÓN DE LA REPÚBLICA / LIC. MIGUEL LANZ DURET / MEXICO, D. F. / ... / GERARDO LÓPEZ Y FUENTES / NUMERO 12.896"*. The combination of **Lic. Miguel Lanz Duret** (longtime director of *El Universal* in Mexico City) and the **issue number 12,896** most plausibly identifies the paper as ***El Universal*** (Mexico City) of Thursday 18 March 1950. ^[inferred]

## Position in the corpus

- **First Mexican-located UAP sighting in the corpus**. ^[inferred] All prior Mexico links in the wiki are post-2000 ([[entities/amembassy-mexico|2023 DOS cable]], [[entities/jaime-maussan|Jaime Maussan]] 2023). Robles predates them by **73 years**.
- **Earliest 1950s photographically-evidenced UAP sighting in the corpus.** ^[inferred] The corpus's only earlier photographically-evidenced UAP sighting is the [[references/sighting-rhodes-phoenix-1947-07-07|William Rhodes Phoenix photo]] of 7 Jul 1947 ([[references/project-sign-incident-summaries-1-100|Inc 40 in the Project SIGN 1–100 bundle]]).
- **Predates Project SIGN's terminal sequential incident** ([[references/sighting-jackson-ms-1949-01|Inc 233 Jackson MS, 1 Jan 1949]]) by **~14 months**. The sighting is not in any of the three Project SIGN bundles in the corpus, presumably because (a) it post-dates the SIGN-numbered intake window, which was transitioning to Project GRUDGE in early 1949–1950, and (b) it originates outside USAF intake reach in any case (Mexican civilian + Mexican newspaper). ^[inferred]
- **Reaches FBI HQ paper not via field-office intake but via foreign-mail.** Distinct from every prior FBI-originated UAP-adjacent record in the corpus, which arrived via field-office paths (1957 WH-routed letter, 1958 citizen phone-in, 1966 civilian-org letter, 2023 FD-302 evidentiary intake). The 1956 Barker-jacket clipping ([[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-serial-403]]) reaches HQ paper via **HQ-side clipping of public publications**. The Robles + García Macías case reaches HQ paper via **inbound foreign civilian mail with FBI in-house translation** — a **distinct intake channel**. ^[inferred] See [[entities/fbi-hq-62-83894-file]] for the multi-channel-accumulation reading this case forces.

## Why this matters for 62-HQ-83894's file function

Serial 403 (1956) framed the file as a **topic-clearinghouse for publicly-available materials** (a clipped book jacket). Serial 220 (1950) **broadens** that reading: the file also accumulates **unsolicited inbound civilian saucer-correspondence**, including foreign-language items the FBI **translates in-house** (Mrs. Sophia Saliba) before filing. The two-serial spread shows the file is a **multi-channel UAP-topic accumulation file**, not a single-channel clearinghouse. ^[inferred] See the parent file's revised reading at [[entities/fbi-hq-62-83894-file]].
