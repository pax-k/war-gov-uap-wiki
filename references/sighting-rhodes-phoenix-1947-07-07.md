# Sighting — Rhodes / Phoenix Photo (7 Jul 1947)

A **single-witness photographically-evidenced sighting** at **4333 N 14th St, Phoenix AZ** (37° 27' N, 112° 3' W) on **7 July 1947 at 1600 local**, by **William Albert Rhodes**. Filed as **Project SIGN Incident 40** in [[references/project-sign-incident-summaries-1-100|the 1–100 bundle]]. ^[extracted]

This is the **corpus's first photographically-evidenced UAP event**. Rhodes produced **two photographs** — one at the object's lowest altitude (~2,000 ft) during the second spiral, and one as it turned out of its second spiral and started upward at 45°. ^[extracted] (An earlier event, Inc 11 Frank Ryman / Lake City Seattle, 4 Jul 1947, attempted a photograph but produced only a "tiny light spot against the dark background of the evening sky" — not a morphology-resolving photograph.)

## Why this event matters

### Corpus firsts established by this case

1. **First photographically-evidenced UAP event in the corpus**. Two photographs preserved in the file. ^[extracted]
2. **First documented "cockpit canopy" morphology in the corpus**. Rhodes describes a clearly-visible cockpit-like dome at the center of the elliptical body extending toward the back and beneath. ^[extracted] This morphology recurs in **Inc 79 Minozewski Apr 1947 Richmond pibal** (which describes a "**dull metallic dome-like-round top**"), establishing a continuous April–July 1947 morphology signature. ^[inferred]
3. **First "tapered airfoil from wing tip to wing tip" morphology** in the corpus. Recurs in flying-wing-class observations (Inc 37 J. L. Clark, 14 Oct 1947 Phoenix) and forms the basis for the [[concepts/horten-flying-wing]] candidate-explanation track that AMC pursued formally in 1948. ^[inferred]

### The "Flapjack" hypothesis ruled out

Rhodes initially considered the [[concepts/horten-flying-wing|Navy's "Flying Flapjack" XF5U-1]] as a candidate identification, then rejected it: *"He thought that the object was the Navy's new 'Flapjack Plane' but altered his opinion since the object seemed to have no landing gears and no propellers. (The Navy ship is illustrated as having both)."* ^[extracted]

This is **the earliest preserved witness-side ruleout of a conventional aircraft hypothesis** in the SIGN intake stream — Rhodes effectively does the agent's identification work as part of his own statement. ^[inferred] The Flapjack hypothesis was a **persistent conventional candidate** through the 1947 wave (cf. Inc 29 Richard Rankin, 14 Jun 1947 Portland, who explicitly called the objects "**flying flapjacks**" matching XF5U-1 illustrations).

### Pre-photographic chain-of-custody

The bundle does not preserve the photographs themselves — only the witness's textual description and the evaluation that *"Rhodes managed to take one photograph of the object at its lowest point (about 2000 ft) and another as it turned out of its second spiral and started upward."* ^[extracted] The photographs' subsequent custody chain (whether they reached AMC T-2 in original form, were retained by Rhodes, or transferred to FBI / Project GRUDGE) is **not in the 1–100 bundle**. ^[ambiguous]

(Subsequent UAP historiography — outside the bundle's scope — frequently identifies Rhodes' Phoenix photos as one of the first physical-evidence specimens that reached USAF investigation. The bundle's silence on the post-1947 chain-of-custody is consistent with the photos being treated as separately-filed evidence.)

### Wind / acoustic anomaly

Rhodes notes that *"the 10–15 MPH wind velocity which caused a rustling of tree leaves might have made it impossible for him to hear any noise connected with the object"* — i.e., he flagged for the agent that **his negative-acoustic finding was confounded by ambient wind noise**. ^[extracted] This is a rare instance of **witness-side methodological self-skepticism** in the early SIGN corpus. ^[inferred]

## The same-week Phoenix and Arizona context

Two further 1947 Arizona events are filed in the bundle:

- **Inc 78 — Lt William G. McGinty USN** (P-80 student Williams Fld) — 30 Jun 1947 0910 MST: 2 circular 8-ft light-grey objects "**inconceivable speeds straight down**" over Grand Canyon, descending from 25,000 ft. ^[extracted]
- **Inc 37 — J. L. Clark + Mr Anderson** (mine owner / pilot + AAF gunner-pilot) — 14 Oct 1947 1200: **black flying-wing-without-tail object**, ~3-ft-as-seen, 350 MPH NW→SE at 8/10,000 ft, 45 sec, near Cave Creek AZ. ^[extracted]

The Rhodes Phoenix event sits between these two, establishing **a 1947 AZ-airspace cluster** spanning Grand Canyon → Phoenix → Cave Creek with three distinct morphologies (8-ft sphere descending, 20–30 ft tapered ellipse with cockpit dome, black flying-wing). ^[inferred]

## Open questions

- **Photograph custody chain post-1947.** The bundle preserves only the textual narrative; the photographs themselves are not in the 1–100 bundle. ^[ambiguous]
- **The 1/4–1/2-mi-radius spiral.** Rhodes' spiral-radius estimate, combined with the 400–600 MPH speed and the 5,000-to-2,000-ft altitude change, implies a **specific descent g-load** — recoverable from the witness's geometry if his estimates hold. The bundle does not pursue this analysis. ^[inferred]
- **The cockpit-canopy + dome morphology** matches the Apr 1947 Minozewski pibal observation (Inc 79) and **also matches the post-2017 Navy Tic-Tac descriptive vocabulary** (lozenge-with-dome-like-cabin). Whether the 1947 cockpit-canopy / dome morphology represents a continuous signature into the modern corpus is an open synthesis question. ^[inferred]
