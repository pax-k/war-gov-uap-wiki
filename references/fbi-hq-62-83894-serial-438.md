# FBI HQ File 62-HQ-83894 — Serial 438 (Byrnes Albuquerque Socorro NM 24 Apr 1964 report)

A **40-OCR-page Mistral-OCR'd declassified artifact** from the **FBI Central Records Center / FBI HQ**. The 40 OCR pages decompose into a Records Center cover slip, **four sequential carbon-copy distributions of the same 10-page Byrnes-authored narrative**, and a 2-page hand-drawn ground-trace diagram set. ^[inferred] Filed at **Class 0062 / Case 83894 / Sub —/ Vol 1 / Serial 438 ONLY** with secondary marker **HQ-0000-86775B: HQ**, processing tag **`8/11/1274161 RRP0031XQ3`**, *DO NOT DESTROY*-class declassification. *"Declassification authority derived from FBI Automatic Declassification Guide, issued 24 May 2007"*. ^[extracted]

This is the **third artifact ingested** in the planned 17-serial 62-HQ-83894 wiki series — and the **first counter-example to the prior file-function reading**. Serials 220 (1950) and 403 (1956) jointly framed 62-HQ-83894 as a **content-accumulation-only file** (clipped publications + inbound civilian mail, no FBI-authored investigative content). Serial 438 is **FBI-authored field investigative product** — same agency, same HQ file, fundamentally different intake channel. ^[inferred] See [[entities/fbi-hq-62-83894-file]] for the refined parent-file reading.

**Section-9 cross-retention.** ^[extracted] The same Byrnes report is **also retained in the sequential filing of [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-9|62-HQ-83894 section 9]]**, where it sits alongside the **27 Apr 1964 Albuquerque teletype (serial 435)** that includes the **parallel 26 Apr 1964 La Madera NM Orlando Gallegos sighting** (~150 mi north of Socorro, blue-white-flame port-hole object 12-14 ft high) and the **28 Apr 1964 HQ → Albuquerque airtel (serial 436)** containing the canonical **Director's hedge**: *"Our policy is not to investigate but assure that Air Force is cognizant. Military is investigating and Albuquerque Office is maintaining liaison."* ^[extracted] The hedge confirms the Bureau's [[concepts/fbi-saucer-policy-stasis-1958-1962|policy stasis]] held even for the file's most kinematically anomalous case. ^[inferred]

The artifact documents the **24 April 1964 Lonnie Zamora landing case at Socorro, New Mexico** — see [[references/sighting-socorro-nm-1964-04-24]] for the consolidated sighting page.

## What's in the artifact

The 40 OCR'd pages (Mistral OCR pages 0–39) decompose into **three documentary layers**:

1. **Page 0 — FBI Records Center routing/cover slip.** Carries the Class–Case–Sub–Vol–Serial filing block (`0062 83894 1 438 ONLY`), the secondary marker `HQ-0000-86775B: HQ`, the processing tag `8/11/1274161 RRP0031XQ3`, and the FBI 2007 Automatic Declassification Guide note. ^[extracted]

2. **Pages 1–36 — four sequential carbon-copy distributions of the same 10-page Albuquerque report.** Each copy is the same Byrnes-authored narrative repeated verbatim (with minor OCR drift across the four passes). ^[inferred] Page-numbering breakdown:
   - **Copy A**: artifact pages 1–10 (Albuquerque cover sheet + 10-page narrative, broken out into a 3-page "physical-trace observation by SA Byrnes" section + a 7-page "data obtained from Officer Zamora" interview transcript)
   - **Copy B**: artifact pages 11–16 (same content, OCR-pass 2)
   - **Copy C**: artifact pages 17–26 (same content, OCR-pass 3)
   - **Copy D**: artifact pages 27–36 (same content, OCR-pass 4)
   The four-copy distribution is consistent with **standard FBI carbon-routing practice** — original retained at Albuquerque field office, copies distributed to HQ + FBI Records Center + (likely) additional addressees. ^[inferred]

3. **Pages 37, 39 — hand-drawn ground-trace diagrams** showing the four "footprint" rectangular depressions, with measurements (`9' 7½"`, `14' 9½"`, `10½"`, `90°` orientation), three burned-grass patches, and "apparent direction of burn or heat" notation; a fifth burn marked outside the four-depression quadrilateral. ^[extracted] Page 38 contains an OCR'd footer reading **`62-83894-738`** — almost certainly an OCR digit-confusion error (4 ↔ 7) or a stamping irregularity in the original; the cover-slip on page 0 reads serial `438`. ^[ambiguous]

The artifact is the **first FBI-authored investigative product in the 62-HQ-83894 file as ingested in the wiki**. ^[inferred] No earlier serial in the file (220, 403) carries any FBI-authored investigative narrative.

## What the report contains

### Part 1 — physical-trace investigation by SA Byrnes (artifact pages 1–3 / Albuquerque pages 1–2)

The lead author is **[[entities/d-arthur-byrnes-jr|Special Agent D. Arthur Byrnes, Jr.]]**, FBI Albuquerque field office. ^[extracted] On the **late afternoon of 24 April 1964**, Byrnes was conducting unrelated business at the **State Police office in Socorro, New Mexico**, when the office received a radio call from **[[entities/lonnie-zamora|Officer Lonnie Zamora]]** (Socorro Police Department) reporting that *"some unknown object … 'landed and has taken off'"* about one mile southwest of the town. ^[extracted] Byrnes finished his work and **proceeded to the site at approximately 6:00 PM the same day**, where he found Zamora, **Sergeant M. S. Chavez** (NMSP), **Officer Ted Jordan** (NMSP), and **Undersheriff Jim Luckie** assembled. ^[extracted]

Byrnes's witness-character note for Zamora is **load-bearing** for the FBI-side reading of the case:

> *"It may be noted that it has been the observation of Agent Byrnes that Officer Zamora, known intimately for approximately five years, is well regarded as a sober, industrious, and conscientious officer and not given to fantasy. Officer Zamora was noted to be perfectly sober and somewhat agitated over his experience."* ^[extracted]

Byrnes's **direct on-site physical-trace observations** (the FBI's own evidentiary work product, not Zamora's account) are: ^[extracted]

| Trace | Geometry | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| **Four indentations** | "Approximately sixteen by six inches rectangular", ~2 inches deep, "an object going into the earth at an angle from a center line", "pushed some earth to the far side" | Quadrilateral arrangement, consistent with landing-leg footprints |
| **Three burned grass clumps** | Inside the four-depression quadrilateral | Other clumps in the same area "appeared not to be disturbed" |
| **One burned area** | Outside the four-depression quadrilateral | — |
| **Three smooth circular marks** | ~4 inches diameter, ~⅛ inch deep, "as if a jar lid had gently been pushed into the sand" | Penetrating sandy earth |

Byrnes notes that **no other person was in the area** the night of 24 April 1964, **no other object** was noted, and **no houses or inhabited dwellings** were in sight. ^[extracted]

Standard FBI control disclaimer footer: *"This document contains neither recommendations nor conclusions of any kind. It is the property of the FBI, and is a loan to your agency; it and/or its contents are not to be distributed outside your agency."* ^[extracted] The same control disclaimer appears on the 1957 Krasuski Blank Memo and on both 2023 FD-302s — i.e. this is the **standard FBI investigative-product disclaimer**, not unique to the 1964 case. ^[inferred]

### Part 2 — Zamora interview transcript (artifact pages 7–10 / Albuquerque pages 1–10)

A 10-page first-person Zamora narrative — collected by Byrnes the night of 24 April 1964 and the early morning of 25 April 1964. ^[extracted] Identifying block:

> *"Lonnie Zamora, 606 Reservoir Street, Socorro, New Mexico, 835-1134, Officer Socorro Police Department about five years, office phone 835-0941, now on 2:00 P.M. to 10:00 P.M. shift."* ^[extracted]

The narrative reads as a **carefully-collected witness statement** — Zamora's own phrasing preserved verbatim, time-anchored at granularity of seconds for the close-encounter window, and explicitly retains epistemic hedging (*"three blocks ahead", "very rough guess", "took I guess about six seconds"*). ^[inferred]

Sequence as Zamora narrates it:
1. **5:45 PM 24 Apr 1964** — Zamora was chasing a speeding black 1964 Chevrolet south from the Socorro Courthouse, three blocks behind. (Zamora identifies the suspect-driver as possibly *"Floyd Reynolds' boy, Vivian, about 17"*.) ^[extracted]
2. **Heard a roar + saw a flame** to the southwest, ~½–1 mile distant. *"Came to mind that a dynamite shack in that area had blown up."* The flame was *"bluish and sort of orange too"*, *"a stream down — a funnel type — narrower at top than at bottom"*, *"about twice as wide at bottom as top, and about four times as high as top was wide"*. Zamora abandoned the chase to investigate. ^[extracted]
3. **Drove up a steep gravel hill** (60-ft, "loose gravel and rock"), required three attempts. The roar persisted. *"Sound distinctly from high to low until it disappeared."* Both car windows down; no other spectators. ^[extracted]
4. **First sighting of the object** at **150–200 yards south of the road**, off-road. *"Looked, at first, like a car turned upside down."* Saw **two people in white coveralls** very close to the object. *"One of these persons seemed to turn and look straight at my car and seemed startled — seemed to quickly jump somewhat."* Object was *"like aluminum — it was whitish against the mesa background, but not chrome"*. He saw what appeared to be **two slanted "legs"** at the bottom of the object — *"the object might have been about three and a half feet from the ground at that time"*. ^[extracted]
5. **Radio call** to Sheriff radio operator [[entities/nep-lopez|Nep Lopez]]: *"Socorro 2 to Socorro, possible 10-44 (accident); I'll be 10-6 (busy) out of the car checking the car down in the arroyo."* ^[extracted]
6. **As he got out of his car** he heard *"about two or three loud 'thumps,' like someone possibly hammering or shutting a door or doors hard"* — separated by ≤1 second each. The two persons in white coveralls were no longer visible. ^[extracted]
7. **Roar resumed and intensified** — *"started low frequency quickly, then roar rose in frequency (higher tone) and in loudness — from loud to very loud. At same time as roar saw flame. Flame was under the object. Object was starting to go straight up — slowly up."* Flame *"light blue and at bottom was sort of orange color"*, *"possibly a four feet area"*. *"No smoke, except dust in immediate area."* ^[extracted]
8. **Zamora ran** away from the object northward, putting his car between him and the object. *"Bumped leg on car — back fender area. Car facing southwest. Glasses fell to ground, left them there."* ^[extracted]
9. **Object morphology** at close range: *"Object was [REDACTED — hand-drawn shape] in shape. It was smooth — no windows or doors. As roar started, it was still on or near ground. Noted red lettering of some type, like [REDACTED — hand-drawn insignia]."* ^[ambiguous] The OCR renders the redacted hand-drawn shape and insignia as `☐` placeholders — these are **deliberate FBI redactions of Zamora's hand-drawn pictograph**, not OCR failures. ^[inferred] The insignia was **about 2½ feet high and about 2 feet wide**, in the middle of the object. Zamora drew the insignia for Sgt Chavez at the scene before Chavez arrived. ^[extracted]
10. **Object rose to about 20–25 ft** (level of car) over ~6 seconds, then went **southwest in a straight line at 10–15 ft above ground**, *"cleared the dynamite shack by about three feet"* (shack was ~8 ft tall). ^[extracted]
11. **Sound transition**: roar abruptly stopped → *"a sharp tone whine from high tone to low tone"* lasting ~1 second → **complete silence**. ^[extracted]
12. **Departure**: object went over Box Canyon / Six Mile Canyon Mountain, traveling fast — *"It had no flame whatsoever as it was traveling over the ground, and no smoke or noise."* Total close-range observation duration: *"possibly twenty seconds — just a guess"*. ^[extracted]
13. **Radio**: Zamora radioed Nep Lopez *"to look out the window, to see if you could see an object."* Lopez asked, *"What is it?"* Zamora answered, *"It looks like a balloon."* ^[extracted]
14. **Cleanup notes**: *"Feeling in good health. Last drink — two or three beers — was over a month ago. Noted no odors. Noted no sounds other than described."* ^[extracted]
15. **Sgt Chavez arrival**: Chavez found Zamora *"sweating … white, very pale"*. They walked to the spot together; Chavez pointed out the tracks. ^[extracted]

### Part 3 — site-trace diagrams (artifact pages 37, 39)

Hand-drawn diagrams of the ground traces with measurements: ^[extracted]

| Diagram | Content |
|---|---|
| Page 37 | Top-down sketch of the four "footprints" rectangle + dirt-burn measurements (`9' 7½"`, `10½"`, `14' 9½"`) + 90° orientation indicators + "general area of footprints" |
| Page 39 | Numbered footprints `#1`–`#4` with inter-footprint distances `9'4"`, `6'4"`, `10'8"`; numbered burns `Buen #1`–`Buen #4`; "apparent direction of burn or heat" arrow |

The diagrams are **field-sketch quality** (not professional drafting) ^[inferred] and are consistent with **at-scene observation by Byrnes, Chavez, and/or Zamora**. ^[inferred] The Spanish-language token *"Buen"* on page 39 (probably a contraction of *"quemado"* "burned" or a pencil shorthand ^[ambiguous]) suggests the diagrams may have been annotated by Chavez or another Spanish-bilingual party. ^[inferred]

## What 62-HQ-83894 contains here, summarized

- A **routing/cover slip** (FBI Records Center filing instrument).
- A **10-page FBI-authored field-investigation narrative** by SA Byrnes — physical-trace observations + Zamora interview transcript — distributed in **4 carbon copies**.
- A **2-page hand-drawn ground-trace diagram set** with measurements.

This is **substantive FBI investigative work product**: same-day on-site physical-trace observation, character assessment of the principal witness, full witness-statement transcript, hand-drawn site documentation. **No FD-302** (the FD-302 form was not the standard FBI narrative-interview instrument in 1964 ^[inferred]); the instrument is a typed Albuquerque-letterhead investigative narrative carrying the same control-disclaimer footer that appears on the 1957 Krasuski Blank Memo and the 2023 FD-302s. ^[extracted]

## What 62-HQ-83894 appears to be — refined again, with N=3

Serials 220 + 403 framed 62-HQ-83894 as a **content-accumulation-only file** (clipped publications + inbound civilian mail). ^[inferred] **Serial 438 is a counter-example.** The 1964 Albuquerque report is **FBI-authored field investigative product**, not clipped or inbound material — same HQ file, structurally distinct content layer.

The **refined reading** is that 62-HQ-83894 is a **UAP-topic file at FBI HQ** that holds **whatever UAP-topic paper crosses HQ regardless of whether the paper is FBI-authored or externally-sourced**. ^[inferred] The intake-channel inventory is now: ^[inferred]

| Channel | Serial | Year | Content type |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) Clipped public publication** | 403 | 1956 | Publisher's book jacket of [[references/barker-1956-they-knew-too-much\|Barker's *They Knew Too Much About Flying Saucers*]] |
| **(b) Unsolicited inbound civilian correspondence** (foreign-language, FBI in-house translated) | 220 | Apr 1950 | Spanish-language saucer letter + enclosed Mexican press clipping + sender's diagrams |
| **(c) FBI field-office investigative report on a high-profile UAP case routed up to HQ** | **438 (this serial)** | **May 1964** | **10-page Albuquerque field-office report on the Socorro NM landing case, with physical-trace observations + witness interview + diagrams, distributed in 4 carbon copies** |

The **agency-posture** reading is also refined: 62-HQ-83894 is **not** "the file in which the FBI accumulates UAP-topic content but never investigates" — it is **the file in which the FBI accumulates UAP-topic content INCLUDING when the content is the Bureau's own investigative work product on a high-profile case**. ^[inferred] The "content-accumulation, not investigation" framing on serial 220 / serial 403 is **partially refuted** by serial 438 — at HQ, the file accumulates investigative product as readily as clippings. ^[inferred]

The file's **date span** now widens to **1950 → 1964 (≥ 14 years)**. ^[inferred] (The terminal upper bound is still unknown; later serials may extend further.)

## Position in the FBI corpus

This artifact bridges the **1957 Krasuski Detroit case** and the **2023 FD-302 era** with a **mid-1960s FBI-authored field investigation**. ^[inferred] The wiki's FBI cross-era table grows from 6 instruments to 7:

| Era | Office | Intake channel | Instrument | Wiki anchor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Apr 1950 | HQ | Unsolicited inbound foreign civilian mail | Records Center cover slip + translated letter + enclosed clipping + diagrams | [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-serial-220]] |
| 1956 | HQ | HQ-side clipping of US publication | Records Center slip + clipped book jacket | [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-serial-403]] |
| Nov 1957 | Detroit | WH-routed citizen letter | Blank Memo + AIRTEL + Director URGENT teletype | [[references/fbi-detroit-100-26505-krasuski-1957]] |
| Apr 1958 | Detroit | Citizen phone-in | SF-64 Office Memorandum | [[references/fbi-detroit-100-18221-serial-844-1958]] |
| **24 Apr 1964 → 8 May 1964** | **Albuquerque (NM)** | **Same-day FBI agent on-site at the local State Police office when the local PD officer reported the sighting** | **Albuquerque-letterhead investigative narrative + Zamora witness statement + hand-drawn site-trace diagrams; 10-page report; 4 carbon copies routed to HQ as 62-HQ-83894 serial 438** | **THIS ARTIFACT** |
| Oct 1966 | Detroit | Civilian UFO-org inquiry | SAC outgoing letter (USAF redirect) | [[references/fbi-detroit-100-26505-krasuski-1957]] |
| 2023 | (redacted) | FD-302 evidentiary intake | FD-302 narrative form ×2 | [[references/fbi-fd302-drone-pilot-redacted-2023]], [[references/fbi-fd302-serial-3-driver-redacted-2023]] |

The **same-day on-site agent presence** intake channel is **structurally novel** for the FBI corpus. ^[inferred] All prior FBI intake required either (a) a routing pathway (citizen letter, phone-in, civilian-org inquiry, WH forward) or (b) a passive accumulation gesture (clipping, inbound mail). The 1964 Socorro case **happens to be witnessed by a local LE officer who radios it in while a federal LE agent is physically present at the State Police office** — a **same-physical-location-and-time accident** that puts the FBI on-scene within ~15 minutes of the radio call, faster than any FBI response time elsewhere in the corpus. ^[inferred]

The **field-office attribution** also extends the corpus: prior FBI artifacts are HQ + Detroit + 2023-redacted-field-office. Serial 438 introduces **Albuquerque** as a new field-office attribution — geographically appropriate (Socorro NM is in the Albuquerque field-office territory ^[inferred]), but methodologically novel for a corpus that until now had **all field-office work concentrated in Detroit**.
