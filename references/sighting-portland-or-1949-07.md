# Sighting — Portland / Troutdale / Mount Hood OR (30 Jul 1949)

A multi-witness pacing-plus-flare-dropping incident in the **Portland, Troutdale, and Mount Hood, Oregon** area between **2100P and 2130P on 30 July 1949**, including an attempted intercept by an airline captain. Filed by McChord FSC under TCM 452 dated 1 August 1949, signed Lt Col Earl J. Livesay; routed to AMC, Attn: MCIAXO-3.

## Witnesses

- **Capt Thrush** — pilot, **Northwest Airlines**, flying a **Martin 202** approaching Portland from the east.
- **Robert Henery** — CAA Tower Operator, Portland Airport.
- **B. J. Brasford** — CAA Tower Operator, Portland Airport (and held names of four additional unnamed witnesses).
- **H. W. Penhallegan** — Civilian Instructor Pilot, Western Skyways, Troutdale, OR.

## Sequence of events

1. **2110P**: Capt Thrush, at 6,000 ft and ~5 mi west of Stevenson WA, observed an object displaying **two red lights and one white light** — "non-standard lighting." Object positioned **behind and slightly to the left** of his aircraft and **maintained that relative position** all the way to Troutdale.
2. **At Troutdale**: Capt Thrush heard Portland Tower instruct other aircraft to **hold over the airport** because of an **unidentified flying object dropping flares**. Two distinct phenomena were now active simultaneously: the pacing-light object behind his M-202, and the flare-dropping object over Portland.
3. **Intercept attempt**: Capt Thrush executed a **180° turn** to intercept the pacing-light object. The object then **turned to the southeast and easily pulled away**. Thrush's indicated airspeed during the attempted intercept was **210 mph**.
4. **Penhallegan's vantage** (Troutdale traffic pattern, 1,300 ft indicated): saw the object at 500–800 ft above his altitude, ~20 mi to the east.
5. **Conventional-air-traffic ruleout**: a B-29 (AF-7758) was over Portland at 2130P at 7,500 ft northbound. Pilot questioned: did **not drop flares**, did **not circle Portland**, did **not see anything unusual**. ^[extracted]

## Signature class

- **Behavior**: pacing a crewed aircraft, holding a fixed relative position, breaking off on intercept, accelerating away easily.
- **Lighting**: non-standard 2-red / 1-white configuration.
- **Co-occurring "flare dropping" object** over Portland — a distinct morphology not characterized further in the report.

## Significance

The pacing-then-evasive-on-intercept pattern is functionally **identical to the [[concepts/uap-aircraft-engagement|UAP-aircraft engagement]] morphology** documented in the 2025 [[references/sighting-helicopter-orb-encounter-2025|helicopter orb encounter]] — including the specific "easy out-acceleration on intercept attempt" detail. The 1949 instance is **76 years earlier** but tactically identical. ^[inferred]

The behavior is also consistent with the [[concepts/foo-fighter-phenomenon|foo-fighter pacing]] reports the [[entities/415th-night-fighter-squadron|415th NFS]] filed at SHAEF in 1944–45 — same pacing, same evasive-on-intercept response. ^[inferred] See [[synthesis/foo-fighters-to-flying-discs]].

This is the **first explicit aircraft-vs-UAP intercept attempt** in the 1949 corpus and the strongest piece of behavioral continuity across the SHAEF / SIGN / modern eras for the engagement-class morphology. ^[inferred]
