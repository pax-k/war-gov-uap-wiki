# Sighting — Mantell / Godman Field 12-hour cross-state cluster (7 Jan 1948)

A **12-hour multi-station UAP observation sequence** on **7 January 1948** running from Edwardsville IL at 0720 CST through nine Kentucky waypoints to Godman Field at Ft Knox, then onward to Lockbourne AAB and Clinton County AAB (Wilmington) in Ohio by 1955 EST. Filed across **17+ correlated Project SIGN incident sheets** in [[references/project-sign-incident-summaries-1-100|the 1–100 bundle]]: **Inc 30 / 30a / 30b / 30c, Inc 32, Inc 33 / 33a / 33b / 33c / 33d / 33e / 33f / 33g, Inc 48 / 48a / 48b / 48c / 48d**, plus aftermath **Inc 49 (Danville KY)** and **Inc 93 (Hartford CT, 11 Jan 1948 follow-on)**. ^[extracted]

The sequence is the **corpus's first UAP-attributed pursuit fatality**: Flight Leader **Capt Thomas F. Mantell** (NG 869, Kentucky Air National Guard) crashed and was killed after his last radio contact with Godman tower at approximately 1515 CST, while pursuing the object at 20,000 ft. ^[extracted]

## The pursuit and crash

Per **Inc 33** (T/Sgt Blackwell), **Inc 33A** (Lt Orner), **Inc 33D** (Capt Carter), **Inc 33E** (Col Hix), and **Inc 33F** (NG 869 radio-conversation reconstruction), the radio-contact sequence is:

- **Initial contact** (~1445 CST): NG 869 reports object at 12 o'clock high, climbing away, "going about 180 MPH — half my speed".
- **At 15,000 ft**: NG 869 reports *"Object directly ahead and above me now and moving about half my speed. **It appears metallic of tremendous size**. I'm trying to close in for better look."*
- **Course revision**: NG 869 reports the object's speed appears now to match his own — approximately 360 MPH.
- **Final contact** (~1515 CST): NG 869 states *"going to 20,000 ft and if no closer will abandon chase"*.
- **No further contact** with NG 869 by Capt Carter (Operations Officer), T/Sgt Blackwell (Tower Operator), or Lt Orner (Detachment Commander). ^[extracted]

NG 800 (wing-man) reported NG 869 "had disappeared". NG 800 returned for fuel and resumed search going to 33,000 ft, 100 mi south, but did not sight the object. NG 800's later assessment: *"it appeared like the reflection of sunlight on an airplane canopy"*. ^[extracted]

The SIGN sheet adds an analytical footnote on Mantell's likely physiological state: *"Apparently, Mantell blacked out at 20,000 ft or proceeded on since the object apparently appeared closer (if such were the case) and then crashed thru lack of oxygen."* — Inc 33D Capt Carter's note. ^[extracted] **The bundle does not preserve the physical accident report**; the inference of hypoxia / lack-of-oxygen is investigator-attributed not coroner-attributed in the file. ^[ambiguous]

| 33B | PFC Stanley Oliver | "**Ice-cream cone topped with red**" — apparently white with red above |
| 33C | Capt J. F. Duesler Jr. | "**Tear-drop, round; later, object seemed fluid**"; silver-bright with no tail; "**appeared 1/10 the size of a full moon**" |
| 33D | Capt Cary W. Carter | "**Round — at times cone shaped**"; whiter than passing clouds; visible through cirrus |
| 33E | Col Guy G. Hix | "**1/4 size of full moon, white, like an umbrella**"; red border at top and bottom alternately via 8× binoculars |
| 33C postscript | Lt Col E. G. Wood | "**Black spot in center at all times**" during evening re-observation; color changing white→blue→red→yellow |
| 30 (Lockbourne) | Capt McGee | "**Oval as if looking at spotlight**, white→yellowish→orange, descending and burning out" |
| 48a (Wilmington) | T/Sgt Ziegler | "**Same color as star only very much brighter, sometimes changing to a more reddish hue then turning white or yellow**, faint exhaust trail when moving" |
| 48b (Wilmington) | Cpl James Hudson | "**Triangle**: when descending the point turned down, when climbing right side up; surrounded with burning gas; green mist trail on ascent/descent" — **4 penciled sketches preserved** |
| 48c (Wilmington) | S/Sgt Haag | "**Resembled the complete wing of an aircraft on fire**"; gain/lose altitude rapidly with little forward motion; red↔green color-change on movement |

The composite is a **shape-shifting / color-changing / fluid-outline luminous object** that variously appears umbrella, parachute, ice-cream cone, tear-drop, cone-with-mist, star, triangle, or burning-aircraft-wing depending on observer / time / instrument. ^[inferred] The Inc 33C "**fluid**" descriptor and Inc 33E "**black spot in the center at all times**" are unique morphology markers in the early corpus. ^[extracted]

## The Venus / comet rationalization — and its rejection

The bundle's analytical footnote (Inc 33C, Capt Duesler) records: *"Later, an astronomer was contacted who attempted to account for this phenomena as either Venus or a comet. (?)"* — with the **question mark in the original text**. ^[extracted]

Capt Carter's footnote on Inc 33D explicitly rejects this rationalization: *"Does not seem to tally with report that the phenomena was 'Venus or a comet'"*. ^[extracted]

The Venus rationalization recurs in subsequent SIGN history (it becomes the [[concepts/project-grudge|Project GRUDGE]] / 1949 official explanation) but is **explicitly questioned by the original investigators inside the SIGN file itself**. ^[inferred]

The phenomenon's behavior also breaks the Venus identification on multiple grounds preserved in the file: NG 869's report of the object at 15,000 ft moving at 360 MPH (Venus is at 26 million miles); the **vertical-axis shape-flip** documented by Cpl Hudson at Wilmington (cone-up to cone-down); the **stationary 1.5-hour position** reported by Col Hix (Venus moves with diurnal rotation); the **ground-fire-correlated descent** at the Wilmington tail. ^[inferred]

## Why this event matters

### Corpus firsts established by this case

1. **First UAP-attributed pursuit fatality in the US institutional record**. Capt Thomas F. Mantell's death is the corpus's first preserved UAP-pursuit casualty. ^[extracted]
2. **First multi-state cross-correlation event**. The 12-hour trajectory across IL → KY → OH waypoints with **independent observers at each station** establishes a same-event-multi-vantage record-keeping pattern that persists into the SIGN/GRUDGE/BLUE BOOK era. ^[inferred]
3. **First multi-rank chain-of-command observation**. Witness pool includes a base CO (Col Hix), Operations Officer (Capt Carter), Detachment Commander (Lt Orner), Tower Chief (T/Sgt Blackwell), enlisted operator (PFC Oliver), and visiting senior officer (Lt Col Wood) — all observing the same object from the same control tower with overlapping but distinct descriptions. ^[extracted]
4. **First theodolite-tracked UAP** (Inc 33A, Lt Orner's evening re-observation from the hangar roof using the Weather Station theodolite). ^[extracted]
5. **First astronomer-class corroborating witness**. Dr Sr Seyfert (Vanderbilt Univ) at Nashville TN, 1630–1645 CST, sees the object SSE 25,000 ft. ^[extracted] **Combined with Inc 77 (John F. Cole, astronomer, S Brooksville ME, 3 Jul 1947)**, this gives the corpus two professional astronomers as 1947–1948 SIGN witnesses.
6. **First sketch-artifact production**. Cpl James Hudson at Clinton County AAB produced **4 penciled sketches** of the object's morphology shifts (Inc 48b). ^[extracted]
7. **First "abandon chase if no closer" pre-radio-cutoff statement** in the corpus. Mantell's last preserved decision: *"going to 20,000 ft and if no closer will abandon chase"* — establishes that the pilot was operating with explicit go/no-go criteria, not blind pursuit. ^[extracted]

### The 1948 engagement-class chronology — corrected

The wiki's [[concepts/uap-aircraft-engagement]] page previously anchored the 1948 engagement-class chronology at:

1. Chiles–Whitted Inc 144 (24 Jul 1948) — earliest 1948 close-approach
2. Gorman Inc 172 (1 Oct 1948) — longest-duration mutual maneuvering
3. Andrews Inc 207 (18 Nov 1948) — sensor-stimulus response

**The Mantell event predates all three** by 6+ months and represents a **structurally distinct fourth sub-signature**: **fatal-pursuit engagement with radio cutoff and unrecovered last-known position**. ^[inferred]

The Mantell sub-signature is the corpus's earliest fatal-pursuit-class event; it does NOT recur in the 1948–49 record (the Chiles–Whitted, Gorman, and Andrews events all end with the witness aircraft intact). ^[inferred]

### Cross-bundle propagation

The Mantell event is referenced **forward** in the [[references/project-sign-incident-summaries-101-172|101–172 bundle]] (Inc 144 Chiles–Whitted's prose explicitly contrasts "high-velocity engagement-class" cases against the Mantell hover-class). ^[inferred] The cross-reference establishes that **SIGN's investigators were treating Mantell as a foundational data point** when classifying subsequent 1948 events.

## Open questions

- **The accident-report continuation file.** Mantell's physical-accident report is **not in the 1–100 bundle**. Whether it is in NARA RG 38 Box 7 (uningested) or routed to Air Force Safety Center adjacent files is open. ^[ambiguous]
- **Venus / comet rationalization upstream.** The SIGN file documents the Venus rationalization being **proposed and questioned** in real-time. Whether Project GRUDGE's 1949 adoption of the Venus explanation as official represents a substantive re-evaluation or a policy decision overriding the SIGN investigators' own contemporary doubt is open. ^[ambiguous]
- **The Madisonville Finch-telescope observation.** Madisonville KY's report of a 250–300-ft circular object at 4 mi altitude going 10 MPH SW (Inc 33g) is **measured via Finch telescope** — making it the corpus's earliest civilian-instrument-measured UAP. ^[extracted] Whether the dimensional estimates can be reconstructed against modern Venus angular-diameter measurements at the relevant date/time is open. ^[inferred]
