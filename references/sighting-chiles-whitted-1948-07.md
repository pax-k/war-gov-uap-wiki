# Sighting — Chiles–Whitted EAL DC-3 Encounter (24 Jul 1948)

A close-approach UAP-aircraft encounter on **24 July 1948 at 02:45 local**, **20 miles south of Montgomery, Alabama**, filed as **Project SIGN Incident 144** in [[references/project-sign-incident-summaries-101-172|the 101–172 incident summary bundle]]. The witnesses were **Captain Clarence Shipe Chiles + Co-Pilot John B. Whitted** of Eastern Air Lines flying a DC-3 toward Mobile/New Orleans, plus passenger Mr McKelvis. ^[extracted]

A **second pilot, Louis Feldwary** (LGA-based, separate aircraft enroute Blackstone VA → Raleigh-Durham), filed an independent corroborating sighting (**Inc 144a**) at **02:30** on a 230° course observing only the trail "covering an 80°–90° arc laterally" on the western horizon. The 15-minute time difference and 400–475 mile geographic separation between the two events is consistent with a single high-velocity object's trajectory. ^[extracted]

## Why this is engagement-class — and why that matters

Compared to the operational definition in [[concepts/uap-aircraft-engagement]], the Chiles–Whitted event satisfies:

1. **Closes** to within an apparent collision-avoidance distance of the DC-3 — Chiles' attached statement (per the [[references/project-sign-incident-summaries-101-172|incident bundle]]) describes the object pulling up sharply with a tremendous burst of flame to clear his aircraft, with the object passing close enough above the cockpit to require an evasive observation. ^[extracted]
2. **Active behavior** — the object did not simply pace or drift; it **executed a pull-up maneuver coincident with the airliner's position**. ^[extracted]
3. **Sensor-stimulus response** is **suggestive but not established** — the burst of flame timed to the close approach could be propulsion-correlated to evasion or could be a viewing-geometry artifact. ^[ambiguous]

The 24 Jul 1948 timestamp places this event **5 months before** [[references/sighting-andrews-1948-11|Andrews Inc 207 (18 Nov 1948)]], which the [[references/project-sign-incident-summaries-173-233|173–233 bundle]] flagged as the corpus's first 1948 engagement-class event. **Chiles–Whitted is the earlier precedent.** ^[inferred]

## Historical significance

The Chiles–Whitted case is one of the most-cited 1948 sightings in subsequent UAP historiography (frequently paired with the [[references/sighting-jackson-ms-1949-01|Jackson MS Tom Rush case]] of 1 Jan 1949, which uses the same cigar-class morphology). The 173–233 bundle's **Inc 233 cigar-shaped 60-ft object** observed by Tom Rush 5 months later in Mississippi shares the same descriptive vocabulary as Inc 144 — both EAL-pilot-class witnesses, both Southeast US, both wingless cigar-class. ^[inferred] This suggests the cigar-class morphology was **stable across the second half of 1948** in the SE US-airspace SIGN intake stream.

## Cross-references in the bundle

- **Inc 121 (9 Jan 1948 Cartersville GA, 2330)** — same airline (EAL), pilot **Hugh DuBose**, sky-blue circular pot-shaped 400-mph 3000-ft object. Predates Chiles–Whitted by 6.5 months. EAL appears to have been the **most-frequently-witnessed civilian airline** in the 1948 SIGN corpus.
- **Inc 137 (7 Jul 1948 Chapel Hill NC, 2114)** — 17 days earlier — three cigar-shaped objects observed for ~1 minute at high speed by a UNC mathematics instructor and Capt H.W. Daniels (Inf. Reserve). Same morphology, multi-object formation rather than single-object close approach.

## Source attribution

The 25-field "Check-List Unidentified Flying Objects" form is at OCR page 113 of [[references/project-sign-incident-summaries-101-172|the 101–172 bundle]]; Inc 144a is at page 114. The bundle does not include the full Chiles + Whitted attached witness statement at OCR-readable quality, but cross-references the **Atlanta Constitution of 25 July 1948** for the speed and tactics figures. ^[extracted]

## Open questions

- Did Chiles or Whitted ever provide a sketch with the SIGN intake form? Field 24 records "2 sketches" — these are not in the OCR'd bundle. ^[ambiguous]
- The "Mr McKelvis" passenger witness — who was he? The form lists "Occupation - N/S". Tracking him would resolve whether this was an unsolicited passenger or an EAL-deadhead pilot. ^[ambiguous]
- Did Project SIGN later evaluate Chiles–Whitted as a misidentified meteor (the position commonly attributed to [[entities/lincoln-la-paz|La Paz]] and J. Allen Hynek in later historiography)? The 101–172 bundle does not contain such an evaluation memo — that analysis is post-bundle. ^[inferred]
