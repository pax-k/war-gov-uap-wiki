# Sighting — Fort Monmouth NJ Radar Tracking, 10-11 September 1951

A series of **three radar-tracking events** at and near **Fort Monmouth, New Jersey** on **10-11 September 1951**, recorded by the U.S. Army Signal Corps Electronic Warfare radar systems then resident at Fort Monmouth. ^[extracted] Reported by Andrew J. Reid (G-2 Fort Monmouth) on **20 September 1951** to the FBI Newark field office, which forwarded the report to FBI HQ via URGENT teletype (signal: WASH 7 FROM NEWARK 9-20-51 DIRECTOR / Flying Saucers / UNCONVENTIONAL AIRCRAFT OBSERVED SEPTEMBER TEN AND ELEVEN, FIFTY ONE IN VICINITY FT. MONMOUTH, NJ). ^[extracted] Captured in [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-6|FBI HQ 62-HQ-83894 section 6]] at serial ~287 (pages 123-129). ^[extracted]

## The three radar events

The teletype carries the verbatim G-2 narrative ^[extracted]:

### Event 1 — AN/MPG-1, 10 Sep 1951, 1110 hours, SE of Fort Monmouth

> On September 10, 1951, an AN/MPG-1 radar set picked up a fast moving low flying target, exact altitude undetermined at approximately 1110 hours, southeast of Ft. Monmouth at a range of about 12,000 yards. The target appeared to approximately follow the coast line, changing its range only slightly but changing its azimuth rapidly. The radar set was switched to full aided azimuth tracking which normally is fast enough to track jet aircraft, but in this case was too slow to be resorted to. Target was lost in the N.E. at a range of about 14,000 yards.

This target also *"presented an unusually strong return for an acft being comparable in strength to that usually received from a coastal ship. The operator initially identified the target as a ship and then realized that it could not be a ship after he observed its extreme speed."* ^[extracted]

### Event 2 — SCR-584, 10 Sep 1951, 1515 hours, N of Fort Monmouth

> On 10 September 1951, 1515 hours, an SCR 584, serial no. 433 tracked a target which moved about slowly in azimuth N of Fort Monmouth at a range of about [32,000 yards]. Azimuth was changing at approximately 350 to 300 mils at a range of approximately thirty thousand yards, approximately altitude thirtyone thousand feet. The set track automatically is azimuth and elevation and was aided range tracking and capable of tracking targets up to a speed of seven hundred mph. In this case, however, both sets found it impossible to track the target in range due to its speed and the operators had to resort to manual range tracking in order to hold the target. The target was tracked in this manner to the maximum tracking range of thirtytwo thousand yards. The operator [estimated] the target to be moving at a speed several hundred mph higher than the maximum aided tracking ability of the radar sets.

The target *"provided an extremely strong return echo at times even though it was [at] the maximum range, however, echo signal occasionally fell off to a level below normal return. These changes coincided with maneuvers of the target."* ^[extracted]

### Event 3 — SCR-584, 11 Sep 1951

> On September eleven, fifty one at about [1:30] (operator believed) the target must have risen nearly vertically. Target fixed its rise in elevation at an elevation angle of approx. fifteen hundred mils, at which time it proceeded to move at an extremely rapid rate in range in a southerly direction. Once again the speed of the target exceeded the aided tracking ability of the SCR five eight four set so that manual tracking became necessary. Radar tracked the target maximum range of thirtytwo thousand yards at which time target was at an elevation angle three hundred mils. The operator did not attempt to judge the speed in excess of the aided tracking rate of seven hundred mph.

Weather: *"fair when the observation was made September Tenth and cloudy for the September Eleventh report."* ^[extracted] **Three witnesses present** at the second and third events (Event 1 had a single operator). ^[extracted]

## Radar systems involved

The two radar systems referenced ^[inferred]:

- **AN/MPG-1** — US Army Signal Corps mortar-locator / Plotting Group radar (M-9 GFCS family); typical capability includes automatic range/azimuth tracking with limited aided-azimuth rates for aircraft-class targets. ^[inferred]
- **SCR-584** — US Army Signal Corps SCR (Signal Corps Radio) 584 microwave fire-control radar, developed at MIT Rad Lab during WWII for AA fire control; later widely repurposed for tracking research. The SCR-584's documented aided-range-tracking limit was **~700 mph** at the period — corresponding to the target's documented overrun. ^[inferred]

**Both radar systems failed to track the target in aided-mode** ^[extracted] — operators had to fall back to manual tracking, which only permits **position tracking**, not velocity / speed measurement. The 700 mph limit is the AF / Army 1951 jet-aircraft-tracking design margin, and the target **exceeded** this margin. ^[inferred]

The institutional routing path: **G-2 Fort Monmouth → G-2 Governors Island NY → FBI Newark → FBI HQ → AF (request)** ^[inferred] — a structural deviation from the BB-57 / SAC Letter #38 routing (which calls for direct OSI handoff at field-office level). The Newark SAC's choice to URGENT-teletype the Director rather than handle locally suggests the **case was already flagged as exceeding routine handling**. ^[inferred]

## Institutional significance

The Fort Monmouth case is structurally distinctive in the FBI corpus ^[inferred]:

1. **First AN/MPG-1 + SCR-584 radar-tracked UAP case** in the FBI corpus.
2. **First Army G-2 → FBI HQ direct channel** for a UAP radar event — extends channel (e) (inter-agency directive accumulation) to the **Army-side analytical channel**.
3. **First documented multi-radar multi-day institutional engagement** with a UAP target in the FBI corpus — and a structural successor to the [[references/sighting-oak-ridge-adcock-1950-03|Oak Ridge / Adcock]] (March 1950) case but at scale and on institutional equipment.
4. **Predates the famous July 1952 Washington DC radar incidents by ~10 months** ^[inferred] — and **predates the [[references/fbi-sac-letter-83-1952|SAC Letter #83]] reaffirmation of BB-57 / SAC Letter #38 by ~11 months**. The Fort Monmouth case is thus the **late-pre-1952-wave radar inflection** that does not yet trigger HQ-level protocol revision. ^[inferred]
5. **Speed signature**: target exceeded the design margin of two distinct radar systems at two distinct operating frequencies. The **>700 mph + vertical-rise + azimuth-overrun** signature is the highest-performance UAP target documented in the FBI corpus prior to 1952. ^[inferred]

The case is **structurally adjacent to** but **not the same as** the **24 September 1951** Fort Monmouth radar event that historians sometimes cite as the trigger for [[concepts/project-blue-book|Project Blue Book]]'s revival ^[inferred] — the 10-11 Sep 1951 events are a separate **pre-Blue-Book-revival** datapoint ^[inferred]. The 1951 Fort Monmouth events are sometimes credited with prompting Gen Charles P. Cabell's January 1952 directive to revive the AF's UFO program (which became Project Blue Book in March 1952) ^[inferred]; whether the section-6 captured events are the **same events** historians cite or a **distinct earlier cluster** is unresolved by the FBI corpus alone ^[ambiguous].
