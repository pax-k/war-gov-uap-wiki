## Description

Three independent two-agent teams of federal law enforcement special agents (USPER1 through USPER6) describe seeing **orange "orbs"** in the sky **emit/launch smaller red "orbs"** in groups of two to four, with three being the general consensus. The pattern repeated at least five times across two days.

Per slide narrative:

- Each occurrence: orange orb appears, launches red orbs, then disappears.
- The orange orb was visible only **one or two seconds**.
- Red orbs typically moved **horizontally away** from the orange "mother" orb.
- One witness reported a red orb "heading up at an angle"; another stated red orbs would sometimes "swoop down" after launch.
- Witnessing teams were in **varying locations and vantage points** — independent corroboration. ^[inferred]

## Open questions

Due to the sequential nature of the events, the source explicitly notes it is **not known** whether there was a single orange "mother" orb releasing groups of red orbs over time, or whether **multiple orange orbs** were involved.

## Why it matters

- Multi-team, multi-vantage corroboration is unusually strong for a UAP report — see [[synthesis/federal-le-uap-witness-pattern]].
- The "mother orb launching daughter orbs" morphology is rare in the public UAP literature; this report is one concrete instance feeding [[concepts/orb-phenomenon]].
- The dusk timing and the very brief (1–2 s) appearance of the orange orb are consistent with a low-duty-cycle emission event ^[inferred] — relevant to any explanatory hypothesis (flares, drone-launched submunitions, optical artifact, or unknown).
