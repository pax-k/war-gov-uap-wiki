# Sighting — Newfoundland Cluster (Jul 1947)

A **6-event multi-witness UAP cluster** in **Newfoundland between 9 July and 23 July 1947**, documented by [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-serial-130|62-HQ-83894 serial 130]] OCR pp. 14-34. ^[extracted] The cluster routes through **two independent institutional channels**: ^[inferred]

1. **Newfoundland Base Command, ATLD-ATC, Fort Pepperrell** → 1388th AAF Base Unit Intelligence Office (Capt William H. Smith, AC) → Atlantic Division ATC, Fort Totten LI → CG ATC Wash → AAF HQ.
2. **Newfoundland Constabulary, Grand Falls Station** (Const Eric Kearsey) → St. John's → AAF/FBI via the Final Report of Sighting transmittal chain.

This is the **earliest non-continental-US multi-witness UAP cluster** in the wiki corpus ^[inferred] and the **earliest cluster in the corpus where civilian + military + law-enforcement witnesses are all preserved** in the same institutional packet. ^[inferred]

## Routing

The Newfoundland Base Command Final Reports of Sighting traveled: ^[extracted]

> 1388th AAF BU NBC ATLD-ATC Harmon Field (28 Jul 1947) → Atlantic Division ATC Fort Totten LI (6 Aug 1947) → CG ATC Washington (Attn: Chief of Staff)

The Newfoundland Constabulary's separate report on the 9 Jul Grand Falls cluster (#1, #2) traveled via **Const Kearsey's deposition through the St John's headquarters**, signed and sworn 13 Jul 1947, and was attached as Inclosure 4 to the 1388th AAF BU's transmittal package. ^[extracted]

The packet appeared at **CG ATLD ATC Fort Totten NY 14 July 1947 142323Z** as **incoming-classified-message TWX from CG ATLD-ATC Fort Totten** ^[extracted] — i.e. **5 days after the events**, considerably faster than any continental-US Final Report in the same packet. ^[inferred] The fast-routing speed reflects Newfoundland's **operational ATC-AAF instant-relay norm** to AAF HQ via the Atlantic Division. ^[inferred]

## The Mehrman / Woodruff / Leidy interrogation

The **16 July 1947 interrogation** by Capt William H. Smith AC (Intelligence Officer) of the three Harmon Field mechanics is the **most thoroughly-witness-corroborated single sighting in the Newfoundland tranche**, with **3 sworn witnesses + Kodachrome trail-photographs**: ^[extracted]

### Woodruff (16 Jul 1947 1415 HFT)
- "I saw the object break out where the clouds opened and it left its trail behind it."
- "It cut a straight path right through the cloud."
- Trail: "**dark blueish in color similar to a high power light. The trail was from approximately fifteen (15) to twenty (20) miles long**."
- "**This was as straight as an arrow**" — explicitly distinguished from a meteor.
- Course: NNE.

### Mehrman (16 Jul 1947 1430 HFT)
- "**A diesel exhaust would be similar to it**" — for the trail color.
- "**More or less a band across the sky in a straight path**."
- "**No noise**."

### Leidy (16 Jul 1947 1450 HFT — half-hour-after-Mehrman)
- "I had my camera so took a picture of it."
- Camera: **Argus F-2, Kodachrome film**.
- **Two photographs taken**.
- "**Travelling at a terrific rate of speed in a circle because of the area in the path**" — Leidy describes a curved-path interpretation different from Woodruff's "straight as an arrow"; ^[ambiguous] this discrepancy is consistent with witness-disagreement about path geometry given limited observation time.
- Leidy explicitly agrees to provide the four rolls of film to the Army.

The **photographic record** here is the **earliest civilian Kodachrome UAP-trail photographic evidence** in the corpus. ^[inferred] The corpus does not confirm whether the photographs were ever recovered/developed; the Final Report of Sighting (Item 11) states *"Mr. Robert W. Leidy reported that he took two (2) Kodachrome snap shots of the trail"* but the disposition of the Argus negatives is unrecorded. ^[ambiguous]

## The Cordroy civilian witnesses

The **17 July 1947 statements** at Cordroy taken by **Mercedes Burke** of the Intelligence Office Harmon Field cover three civilian witnesses (Legge, Evans, Samms + mother): ^[extracted]

### Legge (17 Jul 1947 statement)
> "It definitely was not a shooting star … nor was it an airplane, it was too 'lit up' … I'd say it was traveling at the rate of a shooting star but much closer. I'd say roughly at 6,000 feet. It was visible for about fifteen (15) seconds … The trail behind made the whole object look like a cone."

### Evans (17 Jul 1947 statement)
> "If it was a shooting star, I'd never seen one as large before, and it was much brighter than anything I've ever seen in the sky … It looked to be a round object … it had a tail on it which showed yellowish, but not as bright as the circle which appeared and nearest I could describe it would be sort of red and yellow."

### Samms (17 Jul 1947 statement, age 10)
> "Last Thursday night I was out by our house, I happened to look up and saw what I thought was an airoplane. It was flying at the height of a plane that is flying high. Then I thought it looked strange for an airoplane, because it was all lit up so bright, planes do have a light or two, but **this thing was very bright - sort of a bright reddish yellow.** I saw it only for a minute because it was travelling at a teriffic speed."

The **10-year-old witness** is structurally significant: ^[inferred] Albert Samms's deposition is **one of the earliest preserved child-witness UAP statements** in the corpus, and is **mother-corroborated** (Mrs John Samms's deposition explicitly confirms Albert's contemporaneous narration: "Albert rushed in and told me about this thing he'd just seen … he said it couldn't have been a plane because a plane wouldn't shoot down like that did, besides it was too 'lit up'"). ^[extracted] The intra-family corroboration of a child-witness UAP report — taken under formal Army-Intelligence-Office deposition the same day as adult witnesses — is **structurally distinct from later-corpus child-witness handling**. ^[inferred]

## The Newfoundland Constabulary report (9 Jul Grand Falls)

Const **Eric Kearsey** of Newfoundland Constabulary Grand Falls Station files his **13 July 1947** report describing his own sighting + his family + Jackman's prior sighting: ^[extracted]

> Upon arrival at home I joined my wife, my mother-in-law, and Mr. John Jackman a resident of St. John's and friend of the family, who were sitting on the front steps of the house. I was asked by my wife if I had seen the "flying saucers" I thought she was joking … Jackman said that **four round shaped figures had passed overhead at a terrific speed and were flying side by side**. On looking skywards again my wife, Jackman, and myself, saw an object which I would describe as being **a huge jellyfish flash across the sky** … there seemed to be a **phosphorus glow** about it, it was round in shape and about the size of a barrel head, it appeared to be **flying in a rocking motion**.

Kearsey is **AFBIR-CO Report #14** — see [[references/afbir-co-18-sighting-analysis-1947-07-30]]. AFBIR-CO's classification of Kearsey's signature: "**Egg-shaped, or like barrel head**" / **phosphorous color** / **"None reported" deviation** / **CAVU weather**. ^[extracted]

## Why this cluster matters

### First non-continental-US multi-witness UAP cluster in the corpus

The wiki's prior 1947 corpus is **continental-US-only**. Newfoundland's geographic distinctness is institutionally significant: ^[inferred]

1. The cluster routes through **ATC's Atlantic Division**, not 4AF Hamilton Field, opening a **second AAF-side intake channel for the wave** parallel to the dominant 4AF channel.
2. The cluster includes a **non-US law-enforcement witness** (Newfoundland Constabulary), introducing the **non-US-LE-witness intake channel** that would later expand under the [[concepts/diplomatic-channel-uap-reporting|diplomatic-channel]] umbrella (1985 PNG, 1994 Kazakhstan).
3. The cluster includes the **earliest preserved Kodachrome UAP-trail photograph** in the corpus.

### Morphological signature distinct from continental-US-1947 reports

The Newfoundland-cluster object signatures are **bluish-black-trail / 15-mi-long / phosphorus-glow / circular-like-wheel C-54-sized / red-and-yellow color** — distinct from the **silver-disc / no-trail / no-sound** signature dominant in [[references/afbir-co-18-sighting-analysis-1947-07-30|AFBIR-CO Reports 1, 3, 9, 13]]. ^[inferred] The **strong-trail signature** is shared only with [[references/sighting-arnold-mt-rainier-1947-06-24|Arnold]]'s no-trail observation negatively, and with [[references/sighting-cascade-mts-johnson-1947-06-24|Johnson Cascade Mts]]' weak-tail observation. ^[inferred] **The Newfoundland tranche may represent a distinct phenomenological class** — possibly atmospheric reentry / meteor-related, possibly genuinely UAP, ^[ambiguous] but the corpus does not yet resolve this.

### The "Something is really flying around" 6-claim summary

OCR p. 35 of serial 130 (immediately following the Const Kearsey 13 Jul 1947 report at OCR p. 34, page-counter "27") preserves an **early 6-claim object-physical-appearance summary**: ^[extracted]

> (a) This "flying saucer" situation is not all imaginary or seeing too much in some natural phenomenon. **Something is really flying around.**
>
> (b) Lack of topside inquiries, when compared to the prompt and demanding inquiries that have originated topside upon former events, give more than ordinary weight to the possibility that **this is a domestic project, about which the President, etc. know.**
>
> (c) Whatever the objects are, this much can be said of their physical appearance:
> 1. The surface of these objects is metallic, indicating a metallic skin, at least.
> 2. When a trail is observed, it is lightly colored, a Blue-Brown haze, that is similar to a rocket engine's exhaust. Contrary to a rocket of the solid type, one observation indicates that the fuel may be throttled which would indicate a liquid rocket engine.
> 3. As to shape, all observations state that the object is circular or at least elliptical, flat on the bottom and slightly domed on the top. The size estimates place it somewhere near the size of a C-54 or a Constellation.
> 4. Some reports describe two tabs, located at the rear and symmetrical about the xxxx axis of flight motion.
> 5. Flights have been reported, from three to nine of them, flying good formation on each other, with speeds always above 300 knots.
> 6. The discs oscillate laterally while flying along, which could be snaking.

The page's document-attribution is **ambiguous** ^[ambiguous] — its position between OCR p. 34 (Const Kearsey's report) and OCR p. 36 (4AF Springer 25 Aug 1947 cover letter to AAF Wash) suggests it is a **Newfoundland-tranche analytical wrap-up**, possibly written by Capt William H. Smith AC (Intelligence Officer at 1388th AAF BU) or by a 4AF analyst. ^[inferred] The 6-claim summary's analytical conclusions — particularly **(b)'s "domestic project … about which the President, etc. know"** hypothesis — **directly anticipate the [[entities/g-d-garrett|Garrett admission]]** of mid-Sep 1947, which postulated that flying discs were a **classified US Government experiment**. ^[inferred] The Newfoundland-tranche analyst's reasoning is **structurally identical to Garrett's** — both invoke the absence of topside official concern as evidence of **insider knowledge**. ^[inferred]

The 6-claim summary's content also **partially anticipates the AFBIR-CO 30 Jul 1947 analysis** ([[references/afbir-co-18-sighting-analysis-1947-07-30]]), which arrives 2-3 weeks later: both characterize objects as **circular/elliptical, C-54-Constellation-sized, formation-flying, no-sound, lateral oscillation**. ^[inferred] AFBIR-CO **may have drawn on** the Newfoundland tranche's analytical priors when assembling the 18-sighting 21-field breakdown. ^[inferred]
