# Sighting — Andrews AFB Pursuit (18 Nov 1948)

A two-aircraft, two-pilot **active pursuit** event over Andrews AFB at Camp Springs, MD on the evening of **18 November 1948**, filed as **Project SIGN Incident 207 / 207A** in [[references/project-sign-incident-summaries-173-233|the Sep 1948 – Jan 1949 incident summaries bundle]]. This is the **earliest 1948 corpus event** that meets the operational criteria of [[concepts/uap-aircraft-engagement|UAP–aircraft engagement-class behavior]] — specifically, a UAP that closely tracked a chase aircraft, performed evasive vertical climbs, and dropped behind the chase aircraft to continue circling the field after the pilot's identification attempts.

## Witnesses

- **2d Lt Kenwood W. Jackson, AFRes (AO-944113)** — telephone cableman in civilian life; primary observer in the chase aircraft. 1326 28th St SE, Washington DC.
- **2d Lt Glen L. Stalker, USAFR** — corroborating co-witness in the chase aircraft.
- **2d Lt Henry G. Combs, USAFR** — second-aircraft witness (Inc 207A), filed under separate sheet at 2200 hours, observing **the same object** from an aircraft over Andrews AFB. 4511 S. Capitol St., Washington DC.

The Jackson + Stalker statement is co-signed; the Combs sheet is filed independently. ^[extracted]

## Pursuit narrative

Object was flying at 1700 ft NE → SW across Andrews field at 2145. The chase aircraft (already at 4000 ft) descended to **match the object's altitude**. The object then re-crossed Andrews from SW → NE. Plane and object **flew in circles, with the object on the outer circle**, at the same altitude. Plane **followed it to 7000 ft** and made 3–4 passes attempting identification. As the plane descended at ~240 mph, **the object would climb vertically, drop below the plane from behind, and continue circling the field**. ^[extracted]

In a final identification attempt, the chase aircraft **switched on its landing light, and the object momentarily gave off a dull glow.** The object then headed NE climbing rapidly. The plane lost sight at 8000 ft at 22:03. ^[extracted]

The Combs second-aircraft observation at 2200 hours describes the same general object signature — "oblong ball, no wings or tail surfaces", "smaller than a T-6 type aircraft", continuous glowing white light, 1700 → 7500 ft altitude band — corroborating Jackson + Stalker from a separate platform. ^[extracted]

## Why this report matters

This event has been **systematically under-recognized** in the wiki: the prior version of [[concepts/uap-aircraft-engagement]] explicitly stated that "the 1948 USAF Flying Disc file does **not** document UAP-aircraft engagement of this kind." Inc 207 directly contradicts that. The behavioral signature here meets all four operational criteria for engagement-class behavior:

1. **Close approach** — the object operated at altitudes (1700–8000 ft) overlapping the chase aircraft's flight envelope, within the same circle radius.
2. **Sustained co-location** — 15–18 minutes of paired motion in concentric circles is qualitatively different from any "passive hover" report in the 1948 corpus.
3. **Target-following / vertical-axis evasion** — the object's "vertical climb / drop behind from above" maneuver is structurally outside the 1948 conventional aviation envelope and is a clean **target-tracking** behavior. ^[inferred]
4. **Sensor-stimulus response** — the object's apparent **brightening** when the chase aircraft turned on its landing light is one of only two **interactive-response** signatures in the 1944–1949 corpus (cf. the [[concepts/foo-fighter-phenomenon|foo-fighter pacing reports]]), and the only one against a USAF-pilot observer in CONUS. ^[inferred]

The event sets a 1948 precedent for the modern engagement-class behavior catalogued in [[references/sighting-helicopter-orb-encounter-2025|the 2025 helicopter-orb encounter]] — long before the modern "drone-tasking" alternative explanation becomes available. ^[inferred]

## Provenance and reliability

- The two-aircraft, three-witness configuration upgrades this event substantially over single-pilot sightings. ^[inferred]
- The chase aircraft made repeated **active identification attempts** (climb-to-altitude, three-to-four ID passes, landing-light illumination) — the witness statement is therefore not bare-eye casual observation but a **structured search-and-identify procedure** by trained airmen. ^[inferred]
- The summary sheet does not preserve the chase aircraft's tail number or squadron. ^[ambiguous]
- The object's behavior is not consistent with a balloon, helicopter, or 1948-era jet aircraft. The summary sheet's evaluators did not propose a candidate identification. ^[extracted]
