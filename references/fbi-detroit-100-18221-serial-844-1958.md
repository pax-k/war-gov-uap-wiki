# FBI Detroit Office Memorandum — Weaver Sighting (17 Apr 1958, Serial 844)

A **one-page Standard Form No. 64 "Office Memorandum, UNITED STATES GOVERNMENT"** (carbon, declassification authority "FBI Automatic Declassification Guide, Issued May 24, 2007") from **Special Agent Robert Ross Reynolds** to **SAC, Detroit**, dated **17 April 1958**, subject "**UNIDENTIFIED FLYING OBJECTS**". The artifact is filed as **serial 844 of FBI file 100-18221** (Detroit field office), with a cross-reference to file `100-26505`. Screened by NARA (RF) on 11-25-2025 under FOIA #90288, document ID `34715588`.

It is the **second-earliest FBI-originated UAP-adjacent record** in the wiki — predating the [[entities/fbi|FBI's]] [[references/fbi-fd302-drone-pilot-redacted-2023|2023 FD-302 pair]] on the Sept 2023 contractor-convoy incident, but **post-dating the [[references/fbi-detroit-100-26505-krasuski-1957|November 1957 Krasuski file (100-26505)]] by ~5 months**. ^[inferred] The cross-reference to file `100-26505` on this memo's footer points back at Krasuski's already-existing UFO-adjacent file in the same field office. ^[inferred]

## What the memo records

A **citizen phone-in to the FBI Detroit field office** at **0408 hrs, 17 April 1958**, by:

- **David Weaver**, age 23, of **15801 Decosta, Detroit, Michigan**, telephone **KEnwood 6-7546**.
- Identified himself as the son of a Detroit policeman, with prior Civil Air Patrol (CAP) experience but "not too familiar with the identification of aircraft."
- Stated he was returning home from his Detroit employment when he made the observation.

**The observation as reported by Weaver:**

- **Object**: described as **"a circular object with a crystal-type dome that reflected lights."**
- **Heading**: passing "in a northern direction from southwest."
- **Track**: "crossed the City three blocks south of Six Mile at Lamphere Street."
- **Witness's first action**: tried to telephone **Selfridge Field** (the AF base NE of Detroit) to reach the Air Force; could not get through.
- **Second action**: telephoned the FBI Detroit field office instead.

**Disposition by FBI Detroit:**

- Reynolds memo's **Recommendation** section reads in full: "Advise proper air force authorities."
- The endorsement records that "**Lt. Col (Bermnie) E. Prater advised 4/17/58, no letter rec. mps**" — i.e., the Air Force POC was telephonically advised the same day; no follow-up letter received.

## Provenance and routing

The memo's footer file marker `100-18221-844` shows that **at least 843 prior serials** existed in FBI Detroit file `100-18221` by 17 April 1958, and that this was filed as item 844 in the same case file. The cross-reference to `100-26505` indicates a **second related file** in Detroit's `100`-series caseload (the `100-` prefix is FBI's standard "Domestic Security" classification). That second file has now been **located and ingested** as [[references/fbi-detroit-100-26505-krasuski-1957|the Krasuski file (Nov 1957 + 1966 OUFORA)]] — the cross-reference points at an **already-existing UFO-adjacent case** ~5 months prior to the Weaver memo. ^[inferred] Whether file `100-18221` is itself a UFO-subject case or a broader security file under which UFO phone-ins were sub-filed is **unresolved** by this single serial. ^[inferred]

The receipt stamp uses Detroit's standard intake apparatus (`SEARCHED / INDEXED / SERIALIZED / FILED`) — the same routing skeleton seen on later FBI documents. The 1958 routing is structurally similar to the later [[entities/fbi|FBI]] FD-302 / case-file workflow: a **citizen contact → SA memo → SAC routing → external POC notification**. The form factor differs — a Standard Form 64 office memorandum is a *routing instrument*, not a *witness-interview evidentiary form* like the FD-302; consistent with the report being **the SA's secondhand summary of a phone call**, not a sit-down interview. ^[inferred]

## What this serial *does* and *does not* establish

**It establishes:**

- That as of April 1958, the FBI Detroit field office was an **available citizen-contact channel for UFO sightings**, and that Detroit's intake protocol on receiving such a call was: **(a)** record an SA memo to the SAC, **(b)** advise the local Air Force POC by phone, and **(c)** serialize and file in the case folder.
- That the citizen, **David Weaver**, attempted to reach the **Air Force first** (via Selfridge Field) and only contacted the FBI when that channel failed — i.e., the public still recognised the **Air Force** as the lead UAP-reporting authority, with the FBI as fallback. ^[inferred] Consistent with [[concepts/flying-disc-reporting-protocol|Cabell's 1948 directive]] having ten years to propagate into citizen awareness.
- That the FBI's 1958 **administrative response** to a UFO call was *not* to investigate; it was to **route the report to the Air Force**. The FBI **did not record the kind of sighting detail** (10-element protocol, sketched track, multi-witness corroboration) that a Project SIGN / BLUE BOOK intake would produce.

**It does not establish:**

- Whether Selfridge / "Lt. Col Prater" actually opened a Project BLUE BOOK file on the Weaver sighting. ^[open]
- Whether file `100-18221` is **subject-named** "UFO" or whether UFO matters were sub-filed under a broader Detroit security case. ^[inferred]
- Anything about the object's actual nature; the report contains **one descriptive line** ("circular object with a crystal-type dome that reflected lights") and one trajectory line. The phenomenology is too thin to support morphology classification beyond *circular + dome* — though the descriptor is **on-thesis** for the **classic 1950s "saucer-with-dome" morphology**. ^[inferred]
