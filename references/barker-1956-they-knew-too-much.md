# Gray Barker, *They Knew Too Much About Flying Saucers* (1956)

A 1956 popular non-fiction book by **[[entities/gray-barker|Gray Barker]]**, published by **University Books, Inc., 404 Fourth Avenue, New York 16, NY**, with jacket design by **Gropper Associates, Inc.**, listed at **$3.50**. ^[extracted]

**The wiki does not hold the book's text** — only the **publisher's promotional jacket copy** (front flap + back flap + author photograph credit), as clipped and filed by the FBI in **HQ file 62-HQ-83894 serial 403** (see [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-serial-403]]). ^[inferred] This page is therefore a **reference-stub**: it documents what the wiki knows about the book, distinguishes between extracted-from-jacket-copy claims and inferred / contextual claims, and serves as an anchor for any future ingest that brings the book itself into the corpus.

**Important: the FBI separately acquired the actual book in Feb 1959.** ^[extracted] [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-9|Section 9]] of the same HQ file (serial 403, 12 Feb 1959) records that **Chicago SAC mailed a copy of *They Knew Too Much About Flying Saucers* to FBI HQ** with the cover note: *"Per Bureau request, enclosed herewith is a copy of the book … by GRAY BARKER, noting this book was published by University Books, Inc., 404 Fourth Avenue, New York 16, N.Y. … ENCLOSURE - retained by works."* ^[extracted] So the FBI HQ at one point held **both** the publisher's jacket-copy clipping (separately serialized as 62-HQ-83894 serial 403, the artifact in this wiki) **and** an actual book copy (also section-9 serial 403). ^[inferred] These appear to be **two distinct serial-403 retentions** ingested into the file at different times, or — alternatively — the wiki's existing serial-403-as-jacket-clipping reading is incomplete and the **same serial 403 spans both items**. ^[ambiguous]

## Provenance and what's actually in the wiki

The artifact in `sources/65_hs1-834228961_62-hq-83894_serial_403.json` reproduces three pages: ^[extracted]

1. **Page 1** — FBI Records Center cover slip (not Barker content).
2. **Page 2** — front jacket flap of the book.
3. **Page 3** — back jacket flap (continuation) plus the **Buffington Studios** author photograph credit.

Pages 2 and 3 together reproduce the full jacket-copy summary of the book's thesis but **none of the book's actual contents**. ^[extracted] Whatever the book actually argues, the wiki currently knows it only through publisher-authored marketing prose — which is **not an independent source**, and which was written to sell copies. ^[inferred] Treat the jacket-copy claims as *thesis paraphrases* rather than as Barker's own statements verbatim. ^[inferred]

## The book's central thesis (per the jacket)

The jacket-copy summary of Barker's argument: ^[extracted]

> *"One by one, the leading figures among flying saucer researchers, who have challenged the government denial that saucers come from outer space, have been silenced. Outwardly, nothing seems to have happened to these men. They are still alive, still living where they used to. But they no longer publish saucer research material and they will not talk about saucers or why they no longer will speak of them. Three men in dark suits have visited these saucer researchers. Nobody knows what they said, but it was enough to reduce their hearers to silence."*

The jacket frames the *"three men in dark suits"* as of unknown affiliation — *"They might be government agents, they might even be men from outer space, or they might have muscled into a situation fraught with many possibilities."* ^[extracted] No specific instance, name, or date is given in the jacket copy.

## Significance — folklore-trajectory

Barker's 1956 book is widely cited as **the originating source of the modern [[concepts/three-men-in-black|"Men in Black" / MIB folklore]]**. ^[inferred] The MIB trope subsequently spread through 1960s–80s civilian UAP-research culture, into popular fiction (the 1990 Lowell Cunningham comic-book series and the 1997 Sonnenfeld film franchise), and into broader contemporary cultural reference. ^[inferred] The wiki is not the place to chronicle that trajectory; what matters here is:

- The MIB trope was **already in commercial publication by 1956** — i.e. it pre-dates [[concepts/observer-suppression-behavior]] anchor cases (the 1955 [[references/iaf-ir-193-55-russell-1955|Russell trans-Caucasus]] sighting in which Soviet trainmen actively suppressed continued observation). The two suppression patterns are categorically distinct: Russell's case is *contemporaneous suppression at the time of observation by adjacent operators*; Barker's is *post-hoc suppression of public discourse by unknown actors*. ^[inferred]
- The trope appears here in the wiki **only because the FBI clipped the publisher's jacket and filed it at HQ**. ^[inferred] The FBI did not author it, did not endorse it, and (so far as serial 403 reveals) did not engage with it substantively. The clipping action is what is wiki-relevant.

## Endorser

The jacket reproduces a partial endorsement attribution to **H. G. Rhawn**, *"publisher and owner of the daily Clarksburg News, the author's home town paper"*, who is described as *"carefully disclaiming any credence in flying saucers"* but concluding that *"when so sober and successful a business man as Mr. Barker finds the field important enough, flying saucers deserve serious investigation"*. ^[extracted] This is structurally interesting as a **hometown-newspaper-publisher endorsement** of a saucer book by the local-business-community lens, but the text is paraphrase by University Books, not a verbatim Rhawn letter.

## Open threads

- Locate the **book itself** to verify the jacket-copy paraphrases against the book's actual text. The publisher (University Books, Inc., 1956) is no longer a going concern; copies should be findable in research libraries and the secondhand market. ^[open]
- Identify which **specific researchers** Barker claims were *"silenced"*. The book itself almost certainly names them; the jacket does not. ^[open]
- Identify whether the **FBI's other 62-HQ-83894 serials** carry **other Barker material** — magazine clippings of *The Saucerian*, additional book-jacket clippings of his subsequent books, or correspondence to/from Barker. ^[open]
- Determine whether the *"three men in dark suits"* trope has **any pre-1956 publication**, or whether Barker's book is genuinely its first commercial appearance. ^[open]
- Cross-reference any **AAPRO / NICAP** records of Barker — both organizations were active in the same period and would have known him. ^[open]
- Determine whether the FBI's clipping reflects a **standing practice** of monitoring saucer-research books (as opposed to an episodic clipping). The 17-file 62-HQ-83894 series may resolve this. ^[open]
