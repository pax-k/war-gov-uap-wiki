# Sighting — Barnaul (Siberia) airport closure, 27 January 2001 (AFP / Interfax)

A **27 January 2001 Agence France Presse wire** — sourced to the **Interfax news agency** and quoting local aviation company director **Ivan Komarov** — reporting that **Barnaul airport** in southern Siberia was *"shut down for an hour and a half on Friday"* when a luminescent UAP was detected hovering above its runway. Verbatim from the AFP wire:

> *"MOSCOW, Jan 27, 2001 -- (Agence France Presse) An airport in southern Siberia was shut down for an hour and a half on Friday when an unidentified flying object (UFO) was detected hovering above its runway, the Interfax newsagency reported. The crew of an Il-76 cargo aircraft refused to take off, claiming they saw a luminescent object hovering above the runway of the Siberia's Barnaul airport, local aviation company director Ivan Komarov was quoted as saying. The crew of another cargo plane, refusing to use the runway for the same reason, landed their jet at another airport, Komarov said. The UFO took off and vanished from the airport 90 minutes later, according to the report."*

The wire is preserved at **page 92 of the [[references/cometa-report-1999|COMETA archival bundle]]** as bundled US-press / wire-service material — included with [[entities/leslie-kean|Leslie Kean]]'s May 2001 **Providence Journal** feature (which opens by referencing this event) and Kean's **Boston Sunday Globe** May 2000 piece.

## Date reconciliation

- **Wire datelined**: Moscow, 27 January 2001.
- **Reported event-day**: "Friday" — the closest Friday to a 27 Jan dateline is the same day, **Friday 26 Jan 2001**, since 27 Jan 2001 was a Saturday. The wire is filed *next-day* relative to the event proper. ^[inferred]
- **Kean's Providence Journal piece** (3 May 2001) references this as having occurred *"back in January"* and characterizes it as a 15-hour closure (vs. the AFP wire's 90 minutes / 1.5 hours). The discrepancy is likely a reporter conflation or a typo (15 vs. 1.5); the AFP wire-supported figure is 90 minutes. ^[ambiguous]

## Witnesses and corroboration

- **Crew 1**: Il-76 cargo aircraft crew, refused takeoff.
- **Crew 2**: A second cargo plane crew refused to use the same runway for the same reason; diverted to a different airport.
- **Source authority**: **Ivan Komarov**, local aviation company director (named).
- **Wire chain**: aviation-company-director → Interfax (Russian news agency) → AFP (French wire) → bundled into COMETA archival packet.

The case is **commercial-aviation operational** — two cargo-crew refusals to use the runway constitute an *operational* response, not just a sighting report. ^[inferred] No Russian military intercept or scramble is mentioned in the wire.

## Morphology

- *"Luminescent"* — single descriptor; no shape, no size, no colour given in the wire.
- **Hovering** — stationary over runway for the ~90-minute closure.
- **Departure**: *"took off and vanished"* after 90 minutes — i.e., the object went from stationary to leaving, with no reported intermediate trajectory.

## Position relative to corpus

- **First post-2000 Russian sighting** in the wiki. The earlier Russian / USSR sightings (Pereslavl-Zalesski 1990, Kapustin Yar 1989, the KGB 1991 declassification) are pre-1991 USSR — this is the corpus's first **Russian Federation** sighting.
- **First commercial-aviation runway-shutdown sighting** in the wiki. Other commercial-aviation cases in the corpus (Air France AF 3532, Aerolineas Bariloche, JAL 1986) are in-flight encounters, not runway-closures.
- **Operational refusal** is the structural anomaly: the cargo crews exercised pilot-in-command authority to ground their aircraft. The wire does not specify any flag-state ATC intervention. ^[inferred]
- **Wire-service intake channel.** Indirect chain: aviation-company-director → Russian wire (Interfax) → Western wire (AFP). Functions in the corpus as a non-government, non-witness-direct intake mode. Pairs with the [[concepts/diplomatic-channel-uap-reporting|diplomatic-channel pattern]] as a separate civilian-press intake mode. ^[inferred]
