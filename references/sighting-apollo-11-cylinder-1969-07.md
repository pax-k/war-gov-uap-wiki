# Apollo 11 Day-3 "Cylinder / Open-Suitcase" Translunar Object Sighting (~18 Jul 1969)

A **three-witness instrument-mediated sighting** of an unidentified object made by **all three Apollo 11 crewmen** — **[[entities/neil-armstrong|Neil Armstrong]]**, **[[entities/buzz-aldrin|Buzz Aldrin]]**, and **[[entities/michael-collins|Michael Collins]]** — approximately **one day before lunar arrival** during Apollo 11's Day-3 translunar coast (~**18 July 1969 UTC**, MET ~Day 3). ^[inferred] The observation is preserved in the **§6 Translunar Operations** section of the **[[references/nasa-uap-d4-apollo-11-technical-crew-debriefing-1969|July 31 1969 Apollo 11 Technical Crew Debriefing Vol I, pages 6-33 through 6-36]]**. This is the corpus's **first sub-class E (strong-positive) orbital UAP-adjacent observation** — see [[concepts/orbital-uap-sighting]].

## What makes this a sub-class E observation

The corpus's [[concepts/orbital-uap-sighting|orbital-class typology]] previously had four sub-classes (A, B, C, D — see the sister concept page). The Apollo 11 Day-3 observation introduces **sub-class E (strong-positive)**, distinguished from sub-class A (canonical bogey-class call) by all of:

1. **Multi-witness simultaneous observation.** Three crewmen, sequential PTC viewing geometry, in agreement on the basic phenomenology (bright object, sizeable, near limit of resolution, partly resolvable into geometric shapes). ^[extracted]
2. **Instrument-mediated optical resolution at multiple magnifications and focal settings.** Cabin window → monocular → sextant in the LEB. The morphology shifts predictably with focus, allowing the crew to identify the **off-focus cylinder reading as an instrument artifact**. ^[inferred]
3. **Conventional candidate explicitly eliminated by independent ranging data.** Mission Control reported the [[concepts/saturn-v-s-ivb-stage|S-IVB]] at **6,000 miles away** — i.e., **far outside the optically-resolvable distance** for an object near the limit of unaided-eye resolution. ^[extracted] (At 6,000 miles, the S-IVB's ~17-meter length subtends ~0.1 arcseconds — well below the human eye's ~60-arcsecond limit and below the LM monocular's likely magnification capability.) ^[inferred]
4. **Multiple secondary conventional candidates ruled out.** Urine-dump particles ruled out by Collins; LM jettison panel ruled out by both shape and chronology; only "high-gain antenna fragment" survives as a candidate. ^[extracted]
5. **Morphology resolved across multiple optical configurations.** "L shape" / "open suitcase" (monocular); "two connected rings" / "hollow cylinder" / "tumbling ... look right down in its guts" (sextant off-focus); "open-book shape" (sextant in-focus). The crew themselves identify the cylinder reading as a focus artifact, leaving the **open-suitcase / open-book / L-shape morphology** as the most reliable observation. ^[inferred]

By contrast, the **GT-7 1965 sub-class A "bogey at ten o'clock high"** call is single-witness Borman, no morphology data, no instrument resolution, and the **Titan-II booster hypothesis is internally consistent** with the source record. ^[inferred]

## On the cylinder-vs-L-shape morphology resolution

The transcript captures a **focus-dependent ambiguity** in real time:

> **ARMSTRONG**: *"Or really two rings."* / *"Two rings. Two connected rings."*

> **COLLINS**: *"No, it looked like a hollow cylinder to me. It didn't look like two connected rings. You could see this thing tumbling and, when it came around end-on, you could look right down in its guts. It was a hollow cylinder. But then you could change the focus on the sextant and it would be replaced by this open-book shape. It was really weird."*

> **ALDRIN**: *"I guess there's not too much more to say about it other than it wasn't a cylinder."*

The interpretive close **converges on**:

- The **off-focus** cylinder/two-ring reading was **partially an instrument artifact** (Aldrin: *"with the sextant off focus what we saw appeared to be a cylinder"*). ^[extracted]
- The **in-focus open-book / L-shape / open-suitcase** reading is the more reliable morphology. ^[inferred]
- The **tumbling motion** (Collins: *"You could see this thing tumbling"*) is consistent with a passive ballistic body, not a controlled platform. ^[inferred]

**This is structurally significant**: prior corpus on-orbit observations have either no morphology (GT-7) or single-instrument morphology ([[entities/apollo-12|Apollo 12]] AOT). Apollo 11 d4 §6 is the corpus's **first multi-instrument morphology resolution**, and the crew themselves perform an **on-the-fly instrument-artifact analysis** to separate object morphology from optical artifact. This is **astronaut-class observational discipline** that strengthens the signal beyond what any single optical configuration would provide.

## On the S-IVB elimination

The single most important conventional-explanation closure: **Mission Control reported the [[concepts/saturn-v-s-ivb-stage|S-IVB]] at 6,000 miles range** at the time of observation. ^[extracted]

The Apollo 11 S-IVB (the third stage of Saturn V, designation **AS-506S-IVB**) was **deliberately targeted for solar orbit** after Apollo 11's spacecraft separated and Trans-Lunar Injection (TLI) was performed. The standard procedure was:

1. **TLI burn** by S-IVB → Apollo CSM/LM stack on translunar trajectory.
2. **CSM/LM separation from S-IVB** ("transposition, docking, and extraction").
3. **S-IVB slingshot maneuver** — propellant venting and a small burn to deflect the S-IVB onto a **lunar-flyby + solar-orbit trajectory**, ensuring it cleared the spacecraft's path. ^[inferred]
4. By **Day 3** (~48 hours post-TLI), the S-IVB was already **thousands of miles away** from the spacecraft on its diverging trajectory. ^[inferred]

The **6,000-mile range datum** is consistent with this maneuver geometry. ^[inferred] At 6,000 miles, the **S-IVB cannot have been the observed object** for the simple reason that the resolution required to see "L-shape" / "two rings" / "tumbling" through a monocular at that range is **not within the capability of the instrument and the human eye combined**. ^[inferred]

This is the **first conventional-explanation closure in the corpus that relies on independent ranging data rather than witness self-attribution or absence of follow-up**. ^[inferred]

## On the chronology of the LM-jettison candidate

Collins offers a tentative spacecraft-origin candidate:

> **COLLINS**: *"Skipping ahead a bit, when we jettisoned the LM, you know we fired an explosive charge and got rid of the docking rings and the LM went boom. Pieces came off the LM. It could have been some Mylar or something that had somehow come loose from the LM."*

This candidate is **chronologically incoherent**: ^[inferred]

- Apollo 11 **LM "Eagle" ascent stage jettison** occurred on **21 Jul 1969 UTC** (Day 6 of the mission, ~5 hours after the lunar liftoff) ^[inferred] — i.e., **~3 days after the Day-3 observation**.
- For Day-3 debris to originate from a Day-6 jettison event, **the debris would need to time-travel backward** — the candidate is impossible on this basis alone. ^[inferred]

Collins appears to be reasoning aloud through possibilities; Aldrin's response (*"it didn't appear to have that shape at all"*) declines the candidate on shape grounds without addressing the chronology. The transcript itself flags this with *"Skipping ahead a bit"* — Collins is consciously moving forward in the mission timeline to draw an analogy, not asserting that the Day-3 observation **was** LM jettison debris.

The **high-gain antenna fragment** candidate is more chronologically plausible, given the *"high gain antenna problems about this time"* contemporaneous correlation. ^[extracted] But it is also speculative — Collins offers no specific failure mode that would shed an antenna piece large enough to be visible at the resolved morphology.

## On the bump

> **COLLINS**: *"There was something. We felt a bump or maybe I just imagined it."*

> **ARMSTRONG**: *"He was wondering whether the MESA had come off."*

> **COLLINS**: *"I don't guess we felt anything."*

The **bump testimony is internally retracted within the same exchange** — Collins's first sentence is uncertain (*"or maybe I just imagined it"*), and his second sentence is a retraction (*"I don't guess we felt anything"*). The wiki should not record this as a corroborated physical-effect signal. ^[ambiguous] However, the **temporal coincidence with the high-gain antenna anomaly** is preserved in the transcript and may be a real correlation of two independent spacecraft-internal events (an anomaly that was real and a possibly-imagined bump) **separately from** the unidentified-object observation. ^[inferred]
