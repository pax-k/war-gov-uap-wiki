# Sighting — Seattle WA (22 Aug 1949)

Two related sightings on the evening of **22 August 1949** in the Seattle area, reported by McChord Flight Service Center (Lt Col Earl J. Livesay) on **23 August 1949** under file TCM 452 and routed to AMC, Attn: **MCIAXO-3**.

## Sighting 1 — over the city of Seattle, 1845P

Three NCO controllers from the **143rd National Guard Aircraft Control and Warning Squadron** (Boeing/Sand Point area) reported a single circular object passing N → S over Seattle.

- **Witnesses** (all assigned to 143rd NG ACW Sqdn, address 6736 Ellis Ave, Seattle 8 WA):
  - Sgt Jack Faulkner — on duty at the Squadron when phoned by Studeman.
  - T/Sgt T. D. Mullen — at his home in south Seattle.
  - Sgt Roger H. Studeman — at his home in north Seattle; first witness; phoned Faulkner.
- **Object signature**:
  - Circular, **75–100 ft diameter**, **10–15 ft depth** (oblate).
  - **Shiny aluminum**, reflecting sun.
  - **500–600 mph** estimated speed; **~10,000 ft altitude**.
  - Heading **N → S**, no maneuvering.
  - Sound: "similar to a jet aircraft."
  - No exhaust trail.
- **Multi-vantage corroboration**: Faulkner stepped outside the Squadron office on receiving Studeman's call and saw apparently the same object. Mullen, independently from the south end of the city, saw the same object and reported via the **CAA Control Tower at Boeing Field**, which relayed to McChord FSC. Both Faulkner and Studeman stated "it bore no resemblance to any aircraft they had ever seen before." ^[extracted]

## Sighting 2 — 30 mi WNW of Sea-Tac Airport, 1920P

Approximately 35 minutes later, two FAA Air Route Traffic Control Center controllers tracked an object visually consistent with an **F-84** jet fighter at 4,000–5,000 ft heading S → N.

- **Witnesses** (Seattle Air Route Traffic Control Center, P.O. Box 3224, Seattle 14 WA):
  - Ben Frieman — ARTC Controller.
  - Howard Watson — Senior ARTC Controller.
- The witnesses notified McChord FSC because they were aware that **no jet units were stationed in the area**. McChord FSC then checked all military bases in the area for any local jet/fighter activity — the result was a **negative report**. ^[extracted]
- Object signature: similar to F-84 in shape/size/color/speed; ~4,000–5,000 ft; S → N; no maneuvering; no sound; no exhaust trail.

## Significance

- **Trained military observers** (NCO ACW controllers, ARTC controllers) form the entire witness pool — no civilian-only links in this report. This is institutionally one of the higher-credibility sightings in [[references/usaf-flying-discs-1949|the 1949 bundle]] under the [[concepts/flying-disc-reporting-protocol|Cabell template]] item c.
- The **size estimate (75–100 ft × 10–15 ft)** is consistent with a Horten VIII-class flying wing in plan-view but is *not* consistent with the same target making a **jet-engine sound** at the reported altitude — Horten airframes were piston pushers. ^[inferred]
- The two sightings on the same evening from different vantage points and 35 minutes apart could reflect: (a) two separate phenomena, (b) one phenomenon transitioning from a reflective metallic mode to a more aircraft-like mode, or (c) the second sighting being an unconnected genuinely conventional jet whose flight plan was simply absent from McChord's records. The source does not adjudicate. ^[ambiguous]
