# Sighting — Mountain Home ID (24 Jul 1949)

A single-witness but **high-credibility** delta-wing-formation sighting **10 miles northwest of Mountain Home, Idaho**, at **1205 MST on 24 July 1949**. Filed by McChord Flight Service Center, file TCM 452, 25 July 1949, signed Lt Col Earl J. Livesay; routed to AMC, Attn: MCIAXO-3.

## Witness

**Harry Clark** — Airport Manager, Ritchie Field, Nampa, Idaho. The McChord covering letter notes that **Capt John S. Batie** of McChord knows Clark personally and "states that in his opinion Mr. Clark is a reliable witness." ^[extracted] Clark was in the air at the time, piloting a Cub Cruiser between Burley and Nampa, ID.

## Object signature

- **Number**: seven (7) objects in **V-formation**.
- **Shape**: **delta wings, no protrusions**, apex angle **30–45°**, base of triangle "slightly curved convex". A **dark circular structure** (consistent with a cockpit) was visible on the ventral surface of each object, "approximately one-fourth the distance from the apex of the delta wing to the rear" — observed only when Clark descended below the formation.
- **Size**: larger than an F-51.
- **Color**: "darker than normal aluminum skin and not shiny… light gray and a dirty white with no markings or diffusion of color."
- **Speed**: faster than an F-51, "at least 600 mph."
- **Heading / maneuver**: first observed at heading ~300°; the formation passed Clark's aircraft, then made a **180° turn to ~120°** — *without banking*. "The objects did not appear to bank during the turn." Outer wing surfaces "appeared to move slightly" before the turn.
- **Altitude**: 8,500–10,000 ft.
- **Sound**: none.
- **Exhaust trail**: none.

## Significance

This is the **single most Horten-thesis-compatible morphology in the entire 1949 file**: tailless delta wings in V-formation with a cockpit-like dark structure on the bottom, dirty-white finish, no exhaust, very high speed. Yet two specific signatures cut hard against the conventional explanation:

1. **Unbanked 180° turn** — physically inconsistent with a fixed-wing airframe of any era. Flying-wing aircraft (Horten Ho IX, V, etc.) bank in turns like every other fixed-wing aircraft. A flat-yawed reversal at 600+ mph by seven aircraft simultaneously is **outside the conventional flight envelope**. ^[inferred]
2. **No sound at all** at <10,000 ft from seven aircraft moving at 600+ mph — explicitly noted in the report. The Mountain Home detail does not match any 1949 aviation morphology, friendly or hostile. ^[inferred]

Combined with the V-formation pattern observed under optical instruments two weeks later in the [[references/sighting-medford-or-1949-08|Medford OR sighting]] (8 Aug 1949), this is the strongest **multi-object structured-formation** signature in the 1949 corpus. ^[inferred]

## Significance for the Horten thesis

The Mountain Home sighting is the closest the 1949 record comes to validating the [[concepts/horten-flying-wing|Horten flying-wing]] hypothesis — and the closest it comes to **breaking it**. The delta-wing morphology fits; the unbanked turn does not. See [[synthesis/horten-thesis-vs-disc-sightings]] for the updated analysis.
