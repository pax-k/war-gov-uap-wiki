# Belmont → Ladd, "Summary of Aerial Phenomena in New Mexico" — 21 August 1950

A 4-page **Standard Form 64 Office Memorandum** from **A. H. Belmont** to **D. M. Ladd** at FBI HQ, dated **21 August 1950**, subject *"SUMMARY OF AERIAL PHENOMENA IN NEW MEXICO — MISCELLANEOUS - INFORMATION CONCERNING"*. ^[extracted] Filed as **62-83894-246** ^[extracted] — the **opening serial of [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-6|section 6]]**. ^[inferred] Initials: `KWD/mp`. ^[extracted] Routing stamps: SEP 8 1950, SEP 18 1950. ^[extracted]

## Purpose section

The memo's three-point purpose ^[extracted]:

> To advise that: (1) OSI has expressed concern in connection with the continued appearance of unexplained phenomena described as green fireballs, discs and meteors in the vicinity of sensitive installations in New Mexico. (2) Dr. LaPaz, Meteor Expert of the University of New Mexico, reported that the phenomena does not appear to be of meteoric origin. (3) OSI has contracted with Land-Air Inc., Alamogordo, New Mexico, to make scientific study of the unexplained phenomena.

These three points form the **paradigm-defining frame** for section 6 ^[inferred] — and the entry-point for **all** of section 6's subsequent green-fireball / Twinkle / LaPaz / critical-facility-anomaly content.

## Nature-of-phenomena classification

The memo classifies the phenomena into **three explicit types** ^[extracted]:

1. **Green fireballs** — objects moving at high speed in shapes resembling half moons, circles and discs emitting green light.
2. **Discs** — round flat-shaped objects or phenomena moving at fast velocity, emitting brilliant white light or reflected light.
3. **Meteors** — aerial phenomena resembling meteoric material moving at high velocity, varying in color.

Color range: *"brilliant white to amber, red and green."* ^[extracted]

**Volume**: *"Since 1948, approximately 150 observations of aerial phenomena referred to above have been recorded in the vicinity of installations in New Mexico. A number of observations have been reported by different reliable individuals at approximately the same time."* ^[extracted]

The **~150 sightings since Dec 1948** count is the FBI corpus's **first preserved NM-area UAP cumulative-volume statistic** ^[inferred] — consistent with the [[references/fbi-san-antonio-vital-installations-1949|22 Mar 1949 SAC San Antonio memo]]'s *"more than 10 prototype events"* and *"about 20 deviation events"* counts at the 90-day mark of the wave. Both counts come from the **same OSI 17th District statistical apparatus**. ^[inferred]

## LaPaz analysis (paraphrased)

The memo paraphrases **LaPaz's 23 May 1950 [[references/lapaz-seventh-report-1950-05-23|Seventh Report]]** ^[extracted]:

> Dr. LaPaz, Director, Institute of Meteoritics, University of New Mexico, submitted an analysis of the various observations on May 23, 1950. He concluded, as a result of his investigation, that approximately half of the phenomena recorded were of meteoric origin. The other phenomena commonly referred to as green fireballs or discs he believed to be U.S. guided missiles being tested in the neighborhood of the installations. Dr. LaPaz pointed out that if he were wrong in interpreting the phenomena as originating with U.S. guided missiles that a systematic investigation of the observations should be made immediately. Dr. LaPaz pointed out that missiles moving with the velocities of the order of those found for the green fireballs and discs could travel from the Ural region of the USSR to New Mexico in less than 15 minutes. He suggested that the observations might be of guided missiles launched from bases in the Urals.

The paraphrase preserves both of LaPaz's **two alternative interpretations**: ^[extracted]

- **Primary (most-likely)**: ~50% meteoric + ~50% **U.S. guided missile testing**.
- **Fallback**: if the US-missile interpretation is wrong, then **USSR Urals-launched guided missiles** with <15-minute flight time.

This is the **first preservation in the FBI corpus** of the Urals-missile fallback hypothesis ^[inferred] — and the **first formal NM-area Soviet-threat UAP framing** in the wiki corpus.

## Project Twinkle introduction

The memo introduces **[[concepts/project-twinkle|Project Twinkle]]** ^[extracted]:

> The Air Force together with Land-Air, Incorporated, have established a number of observation posts in the vicinity of Vaughn, New Mexico, for the purpose of photographing and determining the speed, height and nature of the unusual phenomena referred to as green fireballs and discs. On May 24, 1950, personnel of Land-Air, Incorporated, sighted 8 to 10 objects of aerial phenomena. A 24-hour day watch is being maintained and has been designated "Project Twinkle."

This is the **canonical FBI-corpus founding documentation** of Project Twinkle ^[inferred]:

- **Sponsor**: AMC Geophysical Research Division (per the enclosed 25 May 1950 OSI summary).
- **Contractor**: Land-Air, Inc., Alamogordo NM.
- **Site**: observation posts vicinity of Vaughn NM.
- **Architecture**: 24-hour day watch + photography + speed/height/nature instrumentation.
- **First operational success**: 24 May 1950, 8-10 objects sighted by Land-Air personnel — **the project's apparent first operational day**. ^[inferred]

## FBI-Bureau jurisdiction posture

The memo's posture section ^[extracted]:

> The Albuquerque Office, in a letter dated August 10, 1950, advised that there have been no new developments in connection with the efforts to ascertain the identity of the strange aerial phenomena referred to as green fireballs and discs. The Albuquerque Office advised that Dr. Anthony O. Mirarchi, Project Engineer, had been informed of the Bureau's jurisdiction relative to espionage and sabotage and arrangements have been made so that the Bureau will be promptly advised in the event additional information relative to this project indicates any jurisdiction on the part of the Bureau.
>
> ACTION: None. The above is for your information.

The **AF-contractor-to-FBI-Albuquerque liaison framework** is documented here for the first time in the FBI corpus ^[inferred]:

- **Mirarchi as Project Engineer** is named the FBI's institutional touch-point.
- **Bureau jurisdiction** is explicitly framed as *"espionage and sabotage"* — i.e. the BB-57 / SAC Letter #38 jurisdictional boundary, not UAP-investigation-per-se.
- **Notification trigger**: any Twinkle finding *"indicates any jurisdiction on the part of the Bureau"*.
- **Bureau action**: **none**.

The framework is **operationally identical** to the BB-57 passive-intake posture extended to a scientific-investigation tier ^[inferred] — the Bureau accumulates Twinkle's outputs as **inbound information** rather than as the basis for active investigation. The structural read: **Twinkle is the AF's investigative organ; the FBI is its inbound intake routing partner**. ^[inferred]

## Position in the file

Serial 246 is the **opening serial of section 6** ^[inferred] — and the **frame-setter for the section's 271-page content**. ^[inferred] The 9 Oct 1950 [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-6|Ladd → Director update]] (serial 250) is the **6-week follow-on** that reports OSI Wright Field's preliminary findings *"no space ships or missiles from any other planet or country … no definite pattern of activity"* ^[extracted] — i.e. **the AF investigative organ has already concluded against the LaPaz Urals-missile fallback hypothesis** by 6 weeks into the project. The two memos together establish the **two-track institutional posture** of Twinkle's first quarter:

- **LaPaz / academic**: anomalous pattern (Urals-time-correlation, north-bearing approach, copper-spectral signature).
- **OSI Wright Field / institutional**: *"no definite pattern of activity"*.
