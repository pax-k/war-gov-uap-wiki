# Sighting — Castro's Ranch CA (23 Sep 1948)

A two-witness daylight observation on **23 September 1948 at approximately 1200** at **Castro's Ranch, 4 miles due east of San Pablo, CA and 4½ miles south of Pinole, CA**, filed as **Project SIGN Incident 176** in [[references/project-sign-incident-summaries-173-233|the Sep 1948 – Jan 1949 incident summaries bundle]]. The morphology described — an undulating, translucent, "amoeba-like" body with flapping appendages and a spherical dark core — is **unique** in the wiki's corpus and does not correspond to any other documented UAP class.

## Witnesses

- **Col Horace S. Eakins** (US Army, Retired 1948) — 1024 Esther Drive, Walnut Creek, CA. Formerly a veterinarian; until 1948 served on the Army Mission in Panama; civilian post: California State Department of Agriculture meat inspector. ^[extracted]
- **Mr Bentham** — companion, corroborating witness; the summary sheet records that "Bentham substantiated the fact that there was an object above the bomber." ^[extracted]

(The summary sheet uses "Col Eakins" / "Col Makins" inconsistently — likely an OCR variation; the formal Field 4 listing reads "Horace S. Makins, Col., U.S. Army (RET)". ^[ambiguous])

## Why this event matters

Three features make this sighting structurally distinct in the 1948 corpus:

1. **Apparent biological / organic morphology** — the descriptor "amoeba" with flapping/oscillating appendages is qualitatively different from every other 1948 sighting in the wiki, which describe rigid metallic or fully-luminous objects. The **canvas-like translucent body** + **spherical dark core** + **appendage motion** triple is not consistent with any 1948 candidate aircraft, balloon, parachute decoy, or Horten-derived flying-wing hypothesis. ^[inferred]
2. **Apparent flight differential against a known reference** — the object was observed **above** an in-frame Army bomber. The witness used the bomber as a sizing-and-altitude reference (at ~1 mi above the bomber), which is a stronger basis for spatial inference than abstract estimation. ^[inferred]
3. **Trained-observer credibility** — Col Eakins was a US Army veterinarian / line officer with 30+ years of service, retired in 1948 from foreign service. The incident summary records no character disqualifier (unlike Inc 189 Lt Rubisch, where the squadron commander noted a tendency to exaggerate). ^[extracted]

## Open hypotheses

The sheet's evaluators do not propose a candidate identification. The "canvas-like translucent body" descriptor invites a few non-exotic possibilities the original investigators may not have considered:

- **A wind-deformed parachute** (military or research)? Possible — but parachutes do not typically present "appendages" or sustain "flapping" motion at high speed; and the witness explicitly compared the object's speed to faster than anything he had seen before. ^[inferred]
- **A swarm of birds / debris cluster**? The witness's drawing (referenced as "see attached drawing", not preserved in the OCR) might have settled this; without it, the descriptor "flapping appendages" with "stable spherical center" is hard to match to a flock or debris field. ^[inferred]
- **A high-altitude balloon being torn apart in turbulence**? The 43k-ft cosmic-ray balloon cluster identified in Inc 194 about six weeks later (3 Nov 1948 — 8 MIT balloons "resembled an inflated intestine") is the closest matched-disposition case in the bundle. The Castro's Ranch object was **at much lower altitude** (~1 mi) and showed **organized appendage motion** rather than amorphous tearing — making the balloon hypothesis less compelling here. ^[inferred]
- **A genuine unknown** — held open.

The Castro's Ranch event is preserved here primarily as a **morphology anomaly** worth tracking forward — particularly if any later corpus events show similar "appendaged translucent" descriptors. ^[inferred]

## Provenance and reliability

- The two-witness configuration is structurally stronger than a single-witness sighting. ^[inferred]
- Col Eakins's retired-officer status and post-Army professional posture (state agriculture meat inspector — a stable, technically adjacent role) place him squarely in the trained-observer band. ^[inferred]
- The summary sheet's investigator notes do not record a character-credibility caveat — unusual when sightings invoke unconventional morphology, and arguably an implicit reliability endorsement. ^[inferred]
- The narrative is internally consistent and includes specific physical referents (the Army bomber as in-frame size reference, the dark spherical core's depth-of-color gradient as evidence the center was a globe rather than a flat disc). ^[extracted]
