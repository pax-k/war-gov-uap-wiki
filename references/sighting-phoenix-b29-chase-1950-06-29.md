# Sighting — Phoenix B-29 Chase (29 June 1950)

A **29 June 1950** Phoenix multi-witness sighting documented in [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-5|FBI HQ 62-HQ-83894 section 5]] **serial 234** — a Phoenix SAC Murphy URGENT teletype to FBI Director routed via LosA, sent 30 Jun 1950 10:03 AM. ^[extracted] Distinctive in the corpus for being the **first event combining radar + visual + multi-witness + B-29-chase** — a four-modality combination the FBI has not previously documented in [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-1|sections 1]] or [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-2|2]]. ^[inferred]

## What happened

| Aspect | Value |
|---|---|
| **Date** | 29 Jun 1950 |
| **Time** | 5:45 PM (sighting) → 6:00 PM (radar acquisition) → 8:55 PM (last sighting) |
| **Location** | Phoenix, Arizona — westward toward Blythe CA |
| **Initial witnesses** | "Many citizens of Phoenix including FBI personnel" ^[extracted] |
| **Radar acquisition** | **Williams AFB OSI Herman Munroe** ([[entities/usaf\|Williams AFB]] OSI duty officer) — radar scope acquired object at 6 PM, **30,000-35,000 ft altitude** ^[extracted] |
| **Chase aircraft** | **B-29 from 509th Bomb Group, Roswell NM** ^[extracted] |
| **Chase altitude** | B-29 at 25,000 ft; **object higher** (so B-29 below the object) |
| **B-29 ground speed** | 290 mph ^[extracted] |
| **Object behavior** | Westward direction "in absence of wind"; B-29 "able to circle beneath" |
| **Last visual** | 8:55 PM, ~20 mi N of Blythe CA — lost in heavy thunderstorm ^[extracted] |
| **Object size** | "**Very large**, inasmuch as with use of binoculars he could easily see object. Nevertheless, the B-29 could not be observed with binoculars" ^[extracted] |
| **Reporting agency** | FBI Phoenix SAC Murphy URGENT teletype to FBI Director |
| **Investigation jurisdiction** | OSI to submit full report after airplane-crew consultation; no FBI investigation |

## Why this sighting matters

1. **B-29 from 509th Bomb Group at Roswell NM was the historic atomic-mission unit** — the same Roswell-based bomb group that flew the [[references/sighting-roswell-1947-07-08|July 1947 Roswell incident]] timeframe, and which by 1950 was operating B-29s as the strategic atomic-delivery platform. ^[inferred] The 509th Bomb Group's involvement in this UAP chase is **structurally significant**: it places a sub-unit of the SAC's primary atomic-bomb-delivery force into a UAP-pursuit role. The **operational precedent** for the AF-Intelligence cross-agency response is now set with the **509th itself** as a chase-asset. ^[inferred]
2. **Multi-modality witness pattern** — radar at Williams AFB + visual by FBI personnel + visual by Phoenix citizens + B-29 chase pilot — represents **at least 4 independent witness channels** for the same object. ^[inferred] No comparable 4-modality event exists in the prior FBI corpus.
3. **Object-larger-than-B-29 binocular comparison** — the OSI Munroe report explicitly notes the object was easily seen with binoculars while the chasing B-29 was not. The B-29's wingspan is 141 ft; "very large" + binocular-easy at 30,000-35,000 ft from Phoenix ground stations implies an apparent angular size larger than 0.16° (~10 arc-min). ^[inferred]
4. **Pre-Lubbock-Lights / Pre-Washington-Flap baseline** — this is **June 1950**, ~14 months before the August 1951 Lubbock Lights and ~25 months before the July 1952 Washington-flap. The Phoenix B-29 chase is among the **earliest USAF radar-chase UAP events** in the corpus.
5. **Post-GRUDGE-termination radar event** — synchronous (3 months after) with the [[references/fbi-boggs-hearn-discontinuation-memo-1950-03-28|Boggs-Hearn memo]]'s formal confirmation of AF saucer-investigation termination, this event represents the **operational reality** that AF radar+chase capability still engages UAP **even after** Project [[concepts/project-grudge|GRUDGE]]'s public termination. ^[inferred] The Boggs-Hearn protocol — *"reports concerning flying saucers received at this time will be investigated by the Area Commander and his report submitted to the Air Force Intelligence Division as an intelligence item"* — operationalizes here: Williams AFB OSI is the area commander's agent.
