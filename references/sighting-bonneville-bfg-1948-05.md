# Sighting — W.A. Bonneville / B.F. Goodrich, Plevna–Miles City Montana (17 May 1948)

A **20-minute night roadside sighting** on **17 May 1948 ~2330** between **Plevna and Miles City, Montana** on **US Highway #12 (locally #212)**, by **William A. Bonneville**, Territory Manager for **The B.F. Goodrich Company**, traveling west alone in his car. Filed as **Project SIGN Incident 130** in [[references/project-sign-incident-summaries-101-172|the 101–172 incident summary bundle]]. ^[extracted]

This case is significant for two reasons orthogonal to the morphology of the object itself:

1. **Industrial-witness class** — Bonneville is a national-tire-company territory manager (corporate rather than civilian-individual or military), an observer class otherwise sparse in the 1947–48 SIGN corpus.
2. **Earliest documented MCIAXO-3 routing line** — Bonneville's 23 May 1948 letter triggered a **15 June 1948 elaboration-request letter from Col W.R. Clingerman** at Wright Field carrying the reference **`MCIAXO-3`**. This pushes the documented date of the MCIAXO-3 routing line back ~6 months from its prior 1949 anchor (see [[entities/air-materiel-command]]). ^[extracted]

## What the witness reported

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Date / time | 17 May 1948, ~2330 |
| Location | US Hwy #12, just west of Plevna MT, before the hills; observed from Powder River Bridge (engine off, car stopped) |
| Object count | 1 |
| Distance | At closest approach ~2,500 ft |
| Duration | ~20 minutes |
| Speed | "Twice the speed of Northwest Airlines planes" |
| Direction of flight | West, then slightly south, then west again — **performed arc**, repeated 3+ times |
| Tactics | "Performed arc" (witness's term); came in, hung in NW, turned south toward highway, brightened, then back west into cloud bank |
| Sound | **None** (engine off; "I could not hear motors") |
| Size | "Fairly large compared with an aircraft" |
| Color | White, possibly bluish-white tinge — "as bright as the headlight on a locomotive, only about three times that bright and without direction" |
| Shape | Ball |
| Exhaust trails | "A long bright light shooting from under it" |
| Weather | Moonlit but broken / partly cloudy |
| Manner of disappearance | "Flew off through the heavy cloud bank to the West" |
| Optical aid | Witness's own pair of field glasses |

## Witness credentialing

Bonneville's status as observer is established within the source by:

- **15 years of weekly driving the same highway after dark** (Lemmon SD / Hettinger ND → Miles City MT) — claim of route-familiarity and night-driving experience that lets him rule out "star" and "aircraft" by his own catalog. ^[extracted]
- **Carrying field glasses with him professionally** — and using them during the observation. ^[extracted]
- **Stopping the car and cutting the engine specifically to assess** — a deliberate test of the no-sound hypothesis. ^[extracted]
- **Hesitation to report** — "this was somewhat difficult for me to do because of the crackpot stories making the rounds in some sections about flying saucers etc., but for the life of me this had all the ear marks of just that kind of a deal." ^[extracted]
- **Affirming sobriety** — "I am perfectly sincere and do not drink so the foregoing is absolutely the truth." ^[extracted]

The intake-form summary classifies the object's apparent construction as "N/S — but apparently meteor-like" — but the witness himself rejects meteor and aircraft hypotheses based on duration (20 min, not seconds), motion pattern (repeated arcing back-and-forth, not straight), and the no-motor-sound at 2,500-ft closest approach. ^[extracted]

## The Bonneville → Clingerman letter exchange

The case is preserved in the file as **three distinct documents** stapled together:

### 1. The 25-field intake-form sheet (Inc 130)

Standard Project SIGN Check-List, all fields completed from Bonneville's 23 May 1948 letter content. Filing line: RESTRICTED.

### 2. Bonneville's 23 May 1948 letter

Addressed to **United States Army — Intelligence Div., Washington, D.C.**, on B.F. Goodrich Co. Minneapolis letterhead but mailed from Bonneville's Bismarck ND territory address (415 W. Rosser Ave). 6 paragraphs; includes map showing the observation location. Closes "Yours very truly, The B. F. Goodrich Company / /s/ Wm. Bonneville / Territory Manager".

This routing — to Army Intelligence Division in Washington, not directly to AMC — is structurally identical to the citizen-letter intake pathway documented in the [[references/usaf-flying-discs-1948|Feb–Jun 1948 AFOIR file]] (which routes inbound through Washington-side AFOIR-CO before forwarding to AMC). ^[inferred]

### 3. The 15 June 1948 Clingerman reply (paraphrased via Bonneville's 18 June 1948 second letter)

**Col W.R. Clingerman** at **U.S. Army Air Forces, Wright Field, Dayton, Ohio**, sent Bonneville a follow-up letter on **15 June 1948** asking for elaboration. The letter carried the reference line **"Refer - MCIAXO-3"**. ^[extracted]

This is the **earliest preserved instance of MCIAXO-3** as an AMC office symbol in the wiki corpus — predating the prior 1949 anchor by ~6 months. See [[entities/air-materiel-command]] for the corrected routing-codes table.

The Clingerman letter asked five specific elaboration questions, all answered in Bonneville's 18 June 1948 reply:

| Q | Topic | Bonneville's answer |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | Glowing entire object vs. carrying lights | "Ball of bright white light"; could not determine carrier mechanism |
| (b) | Size in comparison with known object | "About as bright as the headlight on a locomotive, only about three times that bright and without direction" |
| (c) | Speed vs jet or conventional aircraft | "Twice the speed of any aircraft that Northwest Airlines planes fly" |
| (d) | Random circling vs definite flight pattern | Came in faintly, grew larger over hills N of highway, hung in NW, turned south toward highway, brightened, back west, repeated several times |
| (e) | Could disappearance be moonlight-on-polished-surface obscured by clouds? | "No — I have seen aircraft many times on moonlight nights — this thing glowed brightly white light such as any real white brilliant light might... in the black section of the sky over to the North of me" |

Bonneville's reply closes: **"In times like these I feel as before stated anything of this nature which we are not familiar with we are duty bound to report to our Defense Forces who may be better equipped to understand the unfamiliar than we are."** ^[extracted]

The reply is signed **W.A. BONNEVILLE — Territory Mgr for the B. F. Goodrich Company / WAB**.

## What this case anchors for the wiki

### 1. Industrial-corporate witness pool

Bonneville is the **first national-corporation territory-manager-class witness** anchored to a named Project SIGN incident in the corpus. ^[inferred] His occupation has three observational virtues for the SIGN intake-evaluation perspective:

- **Geographic mobility within a regular beat** — he knows the local night sky and ground-traffic patterns at meter-detail across multiple states.
- **Field-equipment proficiency** — he carries and uses field glasses professionally.
- **Reputation discipline** — a regional tire-distribution manager risks commercial reputation by writing "flying saucer" letters to Army Intelligence; the act itself is evidentially load-bearing.

### 2. The MCIAXO-3 anchor

The 15 June 1948 Clingerman letter is the **earliest documented use of MCIAXO-3** in the wiki corpus. Prior to this repair, [[entities/air-materiel-command]] anchored MCIAXO-3 at 1949; the documented date now moves back to **mid-June 1948**, **3.5 months after** the 27 Feb 1948 [[entities/c-p-cabell|Cabell]] AFOIR-CO-5 directive. ^[extracted]

**Col W.R. Clingerman** is identified by this case as the active officer at MCIAXO-3 in mid-1948 — a name not previously anchored in the wiki. ^[extracted]

### 3. Civilian-letter intake pathway

The case documents the **citizen-letter → Army Intelligence Division Washington → AMC Wright Field** pathway operating at scale by spring 1948 — the same pathway that [[references/usaf-flying-discs-1948]] documents through AFOIR-CO. The Clingerman 15 June 1948 reply demonstrates that AMC was **actively soliciting elaboration from civilian witnesses by letter**, not just passively receiving Washington-routed forwards. ^[inferred]
