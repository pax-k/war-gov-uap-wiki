## Why this artifact is in the corpus — and what it does *not* contain

This is the wiki's first **null-content / boundary-case orbital-NASA artifact**. The page-24-4 excerpt records three distinct astronaut observations, **none of which is a UAP report**. Together with the 2001 Moscow figurative-UFO cable ([[references/dos-cable-russia-georgia-2001-10]]) and the 2004 Ashgabat NGO cable ([[references/dos-cable-turkmenistan-2004-11]]), it forms a **third structural mechanism** by which non-UAP material enters the FOIA UAP-keyword release universe: **transcript-keyword false positives** in mission debriefings (here driven by "fireball" — meaning reentry-plasma — and "flashes" — meaning ALFMED cosmic-ray events). ^[inferred] The corpus's **diplomatic-channel** umbrella has its own typology of boundary cases ([[concepts/figurative-ufo-rhetoric]]); this artifact is the **orbital-class** analogue and may anchor a similar typology if d1/d2/d4/d5/d7 confirm the pattern. ^[inferred]

## The three observations on page 24-4

The transcript excerpt is 16 lines spread across three speakers:

### 1. EVANS — reentry "tunnel with bright spot"

> *"...after the brightness of the fireball decreased, I could look back up through the rendezvous window and see what to me was kind of like a tunnel with a bright spot in the middle of the tunnel. Way down the tunnel, way back behind, I could see the fireball."* ^[extracted]

[[entities/ronald-evans|Ron Evans]] (CMP) describes looking **rearward through the rendezvous window during atmospheric reentry**, after the **plasma fireball brightness decreased**. The "tunnel with a bright spot" is the **trailing plasma sheath / wake** visible from inside the [[entities/apollo-17|Command Module]] during reentry — a documented Apollo CM-window phenomenon, not a UAP observation. ^[inferred] The source word *fireball* in this passage refers unambiguously to the **reentry plasma**, not to the bolide / atmospheric-fireball morphology that drives UAP/meteor cataloguing in the wiki's Project SIGN sections (e.g., [[concepts/balls-of-fire-red]], [[concepts/green-fireballs-new-mexico]]). ^[inferred] **Disambiguation note**: any keyword index built on "fireball" will pull this transcript line as a false-positive against UAP corpora — a quantifiable source of FOIA-pipeline boundary cases. ^[inferred]

### 2. CERNAN — "tin can" wisecrack at the recovery carrier

> *"The only unusual sighting I can recall during landing or recovery is when the CMP looked out the window and saw the superstructure of an aircraft carrier and said, 'Oh, we've got a tin can with us.'"* ^[extracted]

[[entities/gene-cernan|Gene Cernan]] (CDR) characterizes the only **"unusual sighting"** during landing/recovery as Evans's **post-splashdown sight of the recovery aircraft carrier's superstructure** through a fogged window — joked as *"a tin can with us"*. The recovery vessel for Apollo 17 was [[entities/uss-ticonderoga|USS Ticonderoga (CV-14)]]. ^[inferred] **The "unusual sighting" framing is internal-jocular**, not a UAP-class observation; the keyword "sighting" in this debriefing line will pull as a false positive against UAP corpora. ^[inferred] EVANS clarifies *"Well, it was kind of foggy on the windows"* — the misidentification was driven by post-splashdown window condensation, not by the ship being unusual. ^[extracted]

### 3. SCHMITT — light flashes and the ALFMED experiment

> *"Transearth we had only a small crescent of an Earth and it was not feasible to do any extensive weather observations. We had light flashes just about continuously during the whole flight when we were dark adapted. I had one which I thought was a flash on the lunar surface. That one period of time when we had the blindfolds on for the ALFMED experiment there were just no visible flashes, although that evening, that night, before I went to sleep I noticed that I was seeing the light flashes again. So, it just seemed to be that one interval either side of it where the light flash was not visible to myself or to the other two crewmen."* ^[extracted]

[[entities/harrison-schmitt|Harrison Schmitt]] (LMP) reports the **cosmic-ray-induced retinal light flashes** that Apollo crews routinely experienced when **dark-adapted in cislunar space**, and references the **ALFMED experiment** (**Apollo Light Flash Moving Emulsion Detector**) — a NASA-instrumented investigation designed precisely to characterize these flashes by correlating crewmen's reports with cosmic-ray track detectors worn over the eyes during a blindfolded sleep period. ^[inferred] Schmitt's notable contribution on this excerpt is **a single flash he interpreted as a flash on the lunar surface** during transearth coast — a candidate for an unrelated phenomenon (transient lunar phenomenon, cosmic-ray-on-CCD, or ALFMED-class retinal event). The source does not resolve which. ^[ambiguous]

The ALFMED interval observation is itself **scientifically substantive** — Schmitt notes that during the blindfolded experiment window the flashes vanished for him and for the other two crewmen, and resumed that night — but the phenomenon is a **documented neuro-optical effect of cosmic-ray ions traversing the retina**, with a NASA-internal investigative apparatus already in place by Apollo 17. ^[inferred] Not a UAP report.

## What the excerpt establishes

- **Three Apollo 17 crewmen — [[entities/gene-cernan|Cernan]], [[entities/ronald-evans|Evans]], and [[entities/harrison-schmitt|Schmitt]] — were debriefed on observations during landing, recovery, and transearth coast.** ^[extracted]
- **Reentry plasma (the "fireball") was visible through the rendezvous window from inside the Command Module after peak brightness decreased**, with a tunnel-and-bright-spot wake morphology. ^[extracted]
- **Cosmic-ray retinal light flashes were experienced "just about continuously" by all three crewmen when dark-adapted**, including during transearth coast. ^[extracted]
- **The ALFMED experiment was conducted on Apollo 17 with all three crewmen blindfolded** for a defined sleep-window interval, during which Schmitt reports the flashes were not visible to himself or the other two crewmen. ^[extracted]
- **Schmitt observed one flash he believed was on the lunar surface** during the transearth phase. ^[extracted] No follow-up resolution in the excerpt.
