# NASA d7 — Skylab 1/2, 1/3, 1/4 Technical Crew Debriefings (Jun 1973 – Feb 1974)

An **11-page Mistral-OCR'd composite excerpt** drawn from **all three [[entities/skylab|Skylab]] mission Technical Crew Debriefings** — **JSC-08053 (SL-2 Jun 30 1973)**, **JSC-08478 (SL-3 Oct 4 1973)**, and **JSC-08809 (SL-4 Feb 22 1974)** — prepared by the **Training Office, Crew Training and Simulation Division** (SL-2/SL-3) and **Crew Training and Procedures Division** (SL-4) at **[[entities/nasa|NASA]] Lyndon B. Johnson Space Center, Houston**. The d7 artifact is the **seventh** and **terminal** file in the wiki's NASA d-cluster (d1 [[references/nasa-uap-d1-apollo-12-transcript-1969|Apollo 12 voice loop]]; d2 [[entities/apollo-17|Apollo 17]] transcript pending; d3 [[references/nasa-uap-d3-gemini-7-transcript-1965|Gemini 7 transcript]]; d4 [[references/nasa-uap-d4-apollo-11-technical-crew-debriefing-1969|Apollo 11 technical]]; d5 [[references/nasa-apollo-17-science-debriefing-1973|Apollo 17 science]]; d6 [[references/nasa-apollo-17-technical-crew-debriefing-1973|Apollo 17 technical]]; **d7 Skylab — this artifact**).

The d7 excerpt contains **three structurally distinct UAP-adjacent observations across the three Skylab missions**:

| Mission | Crew | Phenomenon | Polarity | Wiki anchor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SL-2** (May–Jun 1973) | [[entities/pete-conrad\|Conrad]] / [[entities/joseph-kerwin\|Kerwin]] / [[entities/paul-weitz\|Weitz]] | **In-cabin cosmic-ray retinal light flashes** with **SAA hypothesis** offered + new phenomenology data (left-eye localization, sunburst-vs-streak morphology, 2–3/min rate inside-magnetosphere baseline) | **LEO-baseline cosmic-ray-flash datum** — corpus's first | [[concepts/cosmic-ray-light-flashes]] |
| **SL-3** (Jul–Sep 1973) | [[entities/alan-bean\|Bean]] / [[entities/owen-garriott\|Garriott]] / [[entities/jack-lousma\|Lousma]] | **~10-minute multi-witness observation of a bright reddish co-orbital object** with 10-second rotation period at ~30–50 NM range; morphology unresolved; ground identification not supplied | **Sub-class F (NEW)** — multi-witness range-constrained morphology-unresolved conventional-candidate-not-eliminated | [[references/sighting-skylab-3-red-object-1973-09]] |

The **declassification posture** is the lightest in the d-cluster: SL-2/3/4 covers carry only the FOIA notice, no classification stamp visible in the OCR, in contrast with d4 (CONFIDENTIAL Group 4 / E.O. 11652 declassification path) and d6 (CONFIDENTIAL with 90-day auto-declassification clause). The Skylab debriefings were therefore **distribution-controlled but not formally classified** — at the lower-restriction end of NASA's MSC/JSC technical-debriefing posture gradient. ^[inferred] This may reflect the **post-Apollo institutional shift**: by 1973–74, NASA's mature manned-spaceflight program had moved its standard technical debriefings out of the CONFIDENTIAL bucket. ^[inferred]

The **office structure** is also distinctive: the Skylab debriefings were prepared by the **Training Office, Crew Training and Simulation/Procedures Division** at JSC — the **same MSC/JSC organizational locus** that prepared the **d6 Apollo 17 technical debrief** (4 Jan 1973). The center was renamed **Manned Spacecraft Center → Lyndon B. Johnson Space Center** in February 1973, between d6 (Jan 1973 cover, "Lyndon B. Johnson Space Center" already on cover) and d7 SL-2 (Jun 1973). ^[inferred] The division renaming **Simulation → Procedures** between SL-3 (Oct 1973) and SL-4 (Feb 1974) is a sub-office-level reorganization within the Training Office; **the same office prepared all three Skylab debriefings**.

## SL-2 §23 — Cosmic-ray retinal light flashes with SAA hypothesis

The SL-2 excerpt (pages 23-20 and 23-21) records the **§23 Unusual or Unexpected Visual Phenomenon** discussion among **[[entities/pete-conrad|Conrad]] (CDR), [[entities/joseph-kerwin|Kerwin]] (Science Pilot), and [[entities/paul-weitz|Weitz]] (Pilot)**.

> **KERWIN**: *"Unusual or Unexpected Visual Phenomenon. We saw light flashes. I think all of us saw them. I saw them most often when I was in the sack at night with my eyes closed but awake naturally. They tended to wax and wane in frequency. Someone asked me if that was in conjunction with the South Atlantic anomaly. It may have been. I didn't have the pad with me at that time and I don't know. They were numerous at times - two or three per minute."* ^[extracted]

> **CONRAD**: *"Some of them to me were a spot or sunbursts. Some were streaks. The streaks, in my case, were less frequent than the bursts. Most of them were in my peripheral visual field. Very few in the central visual field. I don't know why."* ^[extracted]

> **QUERY**: *"You could isolate them to one eye, couldn't you?"* ^[extracted]

> **KERWIN**: *"No, I couldn't."* ^[extracted]

> **WEITZ**: *"I would say mine were primarily in the left eye for some reason."* ^[extracted]

> **CONRAD**: *"You have to concentrate but you can determine they are in one eye."* ^[extracted]

> **KERWIN**: *"I did not. That was foolish of me but I didn't try. I'm sure they are in one eye."* ^[extracted]

> **CONRAD**: *"Sometimes I'd be lying there with my eyes half closed, and I'd see a fire sensor wink."* ^[extracted]

> **KERWIN**: *"And you'd have to be careful that you weren't confusing that with the fire flash. Once you've seen a few of each, there is question of which is which. They're not an hallucination."* ^[extracted]

> **KERWIN**: *"We didn't feel it was operationally necessary for anybody to know about it right now."* ^[extracted]

> **WEITZ**: *"I had a couple that I thought were cosmic particles. I saw an entrance streak and an exit streak."* ^[extracted]

> **CONRAD**: *"Yes, I did too."* ^[extracted]

> **WEITZ**: *"Where, bing-bing, it seemed like it was one side of the eyeball, and then the other side."* ^[extracted]

> **CONRAD**: *"One night I remember that there was a long shot then it was blank then there was a long shot in rapid succession, of course, but very definitely in and out - or across the eye."* ^[extracted]

The SL-2 testimony **directly answers the [[concepts/cosmic-ray-light-flashes|cosmic-ray retinal flash concept page's]] long-standing open question** about the **LEO inside-magnetosphere baseline rate**. ^[inferred] Substantive findings:

1. **Phenomenon is observed in low Earth orbit** at altitudes inside the geomagnetic field's main shield. Skylab's altitude (~435 km) is **inside the magnetosphere** but **traverses the [South Atlantic Anomaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic_Anomaly)** — the dip in the inner Van Allen belt where the trapped-particle population reaches very low altitudes, producing locally elevated charged-particle flux on spacecraft. ^[inferred]
2. **Crew offered SAA correlation hypothesis at debrief.** Kerwin: *"Someone asked me if that was in conjunction with the South Atlantic anomaly. It may have been."* ^[extracted] This is the **earliest astronaut-debrief-level statement of the SAA-as-cosmic-ray-flash-mechanism** in the corpus, predating the d6 Apollo 17 ALFMED testimony by 6 months. ^[inferred] (The d6 ALFMED experiment was on **cislunar coast**, outside the magnetosphere — a fundamentally different particle-flux regime. ^[inferred])
3. **Flash rate of 2–3/min** at the wax-and-wane peaks. ^[extracted] This is **2-3× higher** than [[entities/buzz-aldrin|Aldrin]]'s d4 cislunar coast estimate of *"maybe something like one a minute"*. ^[inferred] If the SAA hypothesis is correct, the elevated rate is consistent with peak-SAA-passage flux being higher than baseline cislunar GCR flux — which is the **opposite** of the naive "outside-magnetosphere has more particles" expectation, and is a **physically interesting finding**. ^[inferred] The crew's qualitative *"wax and wane"* observation matches the orbital-period-modulated SAA-passage pattern.
4. **Single-eye localization phenomenology** — **Weitz: "primarily in the left eye"**; **Conrad: "you can determine they are in one eye" (with concentration)**. ^[extracted] This is **new phenomenology data not present in d4 / d6**. The single-eye character is consistent with single-particle-traversing-one-eye chord-track physics; the asymmetry (Weitz left-eye preference) is structurally interesting but not explained in the source. ^[inferred]
5. **Sunburst-vs-streak morphology distinction** — Conrad: *"some of them to me were a spot or sunbursts. Some were streaks. The streaks, in my case, were less frequent than the bursts."* ^[extracted] The two morphology classes correspond to **(a) end-on retinal entry or termination of the particle track ("sunburst")** and **(b) chord traversal of the retina ("streak")** — both predicted by the standard cosmic-ray-on-retina model. ^[inferred] This is **the corpus's first explicit two-class morphology distinction** for cosmic-ray retinal flashes.
6. **Peripheral-vs-central visual field bias** — Conrad: *"Most of them were in my peripheral visual field. Very few in the central visual field."* ^[extracted] This is consistent with **rod-dominated peripheral retina being more sensitive to single-photon stimulation** than cone-dominated foveal retina. ^[inferred] **First astronaut-class observation of this bias** in the corpus.
7. **Entrance-streak / exit-streak double events** — Weitz: *"I saw an entrance streak and an exit streak"*; *"Where, bing-bing, it seemed like it was one side of the eyeball, and then the other side."* ^[extracted] Conrad: *"there was a long shot then it was blank then there was a long shot in rapid succession ... very definitely in and out - or across the eye."* This is **direct phenomenological confirmation** of [[entities/buzz-aldrin|Aldrin]]'s 1969 d4 *"double flashes ... at points separated by maybe a foot"* model — the **same single-particle-two-events chord-track structure** observed by independent astronauts on a different mission 4 years later. ^[inferred]
8. **Hallucination explicitly ruled out**: Kerwin: *"They're not an hallucination."* ^[extracted] **Crew-side observational discipline** datum.
9. **Operational deferral** — Kerwin: *"We didn't feel it was operationally necessary for anybody to know about it right now."* ^[extracted] The crew **chose not to interrupt operations** with real-time reporting of the phenomenon, unlike Aldrin's Day-5/Day-7-of-mission air-to-ground discussion on Apollo 11. This indicates the phenomenon was **already established lore** in the astronaut corps by 1973 — Aldrin's 1969 d4 testimony and the 1972 ALFMED experiment had created a sufficient prior context that the SL-2 crew did not need to flag the observation in real time. ^[inferred]

## SL-3 §7 / §20 — Multi-witness "red object" co-orbital sighting

The SL-3 excerpt records the **bright reddish co-orbital object observation** by **[[entities/alan-bean|Bean]] (CDR), [[entities/owen-garriott|Garriott]] (Science Pilot), and [[entities/jack-lousma|Lousma]] (Pilot)** approximately **a week before splashdown** (~September 1973 ^[inferred]) from the Skylab wardroom window. The observation appears in **two parallel sections** of the SL-3 debrief — **§7 visual sightings (general)** and **§20.0 Visual Sightings (dedicated chapter)**.

The full transcript and observation properties are documented at [[references/sighting-skylab-3-red-object-1973-09]]. Summary:

- **3 witnesses simultaneously** — Bean, Garriott, Lousma; initial spotter Lousma. ^[extracted]
- **~10 minutes of persistent observation** through the wardroom window. ^[extracted]
- **Bright reddish object** — much brighter than Jupiter or any other planet; reddish even when well above the horizon. ^[extracted]
- **10-second rotation period** measured from brightness variation. ^[extracted]
- **~30–50 NM range** estimated from 5–7-second sunset-shadow lag. ^[extracted]
- **~10–20° angular drift** over 10 minutes — co-orbital trajectory. ^[extracted]
- **Morphology unresolved** — *"never did take the shape of an object"* (Lousma); *"we could never make it into anything other than a bright light"* (Bean). ^[extracted]
- **Ground identification not supplied** in the source record despite explicit crew request. ^[extracted]
- **Crew working hypothesis**: *"obviously a satellite in a very similar orbit to our own"* (Garriott) — but the satellite's identity is not established. ^[extracted]

The SL-3 observation is the corpus's **first sub-class F entry** in the [[concepts/orbital-uap-sighting]] typology — see the sighting page for the operational definition and the A/E/F discriminator argument.

Lousma's §20.0 supplementary material includes additional non-UAP observations during the same period: *"thousands and thousands of stars out there ... drifting along the X-axis"* (which led to the **CSM RCS leak** identification — a non-UAP avionics finding); *"the leak in the same manner of the RCS streaming towards"* (CSM RCS again); and the post-insertion *"leak in the same manner"* observation — these are **mission-systems anomalies** with conventional explanations, included for context but not UAP-relevant.

## SL-4 §7 — Flashing lights, sub-class D extension

The SL-4 excerpt (pages 7-8 and 7-9) records the **§7 Visual Sightings discussion** among **[[entities/gerald-carr|Carr]] (CDR), [[entities/edward-gibson|Gibson]] (Science Pilot), and [[entities/william-pogue|Pogue]] (Pilot)**. The relevant UAP-adjacent passage is:

> **CARR**: *"One other area of unusual events that we reported on the dump tapes was that on occasion we saw some lights flashing outside with very a definite motion relative to ours. We presumed that they were other pieces of Skylab, or possibly other satellites. We reported our two or three sightings of that kind as soon as they occurred. We have no special comments concerning them, but we did find it very interesting to be able to see other objects up there with us. The fact that one or two of them appeared to be tumbling was apparently due to the oscillation of the light flashes that we were getting from them."* ^[extracted]

The structural features:

- **Astronaut-spontaneous report** — Carr volunteers the observation as *"one other area of unusual events"*. ^[extracted]
- **Multiple events** — *"two or three sightings of that kind"* over the SL-4 mission. ^[extracted]
- **Real-time reporting** — *"reported our two or three sightings of that kind as soon as they occurred"* (on the dump tapes — the post-pass voice loop). ^[extracted]
- **Phenomenology** — *"lights flashing outside with very a definite motion relative to ours"*; *"one or two of them appeared to be tumbling ... due to the oscillation of the light flashes"*. ^[extracted]
- **Astronaut-self-attributed-conventional in the same transmission** — *"We presumed that they were other pieces of Skylab, or possibly other satellites."* ^[extracted]
- **No follow-up commentary** — *"we have no special comments concerning them"*. ^[extracted]

This is **the second sub-class D instance in the corpus** — paralleling [[entities/alan-bean|Bean]]'s [[references/nasa-uap-d1-apollo-12-transcript-1969|Apollo 12 d1 AOT particle-flash observation]]: astronaut-spontaneous + UAP-adjacent lexical markers (*"flashing"*, *"motion relative to ours"*) + astronaut-supplied conventional explanation in the same transmission (Skylab debris / other satellites). The two sub-class D entries are separated by **4 years and 1 month** (Bean's d1 observation MET ~5d 19h ~ 20 Nov 1969; Carr's d7 SL-4 observation ~Dec 1973 – Feb 1974).

The **structural significance**: sub-class D is now a **two-instance class** spanning **two different mission generations** (Apollo 12 lunar-mission rendezvous-phase vs. Skylab orbital-workshop occupation-phase) and **two different observational instruments** (Bean's [[entities/alan-bean|AOT periscope]] vs. Carr's wardroom / cabin window). The class is **mechanism-stable** across these variations: same astronaut-spontaneous + same-transmission-self-attribution pattern.

Pogue's adjacent §7 passages cover non-UAP **OWS Heat Exchanger lint vacuuming** (operational anomaly) and **EVA water leaks** (operational anomaly) — these are mission-systems content, not UAP-relevant.

The d7 excerpt also preserves several non-UAP physical observations that are corpus-irrelevant but documented for completeness:

- **SL-4 OWS Heat Exchanger lint accumulation** (Pogue, page 7-9) — design issue with missing upstream filters. Non-UAP.
- **SL-4 EVA water leaks** (Gibson, Pogue, page 7-9) — operational issue. Non-UAP.
- **SL-3 fire sensor wink** (Conrad, SL-2 page 23-21 — but Conrad was SL-2 CDR, not SL-3; this passage is from the SL-2 §23 discussion) — Conrad's contextual remark to disambiguate cabin fire-sensor optical signal from cosmic-ray flash phenomenology. Non-UAP.
- **SL-2 sack movement** (Bean, SL-3 page 7-4 line 1) — *"The sack would move over in the corner"* — mission-systems / sleep-restraint observation. Non-UAP. (Note: the *"BEAN"* attribution on page 7-4 is in the SL-3 cover-batch, but Bean was SL-3 CDR, so the speaker assignment is consistent. ^[extracted])

## D-cluster typology — d7 introduces sub-class F

Across the corpus's NASA orbital-class sub-corpus, the d7 ingest extends the d-cluster typology from **5 sub-classes (A/B/C/D/E)** to **6 sub-classes (A/B/C/D/E/F)**:

| Sub-class | Definition | Anchor |
|---|---|---|
| **A** — Positive (canonical bogey-class call) | Single-witness no-morphology no-instrument-mediated bogey-class call. Conventional candidate (booster) is *internally consistent* with the source record. | GT-7 1965 ([[references/sighting-gemini-7-bogey-1965-12-04]]) |
| **B** — Null with UAP-keyword false-positive | Lexical UAP-keyword match in non-UAP semantic context (reentry plasma / fogged window / cosmic-ray flash). | Apollo 17 d6 1973 ([[references/nasa-apollo-17-technical-crew-debriefing-1973]]) |
| **C** — Null without UAP-keyword (whole-document inclusion) | Zero UAP-keyword content. PI testimony or ground-investigator material. | Apollo 17 d5 1973 ([[references/nasa-apollo-17-science-debriefing-1973]]) |
| **D** — Partial-signal (astronaut-spontaneous + astronaut-self-attributed conventional) | Astronaut-spontaneous observation with UAP-adjacent lexical markers and astronaut-supplied conventional explanation in the same transmission. **Now a 2-instance class** with d7 SL-4 Carr extension. | Apollo 12 d1 1969 ([[references/nasa-uap-d1-apollo-12-transcript-1969]]) + Skylab 4 d7 1973–74 (this artifact) |
| **E** — Strong-positive (multi-witness, instrument-mediated, alternative-eliminated, morphology-resolved) | Three-witness simultaneous observation; instrument-mediated optical resolution at multiple focal settings; conventional candidate explicitly eliminated by independent data; morphology resolved. | Apollo 11 d4 1969 ([[references/nasa-uap-d4-apollo-11-technical-crew-debriefing-1969]]) |
| **F** *(new)* — Positive (multi-witness, range-constrained, persistent, morphology-unresolved, conventional-candidate-not-eliminated) | Multi-witness + persistent + range-constrained from witness-side data alone + measured rotation period + co-orbital track + reddish-spectral anomaly. Morphology unresolved. Conventional candidate (another satellite) is the crew's own prior; not internally eliminated, not externally identified. | **Skylab 3 d7 1973** *(this artifact)* |

The **F vs A vs E distinctions** are the corpus's most refined orbital-class typological structure:

- **A / GT-7**: single-witness, no morphology, no range data, no rotation period, single-bearing call. Booster hypothesis internally consistent.
- **F / Skylab 3**: **multi-witness, no morphology, range data (from witnesses), rotation period measured, persistent track, conventional candidate not eliminated.** Differs from A by the multi-witness / range / persistence axis; differs from E by the morphology-unresolved / candidate-not-eliminated axis.
- **E / Apollo 11**: multi-witness, **morphology resolved** (cylinder / two-rings), **range data from Mission Control**, conventional candidate (S-IVB) **explicitly eliminated**, multi-instrument optical resolution.

The **F-class is the corpus's first instance of "the crew describe the object well enough to know what it is *not* (a star, a planet, a normal-trajectory body), but cannot resolve enough to know what it *is*"** — a structurally distinct epistemic position from both A (could be the booster) and E (was not the booster). ^[inferred]

## What the d7 establishes (overall)

- **Skylab is the corpus's first orbital-station mission frame** — distinct from Gemini (rendezvous-class), Apollo (lunar-class), and the late-station-era ISS context.
- **Three distinct UAP-adjacent observations in a single source artifact** — first in the d-cluster (d4 had cylinder + cosmic-ray flashes + lunar-orbit "laser" but those were three observations on one Apollo 11 mission; d7 has three observations across three Skylab missions).
- **First sub-class F entry** in the [[concepts/orbital-uap-sighting]] typology — the SL-3 red object.
- **Sub-class D extends to 2 instances** with the SL-4 Carr flashing-lights observation.
- **The [[concepts/cosmic-ray-light-flashes|cosmic-ray retinal flash concept page's]] LEO-inside-magnetosphere baseline open question** is **substantively answered** by SL-2 §23 — answer: 2–3/min peak rate, single-eye localization, peripheral-field bias, sunburst-vs-streak morphology distinction, SAA-correlation hypothesis offered by crew, double-events directly confirming Aldrin's 1969 chord-track model.
- **NASA's manned-spaceflight institutional posture by 1973–74** had moved standard technical debriefings to a **lower-restriction (FOIA-controlled but unclassified)** posture compared to the d4 (CONFIDENTIAL Group 4) and d6 (CONFIDENTIAL with auto-declass) Apollo-era debriefings. ^[inferred] The d7 covers carry only the FOIA notice. This is the **third NASA disclosure-posture data point** in the corpus's intake-channel gradient (after MSC PAO release-by-default 1965, MSC CONFIDENTIAL technical 1969 d4 / 1973 d6).
- **[[entities/alan-bean|Bean]]'s second corpus appearance** on a structurally different observation — Apollo 12 d1 (sub-class D, water-boiler self-attribution) vs. Skylab 3 d7 (sub-class F, multi-witness range-data co-orbital). **The same observer's two reports occupy two different sub-classes** — paralleling Aldrin's d4 cylinder-vs-flashes split. ^[inferred]
- **[[entities/pete-conrad|Conrad]]'s second corpus appearance** as **SL-2 CDR participant** in the cosmic-ray-flash discussion — first as Apollo 12 CDR on the d1 tracking-light-burnout exchange (non-UAP empirical confirmation of co-orbital debris baseline), now as SL-2 CDR cosmic-ray-flash co-witness.
- **The d-cluster (d1–d7) is now closed** — 6 of 7 ingested (d1, d3, d4, d5, d6, d7); d2 Apollo 17 transcript remains pending. The orbital-class sub-corpus has reached **6-mechanism resolution** (sub-classes A/B/C/D/E/F).

## What the d7 does *not* establish

- **No identification** of the SL-3 red object. ^[extracted]
- **No bearing or precise time data** for the SL-3 observation in the OCR excerpt — the *"channel A"* voice-loop record is referenced but not preserved here. ^[extracted]
- **No morphology** for the SL-3 red object — multiple optical aids tried, all failed. ^[extracted]
- **No size estimate** for the SL-3 red object — without morphology resolution, only brightness and color are constrained. ^[extracted]
- **No NORAD / Space Surveillance Network correlation** preserved in the OCR. ^[open]
