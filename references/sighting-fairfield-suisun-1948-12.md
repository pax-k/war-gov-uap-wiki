# Sighting — Fairfield-Suisun AFB CA (3 Dec 1948)

A two-witness AACS-tower-operator sighting on **3 December 1948 at 2015 PST** at **Fairfield-Suisun AFB**, California. Reported via 1501st Air Transport Wing (Pacific Division MATS), file D/AC/dwm dated **19 January 1949**, signed Maj Arthur Conradi Jr. (Chief of Intelligence). This is the **first complete "Essential Elements of Information / Project SIGN" form** preserved in the corpus.

## Witnesses

- **Sgt Joseph Don Delafayette** (AF 12106504) — honorably discharged 6 December 1948 (i.e., three days after the sighting). Resident of R.D. 1, Ballston Spa, NY.
- **PFC Bruce Earlin McFarland** (AF 16278687) — control tower operator, 1901st AACS Squadron, Fairfield-Suisun AFB. Prior to military service: 3 years as a radio announcer (sporting events). **AGCT score 143** (≥99th percentile). ^[extracted]

The AACS officer in charge characterized both as "competent, reliable… psychologically stable… fully qualified mentally and physically for control tower duty."

## Object signature

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Number** | 1 |
| **Color** | Colorless white light |
| **Shape** | Round light, **30 inches diameter** |
| **Construction** | "Looked like a searchlight with the light intensity of a runway light" |
| **Direction of flight** | NNW → SSE |
| **Initial altitude** | 500–1,000 ft |
| **Pause altitude** | ~3,000 ft (when opposite the tower) |
| **Final altitude** | ~20,000 ft (climbed out of sight) |
| **Time in sight** | ~25 sec total |
| **Speed** | ~400 mph initial → ~200 mph (slowed opposite tower) → 400–500 mph climb-out |
| **Tactics** | Came in low NNW, slowed near tower, hovered briefly at ~3,000 ft, **then climbed almost vertically** to ~20,000 ft and disappeared |
| **Sound** | None |
| **Exhaust trail** | None |
| **Fins / stabilizers / wings / fuselage / propulsion / antenna** | All "not observed" |

## Independent witness statement

A **separate certified statement** by McFarland is preserved as Inclosure 3, dated 12 January 1949:

> "The object first shot into view about 2 miles north of the control tower… climbing on a low trajectory, with a speed estimated at something in excess of 400 miles per hour… the object had a very slight undulating or bouncing motion… took an almost vertical rise or climb… The light was clearly seen with the naked eye, brilliant but by no means blinding. In size it was comparable to one of Fairfield-Suisun AFB's high-intensity runway lights." ^[extracted]

McFarland phoned the AACS Operations Officer during the event hoping to extend the witness pool; the second tower operator continued tracking it during this call.

## Conventional-candidate ruleouts inside the EEI form

Items 4, 6, and 7 of the General section explicitly address candidate explanations:

- **No flying scheduled or observed** in the vicinity at the time. ^[extracted]
- **A winds-aloft balloon was released 35–45 minutes prior** to the sighting and lost from view 10 minutes after release. The form notes this without claiming it as the explanation. ^[ambiguous]
- **Object made no apparent contact with the earth.** No fragments. No radioactivity readings.
- **Annotated photographs and diagrams** of the base were attached as Incl 1 + 2 (not preserved in the OCR).

## Significance

- **First fully populated EEI Project SIGN form** in the corpus, demonstrating the operational maturity of the [[concepts/flying-disc-reporting-protocol|10-element reporting template]] expanded into a 20-element formal-intelligence schema by late 1948.
- The witness signature (an AACS PFC with AGCT 143 + a discharged-three-days-later observer) is a classic example of the trained-observer bias in the early UAP corpus: institutional pipelines preferentially capture reports from precisely the *most* operationally credible witnesses. ^[inferred]
- The "round white light, 30 inches, vertical climb to 20k ft, no sound, no exhaust" morphology is a clean **luminous-orb** signature (cf. [[concepts/orb-phenomenon|orb phenomenon]]). The 1948 instance predates the 1949 [[references/sighting-kodiak-alaska-1949-04|Kodiak]] event by ~4 months and the 2025 [[references/sighting-helicopter-orb-encounter-2025|helicopter orb encounter]] by ~77 years — same morphology class. ^[inferred]
- **The "almost vertical climb to 20,000 ft from 3,000 ft"** behavior is structurally outside the conventional aviation envelope of any 1948 airframe and matches the modern UAP "instantaneous altitude excursion" pattern. ^[inferred]

## Two-source companion: Project SIGN Inc 215 (NEW)

The same event also appears as **Project SIGN Incident 215** in [[references/project-sign-incident-summaries-173-233|the 173–233 incident summaries bundle]]. The two records are **complementary views** of the same event:

| Document | Form type | Detail level | Filing date |
|---|---|---|---|
| The bundled EEI report (this page's primary source) | 20-element Essential Elements of Information / Project SIGN form + Inclosure 3 (McFarland certified statement) | **Maximal** — names both witnesses, AGCT score, prior occupations, conventional-candidate ruleouts | 19 Jan 1949, signed Maj Arthur Conradi Jr. |
| Project SIGN Inc 215 summary sheet | 25-field Incident Summary Sheet | **Minimal** — control-tower personnel unnamed, no AGCT data, no inclosures | filed in the AMC stream, transmitted 22 Mar 1949 |

The Inc 215 summary sheet preserves the **operationally critical descriptors** (30-inch round bright light, 1–2k ft initial → 20k ft vertical climb, 400–500 mph, 8x binoculars, no sound, no exhaust) but lacks the witness-character detail. The EEI form is therefore the **investigative-grade record**; Inc 215 is the **routing-grade record**. ^[inferred]

The two-source presence of this event in the corpus is structurally important: it shows that the **same Project SIGN sighting could be filed twice** through parallel pipelines (the AACS / 1501st ATW / MATS Pacific Division route → the EEI form sent to AMC; and the AMC-internal Incident Summary Sheet that carries a sequential incident number). ^[inferred]
