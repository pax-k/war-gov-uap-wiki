# Project SIGN Incident Summary Sheets 173–233 (Sep 1948 – Jan 1949)

A 144-page Mistral-OCR'd bundle of **58 Project SIGN incident summary sheets** numbered 173 through 233 (gaps at 197, 210, 222), transmitted from Hq Air Materiel Command, Dayton, to D/I HQ USAF on **22 March 1949** under cover routing **R/S S-02378 of 3-9-49**, **EXHIBIT #529** ("Transmittal of Incident Summary Sheets 173 thru 233"). The cover sheet routes the file as `REPORT FILED M-5 AIRCRAFT - UNIDENTIFIED` (signed JONES, 2-27-50) with declassification authority **NND 917033**.

The file was ingested from NARA **Record Group 38** (Records of the Office of the Chief of Naval Operations and Naval Records), file 143685, Box 7 — but the originating agency, reporting protocol, and routing chain are entirely **USAF / AMC / Project SIGN**. The RG 38 placement reflects an ONI archival cross-file, not a Navy-originated investigation. ^[inferred]

## Why this file matters for the wiki

This bundle fills the **previously-empty gap** between [[references/usaf-flying-discs-1948|the Feb–Jun 1948 SIGN startup file]] and [[references/usaf-flying-discs-1949|the Oct 1948 – Jan 1950 continuation file]]. It is the first source in the corpus that:

- documents the **per-incident-numbered** intake stream ([[concepts/project-sign|SIGN]] used a unified sequential incident counter) at scale — 58 cases in a four-month window;
- shows the [[concepts/flying-disc-reporting-protocol|10-element / EEI observational template]] in **routine, not exceptional, use** — every sheet uses the same 25-field schema, with most cases summarized in 2–3 OCR pages;
- captures Project SIGN's **first systematic mass-encounter dataset**, including the December 1948 [[concepts/green-fireballs-new-mexico|New Mexico green fireball wave]] that prompted [[entities/lincoln-la-paz|Dr Lincoln La Paz]]'s analytical memo "Significant Differences between the Fireballs Observed in the Interval Dec 5–13 and Typical Meteors";
- contains the **first explicit UAP–aircraft engagement-class** report in the corpus — Inc 207 (18 Nov 1948, Andrews AFB), where 2nd Lt Kenwood Jackson chased an oval object for ~18 minutes including evasive vertical climbs and target-following maneuvers — directly contradicting the prior wiki claim that 1948 SIGN reports were uniformly passive.

## Routing apparatus and incident-numbering

The 173–233 batch establishes that by late 1948 Project SIGN was operating a **single sequential incident-number stream** for all flying-disc reports — each new sighting received the next integer regardless of geography, classification, or routing channel. The 173–233 numbers fill the gap between the [[references/usaf-flying-discs-1948|1948 file]] (which carries reports without standardized incident numbers, often referenced by date or by routing reference like AFOIR-CO-5) and the [[references/usaf-flying-discs-1949|1949 file]] (which uses the EEI / 20-element format with case-named filing rather than sequential numbers, suggesting the sequential-number system phased out as the case load became larger and more parallel-processed). ^[inferred]

The bundle preserves three artifacts that together establish the **internal Project SIGN paperwork stack**:

1. **The 25-field Incident Summary Sheet** (the predominant form, used for Incidents 173–229A) — descended from the [[concepts/flying-disc-reporting-protocol|10-element Cabell template]] but expanded to 25 fields with an explicit narrative section (Field 23: "Summary of Incident").
2. **The "Check-List Unidentified Flying Objects"** form (Incident 232 only) — a 26-field variant with reordered/renamed fields ("Number of objects observed", "Distance of object from observer", "Apparent construction"). Likely a regional-office variant. ^[inferred]
3. **The "Guide to Investigation"** template (RESTRICTED, GI CO INV 31G 1) at pages 139–142 — a four-page **investigator-facing** worksheet covering radar sightings (page 3) and conventional-aircraft / weather / soil-sample ruleouts (page 4). This is the **investigator's checklist**, distinct from the witness-statement form. ^[inferred]

## Provenance notes

- The 144 OCR pages include the cover R/S endorsement (page 0), 138 incident-content pages, 4 blank-template "Guide to Investigation" pages (139–142), and a 1960 receipt stamp (page 143).
- Three incident numbers (197, 210, 222) are absent from the OCR. Most likely the original NARA Box 7 file does not contain these sheets — possible reasons include withdrawn-during-declassification-review, never-completed investigations rolled into other incidents, or pages physically lost in a prior reproduction pass. ^[ambiguous]
- Incidents 26 and 031 appear out-of-sequence (Inc 26 is cross-filed with Inc 226 — both the same Sandia/Toulouse 6 Dec 48 event; Inc 031 is the OCR-rendered cover for Inc 231's retrospective Schroeder/Abilene 1 Jan 48 event). These are bookkeeping artifacts of the SIGN incident-numbering scheme accumulating retrospective filings against earlier event dates, not new events. ^[inferred]
- Multiple sheets carry handwritten annotations ("Returned Not Sent", "Under investigation", "No Info Rec", "Investigation continuing") — these are office-workflow markers, not OCR artifacts. ^[extracted]

## Why this matters for the wiki

This file is the **single most consequential 1948 ingest** after [[references/usaf-flying-discs-1948|the AFOIR file itself]]:

1. **It closes the Sep–Dec 1948 gap** in the corpus — previously the wiki had Feb–Jun 1948 (`usaf-flying-discs-1948`) and Oct 1948 onward (`usaf-flying-discs-1949` starts 26 Oct 1948 with the worldwide-reporting TWX), with virtually no source material for the September–early-November 1948 window. This bundle covers exactly that window with 58 events.
2. **It establishes the [[concepts/green-fireballs-new-mexico|December 1948 New Mexico green-fireball wave]]** as a tracked Project SIGN dataset — a well-known but poorly-sourced episode in mid-twentieth-century UAP history that here gets primary-source documentation including [[entities/lincoln-la-paz|La Paz]]'s analytical memo.
3. **It documents an aircraft-engagement-class event in 1948** (Inc 207, Andrews AFB) — directly contradicting the prior wiki claim that 1948 SIGN reports were uniformly passive. See [[concepts/uap-aircraft-engagement]].
4. **It triples the population of named USAF intelligence officers** in the wiki for 1948 — adding Col Klingfelman, Maj Boggs, the Hq USAF Guided Missile Group cohort, and the Pacific-theater radar-and-air-intercept teams.
