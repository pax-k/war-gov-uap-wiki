# Sighting — Stewart / Tyler, Ritchie Highway south of Baltimore MD, 29 March 1952

A reported **flying-saucer-shaped object encounter with electromagnetic engine-stoppage effect** on Ritchie Highway near the Harness Track south of Baltimore, Maryland, at **22:45 hours on 29 March 1952**. ^[extracted] Captured in [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-6|FBI HQ 62-HQ-83894 section 6]] at serial ~278 (pages 151-168). ^[extracted] The investigation file is a multi-document OSI "Project Eyeful" investigation conducted by SAs Clifford P. Jones, Oliver R. Weatherholt, and Boyce Royal of the 17th District OSI, dated 12 May 1952. ^[extracted] The case is the **first FBI-corpus EM-engine-stoppage car-encounter case** ^[inferred] and the **first FBI-corpus flying-disc case where the principal witness's prior Communist-affiliation record is fully surfaced in the investigation file**. ^[inferred]

## The two witnesses

- **Donald F. Stewart, age 23**, 224 Warren Avenue, Baltimore MD 30, **clerical worker at B. and O. Railroad**. ^[extracted]
- **George S. Tyler III, age 17**, 122 East Montgomery Street, Baltimore, **third-year student at Southern High School**, on the LaCrosse Team. ^[extracted]

The two were returning from Glenn Burnie MD to Baltimore in Stewart's **British Ford** ^[extracted].

## The sighting

The object was first detected by **a vacuum-cleaner-like sound** ^[extracted]:

> A sound similar to a vacuum cleaner attracted attention. Upon investigation, while traveling north toward Baltimore, a strange object was proceeding in the sky in an approximate north-south direction. It appeared to be moving very slowly (considerably slower than a commercial airliner).

Stewart's first-person description ^[extracted]:

- **50 ft diameter** when at rest above his automobile.
- *"Wavered slightly"* during 3-minute hover.
- **Sound** = *"similar to that of a vacuum cleaner"*.
- After ~3 minutes hovering, **turned on its edge** presenting its flat surface, then *"appeared to roll across the sky at a terrific rate of speed, greater than that of a jet plane"* ^[extracted].
- Disappeared at ~3.5 mi distance to the southwest, **terminal angular size = a 5-inch disk held at arm's length** ^[extracted].
- Initial angle to horizon: **50 degrees** ^[extracted].
- **Light** kept fading; *"sort of a white phosphorous color, bright around the outer rim"* ^[extracted].
- **Estimated altitude**: 200 feet ^[extracted].

## The EM-engine-stoppage effect

Stewart's account ^[extracted]:

> When the object was over the highway the car engine died suddenly and remained dead until it departed. A yellow Pontiac convertible traveling south along the highway experienced the same situation. Occupants in all cars were very frightened.

This is the **first FBI-corpus documented engine-stoppage EM-effect** in a UAP car encounter ^[inferred]. **A second vehicle** (the yellow Pontiac convertible) is reported to have experienced the same effect ^[extracted] — making the effect **multi-vehicle, not single-witness-induced**. ^[inferred]

Aftermath: Stewart's car taken to **Griebel Motors on Light St.**, where they *"over-hauled the entire car"* ^[extracted]. A friend, **Mr. Kenny Johnson** (electrician, former WWII aviation Jet mechanic), reportedly said *"something about wiring being magnetized"* ^[extracted] — proposing **EM-induction-of-permanent-magnetization** as a candidate mechanism for the engine failure. Stewart was *"not sufficiently familiar with the technical situation to recall his exact explanation."* ^[extracted]

## "Project Eyeful" investigation

The investigation is conducted under the name **"Project Eyeful"** ^[extracted] — apparently a Baltimore-area radio/journalism collaboration with Mr. Louis E. Corbin of **radio station WFBR Baltimore**, who *"had for some time been interested in the 'flying saucer situation', and that it had been his intention to write a documentary program for broadcast concerning that subject."* ^[extracted] Corbin received the original tip from a Baltimore newspaper reporter (unnamed). ^[extracted]

Investigation timeline ^[extracted]:

- **24 April 1952** — Corbin Interview #1 with Stewart at WFBR studio.
- **27 April 1952** — Corbin telephone Interview #2 with Tyler.
- **9 May 1952** — Headquarters OSI (Lt Col Lucius L. Free) requests investigation; predicated on info from Lt Col Ray W. Taylor (Air Technical Intelligence Center, Wright-Patterson AFB).
- **10 May 1952** — OSI SA interview with Corbin at WFBR.
- **10 May 1952** — FBI Field Office Baltimore records review.
- **10 May 1952** — Re-interview with Stewart at his Warren Avenue residence; *"Stewart appeared calm and answered all questions without hesitation."* ^[extracted]
- **11 May 1952** — Re-interview with Tyler.
- **11 May 1952** — Interview with Ulius Louis Amoss (Colonel USA Retired, Gibson Island MD).
- **11 May 1952** — Interview with Robert Meadows (Shell Service Station, Ritchie Highway).
- **11 May 1952** — Interview with William Roblecke Jr.
- **12 May 1952** — Records checks at Baltimore City PD + Baltimore County PD + Maryland State Police.

The investigation involved **at least 9 interviews + 4 records checks + 1 vehicle-records check at Griebel Motors** ^[extracted] — making this **the most thorough single-witness UAP investigation in the FBI corpus by the section-6 cutoff** ^[inferred].

## The Communist-affiliation subtext

The investigation surfaced Stewart's prior FBI record ^[extracted]:

> FBI Field Office records reflect no information on STEWART except that he was fired from employment at Baltimore, Maryland, for placing a decal bearing Communist insignia on a truck belonging to the firm by whom he was employed.

Specifically ^[extracted]: *"Donald Franklin Stewart, 224 Warren Avenue, Baltimore, Maryland, was questioned for illegal wearing of the Navy uniform in July 1948. Records also reflect that Stewart was a member of the U.S. Naval Reserve and the investigation was discontinued as no violation. These records also reflect that Stewart was fired from the Baltimore Supply Company, Baltimore, Maryland, for placing a decal of the hammer and sickle and red star on the door of a Baltimore Supply Company truck. This act was committed on 30 July 1948."*

**The hammer-and-sickle subtext** parallels the [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-6|Frank Scully Communist-background-check thread]] in the **same section** (serials 253-254, Oct 1950) ^[inferred] — i.e. the FBI's institutional posture in section 6 is to **route flying-saucer cases through a Communist-affiliation screen**, treating saucer-witness sincerity as **at least partially a counterintelligence question**. ^[inferred] Stewart's case differs from Scully's in that Stewart is **not the saucer-promoter** but the **principal witness** — the Communist-decal subtext provides an **operative impeachment frame** for the institutional posture toward the case. ^[inferred]

## Tyler retraction

Tyler retracts under reinterview ^[extracted]:

> Stewart had seen the "saucer" prior to the time of meeting him and that all the information which he had given was information that Stewart told him. Tyler advised that he did not see a thing and had reported the description of the aircraft as Stewart had told him. He stated that Stewart had told the sighting of the aircraft to him as they returned from Glenn Burnie, Maryland, to Baltimore, and asked him (Tyler) to affirm the report because he feared that no one would believe his st[ory].

This is a **single-witness retraction** ^[inferred] — Stewart's account loses corroboration, and the multi-witness EM-effect frame (Stewart's car + Pontiac convertible) loses its second-vehicle confirmation as well (since Stewart was the source of that detail too). The OSI investigation closes the case as **unconfirmed**.

Independent witnesses contradict the encounter ^[extracted]:

- **Mr. Robert Meadows** (Shell Service Station, 1501 Ritchie Highway): on duty that night; *"to his knowledge no such incident took place"*; *"highly unlikely … that any three (3) minute period could elapse without the passage of several automobiles."*
- **Officer Gavin** (Anne Arundel County Police): no reports of the sighting; *"highly unlikely that an automobile could stop for a period of three (3) minutes on a Saturday night."*
- **Louis F. Tyler** (uncle of Tyler III): *"does not believe his nephew's story … TYLER III can dream up fantastic stories at times. He believes it is a hoax."*
- **Miss Mary Hoover** (226 Warren Avenue): Stewart *"shoots a lot of bull, usually bragging about himself."*
- **Griebel Motors records**: the British Ford was serviced 23 Apr, 8 Dec, 18 Dec 1951, 31 Jan, 20 Mar, **8 May 1952** — routine checkups, **no record of repairs consistent with a major electrical event**. ^[extracted]

## Institutional significance

The Stewart case is structurally distinctive in the FBI corpus ^[inferred]:

1. **First EM-engine-stoppage car-encounter case**, predating later canonical EM-effect cases by ~15 years.
2. **First case where a candidate scientific mechanism for the EM effect** (Johnson's "wiring magnetized" theory) is offered and preserved.
3. **First "Project Eyeful" radio-journalism-led civilian investigation** in the FBI corpus.
4. **First saucer-case where the principal witness's prior FBI security-check record is surfaced as part of the same investigation file** — and the **hammer-and-sickle decal** specifically.
5. **First case where multiple independent witnesses (Meadows, Gavin, Tyler) actively contradict the encounter**.
6. **Closes as a single-witness account** after Tyler's retraction, despite the case's apparent multi-witness opening.

The case is a **methodological exemplar** ^[inferred] of how the FBI's section-6 passive-accumulation posture coexists with active OSI investigation at the witness-interview tier — and how **counterintelligence-screening reflexes** shape the institutional reception of flying-saucer cases in the immediate pre-1952-wave period.
