# Sighting — Gemini 7 "Bogey at 10 O'clock High" (4 Dec 1965, ~04h24m MET)

A short voice-loop exchange at approximately **04 hours 24 minutes mission elapsed time** (i.e., ~ **1954Z, 4 December 1965**) in which **[[entities/frank-borman|Cdr Frank Borman]]** of [[entities/gemini-7|Gemini 7]] reports *"a bogey at ten o'clock high"* to Mission Control Houston, alongside two other unambiguously identified in-frame objects (the spent Titan-II second-stage booster and a separately-observed particle stream). Recorded on **[[references/nasa-pao-t-00763-r1b-gemini-7-1965|NASA PAO Tape T-00763(R1b)]]**.

This is the wiki's first **orbital / outside-atmosphere** sighting record, the first **astronaut-witness-class** sighting, and the first sighting where the originating-agency intake channel is **[[entities/nasa-pao|NASA PAO]]** (release-by-default), not a classified channel. See [[concepts/orbital-uap-sighting]] for the cross-corpus pattern (currently single-instance).

## Witnesses

| Name | Role | Sighting role |
|---|---|---|
| **[[entities/frank-borman\|Cdr Frank Borman]]** | Gemini 7 Command Pilot (CDR) | Initial caller of the bogey at "ten o'clock high"; subsequent caller of the booster and the 3–4 mi particle stream |
| **[[entities/jim-lovell\|Pilot James A. Lovell Jr.]]** | Gemini 7 Pilot | Confirmed the booster from his own vantage at "two o'clock"; described it as a "brilliant body in the sun against a black background with trillions of particles on it"; **does not corroborate the bogey** in the released transcript |

The PAO commentary (page 0) explicitly assigns the *"bogey at ten o'clock high"* call to **Borman**, and the *"trillions of particles on the booster"* call to **Lovell**.

## Source / authority

Released to the public on the same news cycle via **[[entities/nasa-pao|NASA PAO]]**, dubbed off the master tape at Mission Control Center Houston, played for the press at ~04h24m MET. Filed in **NARA RG 255** as tape **T-00763(R1b)**. Documentary artifact: **[[references/nasa-pao-t-00763-r1b-gemini-7-1965|PAO Tape T-00763 R1b transcript]]**.

The transcript is itself a **PAO release product**, not a classified intelligence record. There is no AF Form 112 evaluation grid, no SECRET / TS classification marking, no investigative case file referenced inside this artifact. ^[extracted]

## The three in-frame objects

The PAO commentary explicitly distinguishes **three** objects in this exchange. Conflating them is the most common error in popular retellings — both the source and the wiki's framing keep them separate.

### Object 1 — "The bogey"

- **Witness**: Borman (initial caller). Lovell does not separately confirm a bogey.
- **Bearing**: "ten o'clock high" relative to the spacecraft.
- **Morphology**: **none recorded**. ^[extracted] No shape, color, size, distance, or motion is attributed to the bogey distinct from the booster or the particles.
- **Persistence**: not addressed in the released transcript.
- **Whether distinct from the booster**: **ambiguous in the source**. Houston immediately offers booster-or-natural-sighting as the dichotomy (*"is that the booster or is that a natural sighting?"*); Borman replies *"a what?"* and (eventually) *"we have debris up here — this is an actual sighting"* without explicitly choosing between Houston's two options. ^[ambiguous]
- **PAO framing** (page 0, retrospective): *"the third and unidentified object of course was a bogey... there were several references to the bogey."* ^[extracted] PAO treats the bogey as a *third* object beyond booster and particles, but the source's underlying voice loop does not unambiguously establish that.

### Object 2 — The booster

- **Witnesses**: both Borman and Lovell.
- **Identity**: spent Titan-II second stage from GT-7's launch ^[inferred]
- **Bearing**: Borman *"in sight"* (relative bearing not separately given for him); Lovell at his **two o'clock position**, *"ahead of us at 2 o'clock, slowly tumbling."*
- **Morphology**: *"a brilliant body in the sun against a black background with trillions of particles on it"* (Lovell). The "trillions of particles" are most consistent with **outgassing / venting / paint-fleck / ice-crystal effluent** typical of a spent upper stage in vacuum. ^[inferred]
- **Motion**: slowly tumbling.
- **Persistence**: still in visual range during the entire exchange.

### Object 3 — The particle stream

- **Witness**: Borman.
- **Description**: *"hundreds of little particles going by to the left out about three or four miles."*
- **Distance**: 3–4 mi (statute miles) abeam, off the left side.
- **Track**: *"a path of the vehicle at 90 degrees"* — i.e., **crossing the spacecraft's orbital track at right angles**.
- **Subsequent motion**: *"they've passed now. They're going into polar orbit."*
- **Distinct from the booster's "trillions of particles"**: yes — the booster's particles are co-moving with the booster surface; this stream is on a 90° cross-track and 3–4 mi away.

The cross-track 90° stream is the **hardest object class to attribute** to GT-7's own launch. Plausible 1965-era candidates include another previously-launched object's debris field, a coincidental ice / urine dump from a separate orbital object, or unidentified. ^[inferred] There is no source-side disambiguation.

- No morphology of the bogey. ^[extracted]
- No estimated distance to the bogey. ^[extracted]
- No estimated size of the bogey. ^[extracted]
- No motion description of the bogey. ^[extracted]
- No on-board photographic confirmation referenced. ^[extracted]
- No follow-on questions from CAPCOM about the bogey after step 17 (Houston's question dissolves into Lovell's booster-confirmation, not into more bogey questions). ^[extracted]

The bogey is a **single-bearing, single-witness, no-morphology** call. Almost the entire downstream description in the released exchange is about the **booster** and the **cross-track particle stream**. ^[inferred]

## Witness pedigree

- **Borman**: Air Force Academy graduate, USAF test pilot (Edwards), one of the second NASA astronaut group ("Next Nine"). Disciplined structured observer; later commands Apollo 8 (first manned lunar orbit) in 1968.
- **Lovell**: Naval Academy graduate, USN test pilot (Patuxent River), same astronaut group as Borman. Subsequent commands include Gemini 12 and the Apollo 13 abort.

Both witnesses are at the **highest extant tier** of trained-observer pedigree in the corpus. Test-pilot training, structured spacecraft-systems familiarity, and operational discipline mean their reporting language ("bogey", "ten o'clock high", "going into polar orbit") is **technically precise** in aviation/military terms — none of which by itself identifies the object as anomalous. ^[inferred]

## Conventional-explanation status

The simplest reading of the released exchange is that **the bogey was the booster at initial visual acquisition**, before Borman fully resolved it as the spent stage and visually confirmed it as such on the next radio beat. Houston's immediate disambiguation question (*"is that the booster or is that a natural sighting?"*) suggests Mission Control held this exact prior. ^[inferred]

The argument **against** the simplest reading: PAO's commentary on page 0 explicitly treats the bogey as a third object beyond booster and particles. PAO had access to mission audio, post-event crew discussion, and Mission Control internal traffic that we do not see in the transcript. PAO's framing may reflect resolution beyond the released voice loop. ^[inferred]

The wiki's posture: **neither reading is preferred.** The source establishes that *the call was made* and that *PAO described it as a third object*, without resolving whether the bogey was the booster at initial-acquisition or a separate object. Treat the *"bogey was a separate object"* claim as `^[ambiguous]` in any downstream synthesis.

## Why this entry is in the UAP corpus

Independent of the bogey-vs-booster question, the artifact is in the corpus for these structurally distinct reasons:

1. **Institutional first**: NASA PAO release-by-default channel (see [[entities/nasa-pao]]), opposed to all other 1944–1994 corpus channels. ^[inferred]
2. **Witness-class first**: astronaut crew, on-orbit, public radio loop. ^[extracted]
3. **Domain first**: **orbital / outside-atmosphere** observation — see [[concepts/orbital-uap-sighting]]. ^[extracted]
4. **Terminology first**: aviation-jargon use of *"bogey"* in a primary US-government UAP-adjacent record. ^[inferred]
5. **Mission-control intake first**: the response chain is **CAPCOM → MET-stamped voice loop → PAO release**, not a written investigative file or cable. ^[extracted]

These are real corpus extensions even if the specific bogey call resolves to a misidentified booster. ^[inferred]

## Open questions

- Resolve **bogey vs. booster** with NASA's underlying mission transcript (NTRS / NARA RG 255). The released PAO tape is a derived product — the master voice loop and onboard recorder transcripts may resolve the ambiguity. ^[open]
- Locate **Borman's and Lovell's post-mission debrief statements** on the bogey-particle-booster sequence. Borman's autobiography (*Countdown*) and the NASA Johnson Space Center oral histories with Lovell may carry first-person clarification. ^[open]
- Locate the **GT-7 onboard 16 mm DAC film** for any frames near 04h24m MET. ^[open]
- Cross-check whether **Project BLUE BOOK** (active 1952–1969) opened a case file on the GT-7 bogey. ^[open] If not, that itself is informative — would suggest the bogey was treated as **resolved-as-booster** by USAF intelligence. ^[inferred]
- Identify the **CAPCOM** for the GT-7 day-1 voice loop at ~04h24m MET. The standard GT-7 CAPCOM rotation included **Elliot See, Charles Bassett, and Eugene Cernan**. ^[inferred]
- Cross-check **other Gemini-program "particle" / "luminous body" reports**, particularly Glenn's "fireflies" on MA-6 (Mercury, Feb 1962) and McDivitt's "Gemini 4 cylinder" (Jun 1965), as a comparison class. ^[open]
- Determine whether the **R1b suffix** on T-00763 is a revision of an earlier R1a / R0 release. ^[open]
