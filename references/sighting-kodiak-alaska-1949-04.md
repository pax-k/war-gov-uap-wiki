# Sighting — Kodiak, Alaska (8 Apr 1949)

An ONI **Confidential Intelligence Report** (DIO-17ND **Serial 13-49**, dated **23 April 1949**, subject "US/ALASKA - Meteorological Phenomena - Luminous Energy") describing a multi-witness ball-of-fire event over the **U.S. Naval Operating Base, Kodiak, Alaska**, on the night of **8 April 1949**.

## Witnesses (4 named)

1. **Deputy U.S. Marshal Paul HERBING** — Kodiak, Alaska. Sighting at **2030**: "flaming greenish-blue object plummet across the sky at altitude 2,500 ft, est. speed 1,500 mph", over Anton Larson Bay, W → E, paralleling the E-W runways of NAS Kodiak, ~15 sec visible, vanished near Puffin Island, Chiniak Bay. Distinct from a meteor — "too large and there were no sparks."
2. **Lt Cdr D. SHEPARD, USN** — Operations Officer, US NOB Kodiak. Sighting at **2040**: a reddish ball of fire ~2 ft diameter, W → E at 2,500 ft, 21–20° flat trajectory, "seemed to disintegrate over Chiniak Bay." No opinion on nature.
3. **Lawrence B. SHAW** — Taxicab driver, Arrow Cab. Sighting at **2035**: bright blue object, ~1.5 ft dia, with a 3-ft "pinkish in shading" streamer, over Old Woman Mtn, W → E at 2,500 ft, disintegrating beyond Nyman Peninsula. Shaw was firm it was **not a meteor or shooting star**.
4. **Paul KREUGER** — Bus driver, US NOB. Sighting at **2045**: greenish-blue object 1.5 ft × 10 ft, ~500 ft over hangar #3 of the Air Station, W → E. "Like a large greenish tracer shell."

## Object signature (synthesized)

- **Trajectory**: West to East, paralleling E-W NAS runways, low altitude (500–2,500 ft).
- **Color variants** across witnesses: greenish-blue (Herbing, Krueger); reddish (Shepard); bright blue with pinkish streamer (Shaw).
- **Size variants**: 2 ft (Shepard); 1.5 ft × 10 ft (Krueger); 1.5 ft × 3 ft streamer (Shaw).
- **Flat-trajectory descent** at 21–25°, decelerating into apparent disintegration.
- **Time spread**: ~15 minutes (2030 → 2045) across the four witnesses, suggesting either (a) a single object with a very long visible track, (b) multiple sequential events, or (c) clock-synchronization error among the witnesses. ^[ambiguous]
- **No sound emitted** by the object (per all four witnesses). ^[extracted]
- **Weather** (Navy Weather Central): 3,000 ft scattered clouds, 15 mi vis, NNW wind 15 kts, 27°F.

## ONI evaluation

> "As the above statements are approximately the same in position, altitude, course and time of the luminous manifestation, its existence has been evaluated as **probable**. However, as the shape, size and color of the object have been variously described and as unsuccessful efforts have been made to determine the nature of the phenomena with no further information available, **no valuation of the nature of the atmospheric disturbance has been made**." ^[extracted]

## Significance

- **Earliest Navy-originated** UAP intelligence report in the corpus. The 1944–45 [[references/foo-fighters-shaef-1944-1945|SHAEF foo-fighter file]] was Allied air; the 1948 SIGN material was Air Force; this 1949 Kodiak report adds **ONI / Navy operational intelligence** as a third originating agency from a single intake stream that all converges at Wright-Patterson.
- **CIA on the distribution list** in 1949 — earliest CIA touchpoint with the UAP material in the wiki's corpus. Predates the 1953 CIA Robertson Panel by 4 years. ^[inferred]
- The "ball of fire" / "tracer shell" / "greenish-blue luminous body" morphology is **structurally similar** to the WWII [[concepts/balls-of-fire-red|"Balls of Fire — Red"]] filing class at SHAEF / Air Ministry DDI2. The institutional connection between the WWII night-light reports and the 1949 Kodiak report is implicit — same morphology, different administrative cluster. ^[inferred]
- Kodiak's W → E low-altitude flat-trajectory descent could be described in modern terms as an **earth-grazing fireball** (a known meteor sub-class), but the multi-witness no-sparks observation cuts against that explanation, as does the consistent multi-second duration. ^[inferred]
