# DOS Cable — Mexico Weekly Political Blotter (Sep 11–15, 2023) ¶11

A 7-page **UNCLAS** US State Department cable (`MEXICO 2544`, dated 16 Sep 2023 / DTG `160150Z SEP 23`) from [[entities/amembassy-mexico|AMEMBASSY Mexico]] to `WASHDC, SECSTATE` Routine, signed `SALAZAR`. Released in full on 25 Feb 2026 by John Powers, Acting Director, US Department of State (per the in-document release stamp).

The cable is a **periodic political-reporting product** — Mission Mexico's weekly political blotter — whose body is structured as **eleven topic-bullets**, ten of them on Mexican domestic politics and security (MORENA presidential-contender selection, INE commission designations, Mexico City Security Secretary transitions, killings of Guerrero state prosecutors, the Guadalajara mayor's gubernatorial campaign launch). **Bullet 11** is the only UAP-relevant item: a single paragraph (~190 words) reporting that the **Mexican Congress** heard testimony on **Unidentified Aerial Phenomena** on **12 September 2023**, with named witnesses **[[entities/jaime-maussan|Jaime Maussan]]** (Mexican journalist) and **[[entities/ryan-graves|Ryan Graves]]** (former US Navy pilot, prior US Congressional UAP witness). The hearing was a debate on UAP language in Mexico's **Aerial Space Protection Law** which, per the cable, *"if approved, would make Mexico the first country to formally acknowledge the presence of alien life on Earth."* ^[extracted] Maussan presented **two alleged alien corpses** and videos of Mexican-pilot UAP encounters. Graves later **lamented** that the corpse display *"took away from his and other pilots' experiences with UAP"* and called it Maussan's *"unsubstantiated stunt."* ^[extracted] The cable closes the bullet by noting *"scientists have discredited previous alleged alien corpses Maussan presented as evidence of alien life."* ^[extracted]

This cable is the **third item** in the **`059uap*` FOIA-release series** ingested into the wiki, following the [[references/dos-cable-russia-georgia-2001-10|2001 Moscow figurative-UFO cable]] and the [[references/dos-cable-turkmenistan-2004-11|2004 Ashgabat NGO cable]] — and is **structurally distinct from both**. See the [Why this cable matters](#why-this-cable-matters) section for the typology placement.

## ¶11 — Mexican Congress UAP testimony (verbatim, U-classified)

> (U) Mexican Congress Hears Testimony on Alien Life
>
> 11. (U) Congress heard testimony on unidentified aerial phenomenon (UAP) September 12, from experts including from Mexican journalist Jaime Maussan and former U.S. Navy pilot Ryan Graves, who previously testified before the U.S. Congress. The hearing was to debate language on UAP in the Aerial Space Protection Law which, if approved, would make Mexico the first country to formally acknowledge the presence of alien life on Earth. Experts asked legislators to recognize UAP, guarantee airspace security, and allow UAP to be studied. Experts also presented to Congress two alleged alien corpses and videos of Mexican pilots' encounters with fast-moving flying objects during flight. After the hearing, Graves lamented the display took away from his and other pilots' experiences with UAP and expressed disappointment with Maussan's "unsubstantiated stunt." Scientists have discredited previous alleged alien corpses Maussan presented as evidence of alien life.

The bullet is followed by a Reuters-credited photo (`img-1.jpeg`, p.5) captioned *"Mexican journalist Jaime Maussan presented the alleged remains of non-human beings to Congress."* ^[extracted]

The bullet is **`(U)` — Unclassified** at paragraph level (versus `(SBU)` for almost every other bullet in the cable). ^[extracted]

## Other 10 bullets (high-level summary, non-UAP)

For provenance and corpus-context only — these are not UAP-relevant but establish the cable's substantive subject area:

1. ¶2-3 — **Marcelo Ebrard's challenge to MORENA's presidential contender selection** and his threat to leave the party.
2. ¶4 — **INE Commission Members designated** ahead of 2024 election; pro-MORENA bloc fails to obtain majority on key commissions.
3. ¶5 — **Omar García Harfuch resigns** as Mexico City Security Secretary; signals possible run for Mexico City Head of Government.
4. ¶6 — **Pablo Vázquez Camacho** appointed new Mexico City Security Secretary.
5. ¶7-8 — **Ricardo Monreal** announces he will not run for Mexico City Head of Government; subsequently named Sheinbaum's organization/territorial coordinator.
6. ¶9 — **Criminals targeting and killing prosecutors in Guerrero**: assassinations of FGR delegate García Fernández (12 Sep) and FGE regional prosecutor Salas Cuadras (9 Sep, ~50 gunshot wounds), plus August kidnapping of FGE prosecutor Salgado.
7. ¶10 — **Pablo Lemus (MC) launches Jalisco gubernatorial campaign** at his second annual mayoral address, with national-MC and inter-state political attendance.
8. ¶11 — **(U) Mexican Congress UAP hearing testimony** — the only UAP-relevant bullet.

The 10:1 ratio of non-UAP-to-UAP coverage is the cable's defining structural property. ^[extracted]

## Why this cable matters

This cable is the **third declassified DOS UAP-keyword cable** in the wiki's `059uap*` series partial ingest, after the [[references/dos-cable-russia-georgia-2001-10|2001 MOSCOW 13169]] and [[references/dos-cable-turkmenistan-2004-11|2004 ASHGABAT 1028]] cables. It is **structurally distinct from both** prior `059uap*` items and **opens a new sub-pattern under** [[concepts/diplomatic-channel-uap-reporting|the diplomatic-channel UAP reporting umbrella]] — see [Sub-pattern C below](#sub-pattern-c--host-country-legislative-event-reportage).

### Why this is *not* a [[concepts/figurative-ufo-rhetoric|figurative-UFO]] boundary case

The 2001 Moscow cable was a Type-2 boundary case (figurative-rhetorical UFO trope, no UAP narrative payload). The 2004 Ashgabat cable was a Type-1 boundary case (referential / NGO-name keyword match, no UAP narrative payload). MEXICO 2544 is **neither**:

- The cable contains a **substantive, on-the-record UAP-narrative payload** — a named legislative hearing with named witnesses, an alleged-corpse display, a verbatim quote from a US-Navy-pilot witness, and a Reuters photo. The payload is **thin** (one paragraph in eleven; ~190 words), but it is materially substantive. ^[extracted]
- The keyword-match driver is **literal substantive UAP content** — not an NGO name, not a figurative trope. ^[inferred]
- The drafting officer **forwards** the UAP content to State HQ via the cable; the cable does not invoke "UFO" as a denial register or report on a UFO-themed entity by proper-name accident. ^[inferred]

So the figurative-UFO-rhetoric typology does not absorb this cable. The **diplomatic-channel UAP reporting umbrella** does.

### Sub-pattern C — host-country legislative-event reportage

The cable is the wiki's **first instance of sub-pattern C** of the [[concepts/diplomatic-channel-uap-reporting|diplomatic-channel UAP reporting]] umbrella — *host-country legislative-event reportage*:

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | A **host country's legislature** holds a hearing in which UAP-related testimony is given by named experts. |
| 2 | The host country's legislature, the testimony, and the surrounding political context are **publicly observable** by the US embassy through ordinary host-country media monitoring. |
| 3 | The US embassy ([[entities/amembassy-mexico|AMEMBASSY Mexico]]) **incorporates the hearing as one news item** in a periodic political-reporting product (here, a weekly blotter), allocating ~190 words of an 11-bullet, 7-page cable to it. |
| 4 | The drafting officer's **stance is implicit-skepticism** — relayed by **selectively quoting** the most skeptical participant (Graves) and the most discrediting external evidence (*"scientists have discredited previous alleged alien corpses"*) without authoring a first-person editorial dismissal. ^[inferred] |
| 5 | The cable's **action addressee is `WASHDC, SECSTATE Routine`**; the drafting bureau is **WHA/MEX** ([[entities/state-department|Western Hemisphere Affairs / Mexico desk]]); information distribution includes NSC, OVP, DNI, CIA, DIA, NORTHCOM, DHS, DOJ, SOUTHCOM, all WHA posts, and all US consulates in Mexico (structural to the weekly-blotter format, not driven by the UAP bullet). ^[inferred] |
| 6 | The cable **takes no US-government posture toward UAP itself** — it reports the host-country event, the witnesses' actions, the cross-witness disagreement, and the prior-credibility caveats. **No US-asset query, no US witness, no US witness-side narrative.** ^[inferred] |

Sub-pattern C contrasts with sub-pattern A (foreign-liaison-inquiry, [[references/dos-cable-papua-new-guinea-1985-01|1985 PNG]]) where a foreign service made an asset-disambiguation inquiry, and with sub-pattern B (citizen-witness-relay, [[references/dos-cable-kazakhstan-1994-01|1994 Kazakhstan]]) where US-citizen aircrew relayed a UAP narrative to a US embassy. Sub-pattern C requires **no inquiry, no witness, no asset query** — only that the host country's UAP-relevant event be **publicly observable and politically reportable**. ^[inferred]

The structural feature distinguishing C from A and B is the **passivity of the embassy's role**: the embassy is neither responding to a foreign service's question (A) nor ingesting a citizen witness's account (B). It is **observing and forwarding a host-country political event**. The intake is **media-mediated**, not source-mediated. ^[inferred]

### Embassy-stance gradient extends to **5 cables, 5 distinct postures**

| # | Cable | Year | Embassy stance toward UAP content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [[references/dos-cable-papua-new-guinea-1985-01\|PORT M 00199]] | 1985 | **Forward-and-disambiguate** — "not us" |
| 2 | [[references/dos-cable-kazakhstan-1994-01\|DUSHANBE 00259]] | 1994 | **Forward-and-disclaim** — "we have no opinion" |
| 3 | [[references/dos-cable-russia-georgia-2001-10\|MOSCOW 13169]] | 2001 | **Forward-and-dismiss-trope** — "would be humorous if not for the seriousness" |
| 4 | [[references/dos-cable-turkmenistan-2004-11\|ASHGABAT 1028]] | 2004 | **Forward-and-endorse-NGO** — "worthy of USG attention and support" (about the NGO, not UAP) |
| 5 | **MEXICO 2544** *(this cable)* | **2023** | **Forward-and-relay-quoted-skepticism** — quote Graves' *"unsubstantiated stunt"*, quote *"scientists have discredited"*, no first-person editorial |

The 2023 cable's stance is **structurally novel** in the corpus: it is **not** the embassy's voice that performs the skepticism, but the cable's **selective inclusion of skeptical voices from inside the hearing's own population** (Graves) and from external scientific authority (the discrediting referenced as a flat fact). The drafting officer's editorial choice is **whose voices to include**, not what to assert. This is a **journalistic-style relay** stance distinct from the prior four cables' first-person embassy-voice postures. ^[inferred]

This cable carries `PGOV, PREL, ASEC, TSPA, KJUS, KCRM, MX`. None is `UFO` or `UAP`. The 5-cable working corpus now reads:

`UFO`-tag absence: **5-of-5** (extends from the prior 4-of-5 observation on the four-cable corpus). ^[extracted]

### **TSPA** as the de-facto UAP-substantive routing tag — **3-of-5**

A **stronger** positive observation also surfaces here: **`TSPA`** ("Transportation, Space, Aviation" — a DOS subject-routing tag) appears on **three of the five** cables — 1994 Kazakhstan (substantive UAP), 2004 Ashgabat (NGO-name boundary), and **2023 Mexico (substantive UAP, sub-pattern C)**. The 1994 cable used the variant spelling `TSAP`; 2004 and 2023 use the canonical `TSPA`. ^[extracted]

The pattern across the three cables: **whenever the cable's substantive subject involves space, civil aviation, transportation infrastructure, or airspace-related events**, `TSPA`/`TSAP` enters the TAGS field. ^[inferred] The 1985 PNG cable does not carry `TSPA` because its routing question was *Pol-Mil* (military overflights, "is this us?"), not space-and-aviation. The 2001 Moscow cable does not carry `TSPA` because its routing question was *bilateral state-relations / OSCE / UN-multilateral*. ^[inferred]

This makes `TSPA` the **closest-to-de-facto** subject-tag for UAP-substantive matters in the DOS schema — though it is **not** UAP-specific (the 2004 cable carries it because of civil society / aid-and-NGO routing, which is different from UAP). The DOS schema **does not have a UAP tag**; `TSPA` is the closest substitute for *aviation-and-airspace* routing.

### First **post-2017-disclosure-era** DOS UAP-keyword cable

Of the five DOS UAP-keyword cables in the corpus, four (1985, 1994, 2001, 2004) **predate** the December 2017 New York Times disclosure (the *"Glowing Auras and Black Money"* / AATIP article that broadly relegitimized UAP as a US-government-handled topic). MEXICO 2544 is the **first cable in the corpus** drafted after that institutional inflection point. Both Maussan and Graves are post-2017 figures with US Congressional testimony pedigree (Graves) or prior debunked-evidence reputation (Maussan). The cable's relaxed UAP-substantive treatment — UNCLAS, no caveat-loaded subject line, plain referencing of Maussan, no special handling indicators — likely reflects the post-2017 institutional climate. ^[inferred]

### First **Western-Hemisphere** DOS UAP-keyword originating post

The four prior DOS UAP-keyword originating posts in the corpus (Port Moresby, Dushanbe, Moscow, Ashgabat) are all in the Indo-Pacific, Central Asia, Russia, or Eurasia. AMEMBASSY Mexico is the **first Western-Hemisphere** originating post. ^[inferred] The geographic distribution remains thin (n=5 cables; n=5 unique posts), but the corpus now spans four of the six WHA / non-WHA regional groupings.

### First **periodic political-reporting product** in the corpus's DOS layer

Prior cables in the corpus's DOS layer were **single-event cables** dedicated to the cable's substantive subject — a sighting (1985, 1994), a bilateral airspace dispute (2001), an NGO grant assessment (2004). MEXICO 2544 is **structurally different**: it is a **periodic weekly product** (Mission Mexico's Political Blotter) that aggregates 10+ unrelated news items into a single cable. The UAP-relevant content is **embedded** in a non-UAP-primary cable. ^[inferred]

This has three consequences for the diplomatic-channel umbrella:

1. **The unit of analysis shifts from cable-as-a-whole to bullet-as-a-unit.** Earlier cables had `cable.subject ⇔ UAP-content`; this cable has `cable.subject = political blotter, bullet-11.subject = UAP`. ^[inferred]
2. **The drafting officer for the UAP bullet is *not* the cable's signing officer.** Salazar signs the whole product; one of POL or EXEC officers (Cortázar, Karimi, Naranjo, Tejeda, Moreno) drafted the UAP bullet, and probably Karimi (PD-cleared) or Cortázar (POL-cleared) did so. ^[inferred] **First instance** in the corpus where the drafting officer of the UAP-relevant content is structurally distinct from the cable's signer.
3. **The information-distribution list is set by the cable-format default**, not by UAP relevance. Every weekly blotter goes to NSC, OVP, DNI, CIA, DIA, NORTHCOM, DHS, DOJ, SOUTHCOM. The UAP bullet inherits this distribution incidentally. ^[inferred]
