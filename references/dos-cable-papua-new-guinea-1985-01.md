# DOS Cable — Papua New Guinea Inquiry Re Overflights (PORT M 00199, 28 Jan 1985)

A 3-page declassified [[entities/state-department|US Department of State]] outgoing cable from [[entities/amembassy-port-moresby|AMEMBASSY Port Moresby]] (signed `GARDNER`) to [[entities/uscincpac|USCINCPAC]] Honolulu and the 43rd Strategic Wing at Andersen AFB Guam, dated `O 280653Z JAN 85`. Subject: **"PAPUA NEW GUINEA INQUIRY RE OVERFLIGHTS"**.

It is the wiki's first **diplomatic-channel UAP-adjacent artifact** — the first archived case where a foreign government's intelligence service formally inquires of a US embassy about anomalous overflights, prompting a US-side query through cable channels rather than through direct DoD intake. It also sits at a previously empty point in the corpus timeline (between the 1963 [[references/hunter-1963-space-alien-race-memo|NASC alien-policy memo]] and the modern AARO era).

The reference-table copy of the cable's `Action` field on page 1 reads `USCINCLANT ROUTINE` rather than `USCINCPAC IMMEDIATE` — almost certainly a clerical mismatch in the modern release-side cataloguing layer (the cable body is unambiguously addressed to USCINCPAC and at IMMEDIATE precedence). ^[ambiguous]

The `INR-10` and `CIAE-00` lines confirm the cable was machine-cleared for the State Department's Bureau of Intelligence and Research and the CIA's Office of Current Intelligence Liaison. `EAP-00` clears it for the Bureau of East Asian and Pacific Affairs.

The `MARR` and `PP` `TAGS` (Military Assistance and Sales — Air Forces; and Political Affairs — Public Opinion) are the State Department subject indexing terms — notably the cable carries **no UFO-specific TAG**. The 1985 State Department cable tagging schema treats this case as a routine military overflight inquiry. ^[inferred]

## Substantive content

The cable is structured as four numbered paragraphs:

1. **¶1.** On 28 January 1985 the US Embassy in Port Moresby received an *informal* inquiry from the **PNG National Intelligence Organization (NIO)** about reported sightings of "high-altitude, high-speed aircraft" over PNG on the evening of **24 January 1985**. The matter rose through the NIO chain because the NIO's officer in **Wewak** reported that local residents had been "frightened by overflights." This drove the **provincial Premier** to call a public meeting on the subject, which the **PNG Prime Minister** attended (he was weekending in his electoral district).

2. **¶2.** NIO summarized the body of reports as "various reports of unidentified aerial phenomena the night of January 24, including fast-moving objects with lights, contrails, and noise." NIO singled out one report it placed "some credence" in: an **Air Niugini pilot** had just taken off from Wewak en route to Port Moresby; over **Angoram (vicinity 4°S, 144°E)**, his **radar picked up aircraft flying south to north at high altitude and high speed**. Visual contrail sightings from "various points around PNG" included "one aircraft moving north to south at 1900 local" and "six-eight aircraft traveling south to north at 2200 local."

3. **¶3.** Embassy queried its records and conducted a `TELCON` with the **43rd Strategic Wing** (B-52 unit at Andersen AFB Guam) and told NIO it knew of **no B-52 overflights and no US aircraft in PNG airspace on 24 January 1985**.

4. **¶4.** Embassy noted the information was "very sketchy" and witnesses "were unsure of the directions in which aircraft were flying," and asked USCINCPAC for confirmation of ¶3 and "any light you might throw on these reports."

A full single-event sighting page is at [[references/sighting-papua-new-guinea-1985-01-24]].

## Why this cable matters

- **First diplomatic-channel UAP intake in the corpus.** Every prior bundle is intra-US: USAF→USAF (1948–50), SHAEF→Air Ministry (1944–45 — coalition partners but a *military* channel), NASC→State (1963 — but again intra-US-government). Here, a *foreign* national intelligence service (PNG NIO) approaches a US embassy, which then queries a US combatant command. See [[concepts/diplomatic-channel-uap-reporting]].
- **First "denied US asset" disposition** in the corpus. The embassy's response — **"we knew of no B-52 overflights and no U.S. aircraft in PNG airspace"** — is the first formal *negative-confirmation* US-side answer to a UAP-adjacent inquiry. The 1948 SIGN reports asked "what is it?"; the 1963 NASC memo asked "what would we do?"; the 1985 cable asks "is this us?" — and answers "no."
- **First case with a non-Anglophone host-government political dimension.** The reports surface to the level of the **PNG Prime Minister** through the provincial Premier of East Sepik Province ^[inferred]; the Prime Minister attends a public meeting on the subject. The cable thus documents a UAP-adjacent matter that becomes a *publicly visible* foreign-political event before a US-side disposition is reached.
- **First UAP-adjacent radar-on-aircraft cue** in the corpus where the radar is on a *civilian* airliner (Air Niugini), not a military or weather radar.

## Source quality assessment

- `source_quality: official` — declassified release of an authentic State Department cable. Identifier `CSP-2025-00040 B-00002707027` is consistent with State Department FOIA/electronic release control numbers. ^[inferred]
- The release is a **modern** (2026-02-25) declassification; the 41-year delay between issuance and release suggests prior FOIA exemption or routine 25-year review delays. ^[inferred]
- Single-source artifact. No corresponding USCINCPAC reply, 43rd SW reply, or PNG-side document is in the corpus yet. The actual *cause* of the 24 January overflights is **not** answered by this cable — it raises the question and parks it.
