# Caldwell "Gray Goose" Helicopter Recovery (Glen Burnie MD, Aug 1949)

On **19-20 August 1949** the **Air Force Office of Special Investigations** (OSI), via Capt. Claudius Belk's Baltimore office, recovered **two pre-1940 disc-rotor experimental aircraft** from a tobacco shed near **Glen Burnie, Maryland**. ^[extracted] The recovered aircraft — a *"Gray Goose"* helicopter and a wooden *"Roto-Plane"* — had been built by **Jonathan E. Caldwell**, a stock-promoting Maryland inventor who fled the state in 1940 after Maryland authorities ordered him to *"cease and desist from selling stock"* in his "Gray Goose Airways, Inc." aviation venture. ^[extracted]

## What was found

Per Washington Star reporting (sub-A pages 95 + 97-99): ^[extracted]

| Element | Description |
|---|---|
| **"Gray Goose" helicopter** | Small helicopter with rotors projecting from a saucer-like disc mounted on a tripod above the cockpit; the rotor disc was designed to act as a wing after the ship reached cruising altitude |
| **"Roto-Plane"** | Plywood "tub" 14 feet in diameter, 16-feet-disc-diameter version; pilot sat in middle near motor mount; four-bladed propellers around top and bottom rims of the tub revolved in opposite directions; resembled "two saucers revolving top to top" |
| **Test flight** (only one ever made) | ~1940 at the old Benning Race track, Washington DC; pilot Willard E. Driggers (1530 Olive St NE) reached ~40 ft altitude for ~60 seconds before controls became unresponsive and he made a damaged landing |
| **Photographs** | Sub-A pages 98 + 99 carry **two Washington Star photographs** — one of the Gray Goose helicopter pre-1940 test flight, one of MD State Troopers Harbaugh + Kosirowsky inspecting the recovered fuselage and Roto-Plane "tub" |

## AF posture

The official AF statement was: ^[extracted]

> The two experimental aircraft found near Baltimore yesterday have absolutely no connection with the reported phenomenon of flying saucers.

But the operational record is more textured: ^[extracted]

- Capt. Belk's OSI Baltimore office had been *"investigating the machines for months"* as **possible prototypes of the flying saucers**.
- An off-record AF officer described the recovered aircraft as *"definite prototypes of flying saucers"*; the service hedged on this characterization the next day.
- The relics were placed in MD State Police storage *"until it can be determined if experts from the Wright-Patterson Air Force Base"* would examine them — i.e. transferred to **Wright-Patterson** ([[entities/air-materiel-command|AMC]] / ATIC). ^[inferred]
- The AF actively sought to **locate Caldwell** (then ~70 if alive, vanished 1940 with wife and son) on the theory he might have built better disc-prototypes elsewhere.

## Position in the FBI corpus

This event is **structurally novel** as the **first FBI-corpus retention of a published photographic AF-side prosaic-resolution event**. ^[inferred] Prior FBI-corpus material on AF prosaic-resolution events ([[concepts/saucer-as-hoax-default|saucer-as-hoax-default]]) was textual (BB-42-driven field-office memos, Kelso plane crash sketches, prank-disc inventories, Mariana Great Falls film). Sub-A pages 98-99 are the **first preserved press photographs** of the recovered material itself. ^[inferred]

The case complements [[concepts/saucer-as-hoax-default]] but **adds a category**: ^[inferred]

- **Hoax** — Twin Falls boys, Alice TX welded-wings, Borden UNM trick-photo, etc.
- **Misidentification of normal aircraft** — Reading PA bomber, Aug 1953 Moby Dick balloons, Quantico Marines new-airliner-navigation-lights (sub-A page 30-33)
- **NEW: pre-saucer-era experimental-aviation residue** — Caldwell Gray Goose 1939-40 aircraft mistaken for 1947+ saucers because they predate the saucer-era and have a literal disc-rotor signature

The Caldwell case is also the **earliest "saucer-prototype" hypothesis empirically tested** in the wiki corpus. ^[inferred] OSI investigated *for months* before recovering and inspecting the aircraft — i.e. the AF was actively running down the pre-1947-experimental-aviation hypothesis as a possible explanation. The negative result (the aircraft were neither secret nor capable of the reported saucer behaviors) closes that hypothesis-thread for the AF. ^[inferred]
