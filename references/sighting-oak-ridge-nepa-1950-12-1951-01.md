# Sighting — Oak Ridge / NEPA / 663rd AC&W Squadron Sequence, December 1950 - January 1951

A multi-event radar+visual sequence at and over the **AEC critical facility at Oak Ridge, Tennessee** between **18 December 1950 and 16 January 1951**, with adjacent FBI Knoxville / 111th CIC Detachment / 663rd AC&W Squadron / NEPA Division of Fairchild Engine and Airplane Corporation engagement. ^[extracted] Captured in [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-6|FBI HQ 62-HQ-83894 section 6]] serials 258-265 and adjacent (pages 91-109). ^[extracted] Structural successor to the [[references/sighting-oak-ridge-adcock-1950-03|Oak Ridge / Adcock 1-8 March 1950]] event and **second institutional engagement** with UAP at the Oak Ridge AEC facility in the FBI corpus. ^[inferred]

## The 12 October 1950 USAF Radar Knoxville precursor

Although primarily a December 1950 - January 1951 cluster, the sequence opens with a **12 October 1950 USAF Radar Installation Knoxville event** ^[extracted] — preserved in the section as **Knoxville-to-Director URGENT teletype 13-10-50 8:26 PM JYC**:

> RADAR DETECTION OF UNIDENTIFIED OBJECTS OVER OAK RIDGE OCTOBER TWELVE, NINETEEN FIFTY, PROTECTION OF VITAL INSTALLATIONS. USAF RADAR INSTALLATION AT KNOXVILLE AT ELEVEN TWENTY-FIVE PM, EST, OCTOBER TWELVE, PICKED UP INDICATIONS OF ELEVEN OBJECTS AND PERHAPS MORE TRAVELING ACROSS CONTROLLED AREA OF ATOMIC ENERGY INSTALLATION AT OAK RIDGE. ALTITUDE OF OBJECTS VARIED FROM ONE THOUSAND TO FIVE THOUSAND FEET, COURSES FROM SOUTH SOUTH EAST TO SOUTHEAST, AND DENSITY FROM READING MADE BY LIGHT AIRCRAFT TO AIRCRAFT EQUAL IN SIZE TO C FORTY SEVEN, SPEED FROM ONE HUNDRED TO ONE HUNDRED TWENTY-FIVE MILES PER HOUR. FIGHTER PLANE ATTEMPTED TO INTERCEPT IN FEW MINUTES AND RADAR SCREEN REFLECTED CONTACT, BUT PLANE REPORTED NO OBJECT COULD BE SEEN EITHER VISUALLY OR ON PLANES RADAR SCREEN. AEC PATROL WAS ALERTED BUT NO O...

**11+ objects** at 1000-5000 ft altitude, courses SSE-SE, sizes from light aircraft to C-47, speeds 100-125 mph; fighter intercept attempted, radar contact verified, **but no visual or airborne-radar contact made by the intercepting fighter**. ^[extracted] AEC Patrol alerted. The visual-vs-radar disjunction is a recurring Oak Ridge pattern. ^[inferred]

## The 18 December 1950 Hood / Hribar / Steele NEPA visual event

The signature event of the sequence: **18 December 1950, 0820-0830 hours**, on the Oak Ridge Turnpike within the Controlled Area approximately one mile from the White Wing entrance and Y-12 plant. ^[extracted] Witnesses ^[extracted]:

- **Lt Col John R. Hood, USAF**
- **Cdr E. W. Hribar, USN**
- **Major James L. Steele, USAF**
- **Major Walter L. Carss, USAF**
- **Mr. James R. Gray** (civilian)
- **Mr. William G. Frey** (civilian) — AF Plant Representative for Security, Office of the AF Plant Representative, Air Material Command, NEPA Division, Fairchild Engine and Airplane Corporation
- **Dr. A. J. Miller** (NEPA Division)
- **Dr. V. P. Calkins** (NEPA Division)
- **Mr. J. Frank Coneybear** (NEPA Division)
- **Mr. A. O. Mooneyham** (NEPA Division)
- **Mr. F. T. Bly** (NEPA Division)

Two separate carpools observed the same object. ^[extracted] Col Hood first sighted *"a very bright reflection through the windshield of the car"*; Major Carss confirmed. ^[extracted] The object's apparent description: *"the circular area appeared to darken, starting at approximately 7:00 to 9:00 o'clock along the perimeter and continuing to darken along the perimeter and inner area until the light was concentrated in approximately 1:00 to 3:00 o'clock position"* ^[extracted] — i.e. a **progressive phase-darkening** signature unlike standard reflective targets. ^[inferred]

Coordination of the **AEC radar log** for the event (in the parallel CIC report by SAC William B. Gray) ^[extracted]:

> 0832 hours - Atomic Energy Commission reported aircraft over Northeast end of area - no paint. (Paint meaning indication on the radar scopes.)
> 0839 hours - Small paint near Southeast corner of area on 190 degree. (Magnetic bearing of objects travel.)
> 0845 hours - Lost contact. Fighter interception was attempted with negative results.

The **15-minute radar+visual tracking sequence** terminating in failed fighter intercept is structurally identical to the 12 Oct 1950 precursor: visual + radar contact, no airborne-pilot visual contact, no successful intercept. ^[inferred]

## The 663rd AC&W Squadron radar events, 20 Dec 1950 - 16 January 1951

The 111th CIC Detachment (FAO #8, Box 379 Knoxville TN) report-series preserves three additional **663rd AC&W Squadron radar events** at McGhee Tyson Airport, Knoxville TN ^[extracted]:

### 20 December 1950, 1247 hours

*"Small paint in area (Oak Ridge Controlled Area). Very, [truncated]"* ^[extracted] — 663rd AC&W and 5th AN Fighter Squadron personnel on radar; weather: ceiling 2700 ft, broken overcast, 7 mi visibility, 37°F, calm winds. ^[extracted]

### 14 December 1950, 1605-1900 hours (~3 hours)

A **~3-hour radar event** on the **663rd AC&W radar scopes** ^[extracted] — weather: ceiling 2100 ft, broken overcast, 7 mi visibility, 37°F, southwest wind at 13 mph. Personnel of the **663rd AC&W Squadron, 30th Air Division**, McGhee Tyson Airport, on duty as **radar operators, supervisors, and experts**. ^[extracted] No photographs.

### 16 January 1951, 2145 hours

**Two bright objects in the sky**, one east of McGhee Tyson Airport. ^[extracted] Witnesses: 663rd AC&W Squadron personnel, AEC Patrol Personnel, policemen at Oak Ridge, Captains L.C.M. Clevenger and Wm. Aiken (Controllers of 663rd). ^[extracted]

The 17 January 1951 letter from the Commanding Officer of the 663rd to his Commanding Officer reports ^[extracted]:

> 1915: An unusual airbourne object was sighted approximately 10 miles WNW of the station, the lighted object was very similar to a star but much brighter and slightly larger than other visible stars at the time, as observed the object seemed to be approximately 6,000 feet above the terrain and descending slowly. As the object appeared to be over or near the Oak Ridge Area, the AEC Patrol Headquarters was notified and th[...]
>
> 2000: F-82 reports clear sailing and no restrictions to visibility, still patroling area for possible pickup of object, still no joy.

The **F-82 night-fighter** dispatched for intercept reported no contact ^[extracted] — confirming the **third intercept failure** in the sequence.

## The Hoover radar-jamming-ionization investigation directive (5 Dec 1950)

The **5 December 1950 Hoover URGENT teletype to SAC Knoxville** ^[extracted]:

> DETECTION OF UNIDENTIFIED OBJECTS OVER OAK RIDGE AREA, PROTECTION OF VITAL INSTALLATIONS. REURTEL DECEMBER FOUR LAST REGARDING POSSIBLE RADAR JAMMING AT OAK RIDGE. ARRANGEMENTS SHOULD BE MADE TO OBTAIN ALL FACTS CONCERNING POSSIBLE RADAR JAMMING BY IONIZATION OF PARTICLES IN ATMOSPHERE. CONDUCT APPROPRIATE INVESTIGATION TO DETERMINE WHETHER INCIDENT OCCURRING NORTHEAST OF OLIVER SPRINGS, TENNESSEE, COULD HAVE HAD ANY CONNECTION WITH ALLEGED RADAR JAMMING.

**Original copy filed in 65-58300-141** ([[references/fbi-san-antonio-vital-installations-1949|Vital Installations file]]) ^[extracted] — confirming the **cross-class filing pattern** the wiki documented at [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-4|section 4]]. The Knoxville SAC's 4 Dec 1950 teletype to HQ is the precipitating event, but the 4 Dec 1950 teletype is not preserved in section 6 ^[ambiguous].

**Institutional significance**: Hoover personally directed an investigation of *"radar jamming by ionization of particles in atmosphere"* over Oak Ridge — the **first FBI Director-level directive on an active scientific hypothesis** for a UAP-class phenomenon in the wiki corpus ^[inferred]. The ionization-jamming hypothesis is structurally aligned with **Cold War countermeasure-warfare framings** (compare with the contemporary Project Twinkle's Urals-missile fallback hypothesis). ^[inferred] The phrase *"alleged radar jamming"* preserves Hoover's institutional skepticism within the directive itself.

## The 8 December 1950 Richmond Army-Intelligence high-alert (FBI Richmond URGENT)

The **8 December 1950 FBI Richmond URGENT teletype to Director** ^[extracted]:

> RE FLYING SAUCERS. THIS OFFICE VERY CONFIDENTIALLY ADVISED BY ARMY INTELLIGENCE, RICHMOND, THAT THEY HAVE BEEN PUT ON IMMEDIATE HIGH ALERT FOR ANY DATA WHATSOEVER CONCERNING FLYING SAUCERS. CIC HERE STATES BACKGROUND OF INSTRUCTIONS NOT AVAILABLE FROM AIR FORCE INTELLIGENCE, WHO ARE NOT AWARE OF REASON FOR ALERT LOCALLY, BUT ANY INFORMATION WHATSOEVER MUST BE TELEPHONED BY THEM IMMEDIATELY TO AIR FORCE INTELLIGENCE. CIC ADVISES DATA STRICTLY CONFIDENTIAL AND SHOULD NOT BE DISSEMINATED.
>
> AUERBACH

This is the **third Cold-War-era institutional reactivation signal** in section 6 ^[inferred] — after Project Twinkle (Aug 1950) and AFOIC-CC-1 (Sep 1950). The Richmond Army Intelligence high-alert is **coincident with the Oak Ridge radar-jamming-ionization investigation** Hoover directed 3 days earlier (5 Dec 1950) ^[inferred]. The institutional read: **early December 1950 was a Cold War alarm spike** at the Army-Intelligence + FBI tier, with Oak Ridge radar anomalies the precipitating context. ^[inferred]

## Routing path

The institutional routing path through section 6 ^[inferred]:

1. **18 Dec 1950 NEPA personnel observation** → AEC Security Office.
2. **AEC Security** → 111th CIC Detachment (Knoxville TN).
3. **111th CIC** → 663rd AC&W Squadron (McGhee Tyson Airport).
4. **663rd AC&W Squadron** → CIC reports of 2 Jan 1951 + 20 Jan 1951 (Object Sighted Over Oak Ridge, Tennessee).
5. **CIC** → 30th Air Division.
6. **FBI Knoxville** → FBI HQ via 10 Feb 1951 SAC Knoxville → Director letter (serial ~263). ^[extracted]
7. **FBI HQ** → 4 Nov 1950 SAC Knoxville → "Object Sighted at Oak Ridge October 20, 23, 24, 26, 1950" (serial ~261).

The **FBI does not actively investigate** — it accumulates the CIC + 663rd AC&W material at HQ. ^[inferred] The Hoover radar-jamming-ionization directive is the **single exception**, requesting active fact-gathering from the Knoxville SAC. ^[extracted]

## Institutional significance

The Oak Ridge NEPA / 663rd AC&W sequence is structurally distinctive in the FBI corpus ^[inferred]:

1. **First multi-month multi-radar institutional UAP engagement** at a single AEC critical facility — extending the Adcock dry-run pattern at scale.
2. **First documented USAF + USN + USAAF joint-service witness pool** for a single UAP event (Hood USAF + Hribar USN + Steele USAF + Carss USAF + civilians NEPA = 4 services + civilian engineering). ^[inferred]
3. **First Hoover Director-level institutional engagement** with an active scientific hypothesis (radar jamming by ionization) for a UAP phenomenon.
4. **First cross-class filing of Oak Ridge UAP material** under the Vital Installations file 65-58300 (serial 141 specifically). ^[extracted]
5. **Confirmed three intercept failures** in 4-week span (12 Oct 1950, 18 Dec 1950, 16 Jan 1951) — the F-82 night-fighter's *"still no joy"* report being the wiki's earliest preserved fighter-intercept-failure language. ^[inferred]
6. **F. T. Bly / NEPA Division** is named — same personnel referenced as the recipient of the parallel Bell report dated 28 Dec 1950 ^[extracted].

The sequence's institutional posture: **passive-accumulation by FBI HQ** of an **active inter-agency multi-radar critical-facility engagement** by AEC + USAF + Army CIC + 663rd AC&W. ^[inferred] The FBI is plugged in but does not lead. The pattern is **structurally consistent with the [[concepts/project-twinkle|Project Twinkle]] AF-contractor / FBI-liaison framework** ^[inferred] — confirming that the FBI's post-cooperation-regime posture is **operational passive accumulation across multiple AEC-critical-facility programs** in 1950-51.
