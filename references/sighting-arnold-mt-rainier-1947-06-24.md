# Sighting — Kenneth Arnold / Mt Rainier (24 Jun 1947)

A 2.5–3 minute aerial observation on **24 June 1947 at approximately 1500 local**, near Mt Rainier WA, by **Kenneth Arnold** — private pilot, dealer in fire-control supplies, of Box 387, Boise ID. ^[extracted] Filed as **Project SIGN Incident 17** in [[references/project-sign-incident-summaries-1-100|the 1–100 incident summary bundle]]. This is the **founding event of the 1947 Flying Disc wave** and the genesis of the public-press term "flying saucer". ^[inferred]

## What Arnold reported

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Date / time | 24 June 1947, ~1500 local |
| Location | Near Mineral, Washington (vicinity of Mt Rainier) |
| Witness aircraft | Arnold's own light plane, eastbound toward Yakima WA |
| Number of objects | 9 |
| Distance from observer | 20–25 mi (almost at right angles) |
| Time in sight | 2.5 – 3 minutes |
| Altitude | ~9,500 ft |
| Speed | **~150 MPH** (clocked between two mountain reference points) |
| Direction of flight | North → South at 170° |
| Tactics | Horizontal flight; *"swerved in and out of the high mountain peaks"*; *"every few seconds 2 or 3 of them would dip or change their course slightly"* |
| Sound | Not reported (Arnold's own engine noise) |
| Size | Wing-span ~ 45–50 ft (estimated against DC-4 furtherest engines as reference) |
| Color | "**Mirror-like**" — sun-reflective |
| Shape | "**Approximately circular**" — *"chain of 9 peculiar looking craft"* |
| Apparent construction | Inferred metallic; could find no tails |
| Weather | CAVU |
| Sketches/photographs | Drawings by Arnold |

The agent's evaluation, recorded verbatim in the incident sheet: *"It was the opinion of the agent interviewing Mr. Arnold that he saw the 'flying discs'. In this regard agent further stated that if Mr. Arnold could write a report of such a character and did not see the objects he was in the wrong business and should be engaged in writing Buck Rogers fiction."* ^[extracted]

The cover sheet appends a contemporaneous note: *"The attached is what Kenneth Arnold later produced. See 'Fate' magazine article by Kenneth Arnold."* ^[extracted] (The *Fate* article appears in the inaugural Spring 1948 issue.)

## What Arnold described in flight terms

Arnold's flight conditions are recorded with unusual specificity in the SIGN sheet:

- He was flying east toward Yakima at 9,200 ft, "trimmed out".
- A DC-4 was visible to his left.
- A 14,000-ft-elevation peak lay 15 mi to his rear.
- "**The sky was clear as crystal.**"
- He first noticed the chain after "**a bright flash suddenly reflected on the plane**".
- He observed the chain against Mt Rainier's snow ("**could observe their outlines against the snow quite plainly**") and clocked the chain's 5-mile length (estimated) over an interval of 1m42s. ^[extracted]

The 150-MPH speed estimate is **explicitly conservative** — it is what *the chain's leading edge* covered relative to ground reference points in a known interval. Arnold himself published a higher speed (~1,200–1,700 MPH) when correcting for the actual peak-to-peak distance after consultation with maps. ^[ambiguous]

## "Flying saucers" — the linguistic origin

The SIGN sheet itself uses **"flying discs"** as the standard term. Arnold's own description recorded in the file is **"chain of 9 peculiar looking craft"**, *"approximately circular"*, *"like a saucer if you skipped it across water"* (paraphrased in the cover note). The press-coined term **"flying saucers"** is a wire-service compression of Arnold's water-skipping analogy, NOT a description of the objects' shape — Arnold himself described them as *"flat and disc-like"* but moving in the **manner** of skipped saucers. ^[inferred]

The annotation *"Seemed to travel in sidewise position and did not appear to whirl or spin"* in the SIGN file is the institutional record of Arnold's own behavioral description — the objects rotated their disc plane to the line of flight rather than rotating about their disc axis. ^[extracted]

## Why this event matters

### Inside the SIGN intake stream

The Arnold report is filed as **Inc 17** out of 100 in the 1–100 bundle. SIGN's intake apparatus did not treat Arnold as the wave's origin event — earlier event-dates are present in the same bundle (Inc 79 Apr 1947 Richmond VA pibal observations; Inc 82 17–21 May 1947 Oklahoma City; Inc 92 19 May 1947 Pikes Peak; Inc 31 Dec 1946 N Arizona). ^[extracted] Arnold's case sits at Inc 17 because of **filing-date ordering**, not event-date ordering — the SIGN intake stream filed cases as they reached AMC T-2, not as they occurred.

### As a corpus founding event

For wiki corpus purposes, Arnold remains the **founding event** because:

1. **It is the earliest Project SIGN incident** with a **fully-completed 25-field intake form**, agent narrative, and explicit institutional credibility evaluation. ^[extracted]
2. **It generated the public-press terminology** ("flying saucer", "flying disc") that made every subsequent observation in the same week filable under a single rubric. ^[inferred]
3. **It anchored the AMC T-2 / pre-SIGN intake apparatus** — Arnold's report (and the Pacific Northwest patrolman wave that followed 10 days later) is what gave SIGN's parent intake a stream of cases to trigger formal stand-up under the Cabell directive. ^[inferred]

### The same-day Cascade Mountains parallel

On the **same day** (24 Jun 1947), 150 mi N of Arnold's track, prospector Fred M. Johnson observed 5–6 round discs with tails over the Cascade Mountains at 6,000 ft from sea level (Inc 68). Johnson's combination compass-and-watch *"reacted in a very peculiar manner, the hand waving from one side to the other but that this condition corrected itself immediately after the discs had passed out of sight"*. ^[extracted] See [[references/sighting-cascade-mts-johnson-1947-06-24]]. **The Arnold-Johnson same-day pair is the corpus's earliest two-witness PNW disc datum** with morphology and EM-effect signatures that are independent of each other. ^[inferred]

### The Lt Gov Whitehead same-day parallel

Also on **24 Jun 1947**, Lt Gov **Donald S. Whitehead** of Idaho saw a *"comet-like object hanging in the western sky"* that *"didn't move but just seemed to go below the horizon with the rotation of the earth"* (Inc 28). ^[extracted] **Three independent same-day witnesses across the Pacific Northwest** — Arnold airborne, Johnson on the ground in the Cascades, Whitehead in Idaho — observed unrelated UAP signatures. ^[inferred]

## Open questions

- **The wave-amplification feedback loop.** SIGN incident-numbering shows the wave's case-volume **jumped sharply between 24 Jun (3 events) and 4 Jul (30+ events)** — exactly the interval over which the wire-service "flying saucer" term propagated nationally. ^[inferred] How much of the 4 July clustering reflects genuine event-density vs. press-amplified attention is open.
- **Arnold's altitude / speed math.** The 150-MPH file figure conflicts with Arnold's later published speed estimates after he reworked the peak-to-peak distance. The SIGN file preserves the **conservative initial estimate**, not Arnold's later refinement. ^[ambiguous]
- **The "swerve in and out of mountain peaks" tactic.** This is one of the earliest **terrain-following / contour-following** behavioral signatures in the corpus — recurring in Inc 75 (Snake River Canyon 13 Aug 1947), Inc 58 (NW of Bethel AK 4 Aug 1947), and structurally similar to the post-2017 Navy *"Tic-Tac"* tracking observations. ^[inferred]

The **Arnold-personally-bitter** quote ("outspoken and somewhat bitter in his opinions of the leaders of the AAF and FBI for not having made an investigation of this matter sooner") is structurally significant: ^[inferred] **Arnold's framing of institutional indifference predates his witness interview by Brown — i.e. by mid-July 1947 Arnold had already concluded the AAF and FBI were ignoring the disc problem**. This is **upstream** of and **independently corroborated by** [[entities/g-d-garrett|Garrett's]] 22 Sep 1947 admission that the AAF "high brass" were "totally unconcerned" — Arnold and Garrett are independently arriving at structurally identical assessments of AAF intelligence-leadership posture. ^[inferred]

## Cross-event linkage — Maury Island and the Davidson/Brown plane crash

[[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-1|FBI section 1]] (Jul-Aug 1947) reveals that the **same 4th AAF investigative team** (Capt William L. Davidson + Lt Frank M. Brown of Hamilton Field) that interviewed Harold Dahl and Fred Crisman about Maury Island on **27 Jul 1947** also interviewed **Arnold**, plus Capt E. J. Smith + Ralph Stevens (United Airlines), and Dave Johnson (Idaho Statesman) on the same Pacific-NW tour. ^[extracted] On **1 Aug 1947** Davidson and Brown's B-25 crashed at Kelso WA, killing both — see [[references/sighting-maury-island-1947-06-21]]. The Tacoma News Tribune (via UP wire) reported the plane was carrying Maury Island disc fragments; analysis identified the fragments as **Tacoma slag-mill product**. ^[extracted] Lt Col C. P. Martin (AAF Intelligence) tells the FBI on 6 Aug 1947 the dead CIC agent was on a *"top secret mission"*, possibly **en route to or from interview with Arnold**. ^[extracted]

This places Arnold's case institutionally **one degree of separation** from the 1947 wave's most catastrophic AAF investigative event. ^[inferred]
