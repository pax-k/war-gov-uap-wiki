# Ladd → Hoover Memo Proposing FBI Saucer-Investigation Termination (25 September 1947)

A **2-page Office Memorandum from [[entities/d-m-ladd|D. M. Ladd]] to "The Director"** dated **25 September 1947**, subject **"FLYING DISCS"**, drafted by **[[entities/s-w-reynolds|SA S. W. Reynolds]]** (initials `SWR:AJB`), filed at **62-83894-121** in [[entities/fbi-hq-62-83894-file|FBI HQ File 62-HQ-83894]] [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-3|section 3]] OCR page 158. ^[extracted] The memo carries Hoover's **handwritten "I agree"** with the date **9/27/47** in the margin and the line **"A proposed Bulletin is attached for your approval"** — making this memo the **literal birth document of [[references/fbi-bureau-bulletin-57-1947|Bureau Bulletin No. 57, Series 1947]]**, the FBI's 1 Oct 1947 instrument terminating its saucer-investigation activity. ^[inferred]

Until this serial's recovery, the BB-57 origin chain was **reconstructed from references** in [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-4|section 4]] and [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-5|section 5]] — the literal proximate proposal-vehicle, the staff officer who drafted it, and the moment of Hoover's approval were all `^[inferred]`. Section 3 promotes them to `^[extracted]`. ^[inferred]

## Verbatim — the proposal text

The memo's two paragraphs:

**Paragraph 1** (background):

> The Bureau was requested by the Air Forces Intelligence to assist the Air Forces in attempting to arrive at an explanation of the above phenomena. The Air Forces indicated that the alleged sightings of flying discs might have been made by individuals of Communist sympathies for the purpose of causing mass hysteria in the United States over the fear of a secret Russian weapon. The Bureau agreed to assist in the investigation of the reported sightings, and the Field was advised in [[references/fbi-bureau-bulletin-42-1947|Bureau Bulletin No. 42, Series 1947, dated July 30, 1947]], that they should investigate each instance which was brought to their attention of the sighting of a flying disc in order to ascertain whether or not it was a bona fide sighting, an imaginary one, or a prank. **The results of the investigation conducted by the Bureau Field Offices in this matter have failed to reveal any indication of subversive individuals being involved in any of the reported sightings.** ^[extracted]

**Paragraph 2** (immediate cause):

> The Bureau has received a communication in the captioned matter from the Special Agent in Charge at San Francisco, dated September 19, 1947, which attached a "restricted" letter that was furnished confidentially to the SAC at San Francisco by Lieutenant Colonel Donald L. Springer, A-2, Army Air Forces, Hamilton Field, California, a copy of which is attached hereto. **It is noted that the letter, which is dated September 3, 1947, is signed "By Command of Lieutenant General Stratemeyer" by Colonel R. H. Smith, Assistant Chief of Staff Intelligence, Headquarters, Air Defense Command, Mitchel Field, New York, and is addressed to the Commanding Generals of the various Air Forces.** This letter is entitled **"Cooperation of FBI with AAF on Investigations of 'Flying Disc' Incidents."** ^[extracted]

(The "restricted" letter is the [[references/adc-d333.5ex-cooperation-1947-09-03|ADC D333.5EX letter]] of 3 Sep 1947, retained at OCR pp. 160 + 183 of section 3 — see [[references/adc-d333.5ex-cooperation-1947-09-03]].)

**Paragraph 3** (substance summary):

> This letter states in substance that it was the original intent of the AC/AS-2, Headquarters, Army Air Forces that whereas the ADC Air Forces would interview responsible observers, the FBI would investigate incidents of so-called discs being found on the ground. Further, it indicates that the services of the FBI were enlisted in order to relieve the numbered Air Forces of the task of tracking down all the many instances which turned out to be **"ash can covers, toilet seats and whatnot."** ^[extracted]

**Recommendation block (the BB-57 proposal):**

> RECOMMENDATION:
>
> 1. It is recommended that the Bureau **protest vigorously** to the Assistant Chief of Air Staff - 2.
> 2. It is also recommended that the **Bureau discontinue all activity in this field** and that the **Bureau Field Offices be advised to discontinue all investigations and to refer all complaints received to the Air Forces.**
> 3. **A proposed Bulletin is attached for your approval.** ^[extracted]

The third recommendation **identifies the attached document as the proposed bulletin**. ^[extracted] The bulletin issued ~6 days later (1 Oct 1947) is BB-57. ^[inferred] The bulletin's literal text is **not transcribed** in the section-3 OCR — the attachment is referenced but not separately preserved in the OCR output. ^[ambiguous] The recommendation's operational paraphrase (paragraph 2: *"discontinue all investigations and refer all complaints received to the Air Forces"*) is **structurally identical** to the four BB-57 verbatim citations preserved in section 4 (12 Feb 1948 / 20 Feb 1948 / 14 Mar 1949 / 25 Mar 1949). ^[inferred]

## Why this is the BB-57 birth document

Three converging facts identify this memo as BB-57's literal proposal vehicle: ^[inferred]

1. **The recommendation matches BB-57's known content verbatim.** All four downstream BB-57 citations preserved in [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-4|section 4]] paraphrase the bulletin as: *"discontinued investigative activities … refer all future reports to the Air Forces … no investigative action by Bureau agents."* The Ladd memo's recommendation says: *"the Bureau discontinue all activity in this field … the Bureau Field Offices be advised to discontinue all investigations and to refer all complaints received to the Air Forces."* The semantic match is verbatim. ^[inferred]
2. **The memo explicitly states a proposed Bulletin is attached for approval.** No other 62-HQ-83894 serial in the corpus contains a "proposed Bulletin" attachment to a Director-recommendation memo on flying discs. ^[inferred]
3. **The temporal alignment is exact.** Ladd 25 Sep → Hoover approves 27 Sep → BB-57 issued 1 Oct (3-business-day Field-office distribution lead time). ^[inferred]

The Reynolds-drafted "SWR:AJB" initials confirm SA Reynolds (FBI Liaison Section) authored the memo — consistent with Reynolds being the FBI side of the AAF-FBI cooperation channel since 9 Jul 1947. ^[inferred] Ladd signed the memo per FBI institutional practice (Asst Director countersigns Liaison-originated policy memoranda). ^[inferred]

## Why Hoover terminated — what the memo signals

The memo's framing of the **proximate cause** is unambiguous: the **3 Sep 1947 ADC Stratemeyer-by-command letter** to the Air Forces' Commanding Generals (the [[references/adc-d333.5ex-cooperation-1947-09-03|D333.5EX letter]]) is treated as a **unilateral redefinition** of the cooperation regime by the AAF: ^[inferred]

- The ADC letter announced to AF subordinate commands that the **FBI's role was to "investigate incidents of so-called discs being found on the ground"** while the AAF would interview "responsible observers" — a **division of labor** that was never the FBI's understanding of the [[concepts/fbi-aaf-saucer-cooperation-1947|cooperation regime]]. ^[extracted]
- The ADC letter's "ash can covers, toilet seats and whatnot" framing reduced the FBI to a **junk-disc-recovery service** — institutionally insulting at FBI Director level. ^[inferred]
- The letter was issued **without coordination with the FBI** — surfacing only because SAC SF Kimball secured a "restricted" copy from Lt Col Springer (4AF A-2). ^[extracted] The FBI had been **operationally surprised** by an AAF directive that named it. ^[inferred]

These three observations parallel **the [[entities/g-d-garrett|Garrett admission]]** (~22 Sep 1947, [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-2|section 2]] serial 86) — i.e., **two AAF-internal events ~10 days apart** independently tell the FBI that the cooperation regime cannot continue: (a) the senior AAF intelligence officer privately tells Reynolds the discs are likely a US Government project, and (b) ADC issues a directive that subordinates the FBI as a junk-recovery service. ^[inferred] Either alone might have triggered termination; together they make termination institutionally inevitable. ^[inferred]

## What the memo does NOT mention

The memo notably **does not cite**:

- The Garrett admission (~22 Sep 1947, [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-2|serial 86]]) — though Reynolds, who drafted the memo, was the recipient of that admission. ^[inferred] Possibly the admission is **deemed too sensitive to memorialize in an all-Director-staff routing memo** that gets stamps from Tolson + 18 other officials. ^[inferred]
- The Schulgen 5 Sep 1947 formal denial letter ([[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-2|serial 84]]) — though that letter was the FBI's only AAF-side documentary basis for treating the discs as **not** a US project. ^[inferred]
- The Fugate negative-handover incident (29-30 Aug 1947, section 2 serials 68 + 80). ^[inferred]

The omission pattern suggests the memo's **public reasoning** is built on the **most institutionally defensible ground** — the ADC letter's unilateral redefinition of the cooperation regime — while the **deeper triggers** (Garrett's private admission, the entire prosaic-explanation framework collapsing) are kept off the institutional record. ^[inferred]
