# NASA Apollo 17 Air-to-Ground Voice Transcript Excerpt (Dec 1972)

A **16-page Mistral-OCR'd composite excerpt** of the **[[entities/apollo-17|Apollo 17]] air-to-ground voice transcription** — covering portions of **Tape 5 (transposition + S-IVB SEP, ~03:25 – 03:42 GET)**, **Tapes 46–47 (translunar coast + S-IVB-as-flasher tracking + ALFMED, ~02:18 – 02:21 GET)**, and **Tapes 59–60 (lunar-orbit operations + Schmitt's "flash on the lunar surface", ~03:15:33 – 03:15:39 GET)**. ^[extracted] The artifact is the **third structurally distinct Apollo 17 corpus document**, completing a **same-mission triple-artifact-class set** alongside the **[[references/nasa-apollo-17-technical-crew-debriefing-1973|d6 MSC-07631 Technical Crew Debriefing (4 Jan 1973)]]** and the **[[references/nasa-apollo-17-science-debriefing-1973|d5 MSC-07632 Crew Debriefing for Science (8 Jan 1973)]]**.

## Why this artifact is in the corpus — and what it does *not* contain

This is the wiki's **first contemporaneous mission voice-loop record from Apollo 17**. The signal class is **null/partial-signal** — the excerpt records four UAP-adjacent observation events, **none of which is an unresolved UAP report**:

1. **S-IVB fragment field at TD&E SEP** (~03:34–03:42 GET) — crew-spontaneous observation of a particle field at S-IVB separation, **identified by the crew themselves** in real time as **debris from the S-IVB**.
2. **Cosmic-ray retinal light flashes** (~02:18:42 GET) — **Cernan's** spontaneous testimony of a *"very bright spot that flashed right between my eyes like a very bright headlight - like a train coming at you"* during pre-sleep hours, plus **horizon-lighting flashes** during the ALFMED experiment context.
3. **S-IVB-as-flasher visual tracking** (~02:18:42–02:21 GET) — multi-witness real-time tracking of a rotating "flasher" object, **identified by the crew (Schmitt led, Cernan corroborated)** as the **S-IVB rotating end-over-side** based on a **bright/dull two-flash signature** (broadside-vs-engine-bell viewing geometry).
4. **Schmitt's "flash on the lunar surface"** (~03:15:38 GET) — real-time observation while in lunar orbit **north of Grimaldi**, with Houston requesting an **X on the map** and committing to **check the seismometers** for a correlated impact event.

The artifact is the **orbital-class voice-loop counterpart** to the [[references/nasa-apollo-17-technical-crew-debriefing-1973|d6 post-mission Technical Crew Debriefing]] — and the **triple-null convergence** (d2 voice loop + d6 technical debrief + d5 science debrief, all three Apollo 17 corpus artifacts independently null for UAP) is the **structurally novel finding**: same mission, three artifact classes, three FOIA / archival intake paths, all converging on no unresolved UAP observation. ^[inferred] This is a **stronger anti-suppression argument** than any single null artifact — the convergence is over the **artifact-class axis** rather than within a single document. ^[inferred]

## The four observation events

### 1. S-IVB fragment field at TD&E SEP (Tape 5/2 – 5/5, ~03:34–03:42 GET)

The first observation begins immediately as the spacecraft maneuvers for **Transposition, Docking, and Extraction (TD&E)**. ^[extracted]

> **CMP (Evans)**: *"Now we've got a few very bright particles or fragments or something that go drifting by as we maneuver."* ^[extracted]
>
> **LMP (Schmitt)**: *"There's a whole bunch of big ones on my window down there - just bright. It looks like the Fourth of July out of Ron's window."* ^[extracted]
>
> **CMP (Evans)**: *"Yes. Now you can see some of them in shape. They're very jagged, angular fragments that are tumbling."* ^[extracted]

The **CC (Bob)** prompt — *"Roger. They look like fluid of some sort?"* — produces an **immediate empirical correction** by Evans: *"Not to me. They look like pieces of something. ... They're very bright."* ^[extracted] Evans then **photographs** the fragment field on **Frame 65 + Frame 70** of the LMP's mag November November ^[extracted] and offers a **crew-supplied conventional attribution**:

> **CMP (Evans)**: *"Well, you know I - I don't know. There are a number of possibilities. ... I got the impression maybe they were curved a little bit, as if they might be - off the side of the S-IVB. ... ice chunks, possibly. Or maybe there's paint coming off of it."* ^[extracted]

The **CC (Bob)** corroborates with a recollected **pre-flight elevator sighting**: *"I noticed on one trip up the elevator last week near one of the flags. ... Looked like it was peeling. Maybe that's what you've got."* ^[extracted] **CDR (Cernan)** then provides the morphology summary: *"My impression is that they are - flat, flakelike particles. Some may be 6 inches across. And, although there's no relative motion between the two, most of them seem to be twinkling. And I think, for the most part, they're all moving away from us."* ^[extracted]

This is the corpus's **second empirical confirmation** of the *"your own debris field"* prior on [[concepts/orbital-uap-sighting]] — the canonical conventional-explanation candidate for any small luminous object near a manned spacecraft on early translunar coast. The **first** confirmation was [[entities/pete-conrad|Conrad]]'s tracking-light exchange on [[references/nasa-uap-d1-apollo-12-transcript-1969|Apollo 12 d1]]. ^[inferred] **Crew-side identification mechanisms** here include **shape inference** ("curved ... off the side of"), **morphology** ("flat, flakelike, 6 inches"), **velocity field** ("no relative motion between fragments ... all moving away from us"), and **timing** ("with the maneuver complete, the fragment field is essentially static").

### 2. Cosmic-ray retinal light flashes — Cernan real-time testimony (Tape 46/4, ~02:18:42 GET)

Hours after the SEP fragment field, Cernan reports — **unprompted** — a vivid pre-sleep flash he saw **on the night before**:

> **CDR (Cernan)**: *"...I had a hard time going to bed, to start with - I saw some of the same peripheral horizon-type things you said were not the type of data you were looking for; but I also saw a - some sets of the streaks. And probably the one most imposing thing I remember is - and the last one I remember before falling asleep - was the fact that there was a very bright spot that flashed right between my eyes like a very bright headlight - like a train coming at you, only with a flash. It's difficult probably to estimate the frequency of any of those because I was in a - sort of a sleep-hazy mode."* ^[extracted]

> **CDR (Cernan)**: *"But then, as today, I saw some that flashed and lit up the horizon and some that lit up peripherally; and I guess, as you say, that's a different kind of data, but I did see them there and they impressed me."* ^[extracted]

> **CMP (Evans)**: *"And it might be interesting to know I've never seen it before today."* ^[extracted]

> **CC (Bob)**: *"Hey, Gene, we appreciate all the data. We were just trying to make the data fit the curve; you know the old trick."* ^[extracted]

> **CDR (Cernan)**: *"I will say one thing, though; no question in my mind but that they're there. ... they're there."* ^[extracted]

This passage is structurally important for **two** reasons:

1. **Cernan as a real-time cosmic-ray-flash witness.** The d6 post-mission debriefing has Schmitt as the only quoted Apollo 17 cosmic-ray-flash speaker; the d2 voice loop has **Cernan as a real-time, in-mission cosmic-ray-flash speaker** with a vivid **"train headlight"** characterization that does not appear in the d6 summary. Cernan's status on [[concepts/cosmic-ray-light-flashes]] upgrades from *corroborating witness only* (d6) to **real-time first-person articulator** (d2). ^[inferred]
2. **Real-time vs. post-mission artifact-class distinction within a single mission.** The d2 / d6 pair is the corpus's first instance where **the same astronaut crew's cosmic-ray-flash testimony is preserved in two artifact classes from the same mission** — contemporaneous voice loop (d2) and post-mission technical debrief (d6). The vocabulary and emphasis differ (d2: vivid metaphor, sleep-hazy uncertainty, crew defensiveness *"no question in my mind but that they're there"*; d6: clinical experimental-design framing). ^[inferred] This artifact-class divergence is itself a structural datum — the wiki's prior cosmic-ray-flash witness pages (Aldrin, Schmitt, Kerwin, Conrad, Weitz) were all post-mission-only. Cernan d2 is the first **contemporaneous-voice-loop cosmic-ray-flash record** in the corpus. ^[inferred]

The CC's *"trying to make the data fit the curve"* line ^[extracted] is also evidence that **NASA-internal cosmic-ray-flash data analysis was already underway in real time** during Apollo 17 — there was a *"curve"* the ground team was trying to fit observation data to. ^[inferred]

### 3. S-IVB-as-flasher tracking (Tape 46/4 – 47/14, ~02:18:46 – 02:20:55 GET)

A second, independent observation event runs in parallel: a **rotating flashing object** observed through the cabin windows during translunar coast. The crew themselves work through identification in real time:

> **CDR (Cernan)** (Tape 46/6): *"Hey, Bob, I'm looking at what - what Jack was talking about; and it's definitely not a particle that's nearby because there is another one I can look at and get a three-dimensional comparison with. It is a - it is a bright object, and it's obviously rotating because it's flashing. It's way out in the distance ... It's apparently rotating in a very rhythmic fashion ..."* ^[extracted]

> **CDR (Cernan)** (Tape 46/7): *"... it's up at about 11:00 about - oh, maybe 10 or 12 Earth diameters."* ^[extracted]

> **CC (Bob)**: *"Roger. We don't doubt it, Gene. And we might work out a set of gimbal angles or something here; maybe we can get a look at it through the optics."* ^[extracted]

> **CDR (Cernan)** (Tape 46/7): *"... it's definitely not - not one of these particles that tends to look like a star out there. It's something physical in the distance. ... Oh, yes."* ^[extracted]

CC then asks Cernan for a **NOUN 20** spacecraft attitude reference and a **mark when the object crosses the XX axis** of the center window. Cernan complies; the mark is logged. ^[extracted]

The **identification** comes from Schmitt:

> **LMP (Schmitt)** (Tape 46/8): *"That's the side and - of the S-IVB - and then the engine bell, Gene."* ^[extracted]
>
> **CDR (Cernan)**: *"One unique thing about it, Bob, is that it's got two flashes. As it comes around in - in rhythmic fashion, you get a very bright flash; and then you get a dull flash. And then it'll come around with a bright flash, and then a dull flash."* ^[extracted]

The **bright/dull two-flash signature** is read by Schmitt (and accepted by Cernan) as **broadside-vs-engine-bell** viewing geometry — the body of the S-IVB reflecting a bright flash when broadside-on, the J-2 engine bell reflecting a dimmer flash when end-on. ^[inferred] Schmitt also notes (Tape 46/8): *"a couple of revolutions ago when I was looking at it, I had a much brighter view and I believe I was looking at it broadside. It looks to me like it may be flashing more or less end-on now."* ^[extracted]

Notably, **Mission Control could not immediately compute the S-IVB position** from the ground side:

> **CC (Bob)** (Tape 46/5): *"FIDO just went out and shot himself, but we'll get working on it."* ^[extracted]
>
> **CC (Bob)** (Tape 46/5): *"... what's humorous on this, Jack, is they have really been working on that S-IVB impact point -, and they've really been - it's been a - a real difficult problem for them so far."* ^[extracted]

This is the **inverse** of the [[references/sighting-apollo-11-cylinder-1969-07|Apollo 11 d4]] case where **Mission Control range data eliminated S-IVB** (6,000 mi) as the candidate. On Apollo 17 d2, the **crew make a visual ID** based on **morphology + rotation signature** without ground-side ranging support. The Apollo 11 / Apollo 17 d2 contrast is the corpus's first example of the **two distinct S-IVB-identification mechanisms** — ground-side ranging vs. crew-side morphology — operating across different missions. ^[inferred]

A later passage (Tape 47/14, ~02:20:55 GET) extends the observation:

> **CDR (Cernan)**: *"Hey, Bob. We got two of those flashers out there. They could be SLA panels. I don't know. They're alike in intensity and pretty regular in the - in the intensity, bright and dim flashes they come out with, and they're widely separated."* ^[extracted]

The **two-flasher** observation suggests the original "flasher" might be **SLA (Spacecraft / LM Adapter) panels** rather than the S-IVB itself, or possibly that **both** the S-IVB and a separated SLA panel are visible. ^[inferred] The source does not resolve which.

### 4. Schmitt's "flash on the lunar surface" north of Grimaldi (Tape 60/1, ~03:15:38 GET)

The most **discretely-anomalous** observation in the d2 excerpt — though still not a UAP report — comes ~32 hours after the S-IVB-as-flasher exchange, while the spacecraft is in lunar orbit observing **Mare Procellarum / Grimaldi** terrain:

> **LMP (Schmitt)**: *"Hey, I just saw a flash on the lunar surface!"* ^[extracted]
>
> **CC (Gordy)**: *"Oh, yes?"* ^[extracted]
>
> **LMP (Schmitt)**: *"It was just out there north of Grimaldi. Just north of Grimaldi. You might see if you got anything on your seismometers, although a small impact probably would give a fair amount of visible light."* ^[extracted]
>
> **CC (Gordy)**: *"Okay. We'll check."* ^[extracted]
>
> **LMP (Schmitt)**: *"It was a bright little flash right out there near that crater. See the crater right at the edge of Grimaldi. Then there is another one north of it. Fairly sharp one north of it is where there was just a thin streak of light."* ^[extracted]
>
> **CC (Gordy)**: *"How about putting an X on the map where you saw it."* ^[extracted]
>
> **LMP (Schmitt)**: *"I keep looking occasionally for - yes, we will. I - I was planning on looking for those kind of things."* ^[extracted]

This is the corpus's **first real-time procedural exchange** for a **Transient Lunar Phenomenon (TLP)** observation. ^[inferred] The dialogue establishes:

- **Schmitt was actively looking for surface flashes** — *"I was planning on looking for those kind of things"* — making this an **expected-class observation** for a scientist-astronaut, not a serendipitous spotting. ^[extracted]
- **Schmitt offered the meteoroid-impact mechanism in real time** — *"a small impact probably would give a fair amount of visible light"* — supplying a conventional attribution himself. ^[extracted]
- **Mission Control's procedural response was to cross-correlate with the [ALSEP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Lunar_Surface_Experiments_Package) seismometers** — *"Okay. We'll check."* ^[extracted] Whether the cross-correlation produced a positive seismometer hit is **not preserved in the d2 excerpt** ^[ambiguous] and is not addressed in either [[references/nasa-apollo-17-technical-crew-debriefing-1973|d6]] or [[references/nasa-apollo-17-science-debriefing-1973|d5]].
- **Schmitt was asked to mark the location on a map** — *"How about putting an X on the map where you saw it."* — establishing a **chain-of-evidence procedural step**. ^[extracted]

The d6 post-mission summary's *"I had one which I thought was a flash on the lunar surface"* line is **the same observation event in summary form** — but the d2 voice loop preserves the **real-time procedural exchange** that d6 collapses to a single subordinate clause. The **partially-resolved** characterization of the d6 / d2 cross-reference is: d2 confirms d6's lunar-surface-flash is **distinct** from the continuous cislunar cosmic-ray-flash sequence (it has a specific lunar location, a specific proposed mechanism, a specific procedural follow-up), while still **not identifying** the actual impactor or confirming the seismometer correlation. ^[inferred]

This observation does **not** earn a dedicated [[concepts/orbital-uap-sighting|sighting]] page — TLPs are not UAP, the proposed mechanism (small meteoroid impact) is conventional, and the **single instance** does not warrant a TLP concept page. ^[inferred] Schmitt's *"I was planning on looking for those kind of things"* framing places it in the **expected-class observation** category for a scientist-astronaut on lunar-orbit operations.

## What the excerpt establishes

- **All three Apollo 17 crewmen — [[entities/gene-cernan|Cernan]], [[entities/ronald-evans|Evans]], and [[entities/harrison-schmitt|Schmitt]] — produced contemporaneous in-mission UAP-adjacent voice-loop testimony** that converges with their post-mission d6 testimony on a **null result for UAP**. ^[extracted]
- **The S-IVB fragment field at SEP is the corpus's second empirical confirmation** of the *"your own debris field"* prior on [[concepts/orbital-uap-sighting]] (after the [[references/nasa-uap-d1-apollo-12-transcript-1969|Apollo 12 d1]] Conrad tracking-light exchange). ^[inferred]
- **Cernan is a real-time cosmic-ray-flash speaker** in the corpus — a status he does not have from d6 alone. ^[extracted]
- **The S-IVB-as-flasher visual ID by morphology + rotation signature** is the corpus's first crew-side S-IVB identification independent of Mission Control range data — the inverse of the Apollo 11 d4 ranging-data elimination. ^[inferred]
- **Mission Control could not compute S-IVB position from the ground side at the time of the Cernan/Schmitt visual ID** — *"FIDO just went out and shot himself, but we'll get working on it"* / *"a real difficult problem for them so far"*. ^[extracted]
- **Schmitt's "flash on the lunar surface" was a real-time TLP-class observation north of Grimaldi**, with Schmitt supplying the **meteoroid-impact mechanism** and Mission Control committing to **cross-correlate with the ALSEP seismometers**. ^[extracted] Whether the cross-correlation produced a positive hit is not preserved in this excerpt. ^[ambiguous]

## What the excerpt does *not* establish

- **No UAP / unidentified-object observation** is present anywhere in the 16-page excerpt. ^[extracted]
- **No bogey, target, contact, or unidentified-vehicle terminology** appears. ^[extracted]
- **The seismometer cross-correlation outcome** for Schmitt's lunar-surface flash is not in the excerpt. ^[ambiguous]
- **Whether the d2 excerpt represents the entire UAP-keyword payload of the parent Apollo 17 transcript** (which spans the full ~12-day mission) or only a keyword-extracted subset is not addressable from the artifact. ^[open]
- **Whether Cernan's pre-sleep "train headlight" flash (~Day 1 evening) had the same proposed mechanism (cosmic-ray retinal flash) as Schmitt's continuous-cislunar testimony** is not explicitly stated in d2 — the linkage is structurally implied by Cernan's *"the same peripheral horizon-type things"* framing and his cross-talk with Schmitt's experimental-context observations, but neither Cernan nor CC names the cosmic-ray mechanism in this passage. ^[inferred]
