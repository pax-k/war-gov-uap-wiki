# Sighting — Jackson, Mississippi (1 Jan 1949)

A two-witness aerial-platform observation on **1 January 1949 at 1700 CST**, 2 miles east of Jackson MS, by a civilian pilot and his wife on landing approach to Dixie Airport. Filed as **Project SIGN Incident 233** (the **terminal incident** in the 173–233 sequence) in [[references/project-sign-incident-summaries-173-233|the Sep 1948 – Jan 1949 incident summaries bundle]]. The same event also surfaces in [[references/usaf-flying-discs-1949|the 1949 USAF Flying Discs file]] as a 1 Jan 1949 entry from the 1949 sighting table — making this the **first event in the wiki's corpus to appear in two distinct primary-source files**.

## Witnesses

- **Mr Tom Rush** — pilot employed by Dixie Air Service, Jackson MS. Ex-AAF pilot. ^[extracted]
- **Mrs Tom Rush** — private pilot. Co-witness. ^[extracted]

Both were passengers in a **Stinson civilian aircraft** flying at ~1,800 ft on landing approach to Dixie Airport. The pilot of the Stinson did not see the object despite Rush attempting to point it out. ^[extracted]

(Address: 402 Mitchell Ave., Jackson, Mississippi.)

## Object signature

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Number** | 1 |
| **Length** | ~60 feet |
| **Cross-section** | 10 ft wide tapering to 4 ft at trailing end |
| **Color** | Dark blue or black |
| **Shape** | Cigar-shaped — "not unlike a sleeve target" / **resembling a tow target**; first report mentioned short stubby wings, second report **denied wings** ("as the object made its turn, it was then that I noticed the object didn't have wings") |
| **Tail behavior** | "Tail even fluttered like a target but there was nothing towing it" |
| **Initial altitude** | 1500–1600 ft, then gradual climb |
| **Closest distance** | Crossed path of plane at 500 ft separation; after turn, as close as 1,200 ft |
| **Initial speed** | ~200 MPH at first sighting |
| **Final speed** | Accelerating to 400–500 MPH (initial report; revised to 400–600 MPH in second report) |
| **Sound** | First report: "similar to helicopter"; second report: "roar" |
| **Tactics** | 50° turn and increased speed as it headed SW |
| **Manner of disappearance** | Climbed toward SW and disappeared |
| **Time in sight** | 10–12 seconds |
| **Weather** | Clear to the SW |
| **Photographs** | None — no camera on board |

## What's distinctive

Three features make this sighting structurally interesting in the corpus:

1. **Cigar morphology with denial of wings on revision.** The Rush sighting is the **earliest unambiguous cigar/cylindrical** event in the wiki without overlapping ball-of-fire or flame description — the morphology that the [[references/usaf-flying-discs-1949|1949 file]] documents as **the second-most-common 1949 morphology class** (Boston 10 Jun 49, 100-ft tubular). ^[inferred]
2. **Apparent tow-target signature without towing aircraft.** The descriptor "looked like a tow target with the tail fluttering" is unusually specific and pilot-vocabulary-anchored. Tow targets in 1948 were operationally familiar to ex-AAF pilots (Rush). The witness's framing is therefore **categorically not "I saw a UFO"** but **"I saw a tow target that wasn't being towed and was way too fast."** This kind of unforced disambiguation is a credibility marker. ^[inferred]
3. **Sound transition from helicopter-like to roar as speed increased.** The acceleration-coupled sound shift (200 MPH "helicopter sound" → 400+ MPH "roar") is consistent with **a real airframe in increasing dynamic pressure** rather than with a sustained-luminous-orb event. ^[inferred] The signature is **not** consistent with a balloon, parachute decoy, or pure-luminosity object.

## Why this is the canonical "bridge" event

Inc 233 is the **last incident** in the 173–233 sequence and **also** appears as the **first 1949 entry** in the 1949 USAF Flying Discs file (under the 1 Jan 1949 / Jackson MS / Thomas A. Rush row in the 1949 sighting table). This dual-file presence makes it the corpus's clearest **bookkeeping bridge** between the 1948 Project SIGN sequential-incident-number scheme and the 1949 EEI / case-named filing convention.

A few entailments follow: ^[inferred]

- The 1949 sighting table identifies the witness as "Thomas A. Rush (airport mgr, Dixie Airport) + 2 civilians." The 173–233 batch identifies the witness as "Mr & Mrs Tom Rush" plus an unnamed Stinson pilot — likely the same three-person observation, with "airport manager" being a more accurate role descriptor than the 173–233 sheet's "pilot employed by Dixie Air Service."
- The 1949 file's entry is two-line; the 173–233 file's entry is the **full witness narrative**, including the wings-vs-no-wings revision and the 50° turn description. The two files should be read together — the 173–233 sheet is the detailed report, the 1949 file is the cross-reference index.

## Provenance and reliability

- Two co-witnesses (the Rushes), one of whom is an ex-AAF pilot. Stronger than typical single-witness civilian event. ^[inferred]
- The third aircraft occupant (the Stinson pilot) **did not see the object** despite Rush attempting to point it out. This is a partial **negative datum** that the summary sheet preserves rather than smoothing over. ^[extracted]
- No flight schedule of any local airfield could account for the object. ^[extracted]
- No photographs preserved.
