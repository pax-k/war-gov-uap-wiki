# NASA d1 — Apollo 12 Air-to-Ground Voice Loop Excerpt (Tapes 90/3, 90/4, 90/9, 93/8, Nov 1969)

A **4-page Mistral-OCR'd excerpt** of the **[[entities/apollo-12|Apollo 12]] air-to-ground voice loop** captured on Mission Control master tapes at MSC Houston, drawn from **Tape 90 (page 90/3, 90/4, 90/9)** and **Tape 93 (page 93/8)** of the dub-off-the-master-tape transcription series. The bundle is the **first** of seven NASA crew-transcript / debriefing files in the wiki's **d1–d7 cluster** (d1 Apollo 12 — this artifact; d2 [[entities/apollo-17|Apollo 17]] transcript; d3 Gemini 7 transcript; d4 [[entities/apollo-11|Apollo 11]]; [[references/nasa-apollo-17-technical-crew-debriefing-1973|d6]] Apollo 17 technical; [[references/nasa-apollo-17-science-debriefing-1973|d5]] Apollo 17 science; d7 Skylab) and is the **first NASA-PAO-era voice-loop artifact** in the corpus from a mission **other than [[entities/gemini-7|Gemini 7]]**. ^[inferred]

This artifact is **partial-signal**: one astronaut-spontaneous on-orbit ephemeral-light observation by **[[entities/alan-bean|Alan Bean]]** through the **AOT (Alignment Optical Telescope)** in which Bean himself offers a conventional water-boiler-effluent explanation, plus one astronaut-confirmed **co-orbital-debris** observation by **[[entities/pete-conrad|Pete Conrad]]**. Neither is bogey-class. The excerpt is structurally distinct from every prior d-cluster entry in the corpus.

## The four observations on the excerpt

The excerpt records four substantively distinct events. Three are **non-UAP** (lift-off-time uplink, AGS DEDA EMI, tracking-light burnout) and serve only as mission-context anchors. The fourth — the Bean AOT particle-flash report — is **partial-signal**: a spontaneous astronaut on-orbit ephemeral-light observation that Bean himself offers a conventional explanation for in the same transmission.

### 1. (Tape 90/3) Lift-off-time uplink and rendezvous chatter — non-UAP

The first page captures the **CMP-CC greeting at LM-CSM proximity** (*"Hello, Houston; Yankee Clipper" / "Yankee Clipper, Houston. Loud and clear."*) and the **lift-off-time uplink** (*"Your lift-off time is 142:03:47"* — from *Intrepid* on the lunar surface to *Yankee Clipper* in lunar orbit, prior to the LM ascent). ^[extracted] The exchange establishes the mission phase and confirms all three crewmen are voice-active on this tape. **No UAP content.** ^[extracted]

### 2. (Tape 90/4) Bean AOT "particles of light, flashes of light" observation — partial-signal

> **LMP-LM** *(Bean)*: *"Roger. When you look out the AOT in the dark quadrant? You can see these lights — particles of light, flashes of light just seem to come from — in this case, I'm looking in quadrant 1 which is the left one. It's coming from behind me, the left, and they're just sailing off in space. I was thinking they're dropping from my water boiler, but it looks like some of those things are escaping the Moon. They really haul out of here and just press off at the stars."* ^[extracted]
>
> **CC**: *"Roger."* ^[extracted]

This is the d1 artifact's **only UAP-adjacent observation** and is the second-corpus astronaut on-orbit ephemeral-light observation after **[[references/sighting-gemini-7-bogey-1965-12-04|Borman's 1965 GT-7 cross-track particle stream]]** ([[entities/frank-borman|Borman]]: *"hundreds of little particles going by to the left out about three or four miles"*). ^[inferred]

The **observation**:

- **Instrument**: the **Alignment Optical Telescope (AOT)** — a passive, non-powered sextant-class periscope mounted on the LM crew compartment ceiling, used by the LMP for in-flight platform alignment via star-sightings. Bean is looking through the AOT in **the dark quadrant** (*"quadrant 1 which is the left one"*). ^[extracted]
- **Phenomenology**: *"particles of light, flashes of light"* — discrete luminous events, multiple, transient (flash-like rather than persistent), with **directional motion away from the observer / from the Moon**.
- **Bearing / track**: *"coming from behind me, the left, and they're just sailing off in space"* — i.e., the particles emerge from a region behind/left of Bean's vantage and depart away into space.
- **Self-attribution (conventional)**: Bean offers two non-UAP candidates — (a) **water-boiler effluent** (the LM's evaporative cooling system vents water vapor / ice droplets to vacuum); (b) **outgassing from the Moon** (*"some of those things are escaping the Moon"*). ^[extracted]

The **CAPCOM response** is a single *"Roger"* — no follow-up question, no debrief callback, no investigative routing. The exchange ends. ^[extracted]

The **"escaping the Moon"** language is striking but **explicable** without invoking UAP: the LM in lunar orbit is in close visual proximity to the lunar surface, and any sub-orbital particle (from past impacts, surface outgassing, or Bean's own LM-vent products) appearing to leave the lunar surface and recede past the spacecraft would produce the described *"haul out of here ... press off at the stars"* visual signature. ^[inferred]

**Why this matters for the corpus**:

- **First astronaut self-attributed conventional-explanation observation** in the corpus's orbital-class sub-corpus. Bean's call is **not** equivalent to Borman's *"bogey at ten o'clock high"* — Bean himself offers the water-boiler / outgassing reading inside the same transmission. ^[inferred]
- **First observation made through the AOT (sextant-class navigation instrument)** as opposed to the **rendezvous window** (Borman GT-7) or **command-module rendezvous window** (Evans Apollo 17 d6). Different instrument, different optical pathway, different observer state (head-down at AOT vs. head-up at window). ^[inferred]
- **Closest structural twin**: **Glenn's MA-6 "fireflies"** observation (Feb 1962) — astronaut sees particle-stream while in-orbit, attributes to spacecraft outgassing (in Glenn's case, frosting on the spacecraft's outer surface flaking off and catching sunlight). The MA-6 case is on **[[concepts/orbital-uap-sighting]]**'s "watch for" list and remains awaiting primary-source ingest; Bean's d1 entry **does not replace** the MA-6 watch-for (different mission, different instrument, different MET regime), but the **phenomenon class parallels are explicit**. ^[inferred]
- **Defines a fourth structural sub-class** of orbital-class entry (see *D-cluster typology contribution* below).

### 3. (Tape 90/9) Bean AGS DEDA "all 8's flash" — non-UAP avionics anomaly

> **LMP-LM** *(Bean)*: *"Got sort of an interesting thing going on AGS right now. I didn't notice earlier, but it may just be because the lights are brighter now. I'm getting an all 8's flash on both the address and the information registers at about one-fifth the brilliance of the normal numbers. And a — It's pulsing every second."* ^[extracted]
>
> **CC**: *"Roger, Al."* ... *"Roger, Al. Fredo is here. He and I have both seen that phenomena on your DEDA during testing of most all the spacecrafts up at Bethpage, and it's probably an EMI."* ^[extracted]

The **AGS** is the **Abort Guidance System** — the LM's secondary inertial-guidance computer (TRW-built, distinct from the primary PGNCS). The **DEDA** (Data Entry and Display Assembly) is the AGS keyboard / numeric display. Bean reports an "all 8's flash" pulsing once per second at one-fifth normal brightness on the address and information registers. CAPCOM (with **Fredo**) confirms the phenomenon was **observed during ground testing at Grumman's Bethpage plant** on most LM spacecraft and identifies it as **electromagnetic interference (EMI)**. ^[extracted]

This is **flatly not a UAP observation** — it is an **avionics-instrumentation anomaly** with a known mechanism (EMI on the DEDA's seven-segment display) and a known precedent (Bethpage ground testing). It enters the corpus only because the page is in the same OCR bundle as the Bean AOT observation. ^[inferred] No new tag, no new concept page, no new sighting page is generated. The exchange is documentary corroboration that **NASA / Grumman's standing test apparatus** had already characterized this anomaly class **before the mission flew**, contrasting with the absence of any standing apparatus to characterize the AOT particle-flash class.

### 4. (Tape 93/8) Conrad tracking-light burnout — empirical confirmation of co-orbital-debris prior

> **CDR-LM** *(Conrad)*: *"Hey, Houston. It looks like our tracking light's burned out. Dick hasn't been able to find us in this sextant. And on the first nightside pass we had little bits and pieces floating along with us and we could tell that the tracking light was flashing on them. And we still have, I've presumed to think, bits and pieces floating along and nothing's flashing on them, so I'm pretty sure it burned out."* ^[extracted]
>
> **CC**: *"Roger, Pete. Our electrical watchers say that the current indicates that your tracking light is on."* ... *"It — It sure does, Pete."* ^[extracted]
>
> **CDR-LM** *(Conrad)*: *"Okay. Now we just turned it off. Now does the current show that?"* ^[extracted]

This is the corpus's **first instance where the canonical "your own debris field" conventional-explanation prior is empirically observed rather than merely inferred**. ^[inferred] The [[concepts/orbital-uap-sighting|orbital-UAP-sighting concept page]] currently asserts (as `^[inferred]`):

> *"The conventional-explanation prior for any small luminous object near a manned spacecraft on orbit is therefore **strongly weighted toward** 'your own debris field' or 'another orbital object's debris field'."*

Conrad's observation provides **direct empirical confirmation** of this prior, in two parts:

1. **First nightside pass**: bits and pieces visibly co-orbiting with the LM, illuminated by the tracking light's flash. ^[extracted]
2. **After tracking-light burnout**: bits and pieces **still present** in Conrad's vantage, but no longer flashing — observable only by their occulting / scattering against ambient lunar-orbit illumination, not by reflection of the tracking-light pulse. ^[extracted]

The exchange between Conrad and CAPCOM also captures a **secondary diagnostic anomaly**: CAPCOM's *"electrical watchers"* see the tracking-light **current draw** even though Conrad reports the lamp filament burned out — i.e., the **electrical-monitoring telemetry alone cannot distinguish a burned-out lamp from an operational one** in the bus-tie configuration in use during rendezvous. ^[inferred] Conrad disambiguates by cycling the lamp off — *"Now we just turned it off. Now does the current show that?"* This is a small but notable **observability-of-spacecraft-state** episode that is structurally on-topic for any treatment of how astronauts diagnose anomalies in flight.

## Mission phase reconstruction

The four MET stamps on the excerpt — `05 19 14 58`, `05 19 27 25`, `05 20 09 34`, `06 00 21 51` — span **5 hours 6 minutes 53 seconds of mission time** in the **MET 5d 19h to MET 6d 00h** window. ^[extracted]

| Apollo 12 mission event (per program record) | MET window | Excerpt event |
|---|---|---|
| Lunar EVA-1 (Conrad / Bean) | MET ~115h to ~119h ^[inferred] | (before excerpt) |
| Lunar EVA-2 (Conrad / Bean) | MET ~131h to ~135h ^[inferred] | (before excerpt) |
| LM ascent ("Intrepid" lift-off from Ocean of Storms) | scheduled MET 142:03:47 (= 5d 22h 03m 47s) ^[extracted] | the **lift-off time uplink** itself is on the excerpt at MET 5d 19h 23m 39s ^[extracted] |
| LM-CSM rendezvous | MET ~145h ^[inferred] | (after excerpt's lift-off-time uplink) |
| LM jettison + ascent-stage deorbit (impact on Moon for ALSEP seismic experiment) | MET ~150h ^[inferred] | — |

The **sextant-search exchange** between Conrad and Gordon (*"Dick hasn't been able to find us in this sextant"*) on Tape 93/8 at MET 06d 00h 21m places it **post-rendezvous configuration** — Gordon in CSM searching for *Intrepid* through the CSM sextant. ^[inferred]

The Bean AOT observation (Tape 90/4 at MET 05d 19h 27m) is **prior to LM ascent** — Bean is using the AOT in the **landed LM on the lunar surface** at the Ocean of Storms, looking out through the AOT in the dark quadrant. ^[inferred] This makes the *"escaping the Moon"* phenomenology much more comprehensible: from a stationary LM on the lunar surface at terminator, particle-flashes appearing to leave the Moon and recede past the LM into starfield are **lunar-surface-origin phenomena**, not in-orbit debris. The "haul out of here" velocity reading is consistent with **escape-velocity ejecta** from very small impacts within Bean's quadrant of view, **water-boiler effluent leaving the LM** at characteristic vacuum-vent velocities (tens of m/s), or even **micrometeoroid impacts on the AOT's reticle** producing flash-like saturation events. ^[inferred] Bean himself prefers the water-boiler reading.

Sub-class D is **structurally distinct** from A/B/C on three axes:

1. **Polarity**: weakly positive (a real spontaneous astronaut on-orbit ephemeral-light observation occurs) but not bogey-class (no *"bogey"* terminology, no Houston disambiguation prompt, no PAO-class third-object framing). The closest A-vs-D analogy: imagine a counterfactual GT-7 in which Borman had said *"a bogey at ten o'clock high — I'm thinking it's the spent stage"* and Houston had replied *"Roger"* with no follow-up. That's the d1 polarity. ^[inferred]
2. **Keyword content**: present (*"lights"* / *"flashes"*) but in **astronaut-spontaneous** context, not in transcript-keyword false-positive context. d1 entries do contain UAP-domain lexical markers; d6's keyword content was lexically present without UAP framing; d5's was lexically absent. d1 is **lexically-present-and-UAP-framed-by-the-astronaut-himself**, then walked back inside the same transmission. ^[inferred]
3. **Astronaut self-correction**: present and **immediate** (within the same radio beat). Borman's GT-7 call has no self-correction. Apollo 17 d6's three observations have **conversational** self-correction (Cernan / Evans clarifying the carrier-superstructure misidentification in subsequent radio beats). Bean's d1 observation is the corpus's **first single-beat astronaut-self-correction** — Bean reports the phenomenology and offers the water-boiler reading in a single uninterrupted transmission. ^[inferred]

The four-sub-class typology now mirrors and **extends** the diplomatic-channel [[concepts/figurative-ufo-rhetoric|figurative-UFO rhetoric typology]]: the orbital-class sub-corpus has reached the same typological resolution (four-mechanism) that the diplomatic sub-corpus has at three. ^[inferred] The d-cluster's remaining files (d2 Apollo 17 transcript, d4 Apollo 11, d7 Skylab) will resolve whether sub-class D is recurrent or singular in d1.

## What the artifact establishes

- **Apollo 12's air-to-ground voice loop captured at least one astronaut-spontaneous on-orbit ephemeral-light observation** — Bean through the AOT, MET ~5d 19h 27m. ^[extracted]
- **The observation was made through a navigation instrument (the AOT), not the LM rendezvous window**. ^[extracted]
- **Bean offered two conventional explanations** (water-boiler effluent + lunar outgassing/ejecta) within the same transmission. ^[extracted]
- **CAPCOM did not pursue the observation** — single *"Roger"* response, no follow-up. ^[extracted]
- **Conrad's tracking-light exchange empirically confirms the canonical "your own debris field" prior** by directly observing co-orbital bits and pieces both with and without tracking-light flash illumination. ^[extracted]
- **The AGS DEDA "all 8's flash" was a known EMI anomaly** characterized at Grumman's Bethpage plant prior to flight — non-UAP. ^[extracted]
- **The Apollo 12 LM was named "Intrepid"** and the CSM "Yankee Clipper". ^[extracted]
- **CAPCOM Jerry was on duty during the rendezvous-prep voice loop** ^[extracted] — likely **Jerry Carr** per Apollo 12 backup-CAPCOM rotation. ^[inferred]

## What the artifact does *not* establish

- **No bogey-class call.** ^[extracted]
- **No PAO-narration commentary** is preserved in the OCR bundle (vs. the GT-7 d3/canonical pair which has explicit *"this is Gemini Control"* PAO sign-offs). ^[extracted]
- **No morphology data** on the AOT particle-flashes (no shape, no color beyond *"lights"*, no estimated distance, no estimated count). ^[extracted]
- **No follow-up debrief reference** in the excerpt — the parent debrief documents (post-mission technical / science debriefings, analogous to MSC-07631 / MSC-07632 for Apollo 17) are **not** in the d1 OCR bundle. ^[extracted]
- **Whether the d1 OCR bundle is the complete Apollo 12 air-to-ground voice loop or a keyword-extracted excerpt** is **not addressable** from the bundle alone. The four pages span a 5-hour mission window with large gaps, suggesting **selective extraction** rather than continuous coverage. ^[inferred]
- **The MET-to-UTC conversions** are inferential (Apollo 12 launch UTC: 14 Nov 1969 16:22 + MET → Tape 90/3 ~20 Nov 1969 11:37 UTC). Use with `^[inferred]`.
