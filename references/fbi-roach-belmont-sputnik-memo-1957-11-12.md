# Roach → Belmont "Sputnik" UFO Memo (12 November 1957)

A **2-page FBI HQ internal Office Memorandum**, **Mr. R. R. Roach to Mr. A. H. Belmont**, dated **November 12, 1957**, subject *"UNIDENTIFIED FLYING OBJECTS (FLYING SAUCERS)"*. ^[extracted] Filed at FBI HQ as **62-HQ-83894 serial 387** (page 5 of [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-9|section 9 OCR]]). ^[extracted] **Action**: *"For information"* — the memo is non-decisional. ^[extracted]

This is the **canonical FBI-side primary source for the Sputnik-era UFO-reactivation narrative** — the conceptual backbone of [[concepts/sputnik-saucer-reactivation-1957]]. ^[inferred]

## Opening line

> *"Ever since the Russians released 'Sputnik' there has been a great increase in the number of 'flying saucers' and other unidentified flying objects (UFO) reportedly seen by people all over the U. S."* ^[extracted]

The memo is dated **39 days after Sputnik 1's launch (4 Oct 1957)**, **30 days after the Vernal UT 'moon-like object' wave (10 Oct 1957)**, and **9-10 days after the [[concepts/engine-stall-phenomenon|Levelland TX engine-stall wave]] (2-3 Nov 1957)**. ^[inferred]

## Cases enumerated

The memo recapitulates the recent ~6-week saucer surge as Air Force Intelligence had reported it to the **Bureau Liaison Representative**: ^[extracted]

| Date | Location | Reported phenomenon | AF disposition (per memo) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **10 Oct 1957** | Vernal, Utah | Object *"looked like the moon"* trailing *"a blue flame that pulsed like a heart"*; allegedly **landed** | *"AF immediately investigated these reports with negative results, inasmuch as no object was located nor was there any evidence of an object having fallen"* |
| ~late Oct/early Nov 1957 | Nebraska | Object *"landed … with six people aboard"*; persons *"talked to a Nebraska farmer and then sped off into space"* | *"non-authentic"* |
| ~late Oct/early Nov 1957 | Albany GA → Miami FL | *"firey object … flashing across the southern skies"* | *"non-authentic"* |
| ~late Oct/early Nov 1957 | Gulf of Mexico | *"Coast Guard cutter had sighted a huge object flying over"* | *"non-authentic"* |
| **2-3 Nov 1957** | Southwestern states | *"persons … driving their cars have allegedly seen UFO's that caused the engines in their automobiles to stop"* | *"non-authentic"* |

The Southwestern engine-stall description is the **Levelland TX wave**, named euphemistically rather than by location. ^[inferred] Roach is **describing the engine-stall behavior generically** — i.e., as a phenomenon class, not as a single incident — even though the Levelland reports of 2-3 Nov 1957 are the proximate trigger. ^[inferred]

## AF Intelligence disposition (per memo)

> *"all the cases mentioned above have for all practical purposes been resolved as being nonauthentic or there was no basis for the sightings. Investigating details that the Air Force had out in the field investigating these matters have not submitted their complete reports but have advised that they are all more or less negative."* ^[extracted]

Per the memo, AF was **following these sightings closely** and *"all reports are submitted to the [redacted in OCR — likely **Air Technical Intelligence Center**, Wright-Patterson AFB]"*. ^[inferred — AF saucer intake in 1957 was ATIC-routed]

## Why this memo matters

1. **Belmont-level routing.** The memo is to the Assistant Director (Domestic Intelligence Division), not buried at SAC-level — a signal of HQ-level attention to the post-Sputnik wave even though the Bureau's policy of non-investigation remains unchanged. ^[inferred]
2. **The Sputnik attribution is the FBI's own framing.** This is **not an external commentator's framing imported into the file** — it is the Bureau's own opening sentence on the post-Levelland surge. The FBI's analytical lens for what was happening in fall 1957 was **the Russian space launch, not the engine-stall reports**. ^[inferred]
3. **The 11/12/57 AF assurance is anchored here.** The annual saucer-policy review memos (Sanders 392, Donahoe 422, Donahoe 425, Wannall 427) all cite *"Air Force on 11-12-57 assured that Bureau will be advised of any developments in this field of interest to us (62-83894-387)"* — the assurance described in this memo is the Bureau's standing reference for AF commitment for the next five years. ^[extracted] See [[concepts/fbi-saucer-policy-stasis-1958-1962]].
4. **No conclusion drawn.** The memo neither **endorses** the AF's "non-authentic" framing nor **questions** it. The Bureau's posture is **disengaged retransmission**: AF says non-authentic; Bureau records that AF says non-authentic; Bureau remains uninvolved. ^[inferred] This is the file's [[concepts/saucer-as-hoax-default|saucer-as-hoax default]] in operational form, post-Sputnik.

## Frame critique

The memo's framing has **two structural blind spots**: ^[inferred]

1. **The Sputnik-attribution premise is psychological, not phenomenological.** Roach asserts a **causal link** between the Sputnik launch and the saucer-report surge without distinguishing *"more reports because more public attention"* from *"more reports because more sightings"*. ^[inferred] The FBI's stance functionally treats post-Sputnik UFO reports as **derivative artifacts of public excitement** about Soviet space activity — a hypothesis the file never tests. ^[inferred]
2. **The Levelland wave is buried behind the Vernal landing.** Vernal UT (10 Oct 1957) gets a **dedicated paragraph**; Levelland (2-3 Nov 1957) is described **only as a generic phenomenon class** ("UFO's that caused the engines in their automobiles to stop"). ^[inferred] The Levelland wave is structurally the **bigger event** by witness count and physical-effect specificity, but the FBI HQ memo gives it **less narrative weight** than a single-witness no-trace landing report. ^[inferred] Possibly because Vernal had no AF-disconfirming finding yet; possibly because Levelland was seen as too parochial. ^[ambiguous]

The memo **does not name Levelland** as a place — readers in 1957 would have known via newspaper context, but the document itself preserves only the phenomenological description. ^[inferred] This is consistent with the file's broader **toponymic abstraction** when describing controversial events. ^[inferred]

## Cross-references in the file

- **62-83894-121** is referenced in serial 392 for the 9-27-47 Hoover letter — the **birth-chain of [[references/fbi-bureau-bulletin-57-1947|Bureau Bulletin 57]]**. ^[extracted]
- **62-83894-373** is referenced in serial 395 (Wolcott letter, 11 Dec 1958) — the *"Aerial Phenomena Research Club, Bartow, FL"* 1957 referral.
- **62-83894-387** (this memo) becomes the file's **standing reference** for AF commitment to keep Bureau informed.

## Open questions

- **Was an earlier (pre-Sputnik) post-Levelland Roach memo drafted and not retained?** ^[open] The 12 Nov 1957 memo is filed *after* both Vernal and Levelland but before the Donahoe/Sanders policy reviews — so timeline-wise it is the **first HQ-level retained acknowledgement** of the post-Sputnik surge. ^[inferred]
- **Did Belmont annotate the memo?** The OCR shows only a routing checkmark and *"October 28"* in the margin (page 7) — the latter is unexplained in context. ^[ambiguous]
- **What was the AF's actual ATIC-side internal assessment of Levelland?** ^[open] The 11-12-57 memo claims AF dismissed Levelland as *"non-authentic"*, but ATIC's contemporaneous Project Blue Book record on Levelland is more equivocal. ^[inferred — based on standard Blue Book historiography]
