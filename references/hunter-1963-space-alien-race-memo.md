# Hunter 1963 NASC Memo — "Thoughts on the Space Alien Race Question"

A 6-page declassified memorandum dated **18 July 1963**, marked **OFFICIAL USE ONLY**, declassification stamp **NND 937051** (declassified by NARS). Routing slip identifies the originating office as the **National Aeronautics and Space Council** (Executive Office of the President, Washington), with file marker **SP 1-NASC / OSA FILE COPY** and the document number **SP 16**.

The memo is addressed to **Mr. Robert F. Packard, Office of International Scientific Affairs, Department of State, Washington 25, D.C.** and signed at the bottom by **Maxwell W. Hunter, II**, listed as a *Member, Professional Staff* of the [[entities/national-aeronautics-and-space-council|NASC]].

- **Author of the substantive memo:** [[entities/maxwell-w-hunter|Maxwell W. Hunter, II]] (signature block, page 6).
- **Recipient:** [[entities/robert-f-packard|Robert F. Packard]], Office of International Scientific Affairs, Department of State.

## Why this source matters

This memo is the wiki's first **policy-side** UAP artifact. The 1948 [[concepts/project-sign|Project SIGN]] material ([[references/usaf-flying-discs-1948]]) and the modern [[entities/aaro|AARO]]-handled material ([[references/western-us-event-2026]], [[references/usper-statement-2025]]) are **operational** — sighting reports, intake protocols, witness statements. The 1963 NASC memo is something different: a senior staff officer in the **Executive Office of the President** treating "what would US policy be if an extraterrestrial intelligence is confirmed?" as a writeable, archive-worthy question — and forwarding the analysis to **State**, the diplomatic instrument.

The fact that this exists at NASC, addressed to State's international-scientific affairs office, treats the alien-contact question as foreseeably a **diplomatic** problem, not solely a defense or scientific one. ^[inferred]

## Core content

The memo argues:

1. **Scientific consensus 1963:** intelligent life within the solar system is "negligible" probability, but extra-solar intelligent life is now considered scientifically plausible — a reversal from a few decades earlier when the prevailing "stellar near-collision" theory of planetary formation made planets themselves seem rare.
2. **The "flying saucer advocate" foil:** Hunter explicitly distances himself from saucer advocates ("In my own mind, I find it difficult to side with the flying saucer advocates") but also from total scientific dismissal ("the almost total impossibility envisioned by most scientists also is disturbing"). See [[concepts/scientific-vs-saucer-advocate-frame]].
3. **Three policy-relevant classes** of alien race a contact event might surface — "chemical Martians," "nuclear-class" interstellar visitors, and "Einstein-violating" (faster-than-light) civilizations — each with sharply different diplomatic implications. See [[concepts/three-class-alien-taxonomy]].
4. **Hint-evidence hypothetical:** if not for scientific confidence, three then-current observations — hot gases emanating from crater **Alphonsus**, infrared "hot spots" on the lunar surface, and the consistent failure of US/Soviet lunar/planetary probes — could be read as "broad hints of intelligent life on the moon." Hunter offers this as a thought experiment, not a claim. ^[extracted-as-hypothetical]
5. **Diplomatic posture:** in any contact case, "a policy of the immediate burying of all Terrestrial hatchets would likely be in order." This is the memo's clearest policy recommendation.
6. **Realistic conclusion:** no preparation is plausible because "no one of consequence is going to take this rubbish seriously unless it happens. At that point, our policy will be determined in the traditional manner of grand panic."

The full policy framing is captured at [[concepts/space-alien-policy-question]].

## Notable extracted claims

- **NASC discussions:** The question of policy toward an alien intelligence has "occasionally, though rarely, arisen" in NASC deliberations, including specifically during deliberations on **BNSP Task I**.
- **Mars-flight numerology:** Hunter treats space flight from Mars as easier than from Terra — Mars escape speed cited as **16,500 fps**, and lunar braking speed under **10,000 fps** — concluding that even a "normal high energy chemical rocket" could carry "almost 10% of its gross weight in payload" Mars→Moon at favorable launch windows, and "almost 50% payload" on the return.
- **Galactic-colonization timing argument:** at half lightspeed with twenty-year stops every 10 light-years for refueling and colonist deposit, a sustained galactic expansion would take **~200,000 years** to traverse the galaxy — set against the "earliest known remains of man… approximately one million seven-hundred thousand years."
- **Reference to "Ozma":** the memo notes contemporary preference for radio search ("listen with Ozma") over close inspection of Mars's "Canali."
- **No corroborating annexes** in the OCR — the memo is self-contained.
