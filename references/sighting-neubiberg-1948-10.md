# Sighting — Flying Saucer Hovering Over Neubiberg AB ~30 Minutes (~Oct/Nov 1948)

A **referenced observation**, not a primary report. Surfaces as a single sentence inside [[references/usafe-tt1524-1948-11|USAFE TT 1524 Item 14]]:

> *"For some time we have been concerned by the recurring reports on flying saucers. They periodically continue to crop up; during the last week, one was observed hovering over Neubiberg Air Base for about thirty minutes."* ^[extracted]

The sentence frames the substantive content of USAFE 14 (the [[entities/swedish-air-intelligence-service|Swedish Air Intelligence Service]] visit, the [[references/sighting-swedish-lake-crash-1948|Swedish lake crash]]). Treat as **second-order witness data** — the report of record here is USAFE's incidental reference to a Neubiberg observation, not a primary witness statement.

## What the dispatch states

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Date of dispatch | 4 Nov 1948 |
| Date of sighting | "**During the last week**" relative to 4 Nov 1948 — i.e., **~28 Oct – 4 Nov 1948**. ^[inferred] |
| Location | **Neubiberg Air Base** (USAFE installation in Bavaria, near Munich). |
| Observers | **Not named in the dispatch.** ^[ambiguous] |
| Object | "**A flying saucer**" — no shape, color, size, or sound information given. ^[ambiguous] |
| Behavior | **Hovering for about thirty minutes.** ^[extracted] |
| Source classification | TOP SECRET (USAFE 14). |
| USAFE evaluation | None given (the sentence is a tempo-of-reporting item, not a sighting report). ^[inferred] |

## Why the dispatch references it

The Neubiberg sentence is the **operational-tempo justification** for the rest of USAFE 14. The dispatch's argument runs: *(a)* flying-saucer reports are recurring and credible enough to address; *(b)* a 30-minute hover at a USAFE air base last week is the latest example; *(c)* therefore USAFE took the question to the Swedish AIS, who returned the "outside the earth" assessment; *(d)* what does Cabell think? ^[inferred]

The sentence is doing rhetorical work for USAFE's policy ask, but the underlying observation appears to be a real one — USAFE would not have invented a hover-over-its-own-air-base example in a TS dispatch to the D/I. ^[inferred]

## Significance to the corpus

- **Earliest documented "30-minute hover"** of a flying-saucer-class object in the wiki. The 1948 [[references/usaf-flying-discs-1948|AFOIR file]] and 1948–50 [[references/usaf-flying-discs-1949|continuation file]] contain hover-and-go cases of seconds-to-minutes; the 30-minute duration is more than an order of magnitude longer. The closest analogues in the corpus are the 1944–45 [[concepts/foo-fighter-phenomenon|foo-fighter pacing reports]] (multi-minute), the 2026 [[references/sighting-fiery-orb-western-us|Fiery Orb sustained-glow hover]] (~1 minute), and the 2025 [[references/sighting-helicopter-orb-encounter-2025|multi-hour helicopter orb encounter]]. **Long-duration hover is therefore a tracked morphology at least as old as 1948.** ^[inferred]
- **First USAFE-installation flying-saucer datum** in the corpus. The wiki's 1948 disc material is dominated by CONUS field installations + Alaska + a handful of overseas reports; this is the first anchored at a *European-theater* USAF base. ^[inferred]
- **First flying-saucer sighting brought into USAF intelligence via the TS sub-track**, rather than via the AFOIR-CO field-installation reporting protocol. ^[inferred]
- **Operational pretext for USAFE-Swedish liaison contact**: the existence of cases like Neubiberg is what USAFE cites to justify having raised the question with Swedish intelligence. ^[inferred]

## What the dispatch does **not** establish

- The **specific date** of the Neubiberg observation (only a 7-day window). ^[ambiguous]
- The **observers** — single witness, multiple witnesses, ground vs. flight-line vs. tower personnel. ^[ambiguous]
- The **morphology** beyond "flying saucer" — no color, size, structural detail, or motion signature beyond "hovering." ^[ambiguous]
- Whether **GCI / radar** picked up the object during the 30-minute hover. (Material inside USAFE 14 frames the Swedish lake-crash chain in detail but is silent on Neubiberg sensor cross-checks.) ^[ambiguous]
- Whether any **separate report-of-record** was filed at Neubiberg's S-2 office, USAFE A-2, or the AFOIR-CO field-report channel. ^[open]
