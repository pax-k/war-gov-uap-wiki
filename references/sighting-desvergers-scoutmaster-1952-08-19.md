# Sighting — Desvergers Scoutmaster, 19 Aug 1952

A claimed close encounter by **D.S. Desvergers**, a **Boy Scout Master** at West Palm Beach, FL, with a 30-ft hovering saucer and a "red blob" causing loss of consciousness — leading to the **first FBI Laboratory physical-evidence examination of a UAP-claimed object** in the wiki corpus. ^[inferred] Captured in [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-7|FBI HQ 62-HQ-83894 section 7]] serial 315 (page 43) as a 29 Aug 1952 D.J. Parsons → Mr. Harbo memo on the FBI Lab examination of Desvergers's burnt cap. ^[extracted]

## The claim

Per the 26 Aug 1952 Air Force letter to FBI requesting examination ^[extracted]:

> Mr. Desvergers, a scoutmaster at West Palm Beach, Florida ... claims that he observed an unidentified flying object in a desolate area. He reported that upon his investigation, an object 30 feet in diameter hovered over his head and shot a "red blob" which caused him to lose consciousness. The cap has some holes burned in it and it is reported that the hair on Desvergers' arms was singed.

## The FBI Laboratory examination

Conducted by **D.J. Parsons** (FBI Lab), **read aloud to Lt Col Free OSI by telephone at 6:30 PM 29 Aug 1952** ^[extracted]:

> 1. There is no residue which would permit a determination as to the nature of the material which caused the burns in the cap. In addition to the obvious burns, there is one minute burned area which is probably too small to have been caused intentionally but more likely by a small hot ember.
>
> 2. The bill and a large part of the edges of the cap are singed but the singeing is not uniform as would be expected if it had been caused at one time by a single flash of flame. The front edge of the cap bill is more severely singed than other portions of the cap. This condition would not be expected if the source of the flame was directly overhead.
>
> 3. When the cap is observed from the front, the left edge of the insignia and the fold in the blue cloth, there is an absence of singeing noted under the fold suggesting the possibility that the cap was not being worn when the singeing took place. It is noted that this fold "smooths out" when the cap is placed on the head.
>
> RECOMMENDATION: That in response to the request of Colonel Free of the Air Force, it is recommended that he be orally advised of the above.

The Lab's analysis is **structurally consistent with hoax framing**: ^[inferred]

- **Non-uniform burn pattern** rules out a single flash from overhead.
- **Front-edge concentration** rules out a directly-overhead source.
- **Absence of singeing under the fold** suggests the cap was **not being worn** when burned.

Without explicitly calling Desvergers's account a hoax, the Lab's recommendation effectively undermines its physical premises. ^[inferred] **First FBI Laboratory physical-evidence examination of a UAP-claimed object in the wiki corpus** ^[inferred] — predates the [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-serial-438|May 1964 Byrnes Albuquerque/Socorro report]] by ~12 years.

## Institutional context

The case is widely cited in UFO literature as a **Project BLUE BOOK case** of late August 1952 ^[inferred] — Capt Edward J. Ruppelt (ATIC) reportedly investigated personally. The FBI Lab's physical-evidence work product was furnished to OSI USAF (Lt Col Free), per the standard post-[[references/fbi-bureau-bulletin-57-1947|BB-57]] passive-intake-and-AF-handoff protocol. ^[inferred] No further FBI investigation conducted. ^[inferred]

The structural significance: ^[inferred]

- **The Air Force escalated to the FBI Lab for physical-evidence forensic capability** that ATIC apparently lacked or chose to outsource — the event is therefore a **counterexample to the BB-57 "no FBI investigation" framing**: the Bureau did furnish forensic-laboratory analysis on demand.
- **The Bureau's deliverable was oral, not written** — Parsons reads the analysis to Lt Col Free by telephone; no formal written report. ^[extracted] This minimizes paper trail and satisfies the "no investigation conducted by this Bureau" framing while delivering substantive technical analysis. ^[inferred]
- **The case is positioned in the wiki between the [[references/sighting-savannah-river-plant-1952-08-08|Savannah River Plant sighting]] (8 Aug 1952) and [[references/lang-new-yorker-1952-09-06|the Lang New Yorker article]] (6 Sep 1952)** — i.e. it occurs at the **peak of the 1952 wave** and helps explain why Branigan's 22 Aug 1952 memo to Belmont concluded that field offices were **not following the BB-57 referral protocol**: cases like Desvergers were arriving at the Bureau itself for forensic work, not flowing through OSI directly. ^[inferred]
