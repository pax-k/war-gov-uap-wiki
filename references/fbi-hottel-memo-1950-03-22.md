# FBI Hottel Memo on Three Saucers in New Mexico (22 March 1950)

A one-page **Office Memorandum** dated 22 March 1950 from **GUY HOTTEL, SAC, WASHINGTON** ([[entities/fbi|FBI Washington Field Office]]) to the **DIRECTOR, FBI** ^[extracted]. Filed in [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-5|section 5]] of [[entities/fbi-hq-62-83894-file|FBI HQ file 62-HQ-83894]] as **serial 209**, indexed `162-83894-209`. ^[extracted] Subject line: **"FLYING SAUCERS — INFORMATION CONCERNING"**. ^[extracted] Drafted by SA **R. H. Kurtzman**; routing: **RHK:VIM**. ^[extracted]

## What it says

The memo reports a **third-hand chain**: ^[extracted]

> The following information was furnished to SA R. H. KURTZMAN by KARL HOWE, Special Investigator, Sex Squad, Metropolitan Police Department:
>
> An investigator for the Air Forces stated that three so-called flying saucers had been recovered in New Mexico. They were described as being circular in shape with raised centers, approximately 50 feet in diameter. Each one was occupied by three bodies of human shape but only 3 feet tall, dressed in metallic cloth of a very fine texture. Each body was bandaged in a manner similar to the blackout suits used by speed flyers and test pilots.
>
> According to Mr. HOWE'S informant, the saucers were found in New Mexico due to the fact that the Government has a very high-powered radar set-up in that area and it is believed the radar interferes with the controlling mechanism of the saucers.
>
> No further evaluation was attempted by SA KURTZMAN concerning the above.

## Why this memo matters — and why its reading needs care

The Hottel memo is **the most-famous declassified FBI UFO document** ^[inferred] and consequently the most-misread. The naive UFOlogical reading: *"The FBI documented crashed saucers and humanoid bodies in 1950."* The institutional-archival reading is **structurally different**: *"A myth-seed laundered through DC PD into FBI HQ files."* ^[inferred]

### The chain of custody

The memo's **information chain** reads (latest → earliest): ^[extracted]

1. SA R. H. Kurtzman (FBI WFO) writes the memo on 22 Mar 1950
2. Kurtzman heard it from **Karl Howe** (Sex Squad, MPD)
3. Howe heard it from **an unnamed "investigator for the Air Forces"**
4. That AF investigator's source is **unspecified**

Each handoff is uncorroborated, none of the named persons (Howe, the AF investigator) are identified by full role/title beyond MPD-Sex-Squad and "investigator-for-the-Air-Forces" respectively. ^[extracted] **Kurtzman's own institutional position**: *"No further evaluation was attempted ... concerning the above"* ^[extracted] — i.e. the FBI itself **did not investigate the claim**, did not confirm it, did not refer it onward as substantiated. The memo is **filed**, not **acted on**. ^[inferred]

### The synchronous Aztec myth-seed channels

The Hottel memo is **the second of three independent FBI intake channels** for the same myth-seed in March 1950 — see [[concepts/aztec-saucer-myth-1950]]:

| Channel | Date | Serial | Origin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Denver SAC URGENT | 9-10 Mar 1950 | 206 | Unnamed Denver University lecturer, "known to" [[entities/george-koehler\|George Koehler]] |
| **Hottel memo (this page)** | **22 Mar 1950** | **209** | **Karl Howe MPD ← unnamed AF investigator** |
| New Orleans SAC | 31 Mar 1950 | 213 | [[entities/george-koehler\|George Koehler]] → Jefferson Armstrong (Wayne Welch advertising Denver) |

All three channels point at the **same myth-seed** — a southwestern-US recovery of disc(s) with 3-foot humanoid bodies — and at least two (serials 206 and 213) trace explicitly to the **[[entities/george-koehler|Koehler]] / [[entities/silas-newton|Newton]] / [[entities/frank-scully|Scully]]** Denver promoter network. ^[inferred] The Hottel memo's source-chain is **opaque** — Howe/Kurtzman/MPD-Sex-Squad does not place the source explicitly in the Denver network — but the **content alignment** (NM, 50-ft, 3-ft humanoids in metallic-cloth suits) and the **synchronous timing** (~22 Mar, between the Denver lecture of 8-9 Mar and the New Orleans Koehler-pitch of 31 Mar) strongly suggest the same myth-seed routed via a different intake path. ^[inferred]

### The "high-powered radar" causal hypothesis

The memo's *"radar interferes with the controlling mechanism of the saucers"* hypothesis ^[extracted] is **not unique to Howe's source**: it is also present in the **Wyandotte Echo / Rudy Fick / "Coulter" channel** (Jan 1950, [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-5|section 5]] serial 203, OSI 13th District Offutt AFB → DSI HQ USAF TWX) ^[extracted] which describes the same 3-ft humanoid + NM/AZ-border-radar-station + crashed-disc framing **2 months earlier** in a non-FBI-routed channel. ^[inferred] This argues that the radar-interference hypothesis is **part of the original myth-seed package**, not Howe's own embellishment. ^[inferred]

### What the memo is and is not

| The Hottel memo IS | The Hottel memo IS NOT |
|---|---|
| A real, declassified, primary FBI document | An FBI investigation of a saucer crash |
| Evidence that the Aztec myth-seed reached FBI HQ via 3+ channels by 22 Mar 1950 | Evidence that saucers crashed in New Mexico |
| Filed in the topic-anchored 62-HQ-83894 accumulator file | Filed in any field-office active-investigation case file |
| Subject of "no further evaluation" by the receiving agent | Followed up with corroborating interviews |
| Chronologically synchronous with [[entities/donald-keyhoe\|Keyhoe]] *True* Jan 1950 + [[entities/frank-scully\|Scully]] *Variety* Jan 1950 | Independent of the [[entities/donald-keyhoe\|Keyhoe]] / [[entities/frank-scully\|Scully]] press cycle |
| A document about post-cooperation-regime saucer-publicity | A document about a real recovery |

The most-economical reading: **the Hottel memo documents the institutional intake of a press-era myth-seed**, not a recovered-saucer claim that the Bureau took seriously. ^[inferred] This reading aligns with the [[references/fbi-boggs-hearn-discontinuation-memo-1950-03-28|Boggs-Hearn memo]] (~6 days later) which explicitly frames the Mar 1950 saucer-reporting wave as **"an outgrowth of recent magazine articles"** ^[extracted].
