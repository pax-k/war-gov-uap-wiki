# FBI Boggs-Hearn Saucer-Discontinuation Memo (28 March 1950)

A one-page **Office Memorandum** dated 28 March 1950 from [[entities/d-m-ladd|D. M. Ladd]] (Assistant Director) to **The Director** (Hoover), routed through Hoover's hand-marked **DO-7 routing slip** ^[extracted]. The memo answers Hoover's hand-routed inquiry — *"Just what are the facts re 'flying saucers'? A short memo as to whether it is true or just what air force etc. think of them"* ^[extracted] — and is filed in [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-5|section 5]] of [[entities/fbi-hq-62-83894-file|FBI HQ file 62-HQ-83894]] as **serial 212**, indexed `162-83894-212`. ^[extracted]

## What it says

[[entities/s-w-reynolds|SA S. W. Reynolds]] obtained the answer the same day (28 March 1950) from **Major Boggs** and **Lt Col J. V. Hearn** of [[entities/usaf|Air Force Intelligence]]. ^[extracted] The memo's two key paragraphs: ^[extracted]

> The Air Force discontinued their intelligence project to determine what flying saucers are the latter part of last year. They publicly announced to the press in December, 1949, that the project had been discontinued. They advised that the press release had been concurred in by the Army and Navy. The reason for the discontinuance, according to Major Boggs and Lieutenant Colonel Hearn, was that after two years of investigation over three-fourths of the incidents regarding flying saucers proved to be misidentifications of a wide variety of conventional items such as lighted weather balloons and other air-borne objects.

> Colonel Hearn pointed out that the Commanders of the various areas are charged with the security of those areas. Reports concerning flying saucers received at this time will be investigated by the Area Commander and his report submitted to the Air Force Intelligence Division as an intelligence item.

> Major Boggs and Lieutenant Colonel Hearn made the observation that many of the reported sightings of flying saucers at this time appear to be an outgrowth of recent magazine articles. They reiterated that the Air Force is conducting no active investigation to determine whether flying saucers exist or what they might happen to be.

The memo concludes with the **paradigm-defining institutional back-reference**: ^[extracted]

> You will recall that the investigation of flying saucers was discontinued by the Bureau in October, 1947, in order that the Air Force could take over such investigations. (62-83894-141-160)

The memo carries a **17-recipient routing-acknowledgment block** with `☑` marks against Tolson, Clegg, Glavin, Ladd, Nichols, Rosen, Tracy, Egan, Gurney, Harte, Mohr, "Petroligator", Quinn Tamm, Tele Room, Nease, Holmes, and Gandy — the entire FBI HQ executive layer. ^[extracted]

## Why it matters

This memo is **the single most institutionally consequential document** in [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-5|section 5]] of 62-HQ-83894 ^[inferred] for four interlocking reasons:

### 1. Both agencies are now formally exited

Together with [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-1|section 1]] (BB42 opens FBI investigation, 30 Jul 1947) and [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-2|section 2]] (Garrett admission ~22 Sep 1947 + Schulgen formal denial 5 Sep 1947), this memo closes the institutional arc: ^[inferred]

| 30 Jul 1947 | [[references/fbi-bureau-bulletin-42-1947\|Bureau Bulletin No. 42]] codifies the cooperation regime | section 1 |
| 5 Sep 1947 | Schulgen formal denial letter to Hoover | [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-2\|section 2]] serial 84 |
| ~22 Sep 1947 | [[entities/g-d-garrett\|Garrett]] admits AAF Intelligence cannot solve the saucer problem internally | section 2 serial 86 |
| **~Oct 1947** | **FBI internal termination** of saucer investigation ([[references/fbi-bureau-bulletin-57-1947\|Bureau Bulletin No. 57]], 1 Oct 1947) ^[inferred] | **62-83894-141-160 (queued section 3)** ^[open] |

The arc is structurally complete in just **~32 months**. ^[inferred] The cooperation regime negotiated as the documentary infrastructure for the FBI's UAP role is **dead** before the Bureau's third sustained UAP wave (1952's Washington-flap) even begins. ^[inferred]

### 2. The "(62-83894-141-160)" parenthetical is the single biggest open thread

This back-reference points at **20 contiguous serials within the same file** (62-HQ-83894) ^[extracted] — i.e. serials 141-160 are physically inside queued section 3 or 4 (between section 2's endpoint at serial 100 and section 5's start at serial 186). ^[inferred] These serials contain **the Bureau's internal October 1947 decision-making** on terminating saucer investigation, almost certainly including the **Bureau Bulletin No. 57 (1 Oct 1947)** referenced in [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-5|section 5]] serial 206 (Denver SAC URGENT teletype reply, 10 Mar 1950) ^[extracted]. ^[inferred]

The decision itself is structurally consequential: it formally terminates the [[concepts/fbi-aaf-saucer-cooperation-1947|FBI-AAF cooperation regime]] **only ~3 months after** the Garrett admission and Schulgen formal denial — i.e. the regime that section 1 spent 52 serials negotiating and section 2 spent 47 serials operationalizing, the FBI **shut down internally within ~6 weeks of the AAF's institutional pivot** away from the secret-project hypothesis. ^[inferred]

### 3. The "magazine articles" attribution

Boggs and Hearn's observation that *"many of the reported sightings of flying saucers at this time appear to be an outgrowth of recent magazine articles"* ^[extracted] is a **paradigm-defining institutional posture** ^[inferred]: it explicitly **frames the post-cooperation-regime saucer-reporting wave as press-induced**, not phenomenologically real. The "recent magazine articles" referenced are: ^[inferred]

- **[[entities/donald-keyhoe|Donald Keyhoe]]'s January 1950 *True Magazine* article** *"The Flying Saucers Are Real"* (and its September 1950 expanded book of the same title published by Fawcett, also referenced in [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-5|section 5]] serial 239) ^[extracted];
- **[[entities/frank-scully|Frank Scully]]'s January 1950 *Variety Magazine* article** (precursor to his September 1950 book *Behind the Flying Saucers*) ^[extracted];
- the **[[concepts/aztec-saucer-myth-1950|Aztec/Scully crashed-saucer myth]]** circulating through Denver oilman networks since November 1949 ^[inferred].

The Boggs-Hearn framing is **synchronous** with the [[entities/donald-keyhoe|Keyhoe]] / [[entities/frank-scully|Scully]] press cycle — i.e. the AF Intelligence interpretation in March 1950 is that the public saucer-attention is **a press-and-magazine phenomenon**, not a renewed AF intelligence problem. ^[inferred]

### 4. The "Area Commander as intelligence-item collector" reorganization

Hearn's institutional-procedural statement — *"Reports concerning flying saucers received at this time will be investigated by the Area Commander and his report submitted to the Air Force Intelligence Division as an intelligence item"* ^[extracted] — describes a **third-tier post-GRUDGE reporting protocol**: not the centralized [[concepts/flying-disc-reporting-protocol|10-element / 50-field AIRMEM #4 EEI]] of [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-serial-164|Cabell's February 1949 directive]], not the [[concepts/fsr-200-4-reporting-regulation|Flight Service Regulation 200-4]] direct-to-AMC routing — but **decentralized to area commanders**, with HQ-USAF intel only receiving incidental items rather than systematic input. ^[inferred] This is the formal institutional substrate for what becomes Project [[concepts/project-grudge|GRUDGE]]'s **reduced-but-not-dead "passive" mode** through 1951 (and the precursor to the eventual Project BLUE BOOK 1952 reactivation). ^[inferred]
