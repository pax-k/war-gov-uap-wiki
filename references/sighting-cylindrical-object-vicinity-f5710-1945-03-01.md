# Sighting — Cylindrical Object, Vicinity F-5710 (1 March 1945)

A daytime sighting filed alongside the WWII "[[concepts/foo-fighter-phenomenon|foo fighter]]" night-phenomena correspondence in [[references/foo-fighters-shaef-1944-1945|SHAEF file 37153]], structurally distinct from the lights-following-aircraft class.

## What the pilots reported

From the 1 March 1945 SHAEF Staff Message Control INCOMING MESSAGE (TOR 012100A March, signed by Maj Gen E. R. **Quesada**, CG IX TAC, addressed to CG SHAEF Main attn A-2 and CG Ninth AF Adv attn A-2): ^[extracted]

> *"Pilots report the following.*
>
> *An aluminum colored cylinder shaped object, about 12 ft long and 1 ft in diameter was observed floating in the air at 9,000 ft. It appeared to be suspended vertically with small fins and a mast projecting from the lower end. The object was attacked and partially deflated, a red flame resulted without smoke. The cylinder did not disintegrate.*
>
> *Photo taken by 107 SQ of 67 TAC/R Group at 011030 hours via F-5710."* ^[extracted]

A SHAEF Air Staff message of 4 March 1945 (signal AI.320) to Air Ministry, USSTAF, 2nd TAF, 1st TAC AF, HQ IXth TAC (rep'd 9th Air Force) re-transmitted the incident under the originator's text "RECEIVED FROM H.Q. NINTH TAC AIR COMMAND DATED 1 MARCH" and asked for photos to be forwarded to Air Ministry, USSTAF and A.C.I.U. with full pilot reports, and *"any further instances also to be reported"*. ^[extracted]

## What happened to the photographs

On 5 March 1945 (signal 051102A), HQ IX TAC reported back to SHAEF Air Staff under the same SHAEF reference: *"PHOTOS OF LONG CYLINDRICAL OBJECTS TAKEN BY 67TH TAC/R PILOTS 011030 VICINITY F-5710 WERE UNSUCCESSFUL, FULL WRITTEN REPORT WILL FOLLOW."* ^[extracted] On 14 March 1945, A-2 RECCE (Lt Col T. G. Ames, SHAEF) confirmed back to 1st US Tac AF that the photos *"proved to be unsuccessful."* ^[extracted]

The full written pilot report referenced in IX TAC's signal is **not in the bundle**. ^[extracted]

## Candidate explanations

- **[[concepts/flak-bombe-hypothesis|Flak Bombe]]** — A.D.I.(K)'s 17 February 1945 reply ("this probably refers to 'Flak Bombe' mentioned in A.D.I.(K) Report No. 562/1944, paragraphs 128 and 129") most plausibly targets *this* sighting class rather than the lights-following-aircraft class — the cylindrical morphology with fins and a mast resembles a weapon system more than a manoeuvring light. ^[inferred]
- **Barrage balloon component / payload** — geometry (vertical 12 ft × 1 ft cylinder with fins, mast at lower end) is at least roughly consistent with a known WWII airborne ordnance payload. The bundle does not name this candidate, however. ^[inferred]
- **DDI2's later reply** ("Me 262 / flak rockets / still something of a mystery") does not address the cylindrical-object class specifically. ^[extracted]

## Why this report is unusual

Three features are hard to reconcile with conventional WWII enemy weaponry: ^[inferred]

1. **Static vertical attitude at 9,000 ft** — most German anti-aircraft munitions are either ascending under power or falling under gravity at this altitude, not suspended vertically.
2. **Partial deflation under fire without disintegration** — implies a non-rigid envelope (balloon-like) rather than a metallic shell.
3. **Red flame on damage, no smoke** — atypical of a fuelled or explosive payload.

These three together are most consistent with a **gas-filled tethered or free-floating balloon** of unknown provenance, but the file does not pursue that hypothesis. ^[inferred]

## Place in the larger UAP picture

This is the **earliest documented "shoot-at-the-UAP" engagement event** in the wiki — predating the [[references/sighting-helicopter-orb-encounter-2025|2025 helicopter encounter]] by 80 years. The structural template — visual contact, weapons engagement, partial damage observed, no disintegration, photographic evidence attempted and lost — is the same template that recurs in modern reports.
