# Sighting — Oak Ridge / Adcock Radar Sequence (1-8 March 1950)

A **9-day multi-agency radar episode** at the Oak Ridge AEC critical facility (Tennessee) ^[extracted], 1 through 8 March 1950, during which **Stuart E. Adcock** — owner-operator of radio station **WROL Knoxville** — reported via a home-modified surplus APN-7 radar set the detection of an unidentified object circling at altitudes between 40,000 and 100,000 feet over the AEC reservation. ^[extracted] Filed in [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-5|FBI HQ 62-HQ-83894 section 5]] across **serials 215-219** (W. T. Brooking and A. H. Belmont memos, 3-14 March 1950) ^[inferred]; a parallel Department-of-the-Army Staff Communications Office TWX trail (serials 210-211) preserves the same event from the **3rd Army CIC / 14th Air Force / G-2 channel**. ^[extracted]

This is the **first radar-tracked critical-facility UAP event** in the wiki FBI corpus. ^[inferred] No prior radar+critical-facility coordination episode appears in the existing FBI material.

| **USAF OSI** | SA W. M. Price (Knoxville liaison); Capt Robert Cross (NEPA radar expert); 14th Air Force HQ Greenville SC |
| **NEPA Oak Ridge Engineering** | Col C. D. Gasser (Engineering Officer USAF Material Command NEPA Site); Capt Robert Cross |
| **Naval Reserve** | LCDR G. R. Shacklett (CO U.S. Naval Reserve Training Center, Alcoa Highway Knoxville) |
| **CIA** | Promised radar technician from Washington; **never actually arrived** at Oak Ridge during the 9-day window ^[extracted] |
| **14th Air Force** | Two representatives arrived in Knoxville 8 Mar 1950 — *"did not feel it sufficiently important for them to remain"* ^[extracted] |

This is the **broadest inter-agency UAP coordination in the wiki FBI corpus** through 1950. ^[inferred] The federation involves 7 distinct agencies + the AEC as the protected facility — but **no agency actually takes investigative responsibility**: the FBI is in observer mode, CIC + OSI are in observer mode, AEC defers to military, the Air Force engineers find the equipment unreliable.

## The "dry-run" closing analysis

The closing-narrative paragraph from the multi-page investigative report (paginated 6-7 of 7 in the archived bundle) ^[extracted]:

> It would appear from the manner in which this incident was handled that despite the fact that all agencies seemed to be operating in the manner prescribed by agreement, and although each was apprised of the action being taken by the other and cooperating in every respect, there nevertheless seemed to be an impressive lack of any agency actually taking the responsibility for the situation and taking any action to verify or disprove the threat. Had a similar incident occurred wherein an actual threat against the physical security of the AEC Installation had been experienced, it is not implausible to believe that a similar confused fixing of final authority would have been found. ... While it is now felt that this entire matter was in a manner of speaking "dry run", it nevertheless warrants some consideration from a procedural standpoint and might be worthy of discussion at some liaison meeting in the future.

This is **structurally significant**: ^[inferred] the FBI Knoxville Liaison Agent's narrative explicitly characterizes the episode as a **"dry-run failure mode"** for AEC-facility air-defense coordination — i.e. **even with no real threat, the inter-agency response was procedurally confused**. The paragraph reads as an institutional lesson-learned, not as a sighting analysis. ^[inferred] The episode's ultimate value to the corpus is its documentation of the **post-cooperation-regime non-investigation posture**: with [[concepts/fbi-aaf-saucer-cooperation-1947|the FBI-AAF cooperation regime]] dead since October 1947 + [[concepts/project-grudge|GRUDGE]] terminated December 1949, the inter-agency coordination on a critical-facility radar event has no clear lead agency. ^[inferred]

## Phenomenology

The reported object characteristics: ^[extracted]

| Aspect | Adcock's description |
|---|---|
| **Altitude** | 40,000 ft (1 Mar) → **100,000 ft** (2-4 Mar) |
| **Range** | ~18 mi from Knoxville home, directly over Oak Ridge |
| **Bearing** | 335-340° |
| **Behavior** | Circling for extended periods; final departures eastward at "200 mph" |
| **Intensity** | "Strongest pip yet" on 4 Mar; "density similar to DC-3" per Army TWX ^[extracted] |
| **Verification** | None — Naval Reserve radar negative; Air Force Capt Cross unable to verify |
| **Equipment** | Surplus APN-7 (US Navy WW2 radar altimeter, K-band) modified for ground-based use; antenna home-built ^[extracted] |
| **Operator credibility** | Skilled radio technician (per WROL Knoxville colleagues); some-degree-of-inebriation noted on at least 2 of 9 days; inconsistent with Cross's radar-theory expectations ^[extracted] |

The **100,000-foot altitude** is structurally implausible for the early 1950 timeframe: ^[inferred] Col Gasser's NEPA assessment — *"while it was highly improbable that aircraft could sustain flight at an altitude of 100,000 feet, it was absolutely possible from a scientific and engineering standpoint"* ^[extracted] — implicitly admits that *no known aircraft* operated at that altitude in March 1950. The Naval Reserve radar's failure to corroborate, plus the surplus-APN-7's known calibration drift when modified, supports the most-economical reading: **equipment artifact + operator alcohol → spurious "pip" misinterpreted as object**. ^[inferred]

## Why this sighting matters

1. **First radar-tracked critical-facility UAP event in the FBI corpus.** ^[inferred]
2. **Broadest inter-agency coordination of a single radar episode** through 1950.
3. **Documents the dry-run procedural-failure mode** — i.e. the post-cooperation-regime non-investigation posture in operational practice.
4. **Captures the FBI's "observer-only no-investigation" protocol** in writing as a Belmont-direct instruction (3 Mar 1950) ^[extracted].
5. **The "CIA radar technician promised but never arrived"** is a structural observation of inter-agency-coordination breakdown ^[inferred].
