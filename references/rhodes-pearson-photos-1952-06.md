# Rhodes / Pearson Photograph Chain-of-Custody Dispute — June 1952

A three-week documentary trail in [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-6|FBI HQ 62-HQ-83894 section 6]] (serials 280-282, 4-17 June 1952) over a **chain-of-custody dispute** between **Dr. William A. Rhodes** (Phoenix saucer-photographer, 7 July 1947) and the **FBI**, mediated through **Drew Pearson**'s TV-program staff (Fred Blumenthal) ^[extracted]. The dispute concerns Rhodes's allegation that *"the FBI borrowed his negatives of the flying discs and when asked for their return the FBI told Rhodes the negatives were not available."* ^[extracted] The chain is the **first FBI-corpus chain-of-custody dispute over UAP photographic evidence** ^[inferred].

## Timeline

### 4 June 1952 — Blumenthal → Nichols inquiry

**11:50 AM**: Fred Blumenthal (Drew Pearson's office) calls FBI and talks with Wick ^[extracted]:

> Pearson has been in contact with Dr. W. A. Rhodes (Phoenix telephone number 5-0048) concerning photographs which the above captioned took of flying discs in the Phoenix area in July of 1947 … Blumenthal states that Dr. Rhodes told Pearson today that the FBI borrowed his negatives of the flying discs and when asked for their return the FBI told Rhodes the negatives were not available. Blumenthal asked whether there is any truth to this statement of Rhodes and what the FBI knew about him.

Pearson's interest: *"using the photographs on his [TV] program."* ^[extracted]

L. B. Nichols → Tolson office memorandum (4 June 1952) cites Bureau File **62-83894, Serials 80, 184, and 225** — i.e. the Rhodes case has been **on file in 62-83894 for 5 years**. ^[extracted]

### 8 June 1952 — Pearson TV broadcast

Drew Pearson's TV program of 8 June 1952 displays *"flying disc photographs"* ^[extracted] — but whether these are the **Rhodes photographs** or different images is **unresolved at the time of the FBI inquiry**. ^[extracted]

### 10 June 1952 — Branigan → Belmont memo

W. A. Branigan recommends that **Bureau Liaison contact OSI** to determine *"whether the photographs displayed by Pearson on his television program of June 8, 1952, are those referred to in the captioned case."* ^[extracted] Branigan's purpose: *"to recommend that Bureau Liaison contact OSI to determine whether photographs allegedly taken by the captioned individual of a flying disc in 1947 were furnished by OSI to the office of Drew Pearson."* ^[extracted]

### 17 June 1952 — V. P. Keay → Belmont follow-up

Keay reports the institutional follow-up ^[extracted]:

> Lieutenant Colonel Leroy Barnard and Lieutenant Colonel E. M. Neville of OSI were both contacted and both advised that no inquiries had been received by OSI from Blumenthal.
>
> The Bureau's Air Force Liaison Representative then contacted Colonel C. M. Young of Air Force Intelligence (A-2) who likewise advised that no inquiries had been received by the Office of the Director of Intelligence from [Blumenthal].

**Result**: Three institutional touch-points (OSI Barnard, OSI Neville, AF Intelligence Young) **all deny receiving any inquiry from Blumenthal**. ^[extracted]

## The Rhodes 1947 case (referenced)

Rhodes is the photographer of the **7 July 1947 Phoenix flying-disc photographs** ^[extracted] — the case originally documented at:

- **62-83894-80** (cited as 4-page Phoenix Office report) ^[extracted].
- **62-83894-184** (1949 follow-on) ^[extracted].
- **62-83894-225** (1950 follow-on) ^[extracted].

The 4 June 1952 Nichols memo recaps the Phoenix Office's handling ^[extracted]:

> On the morning of August 30, 1947, Rhodes delivered the negatives to the Phoenix Office at which time they were accepted with the understanding they were being given to Mr. Fugate, a representative of the Army Air Force Intelligence, United States Army, and that there was little, if any, chance of his getting the negatives back. Mr. Rhodes turned the negatives over to the FBI with the full understanding they were being given to the Air Force and he would not get them back. (62-83894-80, page 4)

The dispute thus reveals **a factual inconsistency**: Rhodes's claim to Pearson that *"the FBI told Rhodes the negatives were not available"* contradicts the **1947 documented arrangement** that Rhodes voluntarily surrendered the negatives **with full knowledge they would not be returned**. ^[inferred]

The wiki's [[entities/fbi-hq-62-83894-file|FBI HQ 62-83894 entity page]] previously documented the **George Fugate / A-2 Fourth Air Force / Hamilton Field** mediated handover and the [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-2|section 2 Hoover Fugate-block teletype]] of 30 August 1947 (serial 68) — i.e. the historical chain is fully consistent in the FBI's institutional record. ^[inferred]

### 17 April 1950 followup

The Nichols memo also records ^[extracted]:

> On April 17, 1950, Mr. Rhodes advised the Phoenix Office that True Magazine would like to secure the photographs which he had made available to Air Force Intelligence. At that time Rhodes was referred to OSI, Fourth Air Force Base, San Francisco, California, since they had been made available to this agency by the Phoenix Office on August 30, 1947.

I.e. Rhodes has been **continuously trying to recover the negatives** since 17 April 1950 — through True Magazine (1950) and now through Drew Pearson (1952). The June 1952 dispute is the **third documented Rhodes recovery attempt** ^[inferred].

## OSI's 8 June 1949 letter

A separate OSI engagement: *"By letter dated June 8, 1949, the Phoenix Office advised the Bureau that on the previous day Mr. Lynn C. Aldrich, OSI representative, Phoenix, Arizona, called the Phoenix Office stating he had been requested by his superiors in San Francisco to obtain detailed information concerning the negatives since Rhodes had requested their return. Aldrich pointed out that Fugate's recollection on the matter was 'hazy.'"* ^[extracted]

The 8 June 1949 OSI inquiry confirms that **Fugate's 1947 handover documentation was incomplete** ^[extracted] — i.e. the chain-of-custody **had a known gap on the AF / OSI side from 1947 onward**, with Rhodes leveraging that institutional gap into a recurring recovery effort.

## Pearson, Blumenthal, and "the Mr. Wick conversation"

The 4 June 1952 Nichols memo also records (from a separate Belmont 17 Jun 1952 followup) ^[extracted]:

> Blumenthal was most appreciative for receiving this information and said he would contact the office of Special Investigations of the Air Force for what assistance they might be to him. He said also he would straighten out Rhodes, informing him the FBI only as a courtesy to the Air Force representatives received the negatives from him and transmitted them.

This is **the closure** of the dispute from the FBI's side ^[inferred]: Blumenthal accepts the institutional explanation, **commits to clarifying Rhodes's narrative**, and redirects to OSI. The FBI's institutional posture: **clarify-the-record passive intervention** — no investigative action beyond fact-confirmation against the 1947 chain-of-custody record.

## Institutional significance

The dispute is structurally distinctive in the FBI corpus ^[inferred]:

1. **First chain-of-custody dispute** over UAP photographic evidence in the FBI corpus.
2. **First documented Drew Pearson UAP engagement** in the FBI corpus — predating the [[references/drew-pearson-saucers-nil-1950-11-25|November 1950 Drew Pearson confidential-AF-files column]] in sub-file A by ~3 years on the FBI-engagement side, although Pearson's earlier 1950 column came earlier in the press. ^[inferred] The contrast: in 1950 Pearson published a column citing confidential AF files (saucers-nil framing); in 1952 Pearson is **actively soliciting** the Rhodes photographs for TV display.
3. **First documented Press-personality → FBI inquiry on UAP photographic evidence**.
4. **First inter-agency OSI + AF Intelligence joint denial** of a press-inquiry receipt — both Barnard and Neville (OSI) and Young (AF Intelligence A-2) deny Blumenthal contact. This **does not preclude** Blumenthal contact through other channels, but rules out the **standard inter-agency routing**. ^[inferred]
5. **Pattern of recurring photographic-evidence-recovery attempts** by a single 1947 photographer-witness — Rhodes's 1949 (Aldrich OSI), 1950 (True Magazine), and 1952 (Pearson TV) attempts span 5 years.
6. **First institutional documentation of the gap between Rhodes's reported narrative and the FBI's recorded narrative** — Rhodes claims FBI custody+denial; FBI record shows voluntary surrender to AF via FBI conduit, with knowledge of non-return. ^[inferred] The wiki's institutional read: Rhodes's narrative had **drifted** over time in the direction of FBI-as-detainer rather than AF-as-recipient.
