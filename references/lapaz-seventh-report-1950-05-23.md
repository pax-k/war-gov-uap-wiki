# LaPaz, Anomalous Luminous Phenomena (Seventh Report) — 23 May 1950

A 5-page scientific report by **Dr. Lincoln LaPaz**, Director of the **Institute of Meteoritics, University of New Mexico**, addressed to **Lt Col Doyle Rees**, Commanding Officer of the **17th District OSI** (Kirtland AFB), dated **23 May 1950**. ^[extracted] The seventh in LaPaz's series of reports on the New Mexico green-fireball phenomena. ^[extracted] Filed verbatim inside [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-6|FBI HQ 62-HQ-83894 section 6]] as part of the **17th District OSI 25 May 1950 NM Sightings Summary** enclosure to the [[references/belmont-ladd-aerial-phenomena-nm-1950-08-21|21 August 1950 Belmont → Ladd memo]] (serial 246). ^[extracted] The report is the **definitive scientific articulation** of the green-fireball-distinct-from-meteor framework in the wiki corpus ^[inferred] and the **scientific predicate** for [[concepts/project-twinkle|Project Twinkle]]. ^[inferred]

## The 11-criteria taxonomy

The Seventh Report consolidates LaPaz's earlier analytical work into **eleven specific differences** between the December 1948 NM green fireballs and typical meteors ^[extracted]:

1. **Horizontal nature of paths.** *"The horizontal nature of the paths of most of the December fireballs is most unusual. Genuine meteors are rarely observed to move in horizontal paths."*
2. **Very low altitude.** *"The very low height of the December fireball … sets it off in sharp contrast from the genuine meteors for which heights of the order of 40 or more miles are normally observed."*
3. **Sub-meteor velocity.** *"The velocity determined for the fireball of December 12 is much less than the velocities determined from typical meteors (and yet is considerably greater than the speeds of the V-2 Rockets or jet planes or of conventional flares)."*
4. **Noiselessness.** *"In the case of meteorites that penetrate to as low levels as that determined for the fireball of December 12, the observed luminous phenomena are always accompanied by very violent noises. No noises whatever have been observed in connection with the various December fireballs so far investigated."*
5. **No progressive brightening.** *"Genuine meteors normally show remarkable variations in brightness, beginning as fine thin hair lines, which are scarcely visible to the observer, and then brightening up to flash out nearly all at once."* The green fireballs do not.
6. **Pronounced north-half-of-sky approach bearing.** *"In the case of genuine meteors the paths are directed toward all points of the compass with equal frequency. On the contrary in the case of the green fireballs, plots of admissible approach sectors show that there is a very pronounced tendency for the paths to come in from the north half of the sky."*
7. **Curious meteor-shower temporal association.** *"The three groups of anomalous greenish luminous phenomena show a curious association with well known meteor showers, although none of these meteor showers normally produce extremely bright green fireballs … This relationship might indicate an attempt to render the green fireballs less conspicuous by causing them to appear only when there is considerable meteoric activity."* ^[extracted]
8. **Copper-salt blowpipe-flame spectrum (5218Å).** *"The remarkably vivid green color reported for most of the December fireballs is rarely observed in the case of genuine meteors. By laboratory test this peculiar color seems to be identical with that given off by copper salts in the blowpipe flame. If this identification is correct, the wave length of the radiation from the green fireballs is near λ=5218Å."*
9. **Intermediate duration.** *"The duration estimates of between 2 and 3 seconds reported for the green fireballs are considerably longer than those (0.4 - 0.5 seconds) for the ordinary visual meteors, but shorter than the duration estimates invariably reported in the case of a genuine meteorite fall (5+ seconds)."*
10. **Horizontal infall direction (preserved from a later memo).** Vertical infall is rarely observed for genuine meteor falls — but horizontal motion is also rarely observed for meteors, leaving the **trajectory geometry doubly anomalous**. ^[extracted]
11. **Time-distribution maximum at 2030 local time.** *"An analysis just completed of the time distribution of the green fireballs so far observed permits us to add an 11th item to the list of differences … The graph of frequency versus local time which accompanies the present report shows that the maximum frequency of sighting of green fireballs (occurring at approximately 2030) coincides in time with neither the frequency maximum for ordinary meteors (occurring at approximately 0300) nor the frequency maximum for meteorite falls (occurring at approximately 1600)."* ^[extracted]

The 11-criteria taxonomy is the **most elaborated single-document framework** for distinguishing UAP-class phenomena from prosaic explanations in the wiki corpus prior to the Project Blue Book scientific advisory era ^[inferred]. The eight extracted in the report's text (criteria 1-9, 11) plus the inferred-implied criterion 10 yield a **cumulative-incompatibility argument**: each criterion alone might admit a meteoric explanation; **all eleven together do not**. ^[inferred]

## The Urals-missile hypothesis

The Seventh Report introduces the **Urals-launch interpretation** in paragraph 5 ^[extracted]:

> Some significance may attach to the fact that the time interval alluded to in paragraph 4 extends from about 7 a.m. to 1 p.m. in the Ural region of the USSR. Since missiles moving with the velocities of the order of those found for the green fireballs for which real path determinations have been possible would travel from the southern Urals to New Mexico in less than 15 minutes, a possible interpretation [is] guided missiles launched from bases in the Urals.

The argument structure: ^[inferred]

- LaPaz's velocity determinations for the December 1948 fireballs are **greater than V-2 rockets or jets** but **less than typical meteors** (criterion 3) — consistent with **guided-missile velocities**.
- The **2030 local NM time = 0700-1300 Urals time** (criterion 11) — i.e. the **Urals daytime working window** — coinciding with the maximum observation frequency.
- A missile travelling at the determined fireball velocities would **traverse the southern-Urals-to-NM distance in <15 minutes** — operationally plausible.
- The **north-of-sky approach bearings** (criterion 6) are **consistent with a polar-trajectory Urals-launched missile** terminating over NM critical facilities. ^[inferred]
- The argument is offered as **"a possible interpretation"** rather than a strong claim ^[extracted].

## LaPaz's recommendations

LaPaz's recommendations are the **operational predicate for [[concepts/project-twinkle|Project Twinkle]]** ^[inferred]:

| Component | Lead | Method |
|---|---|---|
| **Dual-station optical instrumentation** | Roswell + Sacramento NM | photographic / triangulation |
| **Radar investigation** | Dr. L. A. Manning (Stanford) + Dr. Millman | active radar tracking |
| **Ground search / dust collection** | Dr. William Crozier (New Mexico School of Mines) | physical-residue analysis |

The recommendations are quoted from LaPaz's **20 February 1950 letter to Dr P. H. Wyckoff** (Chief, Atmospheric Physics Laboratory, Base Directorate for Geophysical Research) ^[extracted]. The 20 Feb 1950 letter also documents LaPaz's **personal stance shift**: ^[extracted]

> As a result of this comprehensive review … I feel compelled to write you in somewhat different terms concerning my own part in the proposed fireball project than I had in mind when we last discussed this matter. In brief, I have come to the conclusion that, on the basis of the evidence now available to me, I would not be justified in recommending a fireball project. In my opinion, this evidence proves conclusively that the fireballs reported on fall into one of two categories: Those of the first category (the majority) are meteorite falls of unusual, but certainly not of impossible, magnitude, frequency and other characteristics; those of the second category (the minority) are U. S. guided missiles undergoing tests in the neighborhoods of the sensitive installations they are designed to defend.

This is **LaPaz's two-category interpretation** ^[extracted]: ~50% meteoric + ~50% **U.S. guided missiles tested near sensitive installations**. The **Urals-missile** alternative is offered as the fallback hypothesis if the US-missile-testing interpretation is **incorrect**. ^[extracted]

LaPaz repeats his offer to serve as **voluntary consultant** *"rather than on the $40 per day contract specified in Major Oder's letter [29 November 1949]"*. ^[extracted]

## The Datil NM 24-25 February 1950 photograph

The 17th District OSI summary records **Sighting No. 175** ^[extracted]:

> Photograph of Unknown Aerial Phenomena taken at Datil, New Mexico by Cpl Lertis E. Stanfield, Holloman Air Force Base, New Mexico on 24 and 25 Feb 1950. An analysis of the above photograph was made by Dr. Lincoln LaPaz, Head of the Institute of Meteoritics, University of New Mexico, Albuquerque, New Mexico, who reached the following conclusions:
>
> a. The angular diameter of the perfectly round luminous object Stanfield observed was approximately 1/4 of a degree.
>
> b. The angular velocity of the object in the sky was greater than half a degree per minute.
>
> Dr. LaPaz stated that on the basis of the results (a) and (b) above, the object seen by Stanfield was not the moon (for the angular diameter is too small), it was not Venus or any other planet (for the angular diameter was too large), and it was not a bright fixed star slightly out of focus (for the observed rate of motion is double that due to the diurnal rotation of the earth).

LaPaz's **astrometric ruling-out exercise** — ruling out moon, Venus/planet, fixed star — is preserved as a **disc-class** ^[inferred] (rather than green-fireball-class) photographic case under LaPaz's analytical methodology. The case is one of three explicitly-named non-fireball sightings in the catalog ^[ambiguous].

## Institutional consequence

The Seventh Report is the **earliest formal post-Roswell critical-facility-threat scientific document** in the FBI corpus ^[inferred]. Its operational impact:

1. **Project Twinkle is established** under AMC Geophysical Research Division ~12 weeks after the Seventh Report ^[inferred] — implementing the Roswell+Sacramento dual-station observational architecture (in modified form at Vaughn NM).
2. **The two-category interpretation** (meteoric ~50% + US-missile-testing ~50%) becomes the **operational frame** for the AF's posture by late 1950 ^[inferred] — confirmed by the 9 Oct 1950 Ladd memo's OSI Wright Field conclusion *"no space ships or missiles from any other planet or country"*.
3. **The Urals-missile hypothesis** is preserved in the FBI HQ file as a **standing fallback frame** for the green-fireball phenomenon ^[inferred]. It is **structurally aligned with the Cold War threat-assessment posture** — and **structurally opposed to** the [[references/fbi-boggs-hearn-discontinuation-memo-1950-03-28|Boggs-Hearn debunk-by-default]] frame retained 8 weeks earlier in [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-5|section 5]]. The FBI's section-6 file thus holds **both frames in parallel** without resolution. ^[inferred]
4. **The 11-criteria taxonomy** becomes the **methodological template** for later anomalous-pattern UAP analytical work ^[inferred] — though the wiki corpus does not document direct downstream citation.
5. **LaPaz's $0/day voluntary consultant arrangement** is preserved as a **labor-economics datapoint** about the era's UAP scientific labor market — the **academic anomaly-pattern analyst as institutional pro bono volunteer**. ^[inferred]
