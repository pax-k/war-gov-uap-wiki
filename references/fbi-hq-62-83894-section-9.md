# FBI HQ File 62-HQ-83894 — Section 9 (Serials 385-447)

The **ninth section** of [[entities/fbi-hq-62-83894-file|FBI HQ file 62-HQ-83894]] — **290 OCR pages** indexed serials **385 through 447** ^[extracted], spanning **14 November 1957 → 21 July 1966** ^[extracted] (date stamps on individual filings). This is the **15th of the planned 17-artifact wiki series** for the file ^[inferred] and the longest-running single section by date span (~8 years 8 months) — a fact reflecting the bureaucratic cooling that section 9 itself documents (one or two filings per quarter for most of the 1960-1965 period, in contrast to sections 7-8's monthly cadence). ^[inferred]

The section opens **~10 days after [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-8|section 8]] closes** (last section-8 filing 7 Nov 1957; first section-9 filing 14 Nov 1957) and **closes ~3 months before [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-serial-449|serial 449]]** (Oct 1966). It is the **first section ingested whose content is dominated by post-Sputnik / post-Levelland material** ^[inferred] — confirming the open question raised at the close of section 8. ^[extracted]

## Filing-block / processing metadata

- **Class–Case–Sub–Vol filing**: `62-HQ-83894 SERIALS 385-447`. ^[extracted]
- **Marker**: `62-HQ-83894-94` (repeated on cover slip — note: the trailing `-94` appears to be an internal serialization artifact, not a sub-serial marker). ^[ambiguous]
- **Processing tag**: `8/11/1274182 RRP0030KGO`. ^[extracted]
- **Declassification provenance**: *"Declassification authority derived from FBI Automatic Declassification Guide, issued May 24, 2007"*; FOIPA copy stamps **NOV 2** and **MAY 13 1971 BY R232**. ^[extracted]
- **Cover slip note**: *"USE CARE IN HANDLING AND FILE / DO NOT DESTROY / FOIPA"*. ^[extracted]

The May 1971 FOIPA-copy stamp is notable — the FBI made a complete copy of this file ~5 years after its last filing, **before** the FOIA Act's 1974 amendments and well before the file's eventual public-release vintage. ^[inferred] This is the earliest internal-copy date observed across the wiki's 62-HQ-83894 ingest series. ^[inferred]

## Content map (serials 385-447)

The 290 OCR pages decompose into **~63 distinct filings** along **five recurring channel types**: ^[inferred]

| Channel | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Field-office filings to HQ** | SAC airtels and letters routing inbound civilian saucer correspondence to HQ | Dallas serial 385, Havana legat 386, Kansas City 389, Phoenix 396-403 (Bender thread), Cincinnati 426 (Stringfield), Albuquerque 435-438 (Socorro) |
| **Hoover form-letter responses** | Outbound replies from Hoover (or Helen Gandy) to citizen UFO-inquiry letters | Maney 394, Wolcott 395, Mastroberte 428, Black 429, Bryant 430, Hewes 440, Caldwell 443, Powell 444, Snigier 445, Fox 446, Gray 447 |
| **Inbound USAF-OSI referrals** | OSI letters routing civilian inquiries back to FBI | Welch 391 (Torrington CT 5 Dec 1957) |
| **Annual saucer-policy review memos** | Internal FBI memos confirming Bureau policy unchanged since BB-57 | Sanders 392 (Oct 1958), Donahoe 422 (Sep 1960), Donahoe 425 (Oct 1961), Wannall 427 (Oct 1962) |
| **Sub rosa subject files** | Background filings on civilian saucer-research figures | Larry W. Bryant 402 (1959), 430-431 (1963), 439 (1964); George Van Tassel 418 (1960); Frank Stranges thread 423 (1960) |

The **annual review memos** (392, 422, 425, 427) are the section's most distinctive structural feature. Each repeats — almost verbatim — the same null-finding chain: Bureau Bulletin 57 of 10/1/47, SAC Letter 38 of 3/25/49, SAC Letter 83 of 8/29/52, the 11/12/57 Air Force assurance — *"there appears to be no necessity for additional instructions for the field or insertion in the FBI Handbook or Manuals relative to flying saucers."* ^[extracted] This is the **bureaucratic canon-text** of FBI saucer policy — see [[concepts/fbi-saucer-policy-stasis-1958-1962]] for the synthesis page.

## HEADLINE — the Sputnik-era reactivation (serial 387, 12 Nov 1957)

**Serial 387** — Roach → Belmont memo of **12 November 1957** (filed 10 days after Levelland) — is the **canonical FBI-side primary source for the Sputnik-era saucer-reactivation framing**. ^[inferred] The memo opens:

> *"Ever since the Russians released 'Sputnik' there has been a great increase in the number of 'flying saucers' and other unidentified flying objects (UFO) reportedly seen by people all over the U.S."* ^[extracted]

The memo then enumerates the cases that flooded the AF in the prior ~6 weeks: ^[extracted]

- **10 October 1957** — Vernal, Utah: object *"looked like the moon"* trailing a *"blue flame that pulsed like a heart"* allegedly **landed**; AF investigated *"with negative results, inasmuch as no object was located nor was there any evidence of an object having fallen"*.
- **late Oct/early Nov 1957** — Nebraska: an object had landed, *"six people aboard"*, who *"talked to a Nebraska farmer and then sped off into space"*.
- **late Oct/early Nov 1957** — Albany GA → Miami FL: a *"firey object"* flashing across the southern skies.
- **late Oct/early Nov 1957** — Gulf of Mexico: a Coast Guard cutter *"sighted a huge object flying over"*.
- **late Oct/early Nov 1957** — Southwestern states: persons driving *"have allegedly seen UFO's that caused the engines in their automobiles to stop"* — the **[[concepts/engine-stall-phenomenon|Levelland TX engine-stall wave]]** (2-3 Nov 1957) referred to euphemistically without naming the town. ^[inferred]

Per AF Intelligence (per Bureau Liaison Representative): *"all the cases mentioned above have for all practical purposes been resolved as being nonauthentic or there was no basis for the sightings"*. ^[extracted] Serial 387 is the **dedicated reference page** [[references/fbi-roach-belmont-sputnik-memo-1957-11-12]]; the cross-cutting reactivation pattern is concept-paged at [[concepts/sputnik-saucer-reactivation-1957]].

This memo **closes the open question** raised at the end of [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-8|section 8]]: *yes*, section 9 picks up post-Sputnik, and the FBI's HQ-side framing of the reactivation is **explicitly Sputnik-anchored, not Levelland-anchored**. ^[inferred] The Bureau's read is the Sputnik hype is causing a misperception spike — not that genuine new phenomena have appeared. ^[inferred] This dovetails with — and extends — the **[[concepts/saucer-as-hoax-default|saucer-as-hoax default]]**.

## The Bender / Three-Men-in-Black thread (serials 396-403)

A discrete sub-thread spanning **late Dec 1958 → Feb 1959** anchors the Bureau's first sustained engagement with the **MIB (Three Men in Black) myth** as a **civilian rumor about the FBI itself**: ^[inferred]

| Serial | Date | Filing |
|---|---|---|
| 396 | 9 Dec 1958 | Oklahoma City SAC report on **James Maney**, age 13, who had read *Saucerian Bulletin* Vol 3 #4 (15 Oct 1958, ed. [[entities/gray-barker]], Box 2228, Clarksburg WV); the issue editorialized *"You have often wondered where the 3 men came from that silenced [[entities/albert-k-bender\|Bender]]. … Adamski tells me that the 3 men were from the FBI, Air Force Intelligence, and the Central Intelligence Agency"* (allegedly silencing **George Adamski**) |
| 397 | 16 Dec 1958 | Bureau letter to Phoenix re Claude H. Marck Jr.'s 7 Dec 1958 letter |
| 398 | 19 Dec 1958 | Phoenix SAC report on **Claude Harold Marck Jr.** (Scottsdale AZ), interviewed re *"Bender Affair"* — Marck cited [[references/barker-1956-they-knew-too-much\|Barker's 1956 book]] *They Knew Too Much About Flying Saucers* as primary source, noted Barker's *"civilian investigative agency"* in Clarksburg |
| 399 | 30 Dec 1958 | Bureau request to Phoenix to obtain a copy of Barker's book |
| 400 | 30 Dec 1958 | Memo to Phoenix to recontact Marck for publisher address (Marck had moved to Denver) |
| 401 | 5 Jan 1959 | Phoenix follow-up: University Books Inc. located at **806 Dempster Street, Evanston, IL** (per Phoenix Public Library) |
| 402 | 1 Feb 1959 | **Larry W. Bryant's** first FBI letter, asking re FBI participation in UFO investigation, photo analysis, and Congressional testimony |
| 403 | 12 Feb 1959 | Chicago SAC enclosure: actual copy of Barker's *They Knew Too Much About Flying Saucers* mailed to HQ |

Three observations across the thread: ^[inferred]

1. The FBI **acquired and retained the actual Barker book** as a serialized HQ artifact ([[references/barker-1956-they-knew-too-much]] currently holds only the **dust-jacket clipping** from a *separate* serial-403 retention; section 9 confirms the FBI also acquired the full book copy via Chicago SAC mailing to HQ in Feb 1959 — these are **two distinct retention events**). ^[inferred]
2. The FBI's interest in the book is framed as **counterintelligence-adjacent** (the Phoenix file is captioned *"Nationalities Intelligence"*) — implying a non-trivial concern that the MIB rumor's *"FBI/AF Intel/CIA"* attribution might prompt operational embarrassment or be a vector for foreign-influence narrative-shaping. ^[inferred]
3. The thread terminates at serial 403 with **no investigative finding** — the FBI does not, in the file, ever **contradict** the MIB rumor; it merely files the materials that name it. ^[inferred] This is consistent with the file's **passive-accumulation** disposition reading from sections 1-5.

## The Joseph Perry Grand Blanc photo case (serials 410-416, 445)

A multi-year thread on **Joseph F. Perry**, professional photographer / pizzeria proprietor at 3079 Edwards St, Grand Blanc MI, who at ~1 AM, 21 Feb 1960, photographed the moon through a homemade telescope and noted *"a flying object"* in one developed slide. ^[extracted] Serial 410 (3-9-60 Detroit airtel) opens the thread; Perry surrenders the original 35mm slide to the Flint Resident Agency, which forwards it to OSI Selfridge AFB. The case **media-escalates** when the *Flint Journal* (3-27-60) and *Detroit Times* (5-20-60) report the FBI is *"interested in the picture"* — a representation Hoover personally repudiates in correspondence (serial 416, 21 Apr 1960). The thread terminates at serial 445 (15 Apr 1966) — six years later — when a citizen reads about the case in a magazine and writes to inquire. See [[references/sighting-grand-blanc-perry-1960]] for the consolidated case page.

## The Larry W. Bryant arc (serials 402, 430-431, 439)

**Larry W. Bryant** is the section's **persistent civilian-government-critic** figure — a Department of the Army civilian (later editorial clerk at the U.S. Army Transportation School, Fort Eustis VA) who began UFO correspondence with the FBI in **Feb 1959** (serial 402) and resurfaced **Sep 1963** (serial 430-431) with a *"Let's Challenge the UFO Censors"* article submitted to the Defense Department's security review process. ^[extracted] Per the FBI's note on serial 430: *"The G-2 Section of Second Army, Fort Meade, Maryland is conducting a background check of Bryant because he has directed voluminous correspondence to military officials in the area and because he is employed by the Army."* ^[extracted] See [[entities/larry-w-bryant]] for the entity page; the rhetorical frame ties to [[concepts/figurative-ufo-rhetoric]].

## The Socorro NM landing case (serials 435, 436, 438)

**Officer [[entities/lonnie-zamora|Lonnie Zamora]]'s** 24 Apr 1964 landing-class encounter at Socorro NM is filed at HQ across three serials of section 9: serial 435 (27 Apr 1964 Albuquerque teletype reporting the parallel La Madera NM 26 Apr 1964 sighting by Orlando Gallegos), serial 436 (28 Apr 1964 Albuquerque teletype + Director's airtel back instructing *"Submit letterhead memorandum"*), and serial 438 (8 May 1964 Albuquerque report by SA D. Arthur Byrnes Jr.). The full case is paged at [[references/sighting-socorro-nm-1964-04-24]]; the FBI document at [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-serial-438]]. Section 9 confirms the case was filed at HQ as **part of the standard 62-HQ-83894 stream** — i.e., not segregated to a special file — despite being the file's **most kinematically anomalous** event by a wide margin. ^[inferred]

Notable from the section-9 framing: serial 436 (HQ → Albuquerque airtel) instructs *"Assure facts this matter are furnished to local Air Force"* and adds the canonical hedge *"Our policy is not to investigate but assure that Air Force is cognizant"* — **same policy as 1947, applied unchanged 17 years later** to the Bureau's most striking single primary-source UAP intake. ^[inferred] This is the **clearest single test** of the [[concepts/fbi-saucer-policy-stasis-1958-1962|policy-stasis]] reading.

## The La Madera NM Gallegos sighting (serial 435, 26 Apr 1964)

48 hours after the Zamora encounter, **Orlando Gallegos** (age 35, Santa Fe NM) reported observing — at ~1 AM 26 Apr 1964, **~70 miles north of Santa Fe at La Maderia [La Madera] NM** — *"something shaped like a butane tank, possibly twelve to fourteen feet high, and 'long as a telephone pole'"* on the ground, surrounded by **blue-white flame** *"which appeared to come from port holes"*. ^[extracted] Object motionless, silent for ~1 minute, flame extinguished. Father Frank Gallegos *"laughed at him"*; next morning Orlando visited site, saw smouldering area; **NMSP Captain Vigil** confirmed the next morning a *"scorched circular area about thirty to forty feet in diameter"* with **rectangular V-shaped indentations** matching the Socorro case profile. ^[extracted] No second witness. The sighting is **physical-trace coincident with Socorro** within 48 hours and ~150 miles, but receives no separate FBI investigation. ^[inferred] **Worth a future ingest sighting page** but folded here for now.

## Annual saucer-policy review memos (392, 422, 425, 427)

The first occurrence — **serial 392, Sanders → Donahoe, 13 Oct 1958** — is the **most substantive** of the chain. ^[inferred] Sanders compiles a **five-paragraph history of FBI saucer policy**: ^[extracted]

- 1947 — *"at request of Army Air Forces Intelligence, Bureau agreed to cooperate"*; **Bureau Bulletin 42, 7-30-47**, instructed field *"to determine if bona fide sighting, a prank, or hallucination."*
- 9-3-47 ADC letter (the *"trash can covers, toilet seats and what not"* letter cited verbatim by Sanders) → **Bureau Bulletin 57, 10-1-47**: *"all future reports … should be referred to the Air Forces and no investigative action should be taken by Bureau Agents."*
- 9-27-47 Hoover letter to AF cited verbatim: *"personnel and time of FBI could not be dissipated in this manner (62-83894-121)"* — internal cross-reference to the BB-57 birth chain in [[references/fbi-hoover-mcdonald-letter-1947-09-27|section 3]].
- **SAC Letter 38, 3-25-49**: from *"reliable source"* the USSR had been experimenting *"for four years on unknown type of flying disc"* — a proximate USSR-saucer attribution that the AF requested data-collection on.
- **SAC Letter 83, 8-29-52** — referenced but not quoted; also referenced in [[references/fbi-sac-letter-83-1952]] from section 7.

Sanders's closing read: *"Neither Agents Handbook nor manuals contain instructions regarding handling of reports … although policy has been well established by above Bureau bulletins and SAC Letters and no problems have arisen in connection with this matter."* ^[extracted] This is **the canonical FBI-internal narrative of its own saucer-policy posture** — the version that gets recycled annually for the next four years. ^[inferred]

The subsequent reviews (Donahoe 9-30-60 / 422; Donahoe 10-2-61 / 425; Wannall 10-8-62 / 427) are **near-verbatim re-runs** with one update: *"During the past 12 months only one complaint regarding flying saucers has been received."* ^[extracted] The review then disappears from section 9 after 1962 — possibly because the Socorro 1964 case made the *"only one complaint"* claim untenable. ^[inferred]

## File-function refinement

Section 9 **strengthens** the [[entities/fbi-hq-62-83894-file|parent-file reading]] — the file is a **multi-channel passive accumulator** with *bureaucratically punctuated self-reviews*. ^[inferred] Specific refinements:

1. **Annual self-audit is part of the file's form** — the Donahoe/Wannall annual review memos (392, 422, 425, 427) are **internal policy stasis assertions filed inside the topic file**, distinct from the field-office and citizen-correspondence channels. ^[inferred] This is a **fifth channel** type not previously identified, pushing the total channel count from 11 (per [[entities/fbi-hq-62-83894-file]]) to **12 channels** with **annual-self-review** as the new addition. ^[inferred]
2. **The file persists despite triviality** — the 1960-1965 mid-period averages roughly one serial per 6 weeks, and most are **single-page Hoover form-letter replies to citizen inquiries**. ^[inferred] The file is kept open not because the topic is operationally active but because the *correspondence* channel never closes. ^[inferred]
3. **Substantive primary-source content survives in the punctuation** — serial 387 (Sputnik framing), 392 (canonical policy history), 438 (Socorro), 430-431 (Bryant) are widely separated in time but each constitutes a paradigmatic FBI-primary-source on a major UAP-history flashpoint. ^[inferred] The signal-to-noise ratio of the file is therefore **bursty** — low average, high peaks. ^[inferred]
4. **MIB rumor about the FBI itself** is **filed back into the file** — serial 396 (Maney → Saucerian Bulletin Vol 3 #4) and serial 437 (Montello direct query) show the file ingesting **rumors that name itself as a perpetrator**, without ever rebutting them inside the file. ^[inferred] This is the file's most **reflexively interesting** structural property. ^[inferred]

## Open Questions

- **Section 6 (serials 246-301)** and **section 10** remain unscanned. Section 6 covers ~Sep 1950 – Aug 1952 (the [[concepts/aztec-saucer-myth-1950]] post-mortem and the Robertson Panel run-up), and section 10 likely covers post-1966 closeout. ^[inferred]
- **Was the Sanders-Donahoe canonical-history memo (serial 392) drafted from a pre-existing template?** The 4-paragraph Bulletin/SAC-Letter recap appears verbatim in 422, 425, 427 — strongly suggesting Sanders compiled the master text, which then propagated as the annual review's standing language. ^[inferred] No earlier section appears to contain this consolidated history. ^[open]
- **Is the May 1971 FOIPA-copy stamp connected to a specific external request?** No requestor information accompanies the stamp on the section-9 cover. ^[open]
- **Did the Albert K. Bender hush-up actually occur?** ^[ambiguous] The FBI file accumulates Marck's restatement, Maney's restatement (via Barker's editorial), and Montello's inquiry — all alleging FBI/AF/CIA involvement — but **never investigates Bender** and never affirms or denies the MIB attribution. The FBI's conduct is **consistent with both** "the rumor is groundless and we don't dignify it" and "we have a tacit policy of non-engagement on a real action by another agency". ^[ambiguous] See [[concepts/three-men-in-black]] for the rhetorical/historical analysis.
