# Sighting — Honolulu, T.H. (18 Oct 1948)

A single-witness daylight ground observation by a USAF Major rated pilot of a bright silver disc-class object **observed four separate times in a 10-minute period** on **18 October 1948 between 1705 and 1715 HST** at Honolulu, Territory of Hawaii (158° W, 21°5' N). Filed as **Project SIGN Incident 178** in [[references/project-sign-incident-summaries-173-233|the Sep 1948 – Jan 1949 incident summaries bundle]]. This is the **earliest Pacific-theater disc-class observation** in the wiki's corpus.

## Witness

- **Maj Robert C. Drum, USAF** — rated pilot. Residence: 2737 Pacific Heights Rd, Honolulu. ^[extracted]

## What the witness observed

Drum was observing the sky in the north sector and saw a "strangely shaped glistening object" moving NE at ~10,000 ft. The cloud-layer geometry placed the object **between two cloud levels**: an alto-cumulus deck above at ~14,000 ft and broken cumulus below at ~3,500 ft. Because the lower cumulus was moving SW (winds aloft) and partially blocked vision, **Drum could not confirm whether he was seeing the same object four times or four different objects passing through visible clouds**. ^[extracted]

The longest single observation was ~10 seconds. The object's apparent shape (elliptical) Drum attributed to viewing angle rather than to underlying ellipsoidal geometry — comparing the appearance to a silver dollar held at 45° above eye level at arm's length. ^[extracted]

A B-26 and one other aircraft were in the area at the time of the sighting (the summary truncates here). Drum stated he was "certain the object was not a conventional craft." ^[extracted]

## Why this report matters

Three features distinguish this sighting in the 1948 corpus:

1. **Pacific-theater anchor.** The wiki's 1948 disc material was previously dominated by CONUS field installations + Alaska + Western Europe. This is the **first Hawaiian / Pacific Ocean Area** disc-class observation, predating the [[references/usaf-flying-discs-1949|1949 file]]'s Pacific events by ~2½ months. ^[inferred]
2. **Multi-glimpse pattern.** The wiki's prior 1948 disc events are mostly single-glimpse (seconds-to-minute observations). The four-separate-glimpse, ten-minute pattern with cumulus interference is a **morphologically rich** observation — Drum's careful breakdown of the cloud-layer geometry, the angle-vs-shape distinction, and the inability-to-confirm-identity-vs-quantity is a textbook example of the [[concepts/flying-disc-reporting-protocol|10-element protocol]] working as designed. ^[inferred]
3. **Trained-observer single-witness with structural caveats.** Drum is a rated USAF Major; the incident sheet does not record any character or reliability disqualifier. The pattern (one observer, multiple time-separated glimpses) raises an interesting **inference question**: did the object circle/loop in the area, or did multiple co-formation objects pass through gaps in the same direction? Drum withholds judgment, and the sheet preserves that ambiguity. ^[extracted]

## Provenance and reliability

- Single-witness reports carry less weight than multi-witness events; Drum is a trained observer but no companion is named on the sheet. ^[inferred]
- The witness's careful **explicit acknowledgement of uncertainty** ("could not ascertain whether he saw the same disc four different times or four different discs") is a **positive credibility signal** — fabricated or exaggerated reports tend to over-resolve such ambiguities. ^[inferred]
- The cumulus-build-up speed-estimation method (~40 MPH for cumulus drift) is a reasonable visual-reference technique for a pilot. The 200 MPH derived speed is **slow** for a 1948 jet aircraft and consistent with the object behavior (no sound, no exhaust trail, level flight). ^[extracted]
