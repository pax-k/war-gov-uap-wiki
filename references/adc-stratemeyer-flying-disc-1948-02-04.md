# ADC Mitchel Field Stratemeyer Flying-Disc Letter (4 February 1948)

A **CONFIDENTIAL Air Defense Command HQ directive** issued **4 February 1948** by command of **Lt Gen George E. Stratemeyer** (Commanding General, ADC) to the Commanding Generals of the **First, Second, Fourth, Tenth, Eleventh, and Fourteenth Air Forces, Air Defense Command** — instructing that "flying disc" incidents be investigated under Counter Intelligence Incident Cases procedures, and that **investigations will be coordinated with the FBI Office concerned**. ^[extracted] The letter is one of the earliest USAF post-Sep-1947 reorganization-era operational directives in the wiki corpus, and the **proximate trigger** for the FBI's first defense of [[references/fbi-bureau-bulletin-57-1947|Bureau Bulletin No. 57]] in the post-termination period. ^[inferred]

## Verbatim core (page 36)

The letter contains four numbered paragraphs:

> **1.** The numbered air forces of this command are responsible for the prompt investigation and reporting of incidents relating to "flying discs" which occur within their respective areas. Numbered air force A-2s, after evaluating disc incidents, will determine the extent of investigation. Futile expenditure of military funds and manpower must be avoided.

> **2.** "Flying discs" will be investigated as provided for in Counter Intelligence Incident Cases, taking cognizance of the USAF Operating Intelligence Echelon structure. Where it is evident that witnesses, who were together at the time of incident but who are widely separated at the time of investigation would corroborate each other's story, only one witness need be interrogated. **If there is a reasonable indication that a reported incident is a hoax or the fabrication of a publicity seeking individual, no further investigative effort will be expended.** However, a report of such circumstances will be submitted to the FBI office concerned. ^[extracted]

> **2.a.** CIC personnel attached to the numbered air forces of this command may be utilized to prosecute the investigation of subject incidents.

> **2.b.** Investigations will be coordinated with the FBI Office concerned in accordance with instructions contained in letter, this headquarters, D 333.3 EX, subject, "Cooperation of FBI with AAF on Investigation of 'Flying Disc' Incidents," 3 September 1947.

> **3.** Reports will be narrative in style and will cover only those facts pertinent to the subject... Two types of report will be submitted on all incidents.
>   - **3.a.** Spot Reports (fragmentary reports) will be submitted on all incidents and will be followed by a Letter Report.
>   - **3.b.** Letter Reports will be submitted to report the completed investigation of all "flying disc" incidents.

> **4.** Reports will be submitted in duplicate promptly, usually be regular mail. However, Spot reports on incidents of substantial character, i.e., those in which the patent credibility of the witness is beyond challenge and whose technical [...]

## What this letter codifies

1. **Hoax-default doctrine** — paragraph 2 explicitly directs that *"if there is a reasonable indication that a reported incident is a hoax or the fabrication of a publicity seeking individual, no further investigative effort will be expended"*. ^[extracted] This is the **first AF-side primary-source codification** of [[concepts/saucer-as-hoax-default|the saucer-as-hoax-default operating presumption]] in the wiki corpus. ^[inferred] Compare with [[references/fbi-bureau-bulletin-42-1947|BB-42]]'s embedded *"wire-released disc"* prosaic anchor.
2. **Counter Intelligence Incident Cases framework** — flying discs are filed under Air Force CIC procedures, anchoring the institutional treatment of disc incidents to **counterintelligence** rather than **technical-intelligence** procedures. ^[inferred]
3. **Reference to "Cooperation of FBI with AAF on Investigation of 'Flying Disc' Incidents", 3 September 1947** (paragraph 2.b) — this is a **previously-unrescued letter** from ADC HQ Mitchel Field. ^[inferred] The 3 Sep 1947 letter cannot be the [[concepts/fbi-aaf-saucer-cooperation-1947|cooperation regime]] founding (which was Schulgen-Reynolds 9 Jul 1947 / Bureau Bulletin No. 42 30 Jul 1947); it is **plausibly an ADC-internal codification** of cooperation procedure issued in early September 1947, distinct from the FBI's own BB-42. ^[inferred]
4. **Two-tier reporting**: Spot Reports + Letter Reports for every disc incident — the report templates that would later evolve into Cabell's [[concepts/flying-disc-reporting-protocol|AIRMEM #4 EEI schema]]. ^[inferred]

## Why this letter matters in the FBI corpus

The letter is **structurally important** because it documents that the **AAF (now USAF) had not internalized BB-57** ~4 months after BB-57's 1 Oct 1947 issuance: ^[inferred]

1. **SAC SF Kimball's flag** (12 Feb 1948, page 34): *"Inasmuch as these recent instructions of the Headquarters, Air Defense Command, Mitchell Air Force Base, New York, are contrary to the instructions contained in Bureau Bulletin No. 57, Series 1947, dated October 1, 1947, in which it is indicated that all future reports connected with flying discs should be referred to the Air Force and no investigative action taken by Bureau agents, the Bureau is requested to advise this office, as well as other interested Bureau offices, as whether there has been a change in Bureau policy with reference to the handling of matter or whether Bureau Bulletin No. 57 dated October 1, 1947 is still in effect."* ^[extracted]
2. **Hoover's reaffirmation** (20 Feb 1948, page 38): *"You are advised that Bureau Bulletin No. 57 dated October 1, 1947, still applies with respect to flying discs. The Bureau is conducting no investigations concerning flying discs. We will, however, give the Air Forces any information which comes to our attention and will receive any information which the Air Forces volunteer. The Headquarters of the Air Forces in Washington are being contacted by the Bureau in order to insure that there is no misunderstanding as to the Bureau's position in this matter."* ^[extracted]
3. **Reynolds-Martin reconciliation** (1 Mar 1948, page 40): SA S. W. Reynolds discusses the matter with **Lt Col C. P. Martin** of AF Intelligence Division. Martin advises that *"apparently the Air Defense Command at Mitchel Field was not aware of the fact that the Bureau was no longer conducting investigations regarding flying discs. He stated that he would take the necessary action and straighten out the Air Defense Command as to the present policy regarding incidents of flying discs."* ^[extracted]

## Institutional reading

The Stratemeyer letter is **not** an authorized re-enlistment of the FBI in the cooperation regime — it is an **AAF-side bureaucratic continuation** of a defunct cooperation arrangement that **never received the BB-57 update**. ^[inferred] The AAF's institutional knowledge of BB-57 in early February 1948 was **incomplete**: the Pentagon-level Intelligence Division (Lt Col Martin) was aware of the FBI's exit, but the **operational ADC HQ at Mitchel Field was not**. ^[inferred] This is a **first-order intelligence-coordination failure** within the AAF — a cooperation-protocol issued by ADC HQ on 4 Feb 1948 directly contradicts BB-57 issued by FBI HQ on 1 Oct 1947 (4 months earlier). ^[inferred]

The 3 September 1947 ADC HQ letter ("Cooperation of FBI with AAF on Investigation of 'Flying Disc' Incidents") referenced by Stratemeyer in paragraph 2.b is the **proximate ADC-side codification** of the cooperation regime that the FBI had already terminated by Bureau action **28 days later** (1 Oct 1947). ^[inferred] The Stratemeyer letter of 4 Feb 1948 is an **operational continuation** of an instrument the FBI had already ended.

## Position in the cooperation-regime arc

The letter is **the first documented AF-side resistance** to BB-57 — antedating the next AF resistance event ([[references/fbi-sac-letter-38-1949|SAC Letter No. 38]] of 25 Mar 1949 reactivating partial cooperation) by **~14 months**. ^[inferred]
