## What the excerpt contains: HENRY's UV-astronomy testimony

The two transcript pages (119, 120) are continuous remarks by a speaker labeled **HENRY (CONT'D)** — almost certainly **Richard C. Henry** of [[entities/nasa|NASA]] / Johns Hopkins, **principal investigator** on the **Apollo 17 SIM-bay Far-UV Spectrometer (S-169)** and the cislunar UV-astronomy science program. ^[inferred] The "CONT'D" tag indicates this is the **continuation** of a longer statement that began on a page not in the OCR bundle.

Henry catalogues five distinct UV-astronomy results from Apollo 17:

### 1. Coma cluster Lyman-alpha non-detection

> *"...gravitationally holding it together. We thought it might be in the form of ionized hydrogen. We looked for Lyman-alpha radiation, red shifted from the ionized hydrogen, and we didn't see any. We set a lower limit, which certainly excludes the possibility that the Coma cluster is held together by this ionized hydrogen. I think that may leave a real mystery as to what is holding the thing together."* ^[extracted]

A **non-detection** of redshifted Lyman-alpha radiation from the **Coma cluster** of galaxies. Henry's interpretation: this **excludes ionized hydrogen as the binding mass** of the Coma cluster — leaving the **dark-matter problem** open. ^[inferred] The "real mystery as to what is holding the thing together" is, in 2026 retrospect, **the Coma cluster's now-canonical role as Zwicky's 1933 dark-matter benchmark**; Henry's 1973 framing pre-dates the broad dark-matter consensus but is consistent with it. ^[inferred]

### 2. UV background at high galactic latitudes

> *"In the UV, nobody knows, but you never know until you look... we did look at a large number of different points at high galactic latitudes, both north and south. The spectrum that we see is above this dark count..."* ^[extracted]

Apollo 17's UV instrument **detected diffuse UV emission** at **high galactic latitudes** (both North and South Galactic Poles) **above instrument dark-count**. Henry parallels this to the **X-ray** background (already detected; "nobody had expected") and the **gamma-ray** background ([[entities/nasa|NASA]] colleague **Dr. Trombka** — Apollo 15/16/17 gamma-ray spectrometer PI). ^[inferred] The discovery of a UV diffuse background at high galactic latitudes is **first announced** here on the corpus excerpt. ^[inferred]

### 3. Spectrum looks like a hot star — possible extragalactic radiation

> *"The spectrum that we see looks like the spectrum of the hot star; however, we know that there were no hot stars within our field of view. Therefore, the most conservative interpretation, I think, is that what we're seeing is light from hot stars in the galactic plane going up out of the plane and reflecting off interstellar dust. There are certain characteristics of the spectrum, though, that don't fit that theory, and it's at least possible that this is extragalactic radiation."* ^[extracted]

Henry frames the observed spectrum as a tension between **two candidate interpretations** — galactic-dust scattering vs. **extragalactic radiation** — and explicitly **does not resolve** which. The "extragalactic" possibility is a notable speculative branch in early-1973 UV astronomy. ^[ambiguous]

### 4. Solar-system Lyman-alpha hydrogen (OGO-5 / Gary Thomas)

> *"Lyman-alpha hydrogen radiation is a completely separate problem... Gary Thomas at the University of Colorado and Charles Barthum [?] observed this from OGO-5. We obtained just an enormous amount of data on the Apollo that's going to straighten out this picture and clarify it considerably. This is hydrogen that is inside our solar system. It's sunlight reflecting off this. The hydrogen, Gary Thomas thinks, is hydrogen from interstellar space streaming through the solar system..."* ^[extracted]

Apollo 17 carried a successor instrument to **OGO-5** (Orbiting Geophysical Observatory 5, 1968–1972) — generating data to clarify the **interstellar-hydrogen-streaming-through-the-heliosphere** model that **Gary Thomas** (University of Colorado) had developed. ^[inferred] "Charles Barthum [?]" is the OCR's bracketed-with-question-mark rendering of a name the OCR engine could not confidently resolve. ^[ambiguous]

### 5. Spectrum of the Earth (truncated)

> *"One more thing: the spectrum of the Earth. I keep saying 'we,' but these were the guys that were there. We looked at the Earth from outside. A lot of people have observed [TRUNCATED — page ends mid-sentence]"* ^[extracted]

The page-120 OCR ends mid-sentence on Henry's introduction of an **Earth-spectrum-from-outside observation** — a UV remote-sensing characterization of Earth's atmosphere viewed from cislunar distance. Continuation is on page 121, **not in the OCR bundle**.

## Why this artifact is in the UAP corpus

This is the wiki's **strongest null orbital-NASA artifact**. The d6 sister artifact had at least three UAP-domain keyword false-positives ("fireball" / "sighting" / "flash") that plausibly explain its FOIA-keyword inclusion; **d5 has none**. The pages 119–120 selection is a **stronger inferential claim** that the **d-cluster as a whole** was pulled by **document-series identifier** (the seven NASA crew-debriefing documents in this 04/04/2026-stamped tranche) rather than by per-page keyword search. ^[inferred] Together with the d6 / d5 pair, the corpus now has **two structurally distinct boundary mechanisms** for non-UAP material entering the FOIA UAP-keyword release universe at the orbital scale:

1. **Transcript-keyword false-positive** (d6 — page 24-4 contains "fireball", "sighting", "flash" in non-UAP semantic contexts).
2. **Document-series whole-bundle inclusion** (d5 — pages 119–120 contain no UAP-domain keywords, but the parent document is in the same NASA-debriefing series as d6).

Each of these has a **diplomatic-channel sub-corpus analogue** in [[concepts/figurative-ufo-rhetoric|figurative-UFO rhetoric typology]]: Type 1 (referential / NGO-name keyword match — analogous to the keyword route) and Type 2 (figurative / rhetorical — analogous to the false-positive route). ^[inferred] The **document-series whole-bundle** mechanism is **structurally novel** to the orbital-class sub-corpus and does not yet have a diplomatic-channel analogue. ^[inferred]
