# DOS Cable — UFOs Over Georgia: Strange Encounters of an MFA Kind (MOSCOW 13169, 30 Oct 2001)

A 5-page declassified [[entities/state-department|US Department of State]] outgoing cable from [[entities/amembassy-moscow|AMEMBASSY Moscow]] (signed `VERSHBOW`) to `SECSTATE WASHDC` PRIORITY and a CIS / DoD / OSCE info distribution, dated `P 301741Z OCT 01`. Subject: **"UFOS OVER GEORGIA: STRANGE ENCOUNTERS OF AN MFA KIND"**. Classification: `CONFIDENTIAL`, classified by [[entities/george-krol|POLMINCOUNS George Krol]] under reason `1.5 (B/D)`; `E.O. 12958` `DECL: 10/29/21`.

This cable is the wiki's **fourth** archived US State Department cable that mentions "UFO" — and, like the [[references/dos-cable-turkmenistan-2004-11|2004 Ashgabat 1028 cable]] but **structurally distinct from it**, it is **not a UAP-sighting cable**. The cable's substantive subject is Russian-Georgian state relations: specifically, Georgian accusations that Russian aircraft violated Georgian airspace and bombed positions in the Kodori Gorge on 28–29 October 2001, and the Russian Ministry of Foreign Affairs' (MFA) categorical denial. The word "UFO" appears in the cable's subject line as a **rhetorical / sarcastic device** keyed to Steven Spielberg's 1977 film *Close Encounters of the Third Kind* ^[inferred], and in the cable body as a **denial trope** spoken by Russian MFA Georgia desk chief Tereoken: that reports of Russian planes in the area "MIGHT AS WELL HAVE BEEN ABOUT 'UFOS.'" ^[extracted]

The cable belongs to the wiki's UAP corpus by virtue of being released in the FOIA tranche containing UAP-keyword-matched DOS records (the `059uap` series); it is **a second, structurally distinct boundary case** — see [[concepts/figurative-ufo-rhetoric]] for the typology, and [[concepts/diplomatic-channel-uap-reporting]] for the umbrella concept this cable sits outside.

## Substantive content

The cable is structured as 8 numbered paragraphs across two sections, with a narrative SUMMARY (¶1), four reporting paragraphs (¶2–5), a paragraph on Speaker Zvania's Moscow visit (¶6), a paragraph on rumored Russian DefMin Ivanov travel (¶7), and a closing COMMENT (¶8).

### Diplomatic substance (the cable's actual payload)

Russian Deputy Foreign Minister **Mamedov** told Ambassador Vershbow on 30 October 2001 that, according to the **Russian Ministry of Defense**, no Russian planes flew over or bombed positions in the **Kodori Gorge** on 28–29 October 2001, despite Georgian accusations to the contrary (Reftel B = TBILISI 3087). MFA Georgia desk chief **Tereoken** echoed Mamedov's denial. Vershbow stressed to Mamedov that such incidents, if true and continued, **could be disastrous for U.S.-Russian relations and spoil the upcoming summit meeting** of presidents in the U.S. ^[extracted]

Tereoken added that there were **credible reports of [[entities/russia-mfa|Abkhaz helicopters]] bombing the areas** "where the terrorists were" — and that reports of *planes* in the area:

> "MIGHT AS WELL HAVE BEEN ABOUT 'UFOS.'"

This is the cable's only "UFO" payload — a **rhetorical denial trope** by a Russian foreign-ministry official. Tereoken supplied a corollary: "Moscow does not have the technical capability to determine whether there were foreign planes in the region." Pressing the point, he added — in formally non-accusatory terms — that "any side" might have sent planes over Kodori. ^[extracted]

The cable then reports:

- **Gudauta base withdrawal** (Reftel A = MOSCOW 13072) — one trainload of Russian military equipment departed Gudauta and entered Russia the morning of 29 Oct; two more trains were loaded awaiting Abkhaz permission. Russian General Staff personnel in Sukhumi coordinating. ^[extracted]
- **No outside observers** would witness the withdrawal — Tereoken claimed Abkhaz authorities would not tolerate outsiders given the tense situation with Tbilisi; the Russians "had no time to invite outside observers because 'we didn't know until the last minute.'" The embassy pushed back, noting two more trains were already loaded but waiting on Abkhaz permission, so the no-time argument did not hold. The lack of transparency might raise questions about Russian intentions. ^[extracted]
- **Russian troop level at Gudauta**: ~600 personnel; Moscow insists on keeping ~340 as "guards"; Tbilisi wants a much lower figure. ^[extracted]
- **Speaker Zvania's Moscow visit (25–28 Oct)**: Tereoken confirmed the tone was "positive" but the visit "had no practical outcome." Per the Georgian Embassy, Zvania and Russian Duma Speaker **Seleznev** agreed both sides should avoid recriminations and work on a general treaty. ^[extracted]
- **DefMin Ivanov travel**: Tereoken dismissed press reports that Russian Defense Minister Sergey Ivanov had agreed to visit Tbilisi very soon, but did not entirely rule out a coming-weeks visit. He also flagged a possible **Shevardnadze–Putin meeting** on the margins of the 30 Nov – 1 Dec CIS summit in Moscow. ^[extracted]

### Vershbow's COMMENT (¶8) — the embassy's stance on the UFO trope

The closing comment is structurally important — it is the only point in the cable where the US-side embassy stance on the UFO rhetoric is on the record:

> *"IT IS HARD TO ACCEPT OFFICIAL RUSSIAN DENIALS THAT RUSSIAN PLANES WERE NOT INVOLVED. TO POSIT THAT THEY COULD BE UFOS WOULD BE HUMOROUS IF IT WERE NOT FOR THE SERIOUSNESS OF THE VIOLATIONS. MOST LIKELY THE RUSSIANS WANT TO KEEP THE PRESSURE ON THE GEORGIANS AND THE CHECHENS IN THE GORGE IN AN UNSUBTLE EFFORT TO PREVENT THESE GROUPS' MOVEMENT TO ABKHAZIA OR TO RUSSIA. THEIR OFFICIAL DENIALS REFLECT A TRADITIONAL RUSSIAN PENCHANT TO AVOID AN AWKWARD ADMISSION WITH A BOLD LIE."* ^[extracted]

Vershbow's reading: the UFO trope is a Russian face-saving formulation for what is actually a **deliberate continuing pressure operation** in the Kodori Gorge. The embassy treats the UFO posit as **a rhetorical instrument of denial**, not as a UAP claim — and registers the assessment that **denial is the active interpretive frame** the cable pushes back on. ^[extracted]

The cable closes with `VERSHBOW`, then `<< END OF DOCUMENT >>`. An info-line stub `Info: TBILISI, AMEMBASSY ROUTINE` appears in the OCR after the signature, possibly an OCR artifact of the release-page handling.

## Why this cable matters (boundary-case framing)

This is the wiki's **second negative anchor** for the [[concepts/diplomatic-channel-uap-reporting|diplomatic-channel UAP reporting]] umbrella — a cable that the FOIA release indexed under "UFO" but that does **not** instantiate the umbrella pattern. It is structurally distinct from the [[references/dos-cable-turkmenistan-2004-11|2004 Ashgabat cable]], the wiki's first negative anchor:

| Diagnostic | 2004 Ashgabat | 2001 Moscow (this cable) |
|---|---|---|
| What "UFO" refers to in subject line | NGO's actual name (Union of UFOlogists of Turkmenabat) | Sarcastic / film-allusion flourish ("Strange Encounters of an MFA Kind") on a Russian denial of airspace violations |
| Substantive cable payload | USAID grant assessment for an NGO | Russian airspace-violation denial; bilateral Russia-Georgia / CFE / OSCE matter |
| UAP narrative content | None — flavour mention of Muradov's "consulted me" claim | None — Tereoken's denial trope ("might as well have been about UFOs") |
| `UFO` subject tag | Absent (`AORC, TSPA, PREL, PGOV, EAID, OSCI, TX`) | Absent (`PREL, MARR, KCFE, UN, OSCE, GG, RS`) |
| Captioning bureau | EUR/CACEN (regional civil-society) | **IO** (International Organization Affairs — UN/OSCE) |
| Embassy stance toward "UFO" mention | Reports without endorsing | Explicitly **rejects** the UFO posit as denial rhetoric ("would be humorous if it were not for the seriousness of the violations") |
| Boundary type | NGO-name keyword match | **Rhetorical / figurative UFO reference in inter-state denial discourse** |
| Result | Civil-society NGO grant assessment record | Russia-Georgia bilateral state-relations record |

Both cables live **outside the diplomatic-channel UAP-reporting umbrella** but are FOIA-indexed inside the UAP-keyword release universe. They identify **two distinct typological mechanisms** by which "UFO" can appear in a DOS cable without there being any UAP narrative payload — see [[concepts/figurative-ufo-rhetoric]] for the consolidated typology.

## Tag-schema observation strengthens to 4-of-4

This cable extends the wiki's running observation about DOS subject-tag schema:

| Cable | UAP-substantive payload? | `UFO` in subject-line text? | `UFO` tag in TAGS? | Active TAGS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [[references/dos-cable-papua-new-guinea-1985-01\|PORT M 00199]] (1985) | Yes | No | No | `MARR`, `PP` |
| [[references/dos-cable-kazakhstan-1994-01\|DUSHANBE 00259]] (1994) | Yes | Yes ("UFO Over Kazakhstan") | No | `TSAP, EAIR, KZ, TI, TAJIK AIR, (RHODES, ED)` |
| **[[references/dos-cable-russia-georgia-2001-10\|MOSCOW 13169]] (2001)** | **No (rhetorical)** | **Yes ("UFOS Over Georgia")** | **No** | `PREL, MARR, KCFE, UN, OSCE, GG, RS` |
| [[references/dos-cable-turkmenistan-2004-11\|ASHGABAT 1028]] (2004) | No (NGO name) | Yes ("Civil Society and UFOs") | No | `AORC, TSPA, PREL, PGOV, EAID, OSCI, TX` |

**4-of-4** absent. The DOS subject-indexing schema continues to route UAP-keyword cables under their substantive bureau (here: Russia-Georgia bilateral / CFE / UN-OSCE multilateral) rather than under any UAP-specific tag — *even when "UFO" appears in the subject-line text*. ^[inferred] The 4-of-4 row strengthens the prior observation by extending it across **two structurally distinct non-substantive cases** (NGO-name match in 2004; rhetorical-denial trope in 2001) in addition to the two substantive cases (1985, 1994).

## Bureau-routing observation extends to 4 distinct bureaus

A second running observation on DOS bureau routing also extends:

| Cable | Action bureau | Question being asked of HQ |
|---|---|---|
| 1985 PNG | **PM** (Pol-Mil) | "Is this us?" — asset disambiguation |
| 1994 Kazakhstan | **OES/S** (Science) | "What was this?" — narrative forwarding |
| 2001 Moscow (this cable) | **IO** (International Organization Affairs) | "How does this play in UN / OSCE / CFE?" — multilateral framing |
| 2004 Ashgabat | **EUR/CACEN** (regional civil-society) | "Should we fund this NGO?" — aid disposition |

Four cables, four distinct action bureaus. **No two of the four cables share a bureau.** ^[extracted] If the bureau-routing-as-question-sorting hypothesis from the umbrella concept holds (PM = "is this us?", OES = "what was this?"), the 2001 Moscow cable extends that pattern to a **third question class**: "is this a multilateral / treaty-relations issue?" — for which IO is the natural fit. The 2004 Ashgabat cable adds a fourth: "is this a regional aid / civil-society item?" — for EUR/CACEN. ^[inferred]

## Source quality assessment

- `source_quality: official` — declassified release of an authentic State Department cable. The "Declassified in Full / Release in Full" stamp from John Powers (DOS Acting-Director) dated 2/25/2026 matches the release stamp on the 1985 PNG, 1994 Kazakhstan, and 2004 Ashgabat cables. ^[extracted]
- The release identifier prefix `059uap00011` is part of the **NARA RG 059** (Records of the Department of State) `059uap*` series — sister files [[references/dos-cable-turkmenistan-2004-11|059uap00012]] (already ingested) and [[references/dos-cable-059uap00013|059uap00013]] (pending ingest). ^[inferred]
- The two prior `CSP-2025-00040` tranche cables (1985 PNG, 1994 Kazakhstan) and the `059uap*` series cables (2001 Moscow, 2004 Ashgabat) appear to be **two distinct release tranches** of UAP-keyword DOS cables. ^[inferred]
- Single-source artifact. No follow-on traffic from MOSCOW, TBILISI, USOSCE, USUN, BAKU, YEREVAN, or any of the DoD info-addressees is in the corpus.
