# NASA PAO Tape T-00763(R1b) — Gemini 7 Bogey Release Commentary (4 Dec 1965)

A **4-page declassified / publicly-released NASA Public Affairs Office (PAO) tape transcript**, NARA-tagged as item `255_t_763_r1b_transcripts` (Record Group 255, Records of the National Aeronautics and Space Administration). The artifact records the **PAO Release Commentary** for the **GT-7 / GT-6A rendezvous mission** during which Gemini 7 commander [[entities/frank-borman|Frank Borman]] reports *"a bogey at 10 o'clock high"* on air-to-ground voice loop at approximately **4 hours 24 minutes mission elapsed time** (≈ 1954Z, 4 December 1965). It is the wiki's first **manned-spaceflight** UAP-adjacent record, the first **NASA-originated** record, and the first **PAO release-by-default** record — institutionally distinct from every other intake channel in the corpus.

## Why this artifact is institutionally distinct

Every prior 1944–1994 record in the wiki passed through a **classified intake apparatus** (SHAEF Air Staff routing, AFOIR-CO field-installation channel, USAFE TS major-command channel, USAIRA HUMINT, FBI investigative file, DOS cable). This artifact passes through **none** of them. The PAO Release Commentary is a **release-by-default** instrument — its purpose is *to play the tape for the press* on the same news cycle as the event, with brief framing commentary. NASA's intake channel for an in-mission unidentified-object call inverts the SECRET/TS-default posture of every other US-government UAP-adjacent channel in the corpus. ^[inferred]

This is the wiki's first record where the originating agency's institutional incentive on a UAP-adjacent observation is **disclosure**, not classification. Whether the disclosure was complete (vs. selectively edited) is not addressable from this artifact alone. ^[inferred]

## OCR structure caveat

The source bundle contains two structurally different transcriptions of the same air-to-ground audio:

- **Page 0** (typed) is the canonical PAO release transcript with cleaner punctuation and standard naming (`Borman`, `LOVELL`, `BOOSTER`, `ROGER`).
- **Pages 1–3** (handwritten, OCR'd) are a working / informal variant with heavy OCR artifacts: `delivered` for `debris`, `polvorok` for `polar orbit`, `Lowell` / `Bounan` / `bounan` for `Lovell` / `Borman`, `GEMINE` / `Gemyn` / `gemine` for `Gemini`, `Gemcom` / `Gem Comtrol` for `Gemini Control`, `genuine Control` for `Gemini Control`, `delta` for `polar`.

These are the **same underlying audio**, not two independent witness statements. Quotations on this and downstream pages cite **page 0** as canonical and flag where the variant transcriptions disagree.

A **second independent OCR pass** of the same tape was later ingested as [[references/nasa-uap-d3-gemini-7-transcript-1965|d3 — Gemini 7 PAO Tape T-00763(R1b) Transcript (Variant OCR Pass)]] (3 pages: 1 typed + 2 handwritten variant). The d3 pass confirms page 0's typed transcript and reproduces a similar (but distinct) error profile on the handwritten variant pages — including the runaway *"a small pail, a small pail..."* loop where the canonical reads *"path of the vehicle at 90 degrees"*, and *"tuthers"* (d3) vs *"trillions"* (canonical) for Lovell's booster description. The convergence of two independent OCR passes on page 0 raises confidence in the typed transcript; the divergence on pages 1–3 is **a property of the handwritten variant itself**, not of the OCR engine. ^[inferred]

## Three classes of object referenced in the transcript

The PAO commentary explicitly distinguishes **three** in-frame objects, all observed in the same exchange:

| # | Object | Witness | Description | Distance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The bogey** | Borman (first) | "A bogey at 10 o'clock high" — no morphology given | unstated |
| 2 | **The booster** | Borman + Lovell | Spent Titan-II second stage; "brilliant body in the sun against a black background with **trillions of particles** on it" (Lovell); at Lovell's 2 o'clock position, ahead of the spacecraft, "slowly tumbling" | unstated; gradually receding |
| 3 | **The particles** | Borman | "Hundreds of little particles" passing 3–4 miles to the left, on a track "at 90 degrees" to the spacecraft's vehicle path; "they're going into polar orbit" | 3–4 mi |

PAO's framing on page 0 makes the three-object structure explicit: *"the third and unidentified object of course was a bogey... there were several references to the bogey."* ^[extracted] Whether the **bogey** was a separate unidentified object distinct from the booster is itself **ambiguous in the source**: HOUSTON immediately asks *"is that the booster or is that a natural sighting?"*; Borman replies *"a what?"* and (eventually) *"we have debris up here — this is an actual sighting"* — without resolving whether his initial *"bogey at ten o'clock high"* was being separated from the booster he subsequently reports having "in sight." ^[ambiguous] PAO's later commentary (page 0) treats the bogey as a *third* object beyond booster and particles; Borman's call itself does not unambiguously say so.

## The transmission sequence (page 0 canonical)

1. **HOUSTON**: hail; *"Loud and clear, Seven — go ahead."*
2. **S/C BORMAN**: *"A bogey at ten o'clock high."*
3. **HOUSTON**: *"Say again, Seven?"*
4. **S/C BORMAN**: *"I said we have a bogey at ten o'clock high."*
5. **HOUSTON**: *"Roger. Gemini-7 — is that the booster or is that a natural sighting?"*
6. **S/C**: *"A what?"* — apparent miscomms beat
7. **HOUSTON**: *"Say again, Seven."*
8. **S/C BORMAN**: *"We have debris up here — this is an actual sighting."*
9. **HOUSTON**: requests distance / size
10. **S/C BORMAN**: *"We also have the booster in sight."*
11. **HOUSTON** acknowledges
12. **S/C BORMAN**: hundreds of small particles 3–4 mi to left, on a path at 90° to the vehicle, "going into polar orbit"
13. **HOUSTON** clarifies; **S/C** confirms
14. **HOUSTON**: *"Were these particles in addition to the booster and the bogey at ten o'clock high?"*
15. **S/C LOVELL**: *"Roger. I have the booster on my side. It's a brilliant body in the sun against a black background with trillions of particles on it."*
16. **HOUSTON**: direction?
17. **S/C LOVELL**: *"It's about at my 2 o'clock position."*
18. **HOUSTON**: *"Does that mean it's ahead of you?"*
19. **S/C LOVELL**: *"It's ahead of us at 2 o'clock, slowly tumbling. Roger."*
20. **PAO**: closes commentary at 04 hr 24 min MET.

The ambiguity at step 4–5 — Houston *immediately* offers booster-or-natural-sighting as the dichotomy, and Borman does not directly choose between them — is the load-bearing line for downstream interpretation. ^[inferred]

## What the source establishes

- **Three distinct in-frame objects** were called out by GT-7 in the same air-to-ground window: a "bogey", the booster, and a particle stream. ^[extracted]
- The **booster** was visible to both crewmen, in different relative bearings: Borman at "ten o'clock high" (initially), Lovell at "two o'clock". ^[extracted] The "ten o'clock high" / "two o'clock" pair is consistent with two crewmen oriented opposite each other in the spacecraft cabin observing the same object through different windows. ^[inferred]
- The **particle stream** was distinct from the booster and from the bogey; it was on a track at **90° to the spacecraft's track** (i.e., crossing the orbital plane), described as "going into polar orbit". ^[extracted]
- The **booster** carried "trillions of particles on it" — almost certainly **outgassing / venting / paint-fleck / ice-crystal effluent** typical of a spent upper stage in vacuum. ^[inferred] Distinct from the separate "hundreds of particles" in the cross-track stream.
- The **bogey** itself has **no recorded morphology**. The witnesses report **bearing only** ("ten o'clock high"); no shape, color, size, distance, or motion is attributed to the bogey distinct from the other two object classes. ^[extracted]
- The exchange is **05 min 34 sec long** (per the page 3 timing line). ^[extracted]

## What the source does *not* establish

- Whether the "bogey" was a separate object from the booster, or an initial reporting of the booster before Borman fully visually acquired it. ^[ambiguous] The transcript supports both readings.
- Whether the bogey was a UFO in the modern sense. *"Bogey"* in aviation/military terminology denotes any unidentified radar/visual contact — not a confirmed UAP. ^[inferred] The wiki should not silently elevate "bogey" to "UAP" without flagging this.
- Whether NASA Mission Control treated this exchange as anomalous or routine. PAO's framing — playing the tape openly the same day, calling out the bogey explicitly — is consistent with **routine but newsworthy** treatment, not with an attempt at suppression. ^[inferred]
- Whether any photographs, voice-recorder dumps, or post-mission debriefs add detail beyond this PAO release. ^[open]

## Cross-corpus connections

- **First [[concepts/orbital-uap-sighting|orbital / outside-atmosphere observation]]** in the wiki. Every prior witness-side sighting page is from inside the atmosphere or on the ground.
- **First astronaut witness class.** [[entities/frank-borman|Borman]] and [[entities/jim-lovell|Lovell]] are USAF/USN test-pilot-class trained observers — see Borman's later 1969 Apollo 8 commission and Lovell's later Gemini 12 + Apollo 13 commands.
- **First NASA-originating record.** All prior US-military / federal records are USAF / Army / Navy / FBI / DOS — never NASA.
- **First "bogey" terminology** in the corpus. Prior UAP-witness-side language is "flying disc", "saucer", "object", "ball of fire", "foo fighter", "orb". The aviation-jargon use of "bogey" enters here. ^[inferred]
- **First PAO release channel.** The artifact's institutional posture is *broadcast on tape replay to press the same news cycle* — opposite the SECRET / TS / NOFORN / OUO defaults of every other intake channel ([[concepts/usaira-reporting-channel|USAIRA]], AFOIR-CO, USAFE TS, FBI investigative file, DOS cable, NASC OUO).
- **Booster / particle behavior comparator**: the "trillions of particles" on the booster surface is consistent with the spent-stage outgassing that recurs across early-orbital photography of upper stages; not a UAP signature in itself. ^[inferred]
- **Cross-track 90° particle stream**: difficult to attribute to debris from the same launch. *Plausible candidates*: an unrelated previously-launched payload's debris field; ice / urine dump from another orbital object; or unidentified. ^[inferred] No source-side disambiguation.
