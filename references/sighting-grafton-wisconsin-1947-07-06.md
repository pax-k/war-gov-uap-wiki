# Sighting — Grafton WI / Brasky Disc (6 July 1947)

The 6 July 1947 alleged disc strike on **St Joseph's Catholic Church in Grafton, WI** (~45 miles north of Milwaukee), reported by **Father Joseph Brasky** — the **founding artifact of FBI HQ file 62-HQ-83894**, filed as **serial 1**. ^[extracted] The case is paradigm-defining for the [[concepts/saucer-as-hoax-default|hoax-default operating presumption]] that governs the FBI corpus from this point forward. ^[inferred]

## What happened (per the OCR'd press)

Father Brasky's account, given to Milwaukee newspaper reporters: ^[extracted]

> "I usually get up about 5 a.m. on Sundays. I was at the front door of my house, about to take my dog out for a walk, when I heard a 'bang!' like a big firecracker. I stepped outside and found this disk on the church lawn. Although the grass was still wet from the night rain, the disk was too hot to pick up. I noticed the glass ball had been knocked off one of the lightning rods on the church, so I suppose it struck that in landing."

The recovered object: **a steel circular saw blade, ~16-19 inches diameter, ~1/8 inch thick, with saw teeth**, a 1-3/8 inch hole in the center carrying a few wires; at each end of the wire cluster a small condenser-like object ~3 inches long wrapped in still-sticky black tape; stamped *"Approved, Dunlap"* (i.e. **Dunlop tire-brand-name**, or **Dunlap as a manufacturer name**). ^[extracted] An accompanying news photo by Sentinel staff photographer Ernest Anheuser shows Brasky holding the saw in front of the church, pointing at the lightning rod. ^[extracted]

## How the FBI processed it

The case acquired serial 1 status not because the FBI investigated it but because the **United Press wire flagged the FBI by name** in the original story (Brasky claimed to have notified an FBI agent). ^[extracted] The processing trail: ^[extracted]

- **7 Jul 1947 ~10:45 AM**: SAC Johnson (Milwaukee) called by SA H. B. Fletcher (HQ) — Johnson clarifies that Brasky **never contacted FBI Milwaukee**; UP/AP reporters had been hounding him at his residence between 1-6 AM that morning; AP was already declining to release the story because *"the priest was intoxicated and… all that was involved was a circular saw."* ^[extracted]
- **7 Jul 1947 evening**: HQ instruction to Milwaukee — **no investigation, but report any subsequent contact from Brasky immediately**. ^[extracted]
- **8 Jul 1947**: Milwaukee SAC sends formal letter to HQ summarizing his telephone exchanges with UP and AP. ^[extracted]
- **No subsequent investigation. No Bureau lab examination. No physical-fragment retention.** ^[extracted]

## Why it matters institutionally

The Brasky case **is** the founding template for what 62-HQ-83894 will hold in its early months. ^[inferred] Within section 1 (serials 1-52), at least **9 distinct events** match the Brasky pattern — a kitchen-sink fabricated disc with similar hardware: ^[extracted]

| Serial | Location | Date | Object profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Grafton WI | 6 Jul 1947 | Dunlop circular saw + wires + tape-wrapped condenser-tubes |
| 3 | Shreveport-area LA | 7 Jul 1947 | F. C. Hairston disc — revealed prank |
| 4 | Omaha NE | 7 Jul 1947 | Reibold/Sniffen 1¼" silver-dollar disc — pipe ash per Dr. Kenny |
| 16 | Black River Falls WI | 10 Jul 1947 | Hanson 17" cardboard disc with painted aluminum dope + RCA photocell + small motor + propeller |
| 17 | Laurel MD | 11 Jul 1947 | Parker "machinery still buzzing" disc — Gulf Oil sign + garbage-can lid + flashlight bulb + dry cell + buzzer |
| 19 | Shreveport LA | 7 Jul 1947 | Hairston 16" aluminum disc + 2-inch coils + copper wires + "Made in USA"-stamped center object |
| 21 | Twin Falls ID | 11 Jul 1947 | Hasterbrook 30½" disc + plastic dome + "Inspected TS" coil + 8 stove-bolts |
| 23 | Burbank CA | 9 Jul 1947 | Burst-into-flame disc, fire chief held it for FBI |
| 24 | North Hollywood CA | 9 Jul 1947 | Two-convex-discs-fused-with-iron-fin assembly — declared definite hoax by G-2 Maj Hempstead; **Frank V. Brown identifies a 16-yr-old Chevron-station employee as likely fabricator** ^[extracted] |

The shared signature — **disc + wires + tubes / coils / motors + battery + aluminum-paint** — is consistent with **post-Independence-Day-firework era amateur hardware** assembled by *"young high school students"* (Brown's identification on serial 24). ^[inferred] The pattern is described in [[concepts/saucer-as-hoax-default]].

## Brasky himself

Father Brasky is described in the press coverage as: ^[extracted]

- The pastor of **St Joseph's Catholic Church**, Grafton WI;
- A **fishing enthusiast** with a "trinket collection" (bass bottles, foldable Manila fish-pole-as-cane);
- The author of a privately-published "Fish Tales" pamphlet with planned revisions;
- According to Associated Press reporting (per FBI Milwaukee SAC's relay to HQ), **intoxicated at the time of his initial press interview**. ^[extracted]

He **declined to part with the saw blade**, kept it in his trinket collection, and reportedly told reporters *"It may or may not be one of those flying disks that have been reported all over the country. Let's just call it a 'what is it?'"* ^[extracted]

## Open threads

- **"Approved, Dunlap"** stamping — Dunlap as a saw manufacturer is plausible (Dunlap Manufacturing Co. of Indianapolis was an industrial-tool brand sold by Sears Roebuck in this era); this is consistent with prankster having scavenged a discarded saw blade. ^[inferred]
- **Identify the FBI agent Brasky claimed to know** — section 1 is silent on this. ^[open]
- **Confirm the "intoxicated priest" framing** as AP's actual finding (currently extracted from FBI Milwaukee's relay only). ^[open]
- **Establish whether Brasky's saw blade survives** — the priest declined to surrender it; final disposition unknown. ^[open]
