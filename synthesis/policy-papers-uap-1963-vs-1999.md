# UAP Policy Papers — Hunter 1963 vs. COMETA 1999

The wiki's policy-staff layer (per [[synthesis/early-uap-policy-vs-operational-track]]) now has **two artifacts** 36 years apart:

- **[[references/hunter-1963-space-alien-race-memo|Hunter 1963 NASC memo]]** — US, Executive Office of the President, ~6 pages, single-author, OFFICIAL USE ONLY, internal-routing.
- **[[references/cometa-report-1999|COMETA Report 1999]]** — France, private committee of retired senior officers and IHEDN auditors, ~90 pages, multi-author, public-magazine-published.

Both occupy the same conceptual layer: **conditional-policy thought-papers** that ask "what would / should we do if?" — but they instantiate it from different national, institutional, and rhetorical positions.

## What the two papers agree on

- **The conditional policy framing is necessary.** Both write the conditional policy paper, even when they doubt it will be acted on. The form *exists* in both moments.
- **The space-alien policy question is foreseeably diplomatic, not solely military.** Hunter routes via State/OISA; COMETA recommends EU-level diplomatic démarches. Both pre-figure modern multi-track UAP coordination.
- **Asymmetry of physics-command is the policy-relevant variable.** Hunter's three-class taxonomy is exactly about asymmetry-driven posture; COMETA's MHD and §13 thought-experiment are different framings of the same asymmetry.
- **Public unpreparedness is a structural problem.** Hunter: "grand panic." COMETA Ch. 14: psychological / cultural / religious destabilization risks; calls for media preparation.

## What the two papers diverge on

- **Whether UAP cases are real**. Hunter explicitly does not commit. COMETA explicitly commits — *"the extraterrestrial hypothesis is, by far, the best scientific hypothesis."*
- **Whether US is in [[concepts/uap-state-knowledge-classes|class (d)]]**. Hunter offers no view. COMETA strongly hints (without naming) that the US has class-(d) status — implicit in the Roswell appendix, MacArthur/Reagan quotes, and "preferential contacts" framing.
- **Whether substantial action is possible now**. Hunter says no. COMETA says yes (six recommendations).
- **Methodological orientation**. Hunter is pure thought-experiment; COMETA grounds itself in case investigations and scientific methodology (cf. Lebeau's foreword on meteorites).
- **Optimism**. Hunter's tone is wry resignation. COMETA's tone is urgent advocacy.

## Why the divergence?

Three plausible drivers (none mutually exclusive):

1. **36 years of accumulated case data.** GEPAN/SEPRA had run since 1977 (~22 years by 1999); Project SIGN was 1 year old in 1948 (15 years old in 1963). COMETA had a richer empirical foundation. ^[inferred]
2. **Outside-government authorship.** Private associations of senior figures can commit to claims that internal staff cannot. NASC was constrained by interagency-coordination norms; COMETA had no such constraints. ^[inferred]
3. **Different national position vis-à-vis the US asymmetry.** France has standing to demand class-(d) disclosure from a putative class-(d) US; a US staffer writing inside US government cannot. ^[inferred]
