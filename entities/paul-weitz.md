# Paul J. Weitz

**Paul Joseph Weitz** (1932–2017) — **US Navy naval aviator** and **NASA Astronaut Group 5** ("the Original Nineteen", 1966), Pilot of **[[entities/skylab|Skylab 2]]** (25 May – 22 Jun 1973). ^[inferred]

In the wiki's UAP corpus, Weitz enters as a **secondary speaker** on the **[[references/nasa-uap-d7-skylab-technical-crew-debriefing-1973|d7 Skylab Technical Crew Debriefing]]** SL-2 §23 *Unusual or Unexpected Visual Phenomenon* discussion — providing the corpus's **first explicit single-eye-localization observation** and **first entrance-streak / exit-streak double-event direct observation** in [[concepts/cosmic-ray-light-flashes|cosmic-ray retinal flash]] testimony.

## SL-2 d7 §23 testimony

Weitz's contributions:

> **WEITZ**: *"I would say mine were primarily in the left eye for some reason."* ^[extracted]

> **WEITZ**: *"I had a couple that I thought were cosmic particles. I saw an entrance streak and an exit streak."* ^[extracted]

> **WEITZ**: *"Where, bing-bing, it seemed like it was one side of the eyeball, and then the other side."* ^[extracted]

The substantive content for the corpus:

1. **First explicit single-eye preference observation in the corpus** — Weitz's *"primarily in the left eye"* is the corpus's first localized-to-one-eye datum; [[entities/pete-conrad|Conrad]] corroborates that the phenomenon **can** be isolated to one eye with concentration (*"You have to concentrate but you can determine they are in one eye"*), but Kerwin reports he *"could not"* localize. ^[extracted] The eye-asymmetry is **physically consistent** with single-particle traversal of one eye but **not naturally explained** at the gross level — Weitz's left-eye preference is structurally interesting but not characterized in the source. ^[inferred]
2. **First entrance-streak / exit-streak direct observation** — Weitz's *"entrance streak and an exit streak"* and *"bing-bing, it seemed like it was one side of the eyeball, and then the other side"* are **direct phenomenological confirmation** of [[entities/buzz-aldrin|Aldrin]]'s 1969 d4 *"double flashes ... at points separated by maybe a foot"* model. ^[inferred] The 4-year-and-different-mission separation between Aldrin's d4 cislunar-coast observation (1969) and Weitz's d7 SL-2 LEO observation (1973) means **two independent astronauts on two different missions in two different orbital regimes** observed the **same chord-track double-event structure** — strengthening the cosmic-ray-on-retina chord-track model substantially. ^[inferred]
3. **Particle-attribution self-framing** — *"I had a couple that I thought were cosmic particles"* — **astronaut-self-attributed-conventional in the same testimony**. Weitz is the corpus's first witness to use the term *"cosmic particles"* directly. ^[extracted] (Aldrin's d4 1969 framing was *"some sort of penetration"* / *"some kind of an atomic particle"*; Weitz's 1973 framing has been **promoted to direct identification**, consistent with the astronaut-corps lore by 1973.) ^[inferred]

## Witness pedigree

Weitz is the corpus's **first NASA Group 5 ("Original Nineteen")** astronaut. The Group 5 selection (1966) is the first NASA group selected explicitly for the **post-Apollo** program (Skylab + Shuttle), with selection criteria emphasizing **engineering-test-pilot** background. ^[inferred] Weitz is also the corpus's **first witness to fly on both an orbital station mission (Skylab 2)** and **a Shuttle mission (STS-6)** — but only the Skylab 2 testimony is in the corpus to date.
