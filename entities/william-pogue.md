# William R. Pogue

**William Reid "Bill" Pogue** (1930–2014) — **US Air Force fighter pilot** (Korean War combat veteran, USAF Thunderbirds aerobatic team alumnus) and **NASA Astronaut Group 5** ("the Original Nineteen", 1966 — same group as **[[entities/gerald-carr|Gerald Carr]]**, **[[entities/jack-lousma|Jack Lousma]]**, and **[[entities/paul-weitz|Paul Weitz]]**), Pilot of **[[entities/skylab|Skylab 4]]** (16 Nov 1973 – 8 Feb 1974). ^[inferred]

In the wiki's UAP corpus, Pogue enters as a **named speaker** on the **[[references/nasa-uap-d7-skylab-technical-crew-debriefing-1973|d7 Skylab Technical Crew Debriefing]]** SL-4 §7 *Visual Sightings / Unusual Events* discussion — but his preserved §7 testimony covers **OWS Heat Exchanger lint accumulation** and the **PCU composite connector mechanical-failure mode during EVA** (non-UAP mission-systems content), not the flashing-lights observation that **[[entities/gerald-carr|Carr]]** articulates. ^[extracted] Pogue enters the corpus as a **co-witness in the §7 frame** rather than as a primary articulator of any UAP-adjacent observation.

## SL-4 d7 §7 testimony

> **POGUE**: *"The OWS Heat Exchangers: There's a major design flaw there in that filters were not installed upstream of the OWS heat exchanger vanes. When we first arrived, the vanes were so uniformly coated with lint that I thought there was some kind of anodized surface on them. I was never fully convinced that I had done the vacumming job properly; therefore, I fabricated a special tool that fit flush against those surface vanes so that I could exert a good vacuum. Though they are not supposed to be condensate heat exchangers, I sucked quite a bit of condensate water out of them. I tried the best I could to keep those things clean, yet I never did get all that lint pulled out of them. That is why I think we needed a filter in the system."* ^[extracted]

> **POGUE**: *"One thing that was not mentioned on air-to-ground as a possible cause of the problem was the single-point failure that exists in the mechanical way that the PCU composite connector is hooked to the PCU. I was able during EVA, maneuvering through the clothesline ropes, not only to open the lock but also to extend the arm which pulls the PCU composite connector off."* ^[extracted]

These passages are **mission-systems content, not UAP-relevant**. ^[extracted] Pogue is included in the corpus because:

1. He is **a member of the SL-4 crew on the d7 §7 page** where [[entities/gerald-carr|Carr]] articulates the corpus's **second sub-class D observation** (flashing lights, astronaut-self-attributed to Skylab debris/satellites).
2. By convention in the corpus's astronaut layer, all three crew members of a corpus mission frame enter as entities (compare: [[entities/dick-gordon|Gordon]] on Apollo 12 d1 as CMP; [[entities/edward-gibson|Gibson]] on SL-4 d7 as SP).
3. Pogue is the corpus's **fourth Group-5 astronaut witness** (after [[entities/paul-weitz|Weitz]] SL-2, [[entities/jack-lousma|Lousma]] SL-3, [[entities/gerald-carr|Carr]] SL-4) — completing the four-of-six Group-5 Skylab footprint in the corpus.

## Witness pedigree

Pogue is the corpus's **first USAF-fighter-pilot Group-5 astronaut witness** — distinct from the USMC Group-5 cohort ([[entities/jack-lousma|Lousma]], [[entities/gerald-carr|Carr]]) and the USN Group-5 cohort ([[entities/paul-weitz|Weitz]]). His **Korean War combat experience** and **Thunderbirds aerobatic-display flying** make him a structurally interesting witness — combat-experienced fighter pilots are typically over-represented in UAP-witness pedigrees in the broader literature, but Pogue's d7 §7 testimony is unambiguously **non-UAP** (heat exchanger / PCU). ^[inferred]
