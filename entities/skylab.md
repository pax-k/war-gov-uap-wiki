# Skylab

NASA's **Skylab** — the **first United States orbital space station**, launched **14 May 1973** as Skylab 1 (the unmanned station) and visited by **three crewed missions** (**Skylab 2** May–Jun 1973, **Skylab 3** Jul–Sep 1973, **Skylab 4** Nov 1973 – Feb 1974). The station re-entered Earth's atmosphere on **11 July 1979** over Western Australia and the Indian Ocean. ^[inferred] Skylab is the corpus's **first orbital-station mission frame** — distinct from the rendezvous-class missions (Gemini), the lunar-class missions (Apollo), and the modern long-duration station (ISS).

In the wiki's UAP corpus, Skylab enters as the **mission frame** for the **[[references/nasa-uap-d7-skylab-technical-crew-debriefing-1973|d7 Technical Crew Debriefings]]** — a composite OCR bundle drawn from all three Skylab crewed missions (JSC-08053 SL-2 Jun 1973; JSC-08478 SL-3 Oct 1973; JSC-08809 SL-4 Feb 1974). The d7 artifact contributes **three structurally distinct UAP-adjacent observations** to the corpus, one per mission.

## Mission profile (overview)

- **Orbital station**: Skylab 1 — the **converted Saturn V S-IVB third stage** as a "wet workshop" originally, redesigned as a "dry workshop" pre-launch. ^[inferred] (This is structurally interesting in the corpus: the Skylab orbital workshop **is** the S-IVB stage that on Apollo missions was the conventional candidate explicitly ruled out by Mission Control range data — see [[concepts/saturn-v-s-ivb-stage]]. Skylab's S-IVB became the station the astronauts inhabited; on Apollo it was a free body in solar/lunar-flyby orbit. ^[inferred])
- **Launch vehicle**: Saturn V (SA-513) for Skylab 1; Saturn IB for SL-2, SL-3, SL-4 crew launches. ^[inferred]
- **Orbital altitude**: ~435 km (~270 mi) — **inside Earth's magnetosphere**, but Skylab's ground track **traverses the [[concepts/cosmic-ray-light-flashes|South Atlantic Anomaly (SAA)]]** several times per day. ^[inferred] This altitude/orbit regime is **fundamentally different** from the Apollo cislunar-coast environment that produced the [[entities/buzz-aldrin|Aldrin]] d4 1969 first-principles cosmic-ray-flash testimony and the [[entities/harrison-schmitt|Schmitt]] d6 1972 ALFMED instrumented follow-up.
- **Inclination**: ~50° — moderate-inclination orbit, repeatedly passing through the **SAA** and over the geomagnetic equator. ^[inferred]
- **Mission lengths**: SL-2 ~28 days, SL-3 ~59 days, SL-4 ~84 days. ^[inferred]
- **Distinguishing role in NASA program**: Skylab was the **bridge between Apollo and Shuttle** — first long-duration US crewed missions; first orbital workshop; first instance of multi-month medical / behavioral / spaceflight-physiology experiments on US astronauts. ^[inferred]

## Mission lineage

Skylab 1's orbital workshop **is structurally a converted Saturn V S-IVB third stage**, originally planned as a "wet workshop" (orbital fueling and habitation) and redesigned to a "dry workshop" before launch. ^[inferred] This makes Skylab the **only NASA mission frame in the corpus** where the **same physical hardware class (S-IVB)** that served as the Apollo 11 d4 conventional-elimination candidate **served as the Skylab habitable orbital workshop**. ^[inferred] The corpus's [[concepts/saturn-v-s-ivb-stage|S-IVB concept page]] should track this dual role: 1969 Apollo 11 — S-IVB as **free body 6,000 mi from Apollo**, ruled out by ranging; 1973–79 Skylab — S-IVB as the **orbital station itself**.

The **SL-2/3/4 trifecta** spans three mission-tempo regimes:
- **SL-2 (28 days)**: shakedown / initial crewed activation; included the [[entities/paul-weitz|Weitz]] / [[entities/pete-conrad|Conrad]] EVA repair of the deployed solar shade after micrometeoroid-shield loss at launch. ^[inferred]
- **SL-3 (59 days)**: maturity / extended occupation; longest-to-date US crewed spaceflight at the time. ^[inferred]
- **SL-4 (84 days)**: peak duration / behavioral-overload findings ("Skylab strike" episode 28 Dec 1973, when the [[entities/gerald-carr|Carr]] crew briefly stopped accepting flight-plan timeline overload). ^[inferred]

The d7 OCR excerpt does **not** preserve material on the deployment-failure / EVA-repair / shutter-strike contexts — only the §7 / §20 / §23 visual-sightings and unusual-events sections. ^[extracted]

## Institutional context

Skylab missions were operated under the **[[entities/nasa|NASA]] / Lyndon B. Johnson Space Center (JSC) Houston** organizational chart — JSC was renamed from **MSC (Manned Spacecraft Center)** in February 1973, between the d6 Apollo 17 technical debrief (4 Jan 1973, "Lyndon B. Johnson Space Center" already on cover) and the d7 SL-2 cover (30 Jun 1973, also "Lyndon B. Johnson Space Center"). ^[extracted] All three Skylab debriefings (JSC-08053, JSC-08478, JSC-08809) were prepared by the **Training Office, Crew Training and Simulation Division** (SL-2 / SL-3) — renamed to **Crew Training and Procedures Division** between SL-3 and SL-4 (visible on the Feb 1974 SL-4 cover). ^[extracted]

The **declassification posture** for the d7 Skylab debriefings is the **lightest** in the corpus's NASA d-cluster: covers carry only the FOIA notice (5 U.S.C. 552 / NASA Policy Directive 1382.2), no CONFIDENTIAL or SECRET classification stamp visible in the OCR. This contrasts with d4 Apollo 11 (CONFIDENTIAL Group 4 — declassification authority RF 74365 / Vol II 6/1/72 under E.O. 11652) and d6 Apollo 17 (CONFIDENTIAL with 90-day auto-declassification clause). The **post-Apollo NASA institutional shift** moved standard technical debriefings out of the CONFIDENTIAL bucket. ^[inferred]

The mission **straddles the gap between [[concepts/project-sign|Project BLUE BOOK]] closure** (17 December 1969) and the **modern UAP / [[entities/aaro|AARO]]-era institutional layer** (post-2017). No formal USAF / DoD UAP investigative body operated during the Skylab era for civilian astronaut testimony to be routed to. ^[inferred]

## Recurring witnesses

Skylab's d7 ingest surfaces **two repeat witnesses** from the d-cluster:
- **[[entities/pete-conrad|Pete Conrad]]** (Apollo 12 CDR + SL-2 CDR) — corpus's first multi-mission witness (Apollo 12 d1 non-UAP empirical confirmation of co-orbital debris baseline; SL-2 d7 cosmic-ray-flash co-witness). The two observations occupy two structurally different polarities. ^[inferred]
- **[[entities/alan-bean|Alan Bean]]** (Apollo 12 LMP + SL-3 CDR) — corpus's second multi-mission witness (Apollo 12 d1 sub-class D AOT particle-flash observation; SL-3 d7 sub-class F multi-witness red-object observation). The two observations occupy two structurally different sub-classes (D vs F) — paralleling [[entities/buzz-aldrin|Aldrin]]'s d4 cylinder-vs-flashes split (sub-class E vs cosmic-ray-flash). ^[inferred]

The Apollo-12-to-Skylab-2/3 cohort overlap is **structurally not coincidental**: NASA's Apollo-era astronaut corps was the trained-witness pool for Skylab. The d-cluster surfaces this through the recurring witnesses without artifacts having been independently coordinated. ^[inferred]
