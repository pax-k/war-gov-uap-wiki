# Richard B. Russell

US Senator (D-GA), Chairman of the **Senate Armed Services Committee** in October 1955. ^[inferred] *(Senatorial role and chairmanship are general historical background; the source records him only as "Sen. Russell" and "Senator" with travel companions identified as Committee staff.)*

## Significance to the wiki

Russell is the **first-witness** of the **first** of the two flying discs sighted on **4 October 1955, 1910 hours**, between **ATJATY** and **ADZHLJABUL** in the Trans-Caucasus USSR — see [[references/sighting-trans-caucasus-1955-10-04]]. He recognized the object as "drastically different" and called **[[entities/e-u-hathaway|Lt Col E. U. Hathaway]]** and **[[entities/ruben-efron|Mr. Ruben Efron]]** to the train window in time to see it. All three then witnessed the second disc together about a minute later.

Per [[references/iaf-ir-193-55-russell-1955|USAIRA Prague IR 193-55]] (page 3): *"Sen. Russell saw the first flying saucer or flying disc ascend and pass over the train on a northerly course, taking off from the left side of the train's direction."* ^[extracted]

## Why this matters

Russell is the **first Senatorial-class witness** in the wiki's UAP corpus. ^[inferred] As Chairman of the Senate Armed Services Committee, he had:

- the **highest classified-access of any UAP witness** in the wiki's records;
- direct **oversight authority** over the USAF (and by extension the [[concepts/project-sign|Project SIGN]] / Project GRUDGE / Project BLUE BOOK lineage that was running at the time of the sighting);
- the institutional standing to **demand a full briefing** if he wanted one.

Despite that, Russell **did not attend the 13 October 1955 USAIRA Prague debrief**. He was occupied with Acting Chief of Mission Harold Vedler at the time and the Russell party departed Prague aboard Swissair for Rome on 13 Oct. Everything attributed to Russell in [[references/iaf-ir-193-55-russell-1955|IR 193-55]] arrives via Hathaway and Efron. ^[extracted]

## Travel context

Russell's 1955 trip was an organized **Senate Armed Services Committee mission** through the USSR and Czechoslovakia, accompanied by Hathaway (Army staff) and Efron (Committee Consultant). The recovered itinerary fragments from [[references/iaf-ir-193-55-russell-1955]]:

- **Pre-4 Oct 55** — Kiev → Black Sea (Sochi) → Tbilisi (Tiflis)
- **4 Oct 55, 1910 hrs** — sighting between ATJATY and ADZHLJABUL (Trans-Caucasus rail leg)
- **5 Oct 55** — Tiflis observations (banana-shaped helicopter)
- **9 Oct 55** — Dnepropetrovsk Airfield (42 jet fighters + 1 jet bomber)
- **12 Oct 55, ~early morning** — COP (Chop) USSR-Czech border; rail-gauge switch
- **12 Oct 55, ~2145 (`p.m.`)** — arrival Wilson Station, Prague
- **13 Oct 55, 0900–1200** — debrief at Czechoslovak Army-Attaché office
- **13 Oct 55, afternoon** — depart Prague aboard Swissair for Rome

Russell himself appears as the witness only in the sighting and in the 12 Oct request to "see some familiar places" he had visited in **Prague in 1927**. ^[extracted] The 1927 anchor places Russell's pre-Senate-era foreign travel earlier than is typically captured in his biographical record. ^[inferred]

## Russell and the Egorov contact

Russell extended an invitation for **`EGOROV`** — a self-identified retired ~55-year-old Russian pilot encountered between Tiflis and Sochi who claimed to have flown one of the original trans-polar Russian flights to Washington State (1935 or 1936) — to visit his US Senate office if he ever came to the United States. ^[extracted] This is the **only diplomatic gesture by Russell** preserved in the bundle. The probable trans-polar candidates are the **June and July 1937** Chkalov and Gromov flights, not 1935/36. ^[inferred] [[entities/thomas-s-ryan|Lt Col Ryan]]'s commentary: *"It is difficult for me to believe that this experienced pilot in polar navigation would be retired and doing nothing but travelling around the Trans Caucasus region with a young engineer."* ^[extracted]
