# International Flying Saucer Bureau (IFSB)

A **civilian saucer-research organization** founded ~1952 in **Bridgeport, Connecticut** by **Albert K. Bender** — the earliest organized civilian saucer-club retained at FBI HQ in the wiki corpus. ^[inferred] Publisher of the **quarterly magazine *Space Review***. Captured in [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-7|FBI HQ 62-HQ-83894 section 7]] serial 326 (pages 113-124) as the **entire January 1953 issue of *Space Review* (Vol II, No. 1)** retained as a clipped magazine, plus correspondence between IFSB representative letters and Civil Defense Director Robert D. Wolf of Franklin IN. ^[extracted]

## Franklin, Indiana — the showcase chapter

The Jan 1953 issue's **headline article**: **"Franklin, Indiana Joins En Masse — Business Men and Public Officials Join IFSB. To Form Own City Group."** ^[extracted]

> The City of Franklin, Indiana, has gone out fully for the IFSB and is now the only city in the world that has the most members in our organization. Through the great efforts and work of Mr. Louis Frahm, business man; Mr. Jack W. Moore, policeman; Mr. Robert Wolf, civilian defense director, and Mr. Dick Campbell, IFSB Representative for Indiana, this great accomplishment was made possible. At this publication, Franklin can claim 20 members with ten from nearby towns, giving a total of 30. ... Among the members you will find policemen, librarians, mechanics, commercial pilots, business men, bus drivers, students, etc.

The Franklin contingent is **organizationally adjacent to civil defense** — Wolf is the Johnson County Civil Defense Director. ^[extracted] The chapter plans to: ^[extracted]

> Purchase a telescope of suitable power. In addition to this they plan to rig up a 3¼ x 3¼ Graflex camera with an optical type view finder and screen door handle on each side for easy handling. This is the equipment they plan to start with. Later, if finances permit, they may build a portable radar set.

I.e. **IFSB-Franklin functions as a quasi-citizen-Ground-Observation-Corps for saucer reports** ^[inferred] — and Wolf's 20 Jan 1953 letter to Hoover (serial 326) **explicitly proposes cross-recruiting between IFSB members and the official Ground Observation Corps**. ^[extracted]

## Editorial framing

Bender's editorial frames IFSB as a **Columbus-of-the-skies** project ^[extracted]:

> In 1492 Columbus discovered a new world after traveling thousands of miles across the great expanse of unknown waters called the Atlantic Ocean. ... The years directly ahead of us will see another great adventure such as this. A small group of men will assemble in a certain designated place, climb into their ship, a ship vastly different than that of Columbus's time. This ship will be a rocket ship, and its occupants will shoot off into the vast sea of space to find new worlds, new peoples, and new frontiers.

I.e. **IFSB self-frames as a frontier-exploration community**, distinct from both the [[concepts/saucer-as-hoax-default|hoax-default frame]] (debunking-oriented) and the [[concepts/aztec-saucer-myth-1950|Aztec myth-seed]] frame (rumor-oriented). ^[inferred]

## Member theories (the 9 published in Jan 1953)

The "My Theory" section publishes member-submitted theories on saucer origin: ^[extracted]

| Theory # | Submitter | Position |
|---|---|---|
| **6** | Barbara Knorr (CT) | Earth is the only intelligent-life planet in our system; saucers may be the entities themselves |
| **7** | Louie Masonick Jr (MN) | From another planet; observing us; not a menace |
| **8** | Alan Rievman (CT) | From a planet of our Solar System; first atomic explosion alerted them |
| **9** | Victor Root (IL) | Manned ships from intelligent creatures scouting Earth; will not contact us — we are *"too warlike and emotional"* |
| **10** | Alan Stazer (CA) | From Alpha or Proxima Centauri (3rd or 4th planet); same-spectral-type-G0 star as our Sun |

The theories cover **interplanetary (Solar System) + interstellar (Alpha/Proxima Centauri)** but exclude **terrestrial origins** — i.e. IFSB members **explicitly deny the foreign-state-weapon hypothesis** that dominates the Cold-War civilian-mail (cf. Eichoff's pro-saucer rhetoric ^[ambiguous]). ^[inferred]

## "Albert Einstein" datapoint

The Jan 1953 issue records: ^[extracted]

> Mr. Al K. Bender, President of IFSB, received a letter from Professor Einstein with this message: "Having no experience and only superficial knowledge in the field I regret not to be able to comply with your requests."

A **brief courtesy reply from Einstein**, suggesting Bender solicited Einstein's opinion on the saucer phenomenon. ^[extracted] No further detail in section 7. ^[inferred]

## FBI posture toward IFSB

FBI San Francisco-Cincinnati-Indianapolis-New Haven SACs are queried in Jan 1953 (serial 326): *"No references can be located in Bufiles on the I.F.S.B.; *Space Review* or Albert K. Bender."* ^[extracted] No FBI investigation conducted. ^[extracted] The information furnished to Wolf's Civil Defense inquiry: a **non-judgment** that the FBI cannot evaluate organizational integrity. ^[extracted]

The structural parallel: **IFSB anticipates the [[entities/amalgamated-flying-saucer-clubs-of-america|AFSCA]] organizational pattern by ~7 years** ^[inferred] — magazine-publishing civilian saucer organizations with international branches, member dues, and named representatives.
