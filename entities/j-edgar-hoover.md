# J. Edgar Hoover

**John Edgar Hoover** (1895–1972) — **first Director of the Federal Bureau of Investigation**, in office from **10 May 1924 until his death on 2 May 1972** ^[inferred — general historical background; the wiki's sources do not state these dates explicitly]. Hoover is the **single most-cited person** in the wiki's UAP corpus: 488 surface mentions across 77 pages, virtually all of them anchored to his personal authorship, signature, or policy direction within FBI HQ file [[entities/fbi-hq-62-83894-file|62-HQ-83894]] from its 1947 origin through his death.

This page is a **stub hub**. The wiki's primary-source coverage of Hoover is concentrated in document-level pages (FBI bureau bulletins, file sections, letters) and in the [[entities/fbi]] / [[entities/fbi-hq-62-83894-file]] umbrella pages — see those for substantive content. This page exists primarily so the high-volume incoming wikilinks resolve.

## Role in the wiki's UAP corpus

Hoover's institutional fingerprint appears across the corpus through several distinct functional layers:

- **Originator of FBI saucer-cooperation policy (1947)** — the **27 Sep 1947 Hoover → Maj Gen George C. McDonald letter** (filed at 62-83894-121) is the FBI's external-facing termination of saucer-investigation cooperation, sent four days before [[references/fbi-bureau-bulletin-57-1947|Bureau Bulletin No. 57]] issues internally. ^[extracted] Hoover personally approved BB-57 (drafted by SA Reynolds, signed by Asst Director Ladd) with the handwritten *"I agree"* annotation on the 25 Sep 1947 Ladd → Hoover memo. ^[extracted]
- **The Walter Winchell channel** — Hoover personally received UAP tip-relays from columnist [[entities/walter-winchell|Walter Winchell]] across at least three Apr-May 1949 episodes (Atkins/De Jarnett, Mrs Gardner, Robert Ripley/"Japanese saucer"). ^[extracted]
- **Personal investigative directives** — e.g. the **5 Dec 1950 Hoover personal directive** investigating *"radar jamming by ionization of particles in atmosphere"* over Oak Ridge during the NEPA radar+visual sequence; the **6 Nov 1957 URGENT teletype** to SAC Detroit launching the Krasuski / NSC-routed case; the **9 Aug 1954 letter** to OSI/AF on the Frances Swan psychic-channeling case. ^[extracted]
- **The Hoover form-letter response pattern** — the standard FBI policy disclaimer + enclosure of *Know Your FBI* + *99 Facts about the FBI* sent to public correspondents on UFO subjects. ^[extracted] After Hoover's death, **Helen W. Gandy** continued the pattern (e.g. 21 May 1969 reply). ^[extracted] See [[concepts/three-men-in-black]] for the form-letter-as-disclaimer pattern.
- **The post-Hoover transition (1972–1973)** — Hoover's signature appears through May 1969 (serial 473) on Bureau-side UAP correspondence; by 1973 the signing Director is [[entities/clarence-m-kelley|Clarence M. Kelley]], and by 1977 the External Affairs AD [[entities/donald-w-moore-jr|Donald W. Moore Jr.]] signs Blue Book replies. ^[inferred]
- **The Ash-Can-Covers framing** — Hoover cites verbatim the [[references/adc-d333.5ex-cooperation-1947-09-03|3 Sep 1947 ADC D333.5EX letter]]'s **"ash can covers, toilet seats and whatnot"** language as part of his stated rationale for ending FBI saucer investigation. ^[extracted]

## Significance for the corpus

- Hoover is the **policy-locus** for the entire FBI saucer-cooperation regime (1947) and its operational termination (Sep 1947) — a 7-month full-cooperation window that becomes the **doctrinal foundation** of FBI handling of UAP material for the next 25 years.
- The **Schulgen → Hoover** 5 Sep 1947 formal denial letter (serial 84 of [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-2]]) — *"a complete survey of research activities discloses that the Army Air Forces has no project with characteristics similar to those which have been associated with the Flying Discs"* — is structurally addressed to Hoover personally. ^[extracted]
- Hoover's 2 May 1972 death is the boundary marker between two distinct institutional regimes in the wiki's FBI UAP corpus. ^[inferred]
