# Silas Newton

Denver oilman, **official of Newton Oil Co.** (Equitable Building, Denver, Colorado) ^[extracted], described in FBI primary-source material as **"Mysterious Mr. X"** ^[extracted]. Named in [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-5|FBI HQ 62-HQ-83894 section 5]] **serial 213** (New Orleans SAC → Director, 31 March 1950) as the prominent oilman whom [[entities/george-koehler|George T. Koehler]] (315 Franklin Street Denver) cites as the recovery-claim source for the [[concepts/aztec-saucer-myth-1950|Aztec/Scully crashed-saucer myth-seed]]. ^[inferred]

## Wiki primary-source claim

Per the 31 Mar 1950 New Orleans SAC report (serial 213), Koehler told Jefferson B. Armstrong (Wayne Welch Inc. advertising agency Denver) in January 1950: ^[extracted]

> KOEHLER is alleged to have told ARMSTRONG in January, 1950, that he (KOEHLER), knows a prominent Denver oilman named SILAS NEWTON, also known as a Mysterious Mr. X", and an official of the Newton Oil Co., Equitable Building, Denver, Colorado. NEWTON is claiming that he leased land in the Mojave Desert in California and that on this land a flying disc had been found intact, with eighteen three-foot tall human-like occupants, all dead on it but not burned. Further, that the disc was alleged to be of very hard metal and near indestructible.

This is **the primary FBI-corpus mention of Newton**. ^[extracted] The recovery claim — Mojave Desert California rather than the more-common New Mexico location — is **distinct in geographic anchor** from the Hottel memo (NM) and Denver SAC URGENT (NM) accounts of the same myth-seed family ^[inferred]; the convergence of multiple southwestern-US crash-recovery stories in early 1950, all traceable directly or indirectly to Newton-Koehler, is what defines the Aztec myth-seed family. ^[inferred]

## What Newton's Aztec story consists of

| Element | Newton's claim (per Koehler) |
|---|---|
| Location | Mojave Desert, California (leased land) ^[extracted] |
| Recovery state | **Intact disc** ^[extracted] |
| Occupants | **Eighteen** three-foot tall human-like, **all dead on it but not burned** ^[extracted] |
| Material | Very hard, near-indestructible metal ^[extracted] |
| Souvenir | **Radio set** purportedly from the disc, displayed by Koehler to Armstrong ^[extracted] |

The "**18 occupants**" is a **distinctive numerical signature** of the Newton-Koehler channel — it differs from the Hottel memo's "**3 saucers × 3 bodies = 9**" and the Wyandotte Echo's "**~50 saucers in 2 years**" framings. ^[extracted] The variation in numerical detail across channels argues that the **myth-seed accreted detail** as it propagated through the promoter network rather than tracing back to a single canonical event-template. ^[inferred]

Newton's position at the head of this network — the named oilman, the financier, the leased-land claimant — is structurally distinctive: he is the **named source the press cycle traces back to**, while [[entities/george-koehler|Koehler]] is the **circulation hub**. ^[inferred]

## Post-1950 context

Per UFOlogical retrospect (not in the wiki primary-source corpus), Newton + Koehler are subsequently revealed in 1952-53 to have been operating a fraud scheme using **"magnetic-field" oil-detection devices** marketed via the Aztec saucer story ("alien technology"). ^[inferred] ^[ambiguous] The most-economical reading of the FBI-corpus channels: **Newton-Koehler are seeding the press-and-investigative landscape with the Aztec story to support a fraud scheme**, with the FBI files passively absorbing the myth-seed without investigation. ^[inferred] No primary-source FBI record of the post-1953 fraud unwinding is yet in the wiki corpus.
