# Alan L. Bean

**Alan LaVern Bean** (1932–2018) — **US Navy naval aviator** and **NASA Astronaut Group 3** ("the Fourteen", 1963 — same group as **[[entities/dick-gordon|Dick Gordon]]** and **[[entities/gene-cernan|Gene Cernan]]**), Lunar Module Pilot of **[[entities/apollo-12|Apollo 12]]** (14–24 Nov 1969), and **the fourth person to walk on the Moon**. ^[inferred]

In the wiki's UAP corpus, Bean enters as the **primary witness** on the **[[references/nasa-uap-d1-apollo-12-transcript-1969|d1 Apollo 12 air-to-ground voice loop excerpt]]**'s **AOT particle-flash observation** — the corpus's **first astronaut-self-attributed-conventional on-orbit ephemeral-light report**. ^[inferred] Bean is also the corpus's first witness on a **navigation-instrument-mediated** observation (the AOT, a sextant-class periscope), distinct from prior corpus witnesses' rendezvous-window observations. ^[inferred]

## Apollo 12 d1 transcript line (Tape 90/4)

> **LMP-LM** *(Bean)*: *"Roger. When you look out the AOT in the dark quadrant? You can see these lights — particles of light, flashes of light just seem to come from — in this case, I'm looking in quadrant 1 which is the left one. It's coming from behind me, the left, and they're just sailing off in space. I was thinking they're dropping from my water boiler, but it looks like some of those things are escaping the Moon. They really haul out of here and just press off at the stars."* ^[extracted]
>
> **CC**: *"Roger."* ^[extracted]

The **observation properties**:

- **Instrument**: the **[Alignment Optical Telescope (AOT)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alignment_Optical_Telescope)** — a passive, non-powered sextant-class periscope mounted on the LM crew compartment ceiling, used by the LMP for in-flight platform alignment via star-sightings. Bean is using it in **the dark quadrant** (*"quadrant 1 which is the left one"*). ^[extracted]
- **Phenomenology**: *"particles of light, flashes of light"* — discrete luminous events, multiple, transient, with **directional motion** (*"sailing off in space"*, *"haul out of here ... press off at the stars"*).
- **Self-attribution (offered in the same transmission)**: water-boiler effluent (the LM's evaporative cooling system vents water vapor / ice droplets to vacuum) **and / or** lunar outgassing / ejecta (*"escaping the Moon"*).
- **CAPCOM response**: single *"Roger"*; no follow-up question, no debrief callback. ^[extracted]

This is the **only** UAP-adjacent observation on the d1 excerpt. Its corpus significance:

1. **Closest structural twin**: **Glenn's MA-6 "fireflies"** (Feb 1962) — astronaut sees particle-stream while in-orbit, attributes to spacecraft outgassing. The MA-6 case is on **[[concepts/orbital-uap-sighting]]**'s "watch for" list and remains awaiting primary-source ingest; Bean's d1 entry **does not replace** the MA-6 watch-for (different mission, different instrument, different MET regime), but the **phenomenon class parallels are explicit**. ^[inferred]
2. **First astronaut-self-attributed-conventional observation** in the corpus's orbital-class sub-corpus — defining sub-class D in the d-cluster typology (see [[references/nasa-uap-d1-apollo-12-transcript-1969]]). ^[inferred]
3. **First navigation-instrument-mediated UAP-adjacent observation** in the corpus — vs. all prior corpus on-orbit observations through windows (Borman GT-7 rendezvous window, Evans Apollo 17 d6 rendezvous window, Gordon CSM sextant for tracking-light search). ^[inferred]

## Apollo 12 d1 transcript line (Tape 90/9) — non-UAP avionics anomaly

> **LMP-LM** *(Bean)*: *"Got sort of an interesting thing going on AGS right now ... I'm getting an all 8's flash on both the address and the information registers at about one-fifth the brilliance of the normal numbers. And a — It's pulsing every second."* ^[extracted]

CAPCOM (with **Fredo**) confirms the phenomenon was **observed during ground testing at Grumman's Bethpage plant** on most LM spacecraft and identifies it as **electromagnetic interference (EMI)**. ^[extracted] Not a UAP observation — included here as evidence of Bean's **observational discipline** (he flags an unusual visual signature, gets a known-mechanism explanation, and moves on without overinterpretation). ^[inferred]

## Witness pedigree

Bean is in the same **astronaut-class** witness category as the other 1960s–70s NASA witnesses in the corpus. **Distinguishing features**:

- **Cohort-mate of [[entities/dick-gordon|Gordon]] and [[entities/gene-cernan|Cernan]]** — NASA Group 3 ("the Fourteen", 1963).
- **Same mission as [[entities/pete-conrad|Conrad]]** (Group 2) and Gordon (Group 3) — d1 is the corpus's first cross-Group astronaut-class artifact (Groups 2 + 3 in the same crew).
- **Fourth person to walk on the Moon** (after Armstrong, Aldrin, Conrad). ^[inferred]
- **Scientist-adjacent witness** — Bean's later painting career suggests strong visual-observational training that is not formally captured in NASA Group 3's selection criteria but that may have shaped his AOT observation. ^[inferred]

His radio language on the d1 tape is **descriptively rich** (*"particles of light, flashes of light"*, *"sailing off in space"*, *"haul out of here and just press off at the stars"*) compared to the more clipped technical convention of [[entities/frank-borman|Borman]]'s GT-7 *"a bogey at ten o'clock high"*. ^[inferred] Whether this reflects Bean's individual observational style or Apollo-era voice-loop conventions in 1969 vs. Gemini-era 1965 is open. ^[inferred]
