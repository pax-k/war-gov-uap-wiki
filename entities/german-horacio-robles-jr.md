# Germán Horacio Robles Jr.

Mexican civil-engineering student at the **Escuela Nacional de Ingeniería** (National School of Engineering, UNAM in Mexico City) ^[inferred], reported in a 1950 Mexican newspaper as the photographer of a *"flying saucer"* observed at **~8,000–9,000 feet altitude** over the **city of Durango, Durango**. ^[extracted] The photographs and his sketches were turned over to the newspaper's editorial offices, and four illustrations (two photographs + two of his sketched conceptions) were published with extensive caption commentary. ^[extracted] The newspaper clipping was enclosed in **[[entities/miguel-angel-garcia-macias|Miguel Ángel García Macías]]'s** 19 March 1950 unsolicited letter to FBI HQ and is filed under [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-serial-220]].

## What the newspaper reports

Per the newspaper caption (FBI in-house English translation by Mrs. Sophia Saliba): ^[extracted]

- Robles Jr. is *"one of the thousands of persons who have seen 'with their own eyes' the flying saucers or disks"*, but is *"the first to have photographed those strange apparatuses"*.
- He **observed** one of them and **was able to take its picture against the skies of the city of Durango**.
- The four published images are: (1) a photograph of the *"flying saucer"* at altitude over the streets of Durango; (2) Robles Jr.'s **conception drawing** of *"the real form of the strange mechanical objects"*; (3) **another aspect** of the flying saucer (second photograph); (4) a sketched portrait of Robles Jr. produced in the editorial offices after he reported the observations and turned over the photographs.

## Reported morphology

Per Robles Jr.'s own description (relayed by the newspaper):

- The apparatus *"seems to be a **double truncated cone, joined at its bases**"* ^[extracted] — i.e., two cone frusta joined base-to-base, producing a **biconvex / lens-shaped** silhouette.
- It *"moves by something **like jet propulsion**"* and *"displaces itself by **changing position** in order to use its motive force in the direction desired"*. ^[extracted]
- *"For which reason it can be seen under apparently different forms"* — i.e., the silhouette varies with attitude. ^[extracted]

The double-truncated-cone-joined-at-bases morphology is **structurally distinct** from the corpus's existing 1947–48 disc / sphere / cigar / flapjack / amoeba morphology lineup. ^[inferred] No exact analogue in the [[references/project-sign-incident-summaries-1-100|Project SIGN incidents 1–100]] or [[references/project-sign-incident-summaries-101-172|101–172]] / [[references/project-sign-incident-summaries-173-233|173–233]] bundles. The closest near-analogue may be the [[concepts/rotating-disc-morphology|1955 Russell Trans-Caucasus rotating disc]] morphology (still 5 years later), but the geometry is different — Russell's discs were single-disc with rotation; Robles's is a stacked-cone form. ^[inferred]

## Position in the corpus

This is the **wiki's first Mexican-located UAP sighting** ^[inferred] — predating the [[concepts/diplomatic-channel-uap-reporting|2023 DOS Mexico cable]]'s host-country legislative reportage by **73 years** and predating Project SIGN's terminal incident **Inc 233** ([[references/sighting-jackson-ms-1949-01|Jackson MS 1 Jan 1949]]) by **~14 months**. ^[inferred] The sighting is **not** in any of the three SIGN incident-summary bundles in the corpus, suggesting either:

- The sighting **post-dated** Project SIGN's intake window (SIGN was in transition to Project GRUDGE in early 1949–1950); ^[inferred]
- Or the **Mexican-civilian / Mexican-newspaper origin** placed the case outside USAF intake reach, which was CONUS + Alaska + Pacific theatre per the [[references/usaf-flying-discs-1949|1949 file]]'s 26 Oct 1948 worldwide-scope expansion — but in practice the SIGN sequential stream is heavily US-domestic with rare Pacific / Atlantic / European cases. ^[inferred]
- Or the sighting **was** reported through some channel but did not become a SIGN-numbered incident — the corpus has no visibility into Project GRUDGE-era intake. ^[ambiguous]

The **photographic** dimension also distinguishes this sighting from most 1948 SIGN cases: the only photographically-evidenced 1947 case in the corpus is the [[references/project-sign-incident-summaries-1-100|William Rhodes Phoenix photo]] (Inc 40, 7 Jul 1947). Robles's photographs would be the corpus's **second photographically-evidenced UAP event**, but the photographs themselves are **not preserved in the FBI artifact** — only the printed newspaper reproductions are. ^[inferred] The custody chain of the original photographs is unknown; the newspaper transcript in the FBI file is a **secondary reproduction**. ^[inferred]

## Source-record caveats

- The newspaper clipping appears in the FBI artifact as **OCR'd printed page** plus a degraded second OCR pass that mangles his name as *"George Horacio Rodes Jr."* and *"Boracio Rodes Jr."*; the cleaner pass and the Spanish-language original give the name as **"Germán Horacio Robles Jr."**. ^[extracted] The wiki uses the Spanish-original spelling.
- The newspaper masthead OCR'd by Mistral as *"JUEVES 18 DE MARZO DE 1880"* — almost certainly **Thursday 18 March 1950** (consistent with García Macías's 19 March 1950 cover letter, the FBI New York APR 7 1950 stamp, and the José López y Fuentes / Miguel Lanz Duret editorial masthead context). ^[ambiguous] An OCR digit error (188_ for 195_).
- The newspaper's name is **not preserved** in the OCR — masthead reads *"FEDERACIÓN DE LA REPÚBLICA / LIC. MIGUEL LANZ DURET / MEXICO, D. F. / ... / GERARDO LÓPEZ Y FUENTES / NUMERO 12.896"*; "Federación" + "Lic. Miguel Lanz Duret" plus the issue number 12,896 most plausibly identify *El Universal* (Mexico City), where Lanz Duret was longtime director. ^[inferred]
