# Eugene A. Cernan

**Eugene A. "Gene" Cernan** (1934–2017) — **US Navy naval aviator** and **NASA Astronaut Group 3** ("the Fourteen", 1963), Commander of **[[entities/apollo-17|Apollo 17]]** (7–19 Dec 1972), and the **last person to walk on the Moon** during the 20th century. ^[inferred]

In the wiki's UAP corpus, Cernan enters as a **named speaker** on **two structurally distinct Apollo 17 artifacts**: the **[[references/nasa-apollo-17-technical-crew-debriefing-1973|MSC-07631 Technical Crew Debriefing]]** page-24-4 excerpt (post-mission, only the *"unusual sighting"* line about the misidentified aircraft-carrier superstructure) and the **[[references/nasa-uap-d2-apollo-17-transcript-1972|Apollo 17 air-to-ground voice transcript]]** (real-time, multiple cosmic-ray-retinal-flash and S-IVB-as-flasher passages). The d2 ingest **upgrades Cernan's witness role** from a single post-mission carrier-misidentification line to **the corpus's first contemporaneous-voice-loop cosmic-ray-flash speaker** plus a **co-primary visual identifier of the S-IVB by morphology + rotation signature**. ^[inferred]

## Apollo 17 voice-loop lines (d2)

### 1. Real-time cosmic-ray retinal flash (Tape 46/4, ~02:18:42 GET, Day 2)

> **CDR (Cernan)**: *"...I had a hard time going to bed, to start with - I saw some of the same peripheral horizon-type things you said were not the type of data you were looking for; but I also saw a - some sets of the streaks. And probably the one most imposing thing I remember is - and the last one I remember before falling asleep - was the fact that there was a very bright spot that flashed right between my eyes like a very bright headlight - like a train coming at you, only with a flash."* ^[extracted]
>
> **CDR (Cernan)**: *"But then, as today, I saw some that flashed and lit up the horizon and some that lit up peripherally; and I guess, as you say, that's a different kind of data, but I did see them there and they impressed me."* ^[extracted]
>
> **CDR (Cernan)**: *"I will say one thing, though; no question in my mind but that they're there. ... they're there."* ^[extracted]

The *"very bright headlight - like a train coming at you, only with a flash"* metaphor is the **most vivid contemporaneous cosmic-ray-flash characterization in the corpus** — distinct in vocabulary register from Aldrin's 1969 first-principles *"penetration"* / *"entry and impact"* model statement (post-mission, [[references/nasa-uap-d4-apollo-11-technical-crew-debriefing-1969|d4]]) and from Schmitt's clinical *"just about continuously when we were dark adapted"* summary (post-mission, [[references/nasa-apollo-17-technical-crew-debriefing-1973|d6]]). ^[inferred] Cernan's defensive *"no question in my mind but that they're there"* line — issued in response to CC's *"trying to make the data fit the curve"* — establishes that **NASA-internal cosmic-ray-flash data analysis was already underway in real time** during Apollo 17, and that there was a *"curve"* the ground team was attempting to fit. ^[inferred]

### 2. S-IVB-as-flasher visual ID (Tape 46/4 – 47/14, ~02:18:46–02:21 GET, Day 2)

> **CDR (Cernan)** (Tape 46/6): *"It is a - it is a bright object, and it's obviously rotating because it's flashing. It's way out in the distance ... It's apparently rotating in a very rhythmic fashion ..."* ^[extracted]
>
> **CDR (Cernan)** (Tape 46/8): *"One unique thing about it, Bob, is that it's got two flashes. As it comes around in - in rhythmic fashion, you get a very bright flash; and then you get a dull flash. And then it'll come around with a bright flash, and then a dull flash."* ^[extracted]
>
> **CDR (Cernan)** (Tape 47/14): *"Hey, Bob. We got two of those flashers out there. They could be SLA panels."* ^[extracted]

Cernan provides the **bright/dull two-flash signature** characterization that — combined with [[entities/harrison-schmitt|Schmitt]]'s morphology framing (*"the side and ... of the S-IVB - and then the engine bell"*) — produces the corpus's first crew-side S-IVB visual ID by morphology + rotation period without ground-side ranging support. Cernan also contributes the **NOUN 20** spacecraft-attitude reference and the **mark-when-it-crosses-the-XX-axis** chain-of-evidence procedural step. The later two-flasher observation (Tape 47/14) introduces the SLA-panel candidate. The S-IVB-vs-SLA-panel resolution is not preserved in source. ^[ambiguous]

### 3. SEP fragment field morphology summary (Tape 5/3, ~03:34–03:42 GET, Day 1)

> **CDR (Cernan)**: *"My impression is that they are - flat, flakelike particles. Some may be 6 inches across. And, although there's no relative motion between the two, most of them seem to be twinkling. And I think, for the most part, they're all moving away from us."* ^[extracted]

Cernan's morphology summary closes the SEP fragment-field observation event and supplies the field's velocity-frame characterization (*"all moving away from us"*) — a key empirical datum for the *"your own debris field"* prior on [[concepts/orbital-uap-sighting]].

## Apollo 17 debriefing line (d6, page 24-4)

> **CERNAN**: *"The only unusual sighting I can recall during landing or recovery is when the CMP looked out the window and saw the superstructure of an aircraft carrier and said, 'Oh, we've got a tin can with us.'"* ^[extracted]

The "unusual sighting" referent is the **recovery vessel for Apollo 17** — almost certainly **USS Ticonderoga (CV-14)** ^[inferred] — whose superstructure was misperceived through a **fogged Command Module window** (per Evans's clarifying *"Well, it was kind of foggy on the windows"*). ^[extracted] The line is the corpus's earliest preserved instance of an astronaut-class witness using *"unusual sighting"* in a context that resolves to a **conventional non-anomalous explanation in the same paragraph**. ^[inferred] It is **not a UAP report**. The exchange is a useful illustration of the **transcript-keyword false-positive** mechanism that drives the d6 artifact's status as a corpus null/boundary entry.

## Witness class

Cernan is in the same **astronaut-class** witness category as **[[entities/frank-borman|Borman]]** and **[[entities/jim-lovell|Lovell]]** ([[references/nasa-pao-t-00763-r1b-gemini-7-1965|GT-7 1965]]) — and shares the same trained-observer floor described on **[[concepts/orbital-uap-sighting]]**. With the d2 ingest, Cernan is the corpus's **first multi-artifact-class same-mission speaker** on cosmic-ray retinal flashes — appearing in **both** the contemporaneous voice loop (d2 Tape 46/4 real-time *"train headlight"* metaphor) and the post-mission technical debrief (d6 page 24-4 corroboration of Schmitt). ^[inferred] The contrast between Cernan's metaphor-rich real-time vocabulary and Schmitt's clinical post-mission framing demonstrates that **artifact class is a structural variable** in how astronaut-class observations are preserved — see [[concepts/cosmic-ray-light-flashes]] for the cross-artifact-class comparison.
