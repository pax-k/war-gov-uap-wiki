# D. M. Ladd

**D. Milton Ladd**, FBI Assistant Director, Domestic Intelligence Division ^[inferred] — the **gatekeeper of the FBI's flying-disc engagement** in 1947. ^[extracted] In section 1 of [[entities/fbi-hq-62-83894-file|file 62-HQ-83894]], Ladd is the addressee of nearly every operational memo on flying-disc matters from the field, the Liaison Section, and his subordinates. ^[extracted]

## Role per the FBI corpus

Ladd's position appears as: ^[extracted]

- **Recipient** of E. G. Fitch's 10 Jul 1947 memo forwarding [[entities/george-f-schulgen|Schulgen's]] cooperation request — and the source of the **initial recommendation to decline**: *"I would recommend that we advise the Army that the Bureau does not believe it should go into these investigations, it being noted that a great bulk of those alleged discs reported found have been pranks. It is not believed that the Bureau would accomplish anything by going into these investigations."* — **DML** marginal note. ^[extracted]
- **Recipient** of every subsequent SAC field-office teletype on disc events under the new cooperation regime — Milwaukee SAC Johnson on the Brasky case; LA SAC Hood on Burbank; Butte SAC Bannister on Twin Falls; New Orleans SAC Weeks on Hairston; New Haven SAC on Bailey; Newark SAC McKee on Hackensack; Portland SAC Bobbitt on Maury Island / Davidson-Brown crash. ^[extracted]
- **Liaison-Section memo recipient** for the Schulgen channel — every Reynolds → Schulgen update routed through Liaison goes to Ladd's office. ^[extracted]
- **Author** of the 11 Jul 1947 memo to Mr. E. A. Tamm relaying Milwaukee SAC Johnson's Schaefer/Hanson Black River Falls disc report. ^[extracted]

## The "I would do it but…" addendum

The most institutionally consequential Ladd action in the corpus is his **draft addendum** on the Fitch memo of 10 Jul 1947 (filed 23 Jul 1947). ^[extracted] After his initial decline-recommendation, Ladd writes (annotated as the Director's view by Tamm): ^[extracted]

> **I would do it but before agreeing to it we must insist upon full access to discs recovered. For instance in the La. case the Army grabbed it and would not let us have it for cursory examination.**

This **single sentence** — annotated as Hoover's view — becomes the **founding precondition** of the FBI-AAF cooperation regime. ^[inferred] It is what gives [[references/fbi-bureau-bulletin-42-1947|Bureau Bulletin No. 42]] its disc-access-or-escalation clause: *"in any instances where they fail to make information available to you or make the recovered discs available for your examination, it should promptly be brought to the attention of the Bureau."* ^[extracted]

## Routing pattern

The standard 1947 FBI saucer-routing sequence visible in section 1: ^[inferred]

```
SAC field office
    └──> teletype to Director (with cc Mr. Ladd)
              └──> Liaison Section / Ladd's desk
                        └──> Reynolds → Schulgen / G-2 / War Department
                                  └──> response back to Ladd
                                            └──> field-office instruction or filing
```

Ladd's desk is the **routing hub**. ^[inferred] Memos in section 1 typically carry the standard distribution stamp listing **Tolson / E. A. Tamm / Clegg / Glavin / Ladd / Nichols / Rosen / Tracy**, with **Ladd marked as the action officer** for disc matters. ^[extracted]

## Position in corpus

Ladd is the **operational counterpart to the AAF's [[entities/george-f-schulgen|Schulgen]]**. ^[inferred] The Ladd-Schulgen-via-Reynolds channel is **the** institutional channel for FBI-AAF saucer coordination from July 1947 onward. ^[inferred]

After this 1947 founding period, Ladd's role in subsequent UAP material is **not yet documented in the wiki corpus** (the post-1948 thread runs through Cabell at AAF / USAF rather than Schulgen). ^[inferred]
