# Col C. D. Gasser

A **USAF Colonel** assigned as **AMC Engineering Field Officer / Resident Engineer for Nuclear Energy for the Propulsion of Aircraft (NEPA) Research at Oak Ridge, Tennessee** during 1948-49. ^[extracted] Gasser is named in the [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-4|14 Mar 1949 Fletcher → Ladd memo]] (page 117) as the *"reliable and confidential source"* behind the institutional posture transmitted to all FBI field offices via [[references/fbi-sac-letter-38-1949|SAC Letter No. 38, Series 1949]] (25 Mar 1949) — i.e. the **March 1949 "Russian missile" hypothesis** that flying discs are man-made and likely originate from USSR experimentation. ^[extracted]

## Verbatim characterization (Fletcher → Ladd memo page 117)

> Colonel C. D. Gasser, Resident Engineer, Air Materiel Command, Nuclear Energy for the Propulsion of Aircraft Research, Oak Ridge, Tennessee, has recently and confidentially advised the Bureau that flying discs are believed by the Air Force to be man-made missiles rather than some natural phenomenon and that as much as four years ago it was learned that some type of flying discs were being experimented upon by the Russians. It was further determined from Colonel Gasser that most all of the flying discs seen by persons in the United States approached this country from a northerly direction and returned in the same direction, indicating the strong possibility that they are coming from Russia.

## Substantive claims attributed to Gasser

The Fletcher → Ladd memo and the connected page 86 source-paraphrase (without explicit Gasser attribution but contextually consistent) attribute these claims to him: ^[extracted]

1. **Discs are man-made**, not natural phenomena.
2. **USSR experimentation ≥4 years**: Russian-side disc experimentation has been known since at least 1944-45.
3. **Northern-quadrant trajectory pattern**: most US-observed discs approach from a northerly direction and depart in the same direction.
4. **Trans-polar route hypothesis**: the trajectory is consistent with a great-circle route from Russia to the US continental airspace.
5. **CIA Southern Europe + Southern Asia reports** (paraphrased page 86): "more recent reports have been received from representatives of the Central Intelligence Agency in Southern Europe and Southern Asia to the effect that the Russians were experimenting with some type of radical aircraft or guided missile which could be dispatched for great distances out over the sea, made to turn in flight..." ^[extracted]
6. **Vapor-trail signature analysis**: the vapor trail does not behave like a known aircraft's exhaust + wing-tip trail; instead "vapor trail represents some atmospheric change along the path of the missile, which would not be the case were it any presently known type of aircraft." ^[extracted]
7. **Source-restraint posture**: Gasser explicitly tells his FBI contact (page 86) that he is *"not speaking officially, but as a personal matter of cooperation"*; that *"the matter was being given absolutely no dissemination by the air force or other military personnel"*; and that *"they had not deemed it advisable to advise him of all information pertaining to the missile."* ^[extracted]

## Why Gasser matters

1. **Source of record for the March 1949 institutional posture**: the [[references/fbi-sac-letter-38-1949|SAC Letter No. 38]] of 25 Mar 1949 transmits *"the reliable and confidential source"* substance to all FBI field offices. ^[extracted] Gasser is the originator of the "Russian missile / northerly trajectory" framing at the FBI level. ^[inferred]
2. **AMC Engineering Field Officer at NEPA Oak Ridge**: NEPA (Nuclear Energy for the Propulsion of Aircraft) was the AAF/USAF program (1946-51) studying nuclear-powered aircraft. Gasser's posting at Oak Ridge places him in the **AEC nuclear-research institutional axis** that aligns with the [[references/sighting-kirtland-mass-1949-01-30|Kirtland 30 Jan 1949 mass-event's AEC/AFSWP perturbation]] and the 16 Feb 1949 Los Alamos conference. ^[inferred]
3. **Bridge between AMC Wright-Patterson + AEC Oak Ridge**: Gasser explicitly references *"his conversation with representatives at Wright Field"* (page 86) — i.e. he is a **lateral conduit** between the Wright-Patterson [[concepts/project-grudge|Project GRUDGE]] / AMC T-2 axis and the AEC Oak Ridge nuclear-research axis. ^[inferred]
4. **Source-restraint posture is structurally novel**: Gasser's *"personal matter of cooperation, not officially"* framing is the **first explicit primary-source FBI documentation of an off-the-record AF officer briefing the Bureau on UAP** — a pattern that recurs in [[references/fbi-hottel-memo-1950-03-22|the Hottel memo (Mar 1950)]] (the AF investigator who gave Hottel the "three saucers" story is also unnamed). ^[inferred]
5. **The AEC-photographic-analysis incident** (page 110-112): Gasser's 10 Feb 1949 letter to AEC's Rathman about Major Grant's flying-disc photographic negatives — concluding the negatives are defectively-developed (not authentic disc imagery), recommending declassification. This is the **first documented Gasser-as-FBI-Lab-equivalent role** in the corpus (CC to FBI SA C. C. McSwain). ^[inferred]

## What Gasser was NOT

- **Not** a senior policy figure — he is a Resident Engineer, not an AMC commander or AF Intelligence head. ^[extracted]
- **Not** the Wright-Patterson AMC T-2 / Project SIGN / GRUDGE leadership — those roles are held by [[entities/c-p-cabell|Cabell]] (D/I HQ-USAF), Twining (CG AMC), Brig Gen Putt (Director of R&D AMC). ^[inferred]
- **Not** apparently identifiable with any of the figures in the contemporaneous USAF UAP literature (e.g. McCoy, Garrett, Boggs, Hearn, Watson). ^[inferred]
- **Not** the same person as Gen George E. Stratemeyer (CG ADC, author of [[references/adc-stratemeyer-flying-disc-1948-02-04|the 4 Feb 1948 ADC letter]]). ^[extracted]
