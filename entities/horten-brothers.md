# Horten Brothers

German aviation designers (the file uses the spellings *Horten* and *Horton* interchangeably) who developed the [[concepts/horten-flying-wing|Horten flying wing]] family of tailless aircraft during World War II.

## Mention in the 1948 USAF file

A 16 February 1948 routing memo (AFGIR-CO-5) reports:

> "This Directorate has been advised that contact has been made with the Horton Brothers, and the Assistant Chief of Staff, G-2, of European Command has accordingly been advised by Telecon to use this Memorandum [Collection Memorandum #7, dtd 21 Jan 48] as the basis for interrogation. The results of this interrogation will be forwarded to your Headquarters as soon as received." ^[extracted]

This places the brothers under **active US/G-2 interrogation as part of the Flying Disc investigation in early 1948**, which is the salient fact for [[concepts/project-sign]]. ^[extracted]

## Why USAF intelligence cared

Two reasons surface in the file:

1. The visual silhouette of the Horten flying wings could plausibly account for civilian "disc" sightings — particularly the Horten "Parabola", VIII, and IX. ^[inferred]
2. A June 1947 Moscow attaché cable claimed **1,800 jet-propelled aircraft based on the Horten VIII design** were under construction for Soviet bomber squadrons (ref V-32291-8). If even partly true, this represented a major postwar strategic surprise. ^[ambiguous]

## Documents referenced

- **T-2 Report F-SU-1110-HD** — "German Flying Wings Designed by Horten Brothers". Retained by AFOIR (4 June 1948 memo).
- **RAE Tech Note AERO 1703** — referenced as the open description of the Horten tailless aircraft family.

## Relationship

Reimar and Walter Horten were brothers and design collaborators on the Horten wing series. The 1948 file does not distinguish between them by first name. ^[inferred]
