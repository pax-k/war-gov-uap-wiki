# Ronald E. Evans Jr.

**Ronald E. "Ron" Evans Jr.** (1933–1990) — **US Navy naval aviator** and **NASA Astronaut Group 5** ("The Original Nineteen", 1966), Command Module Pilot of **[[entities/apollo-17|Apollo 17]]** (7–19 Dec 1972). ^[inferred] As CMP, Evans remained in lunar orbit aboard CSM *America* during the lunar-surface phase and conducted the SIM-bay scientific instrument observations. ^[inferred]

In the wiki's UAP corpus, Evans enters as a **named speaker** on **two structurally distinct Apollo 17 artifacts**: the **[[references/nasa-apollo-17-technical-crew-debriefing-1973|MSC-07631 Technical Crew Debriefing]]** page-24-4 excerpt (post-mission, reentry-plasma + carrier-misidentification window-fog lines) and the **[[references/nasa-uap-d2-apollo-17-transcript-1972|Apollo 17 air-to-ground voice transcript]]** (real-time, SEP-fragment-field photography and morphology characterization). ^[inferred]

## Apollo 17 voice-loop lines (d2)

### 1. SEP fragment field — first observation and CC empirical correction (Tape 5/2 – 5/3, ~03:34–03:42 GET, Day 1)

> **CMP (Evans)**: *"Now we've got a few very bright particles or fragments or something that go drifting by as we maneuver."* ^[extracted]
>
> **CMP (Evans)**: *"Yes. Now you can see some of them in shape. They're very jagged, angular fragments that are tumbling."* ^[extracted]
>
> **CC (Bob)**: *"Roger. They look like fluid of some sort?"* ^[extracted]
>
> **CMP (Evans)**: *"Not to me. They look like pieces of something."* ^[extracted]
>
> **CMP (Evans)**: *"They're very bright."* ^[extracted]

Evans is the **first observer** of the SEP fragment field and the first to **empirically correct** Mission Control's *"fluid of some sort?"* prompt with a direct *"Not to me. They look like pieces of something."* ^[extracted] This is a structurally interesting datum: it establishes that **astronaut-class observers actively contradict ground-side default-explanation prompts in real time** when the witness-side phenomenology doesn't match. ^[inferred]

### 2. SEP fragment field photography (Tape 5/3, ~03:38 GET, Day 1)

> **CMP (Evans)**: *"Bob, for the most part, these fragments are not - or are tumbling at a very slow rate. I tried a couple of pictures of them - different settings. You may get an idea of what, at least, the patterns look like."* ^[extracted]
>
> **CMP (Evans)**: *"I'll take two pictures about a minute apart if I can. And it'll be Frame 70."* ^[extracted]

Evans photographs the fragment field on **Frame 65 + Frame 70** of the LMP mag November November ^[extracted] — the corpus's first **crew-side photographic-evidence collection of an in-mission UAP-adjacent observation**. ^[inferred] The film is not in the d2 excerpt; locating the developed Hasselblad imagery would be a productive open question.

### 3. SEP fragment field — crew-supplied conventional attribution (Tape 5/3, ~03:37:34 GET, Day 1)

> **CMP (Evans)**: *"Well, you know I - I don't know. There are a number of possibilities. ... I got the impression maybe they were curved a little bit, as if they might be - off the side of the S-IVB. ... ice chunks, possibly. Or maybe there's paint coming off of it."* ^[extracted]

Evans supplies the **S-IVB-debris attribution** that closes the SEP fragment-field observation as a non-UAP event. ^[extracted] This is the corpus's first instance of a CMP-class astronaut **reasoning out a conventional explanation in real time across multiple radio beats**. ^[inferred]

### 4. CC weather-forecasting-pass observation (Tape 47/14, ~02:20:55 GET, Day 2)

The d2 excerpt also records an exchange in which **Schmitt notes Evans was actively scanning for the booster** while Schmitt himself was looking at Earth weather: *"Ron's been looking for the booster. ... Pass the torch of weather forecasting to Ron."* ^[extracted] This places Evans in the loop of the **S-IVB-as-flasher visual identification** even though he is not a primary speaker on the morphology characterization. ^[inferred]

## Apollo 17 debriefing lines (d6, page 24-4)

### 1. Reentry-plasma "tunnel with bright spot"

> **EVANS** (CONT'D): *"...after the brightness of the fireball decreased, I could look back up through the rendezvous window and see what to me was kind of like a tunnel with a bright spot in the middle of the tunnel. Way down the tunnel, way back behind, I could see the fireball."* ^[extracted]

Evans describes the **trailing plasma sheath / wake** visible from inside the Command Module during atmospheric reentry, looking rearward through the rendezvous window after peak plasma brightness decreased. The "fireball" referent is the **reentry plasma**, not a bolide. ^[inferred] The observation is a **documented Apollo CM-window phenomenon** from this vantage point. ^[inferred]

### 2. Window-fog clarifying remark

> **EVANS**: *"Well, it was kind of foggy on the windows."* ^[extracted]

In the immediate context of [[entities/gene-cernan|Cernan]]'s "tin can" line about the recovery aircraft carrier — this is Evans's own clarification that the misidentification was driven by post-splashdown window condensation, not by anything unusual about the recovery vessel. ^[extracted]

## Witness class

Evans's two debriefing lines are noteworthy precisely because they are **both negative-resolved**: the "fireball / tunnel" line names a phenomenon that sounds anomalous but resolves to known reentry plasma, and the "kind of foggy on the windows" line **explicitly walks back** the carrier-superstructure misidentification. ^[inferred] This is the **first instance in the corpus of an astronaut-class witness self-correcting an apparently anomalous observation in the same debriefing exchange**. ^[inferred] Compare with [[entities/frank-borman|Borman]]'s GT-7 line — Borman's "we have an actual sighting" remark is **not** walked back on the [[references/nasa-pao-t-00763-r1b-gemini-7-1965|released PAO transcript]]; the bogey-vs-booster ambiguity persists. The Evans / Borman contrast may be useful as a reference baseline for orbital-class self-correction patterns once more astronaut testimony enters the corpus. ^[inferred]
