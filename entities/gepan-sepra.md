# GEPAN / SEPRA (CNES UAP Research Programs)

The French civilian-government **UAP research program** housed inside the **Centre National d'Etudes Spatiales (CNES)** — the French national space agency. The program operated under two successive names:

- **GEPAN** — *Groupe d'Etudes de Phénomènes Aérospatiaux Non Identifiés* (Unidentified Aerospace Phenomena Study Group) — established **1977** following the recommendations of the 1976 IHEDN committee chaired by General Blanchard of the Gendarmerie Nationale.
- **SEPRA** — *Service d'Expertise des Phénomènes de Rentrée Atmosphérique* (Atmospheric Reentry Phenomena Consulting Department) — succeeded GEPAN in **1988**, with a more limited UFO-study role.

Per the [[references/cometa-report-1999|1999 COMETA Report]] (Foreword, p1): *"the only civilian government agency known in the world dedicated to the study of UFOs"*. ^[extracted]

Heads named in the COMETA Report: **Claude Poher** (first head of GEPAN), **Jean-Jacques Vélasco** (head of SEPRA at the time of COMETA's publication).

## Methodology

GEPAN developed an "original method for studying rare, randomly occurring phenomena" based on **four-data-stream analysis**:

1. **Witnesses** — physiology, psychology.
2. **Testimonies** — accounts, reactions to questions, general behavior.
3. **Physical environment** — weather, air traffic, photographs, radar data, environmental traces.
4. **Psychosocial environment** — readings and beliefs of witnesses, possible media or group influence.

A **scientific council** chaired by **Hubert Curien** (12 members) guided GEPAN's annual work.

## Classification scheme

GEPAN/SEPRA classifies cases into four categories — referenced as the "first classification of UAPs" and used heavily by the COMETA Report:

| Class | Definition | Approximate share |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Completely identified phenomenon | majority |
| **B** | Probably identified, lacks certainty due to evidence gaps | second-largest |
| **C** | Cannot be identified due to lack of data | smaller |
| **D** | Cannot be identified despite abundance and quality of data | **4–5% of cases** |

**UAP D** cases are the residual class that the COMETA Report builds its argument on. The strangest UAP D cases are sub-classified using **A. Hynek's CE3** (close encounter of the third kind) terminology. ^[extracted]

## Institutional partnerships

GEPAN/SEPRA cooperates with multiple French agencies:

- **Gendarmerie Nationale** — primary collection partner since February 1974 (predating GEPAN itself); >3,000 reports forwarded since 1974, plus ~100 joint field investigations.
- **Civil Aviation Directorate (DGAC)** — civilian-pilot reports under "Airmiss" procedure.
- **Air Force Chief of Staff** — military aeronautical sightings via the Air Operations Center (CCOA, Taverny) and Air Defense Operations Center (CODA).
- **National Weather Service**, **EDF** (electricity), **CEA** (atomic energy), **ONERA** (aerospace research), **CEAT** (aeronautic test center), **CNRS** (scientific research), **DGA** (armaments), **ETCA** (armaments technical institution), **Fleximage** (image processing).

Sample analysis (soil, plant) is done at ETCA. Image processing was performed at ETCA from 1981–1988.

## Notable cases handled

The 1999 COMETA Report cites GEPAN/SEPRA's investigation of:

- **[[references/sighting-trans-en-provence-1981|Trans-en-Provence (8 Jan 1981)]]** ^[inferred-as-target] — landed object, ground depression, INRA chlorophyll/amino-acid analysis (Prof. Michel Bounias).
- **"Amaranth" / Nancy (21 Oct 1982)** — 20-min hover; bush leaf-tip drying.
- **Air France AF 3532 (28 Jan 1994)** — radar/visual correlation at Cinq-Mars-la-Pile CDC.

GEPAN technical memoranda 16, 17, 18 are referenced in the report (Trans-en-Provence, Amaranth, photograph-study procedures).

## ORION sky-surveillance system

The COMETA Report mentions a partial defense-deployment sky-surveillance system called **ORION**, intended for satellite tracking but capable of detecting UFO-class light phenomena:

- **GRAVES** — surveillance radar capable of detecting 1 m² objects at 1500 km.
- **SPOC** — Sky Observation Probe System, CCD-camera-based optical surveillance for orbiting satellites and magnitude 7–8 space debris.
- **SOLSTICE** — proposed 4 m diameter telescope (with potential adaptive optics) for geostationary-orbit (~36,000 km) observation.

## International outreach (COMETA Ch. 9, appendix coverage)

Two events anchor SEPRA's late-1990s international standing in the wiki:

- **1992 — declined USSR/Russian-Academy cooperation offer**. **Vladimir Migouline** and his assistant **Platov**, heads of the expert's group on abnormal phenomena at the Academy of Sciences (USSR/RF), proposed a scientific-and-technical cooperation program to SEPRA. **CNES management did not follow up.** ^[extracted, COMETA §9.3] This is the wiki's first documented declined-cooperation-offer between two civilian-government UAP research bodies; structurally significant given COMETA's broader argument for international cooperation.
- **October 1997 — Pocantico colloquium participation**. The **head of SEPRA** ([[entities/gepan-sepra|Vélasco]]) + **two members** of the scientific council attended the [[references/pocantico-colloquium-1997|Sturrock-moderated Rockefeller-funded UFO physical-evidence colloquium]] at the Pocantico estate. The colloquium's summary document urged international adoption of *"a UFO research organization comparable to that of France."* ^[extracted, COMETA §9.1] This is the wiki's first attestation of SEPRA participating in a non-French science-domain UAP workshop; it represents the *opposite* posture of the declined-Migouline-1992 episode — engaged when invited as scientific peer, declined when offered formal-cooperation framing.

The 1992-vs-1997 contrast is structurally important: SEPRA accepts **scientific-domain engagement** (Pocantico) but declines **state-to-state cooperation** (Migouline). This is consistent with SEPRA's CNES housing — civilian-science rather than diplomatic-instrument. ^[inferred]
