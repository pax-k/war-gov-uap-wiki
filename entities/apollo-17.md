# Apollo 17

NASA's **Apollo 17** — the **last crewed lunar landing** of the Apollo program, launched **7 December 1972** and recovered **19 December 1972**. Crew: **[[entities/gene-cernan|Eugene A. Cernan]]** (Commander), **[[entities/ronald-evans|Ronald E. Evans Jr.]]** (Command Module Pilot), and **[[entities/harrison-schmitt|Harrison H. "Jack" Schmitt]]** (Lunar Module Pilot — only scientist-astronaut to walk on the Moon during Apollo). ^[inferred]

In the wiki's UAP corpus, Apollo 17 enters as the **mission frame** for **three structurally distinct NASA documents**, all produced by Manned Spacecraft Center (MSC) Houston: the **contemporaneous [[references/nasa-uap-d2-apollo-17-transcript-1972|Apollo 17 Air-to-Ground Voice Transcript (Dec 1972)]]** captured in real time during the mission; the **[[references/nasa-apollo-17-technical-crew-debriefing-1973|MSC-07631 Technical Crew Debriefing (4 Jan 1973)]]** prepared by the **MSC Training Office, Crew Training and Simulation Division**; and the **[[references/nasa-apollo-17-science-debriefing-1973|MSC-07632 Crew Debriefing for Science (8 Jan 1973)]]** prepared by the **MSC Science Requirements Branch, Planetary and Earth Sciences Division**. All three excerpts are in the corpus by **FOIA / archival selection** (d2 by transcript-keyword match — "fragments", "flash", "sighting"; d6 by transcript-keyword match — "fireball", "sighting", "flash"; d5 by **whole-document inclusion** in the same NASA-debrief release tranche, since d5's pages 119–120 contain no UAP-domain keywords whatsoever). ^[inferred] Together they form the corpus's **first triple-artifact-class same-mission convergence**: voice loop + technical debrief + science debrief, all three independently null for UAP. ^[inferred]

## Mission profile (overview)

- **Launch vehicle**: Saturn V (AS-512). ^[inferred]
- **Launch**: 7 December 1972, 05:33 UTC, Kennedy Space Center LC-39A. ^[inferred]
- **Landing site**: Taurus-Littrow valley. ^[inferred]
- **Lunar surface duration**: ~75 hours; three EVAs by Cernan + Schmitt with the Lunar Roving Vehicle. ^[inferred]
- **CMP solo activity**: Evans remained in lunar orbit aboard CSM *America* and conducted the SIM-bay scientific instrument observations. ^[inferred]
- **Recovery**: 19 December 1972, **USS Ticonderoga (CV-14)** in the Pacific. ^[inferred]
- **Total mission duration**: ~12 days 14 hours. ^[inferred]
- **Distinguishing role in Apollo program**: only Apollo with a professional geologist (Schmitt) on the lunar surface; final crewed lunar mission of the 20th century. ^[inferred]

The biographical / mission detail above is **not in the page-24-4 (d6) or pages-119–120 (d5) excerpts** and is filled in from general program knowledge — flagged `^[inferred]` accordingly. The d2 Apollo 17 transcript ingest **partially upgrades** the inference: GET timestamps, the TD&E SEP at ~03:34–03:42 GET, the cislunar-coast cosmic-ray-flash testimony at ~02:18 GET, and the lunar-orbit pass over Grimaldi at ~03:15:33 GET (Day 3 lunar-orbit operations) are all extracted from d2 directly. ^[extracted] The d5 science-debrief excerpt — pulled from deep into the UV-astronomy section of MSC-07632 — does **not** add mission-profile data; its content is principal-investigator scientific testimony from the speaker labeled HENRY (almost certainly Richard C. Henry, Apollo 17 SIM-bay Far-UV Spectrometer S-169 PI) ^[inferred] rather than crew testimony.

## Institutional context

Apollo 17 was operated under the **[[entities/nasa|NASA]] / Manned Spacecraft Center (MSC) Houston** organizational chart, the same office that originated the **[[references/nasa-pao-t-00763-r1b-gemini-7-1965|1965 GT-7 PAO release tape]]** seven years earlier. The mission produced **at least three structurally distinct documents** known to the corpus: the **Apollo 17 Air-to-Ground Voice Transcript** (Dec 1972, contemporaneous voice-loop record); the **MSC-07631 Technical Crew Debriefing** (4 Jan 1973, MSC Training Office, Crew Training and Simulation Division — engineering-audience); and the **MSC-07632 Crew Debriefing for Science** (8 Jan 1973, MSC Science Requirements Branch, Planetary and Earth Sciences Division — scientific-audience). The d6 cover carries an **auto-declassification clause** (90 days from publication) that — in practice — appears to have been overridden by NASA's standing classification-review apparatus until a 2026 reviewer pull under E.O. 13526 §3.3(a). ^[inferred] The d5 cover — at least as captured in the OCR bundle — shows **no equivalent classification stamp**, suggesting the science debrief was issued to a less-restricted distribution than the technical debrief. ^[inferred] The d2 voice-loop transcript also shows **no classification stamp** in the OCR — voice-loop transcripts of this kind were typically released through MSC PAO unclassified. ^[inferred]

The d2 / d5 / d6 set establishes that **a single Apollo mission produced multiple parallel documentation streams** spanning **two temporal layers** (real-time voice loop + post-mission debrief) and **multiple sub-offices** of the same center within days or hours of each other — a structural feature of the NASA documentation regime that the corpus had not yet evidenced from the [[references/nasa-pao-t-00763-r1b-gemini-7-1965|1965 GT-7 PAO release tape]] alone. ^[inferred] The triple-artifact-class anchoring of Apollo 17 in the corpus is now the **strongest anti-suppression argument** the orbital-class sub-corpus has produced: three independent intake paths through the NASA documentation regime, all null for UAP, leave no plausible "the UAP material was in another document" rejoinder for this mission. ^[inferred]
