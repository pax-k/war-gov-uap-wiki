# Gray Barker

**Gray Barker** (1925–1984 ^[inferred]) was a **civilian flying-saucer researcher** based in **Clarksburg, West Virginia**, who founded the periodical *The Saucerian* and authored the 1956 book *They Knew Too Much About Flying Saucers* (University Books, NYC). ^[extracted] The wiki carries Barker as an entity because he is the **subject** of the publisher's book-jacket clipping that the FBI clipped and serialized as **62-HQ-83894 serial 403** (see [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-serial-403]]).

## Provenance note

Almost all biographical detail on this page is sourced from the **publisher's promotional jacket copy** (University Books, Inc., 1956) — which is **not an independent source**. ^[inferred] It is publisher-authored marketing material that the FBI clipped, designed to sell Barker's book. Treat the biographical facts as *self-presented* until corroborated by independent sources. ^[inferred] The jacket also paraphrases Barker's central thesis, but does not reproduce his evidence; the wiki should not promote the thesis to extracted-status outside the artifact page.

## Biographical sketch (per the 1956 jacket copy)

- **Age** — *"thirty years"* at the time the jacket was written (1956 publication, possibly written in 1955) → **birth ~1925–26**. ^[inferred]
- **Physical description** — *"a six-footer, handsome, and with a warm, infectious laugh"*; retains *"just a trace of the accent of the West Virginia farming country where he grew up."* ^[extracted]
- **Prior careers** — *"head of an English department in a Maryland school system, an audio-visual education consultant for a large school supplier, theatre manager and motion picture projection technician."* ^[extracted]
- **Current business (1956)** — operates a **film booking and buying agency** based in the **Clarksburg News Building** (Clarksburg, WV — the same building as the *Clarksburg Daily News*, owned by H. G. Rhawn). ^[extracted]
- **Periodical** — publishes ***The Saucerian***, a *"flying saucer periodical"*. ^[extracted]
- **Other writing** — contributes to *"educational journals and motion picture technical magazines"*. ^[extracted]

## Saucer research career

- **1952** — Barker's interest dates to *"one of the most astonishing [saucers] [that] allegedly landed near his home in West Virginia"* in 1952. ^[extracted] This is the **Flatwoods, WV "Braxton County Monster" event of 12 Sep 1952** — a high-profile civilian sighting + alleged-occupant encounter that Barker investigated and wrote up. ^[inferred] Not yet a wiki page; deferred to a future ingest if independent primary-source material on Flatwoods enters the corpus.
- **1952–56** — Barker investigates Flatwoods and *"after several years of close contact with the leading men in the field"* publishes ***They Knew Too Much About Flying Saucers*** (1956, University Books, NYC; jacket designed by Gropper Associates, Inc., $3.50). ^[extracted] See [[references/barker-1956-they-knew-too-much]].
- **Beyond 1956** — Barker continued saucer research and publishing for several decades but the wiki currently has no source material on this period. ^[open]

## Why Barker matters for the corpus

Barker's 1956 book is **the source of the modern "Men in Black" / MIB folklore**. ^[inferred] The book's jacket copy explicitly introduces the *"three men in dark suits"* who allegedly *"silenced … one by one"* leading saucer researchers — a phrase whose folklore-trajectory eventually produced the broader MIB cultural pattern. ^[inferred] The wiki's interest in the present serial is not in Barker's claims as evidence, but in the fact that the **FBI clipped the book's jacket and filed it at HQ**. ^[inferred]

The artifact does not establish that the FBI engaged with Barker's MIB thesis substantively — only that HQ clipped the publisher's promotional copy that summarized it. ^[inferred] Whether **other serials in 62-HQ-83894** show FBI engagement with Barker the person (interviews, name-trace, mail cover) or with Barker's thesis is an open question to be resolved by later ingests in the [[entities/fbi-hq-62-83894-file|62-HQ-83894 series]].

## Position in the corpus

Barker is the wiki's **first civilian-saucer-researcher entity** of the **mid-1950s pre-NICAP / pre-APRO era**. ^[inferred] The corpus's prior civilian-research entries are:

- [[entities/oklahoma-ufo-research-association|Oklahoma UFO Research Association (OUFORA)]] — mid-1960s civilian research org, Oklahoma City; subject of 1966 FBI Detroit redirect-to-USAF letter.
- [[entities/union-of-ufologists-turkmenabat]] — 2004 Turkmenabat NGO, subject of a US Embassy Ashgabat cable.

Barker's profile is structurally distinct from both — he is an **individual civilian researcher / author / periodical-publisher**, not an organization. He pre-dates OUFORA by a decade and pre-dates the institutional civilian-research movement that NICAP (1956–80) and APRO (1952–88) built out. ^[inferred]
