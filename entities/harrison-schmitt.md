# Harrison H. Schmitt

**Harrison H. "Jack" Schmitt** (b. 1935) — **PhD geologist** (Harvard, 1964) and **NASA Astronaut Group 4** ("The Scientists", 1965), Lunar Module Pilot of **[[entities/apollo-17|Apollo 17]]** (7–19 Dec 1972), and the **only professional scientist to walk on the Moon during Apollo** — the first and only scientist-astronaut to land on the lunar surface in the 20th century. ^[inferred] Later US Senator (R-NM), 1977–1983. ^[inferred]

In the wiki's UAP corpus, Schmitt enters as a **named speaker** on **two structurally distinct Apollo 17 artifacts**: the **[[references/nasa-apollo-17-technical-crew-debriefing-1973|MSC-07631 Technical Crew Debriefing]]** page-24-4 excerpt (post-mission, ALFMED passage) and the **[[references/nasa-uap-d2-apollo-17-transcript-1972|Apollo 17 air-to-ground voice transcript]]** (real-time, S-IVB-as-flasher visual ID + lunar-surface flash north of Grimaldi). The d2 ingest **upgrades Schmitt's witness role** to **the corpus's first scientist-astronaut to anchor a real-time TLP-class observation** with a procedural seismometer-cross-correlation follow-up. ^[inferred]

## Apollo 17 voice-loop lines (d2)

### 1. S-IVB-as-flasher visual ID (Tape 46/8, ~02:18:46 GET, Day 2)

> **LMP (Schmitt)**: *"That's the side and - of the S-IVB - and then the engine bell, Gene."* ^[extracted]
>
> **LMP (Schmitt)** (Tape 46/8): *"a couple of revolutions ago when I was looking at it, I had a much brighter view and I believe I was looking at it broadside. It looks to me like it may be flashing more or less end-on now."* ^[extracted]

Schmitt provides the **morphology framing** that — combined with [[entities/gene-cernan|Cernan]]'s bright/dull two-flash signature observation — produces the corpus's first crew-side S-IVB visual ID by morphology + rotation period without ground-side ranging support. ^[inferred] CC's *"Roger, Jack. Is that with the monocular you're looking at it?"* establishes that Schmitt was using the **monocular** for the visual ID. ^[extracted] Schmitt is also the **initial spotter** of the rotating object on the prior tape (Tape 46/4) before Cernan independently locates and tracks it. ^[inferred]

### 2. Lunar-surface flash north of Grimaldi (Tape 60/1, ~03:15:38 GET, Day 3 lunar orbit)

> **LMP (Schmitt)**: *"Hey, I just saw a flash on the lunar surface!"* ^[extracted]
>
> **LMP (Schmitt)**: *"It was just out there north of Grimaldi. Just north of Grimaldi. You might see if you got anything on your seismometers, although a small impact probably would give a fair amount of visible light."* ^[extracted]
>
> **LMP (Schmitt)**: *"It was a bright little flash right out there near that crater. ... Then there is another one north of it. Fairly sharp one north of it is where there was just a thin streak of light."* ^[extracted]
>
> **LMP (Schmitt)**: *"I keep looking occasionally for - yes, we will. I - I was planning on looking for those kind of things."* ^[extracted]

This is the corpus's **first real-time procedural exchange** for a **Transient Lunar Phenomenon (TLP)** observation. ^[inferred] Schmitt was actively looking for surface flashes (*"I was planning on looking for those kind of things"*), supplied the **meteoroid-impact mechanism** himself (*"a small impact probably would give a fair amount of visible light"*), and Mission Control's procedural response was to commit to **cross-correlate with the ALSEP seismometers** and to ask Schmitt to mark the location on a map. The seismometer cross-correlation outcome is **not preserved in the d2 excerpt**. ^[ambiguous] **The d6 page-24-4 *"I had one which I thought was a flash on the lunar surface"* line is the same observation event in summary form** — d2 preserves the real-time procedural exchange that d6 collapses to a subordinate clause. ^[inferred]

### 3. SEP fragment field (Tape 5/2, ~03:34 GET, Day 1)

> **LMP (Schmitt)**: *"There's a whole bunch of big ones on my window down there - just bright. It looks like the Fourth of July out of Ron's window."* ^[extracted]

Schmitt is the **initial spotter** of the SEP fragment field that Evans then photographs and Cernan summarizes. ^[inferred]

### 4. Lunar-orbit geology testimony (Tape 59/19 – Tape 60/2, ~03:15:33 GET, Day 3 lunar orbit)

The d2 excerpt also preserves several minutes of Schmitt's **scientist-astronaut lunar-geology testimony** during lunar-orbit operations — Oceanus Procellarum, Grimaldi, Rima Gamma, Mare Marginis / Crisium, the western edge of Procellarum's mare-highland contact, and the arcuate rilles in the Grimaldi area. The testimony is **not UAP-relevant** but it establishes **Schmitt's observational vantage at the moment of the lunar-surface flash** — he was actively studying the same Grimaldi-region terrain where he then spotted the flash. ^[extracted] This is structurally important: **Schmitt's expected-class observation framing** (*"I was planning on looking for those kind of things"*) is directly supported by his contemporaneous active-scientific-observation context. ^[inferred]

## Apollo 17 debriefing line (d6, page 24-4)

> **SCHMITT**: *"Transearth we had only a small crescent of an Earth and it was not feasible to do any extensive weather observations. We had light flashes just about continuously during the whole flight when we were dark adapted. I had one which I thought was a flash on the lunar surface. That one period of time when we had the blindfolds on for the ALFMED experiment there were just no visible flashes, although that evening, that night, before I went to sleep I noticed that I was seeing the light flashes again. So, it just seemed to be that one interval either side of it where the light flash was not visible to myself or to the other two crewmen."* ^[extracted]

### What Schmitt's testimony establishes

- **All three Apollo 17 crewmen experienced cosmic-ray-induced retinal light flashes "just about continuously" when dark-adapted in cislunar space.** ^[extracted]
- **The ALFMED experiment was conducted on Apollo 17 with a defined blindfolded interval during sleep**, during which Schmitt reports the flashes were not visible to himself or the other two crewmen. ^[extracted]
- **The flashes resumed that night**, after the ALFMED interval. ^[extracted]
- **Schmitt observed one flash during transearth coast that he believed was a flash on the lunar surface** — a candidate for an unrelated phenomenon (transient lunar phenomenon, cosmic-ray-on-CCD, or ALFMED-class retinal event misattributed). ^[ambiguous] **The d2 voice-loop record (Tape 60/1) anchors this to a specific lunar-orbit pass over Grimaldi at ~03:15:38 GET, with Schmitt-supplied meteoroid-impact mechanism and Mission Control commitment to seismometer cross-correlation** — making it a TLP-class observation, not a misattributed retinal event. ^[partially-resolved]

### What it does *not* establish

- **No UAP / unidentified-object observation.** The light flashes are a **documented neuro-optical effect of cosmic-ray ions traversing the retina**, with a NASA-instrumented investigative apparatus (ALFMED) already in place by Apollo 17. ^[inferred] **The phenomenon was already astronaut-class characterized 3.5 years earlier on [[entities/apollo-11|Apollo 11]]** by [[entities/buzz-aldrin|Aldrin]] (primary first-principles observer) and [[entities/neil-armstrong|Armstrong]] (corroborating witness; articulator of the *"neutron or some kind of an atomic particle that would be in the visible spectrum"* model statement) — see [[concepts/cosmic-ray-light-flashes]] and [[references/nasa-uap-d4-apollo-11-technical-crew-debriefing-1969]]. The 3.5-year arc Apollo 11 → Apollo 17 documents NASA's response evolving from "we should investigate this" (1969) to "we have instrumented an experiment to characterize this" (1972). ^[inferred]
- **The "flash on the lunar surface"** observation is **not resolved on the excerpt** — Schmitt notes it as one of the continuous light-flash sequence rather than as a discrete TLP report. ^[ambiguous]

## Witness class

Schmitt is the corpus's **first scientist-astronaut witness** — a structurally distinct sub-class within the astronaut-class observer floor described on [[concepts/orbital-uap-sighting]]. ^[inferred] His debriefing language reflects this background: he reports the ALFMED interval observation with **experimental-design vocabulary** ("one interval either side of it where the light flash was not visible"), implicitly framing the observation in terms of **before/during/after intervals** rather than as a witness narrative. ^[inferred]

This sub-class is structurally relevant because the **2023 NASA Independent Study Team report on UAP** is a scientist-led study of UAP data — and Schmitt's 1972 ALFMED testimony is the corpus's earliest example of a scientist-astronaut providing **post-mission instrument-correlated testimony** about an in-mission optical phenomenon. ^[inferred]
