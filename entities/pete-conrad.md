# Charles "Pete" Conrad Jr.

**Charles "Pete" Conrad Jr.** (1930–1999) — **US Navy naval aviator** and **NASA Astronaut Group 2** ("[The New Nine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_Astronaut_Group_2)", selected 1962 — same group as **[[entities/frank-borman|Frank Borman]]** and **[[entities/jim-lovell|Jim Lovell]]**), Commander of **[[entities/apollo-12|Apollo 12]]** (14–24 Nov 1969), the **second crewed lunar landing**. ^[inferred] In the wiki's UAP corpus, Conrad enters as a **named speaker** on the **[[references/nasa-uap-d1-apollo-12-transcript-1969|d1 Apollo 12 air-to-ground voice loop excerpt]]** at MET ~6d 00h 21m, on the **tracking-light burnout / co-orbital-debris exchange** that **empirically confirms** the [[concepts/orbital-uap-sighting|orbital-UAP-sighting concept page's]] previously-inferred "your own debris field" conventional-explanation prior. ^[inferred]

## Apollo 12 d1 transcript line (Tape 93/8)

> **CDR-LM** *(Conrad)*: *"Hey, Houston. It looks like our tracking light's burned out. Dick hasn't been able to find us in this sextant. And on the first nightside pass we had little bits and pieces floating along with us and we could tell that the tracking light was flashing on them. And we still have, I've presumed to think, bits and pieces floating along and nothing's flashing on them, so I'm pretty sure it burned out."* ^[extracted]

The line is **not** a UAP report. It is the corpus's **first instance where a trained astronaut-class observer empirically confirms the canonical co-orbital-debris baseline** — distinguishing visible co-orbital bits and pieces with vs. without active tracking-light illumination. The exchange resolves a tracking-light filament status question (CAPCOM's electrical-monitoring telemetry alone could not distinguish a burned-out lamp from an operational one in the bus-tie configuration in use during rendezvous) ^[inferred] — but the **observational substrate** is the canonical small-luminous-object-near-spacecraft signature on which the orbital-UAP-sighting concept page's conventional-explanation prior rests.

## Skylab 2 d7 §23 testimony — cosmic-ray retinal flashes (NEW)

Conrad's second corpus appearance is on the **[[references/nasa-uap-d7-skylab-technical-crew-debriefing-1973|d7 Skylab Technical Crew Debriefing]]** SL-2 §23 *Unusual or Unexpected Visual Phenomenon* discussion — as **CDR of the SL-2 mission and one of three witnesses** to **in-cabin cosmic-ray retinal light flashes** with **explicit South Atlantic Anomaly (SAA) correlation hypothesis**. Conrad's contributions:

> **CONRAD**: *"Some of them to me were a spot or sunbursts. Some were streaks. The streaks, in my case, were less frequent than the bursts. Most of them were in my peripheral visual field. Very few in the central visual field. I don't know why."* ^[extracted]

> **CONRAD**: *"You have to concentrate but you can determine they are in one eye."* ^[extracted]

> **CONRAD**: *"Sometimes I'd be lying there with my eyes half closed, and I'd see a fire sensor wink."* ^[extracted]

> **CONRAD**: *"Yes, I did too."* (corroborating Weitz on entrance-and-exit-streak double events) ^[extracted]

> **CONRAD**: *"One night I remember that there was a long shot then it was blank then there was a long shot in rapid succession, of course, but very definitely in and out - or across the eye."* ^[extracted]

The substantive content for the corpus:

1. **Sunburst-vs-streak morphology distinction** — Conrad's *"some of them to me were a spot or sunbursts. Some were streaks. The streaks, in my case, were less frequent than the bursts"* is the corpus's **first explicit two-class morphology distinction** for cosmic-ray retinal flashes. ^[extracted] The two morphology classes correspond to **(a) end-on retinal entry / termination of the particle track ("sunburst")** and **(b) chord traversal of the retina ("streak")** — both predicted by the standard cosmic-ray-on-retina model, with the chord-track being less common because it requires the particle to enter at low angle relative to the retina. ^[inferred]
2. **Peripheral-vs-central visual field bias** — Conrad's *"Most of them were in my peripheral visual field. Very few in the central visual field"* is the corpus's **first astronaut-class observation of this bias**. ^[extracted] Consistent with rod-dominated peripheral retina being more sensitive to single-photon stimulation than cone-dominated foveal retina. ^[inferred]
3. **Single-eye localization confirmation** — Conrad: *"You have to concentrate but you can determine they are in one eye"* — agrees with [[entities/paul-weitz|Weitz]]'s *"primarily in the left eye"* but differs from [[entities/joseph-kerwin|Kerwin]]'s "couldn't isolate". ^[extracted] Conrad's framing ("you have to concentrate but you can determine") is **observational-discipline data**: he experimentally tested the localization rather than passively reporting.
4. **Direct in-and-out / across-the-eye observation** — Conrad's *"in and out - or across the eye"* directly confirms [[entities/buzz-aldrin|Aldrin]]'s 1969 d4 chord-track double-event model. ^[inferred] The **same single-particle-two-events structure** observed by Conrad (Skylab 2, LEO inside-magnetosphere, 1973) and Aldrin (Apollo 11, cislunar coast, 1969) — independent witnesses, different orbital regimes, same phenomenology.
5. **Fire-sensor disambiguation** — Conrad's mention of seeing a fire sensor wink is **observational-discipline context**: he distinguishes the cosmic-ray flash phenomenology from confounding sources of in-cabin optical signal. ^[extracted] Kerwin reciprocates with the disambiguation rule: *"Once you've seen a few of each, there is question of which is which."*

The **structural significance** for Conrad's witness footprint:

**Conrad is the corpus's first (with Bean, second) multi-mission astronaut witness**, but where Bean's two observations occupy two structurally different sub-classes (D and F), **Conrad's two observations occupy two structurally different categories**:

| Mission | Source | Observational role | Polarity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **[[entities/apollo-12\|Apollo 12]] d1** (Nov 1969) | [[references/nasa-uap-d1-apollo-12-transcript-1969]] | Tracking-light burnout / co-orbital-debris empirical confirmation | **Non-UAP** — empirical confirmation of "your own debris field" prior |
| **[[entities/skylab\|Skylab 2]] d7** (May–Jun 1973) | [[references/nasa-uap-d7-skylab-technical-crew-debriefing-1973]] | Cosmic-ray retinal flash co-witness with sunburst-vs-streak morphology distinction + peripheral-field bias | **Non-UAP** — cosmic-ray-flash characterization (orbital-class structural analogue of [[concepts/figurative-ufo-rhetoric\|figurative-UFO rhetoric]] — null-content with UAP-keyword false-positive risk) |

**Both of Conrad's corpus observations are non-UAP** — but both **strengthen the corpus's epistemic infrastructure**: the d1 line empirically confirms the co-orbital-debris baseline; the d7 SL-2 §23 testimony substantively answers the [[concepts/cosmic-ray-light-flashes|cosmic-ray-flash]] concept page's LEO-baseline open question. ^[inferred] Conrad is therefore the corpus's **infrastructure-strengthening multi-mission witness** — distinct from Bean's **observational-discipline-demonstrating multi-mission** role. ^[inferred]

## Witness pedigree

Conrad is in the same **astronaut-class** witness category as **[[entities/frank-borman|Borman]]**, **[[entities/jim-lovell|Lovell]]** (NASA Group 2), and **[[entities/gene-cernan|Cernan]]**, **[[entities/ronald-evans|Evans]]**, **[[entities/harrison-schmitt|Schmitt]]** (NASA Groups 3, 5, 4 respectively) — and shares the same trained-observer floor described on **[[concepts/orbital-uap-sighting]]**. The **distinguishing feature** of Conrad's d1 line is that his observation is a **negative-direction empirical confirmation** of the corpus's standing conventional-explanation prior — i.e., his transmission **strengthens the conventional reading** of small luminous objects near a manned spacecraft on orbit, rather than introducing a new UAP-adjacent observation. ^[inferred]

## Witness-pedigree comparator

- **USN Naval Academy** graduate (class of 1953).
- **Patuxent River Test Pilot School** alum; experimental aircraft test pilot. ^[inferred]
- **NASA Astronaut Group 2** (1962); same group as Borman and Lovell.
- **Command Pilot, Gemini 5** (8-day endurance); **Command Pilot, Gemini 11** (highest-Earth-orbit Gemini); **Commander, Apollo 12** (precision lunar landing); **Commander, Skylab 2** (first crewed Skylab mission). ^[inferred]

His radio language on the d1 tape is consistent with USN aviation convention (*"first nightside pass"*, *"bits and pieces floating along"*, *"I've presumed to think"*) — **technically precise** without leaning on UAP-specific descriptors. ^[inferred]
