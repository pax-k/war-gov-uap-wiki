# Frank Borman

USAF test pilot, member of the second NASA astronaut group ("[The Next Nine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_Astronaut_Group_2)", selected 1962), and Command Pilot of the **[[entities/gemini-7|Gemini 7]]** long-duration / rendezvous mission of December 1965. In the wiki, Borman is the **first-witness caller** of *"a bogey at ten o'clock high"* on the air-to-ground voice loop captured on **[[references/nasa-pao-t-00763-r1b-gemini-7-1965|NASA PAO Tape T-00763(R1b)]]** at approximately **04 hr 24 min mission elapsed time** on **4 Dec 1965**.

## Witness pedigree

Borman is the **first astronaut-class witness** in the wiki, and his pedigree raises the trained-observer floor for the corpus:

- **USAF Academy** graduate (class of 1950).
- **Edwards AFB Test Pilot School** alum; experimental aircraft test pilot.
- **NASA Astronaut Group 2** (1962); selected from a USAF / Navy / Marine test-pilot pool.
- **Command Pilot, Gemini 7**, the **longest manned spaceflight to that date** (~14 days). Performed the first orbital rendezvous (with GT-6A) two weeks into the mission.

His radio language on the GT-7 voice loop is **technically precise** in aviation/military terms ("bogey at ten o'clock high", "we have debris up here", "going into polar orbit"). The use of *"bogey"* is consistent with USAF intercept-controller / ATC convention for any unidentified contact, not specifically with a UAP claim. ^[inferred]

## What Borman is recorded as saying on T-00763(R1b)

| Beat | Recorded transmission |
|---|---|
| 1 | *"A bogey at ten o'clock high."* |
| 2 (repeat) | *"I said we have a bogey at ten o'clock high."* |
| 3 | *"We have debris up here — this is an actual sighting."* |
| 4 | *"We also have the booster in sight."* |
| 5 | *"We have very, very many — it looks like hundreds of little particles going by to the left out about three or four miles."* |
| 6 | *"...it looks like a path of the vehicle at 90 degrees."* |
| 7 | *"They've passed now. They're going into polar orbit."* |

He does **not** at any point in the released transcript:

- describe morphology of the bogey (shape, color, size, motion, persistence);
- explicitly differentiate the bogey from the booster after Houston's prompt;
- state that the bogey was anomalous in any technical sense.

This minimalism is itself informative: on the strict source, Borman's *"bogey"* call is a **single-bearing, single-witness, no-morphology** report. ^[inferred]

## What is *not* established on the source-only record

- That Borman believed the bogey was a UFO. The source contains no such claim. ^[extracted] (Post-mission interpretations attributed to Borman in popular literature exist outside this artifact and are not loaded into the wiki from this source.)
- That Borman saw the bogey as morphologically distinct from the booster. The transcript supports both readings. ^[ambiguous]
- That any classified follow-up on the GT-7 bogey occurred. Not addressable from this artifact alone. ^[open]
