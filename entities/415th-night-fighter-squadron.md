# 415th Night Fighter Squadron

USAAF night-fighter squadron, attached to **XII Tactical Air Command (Adv)** within **First Tactical Air Force (Provisional)** under [[entities/shaef-air-staff|SHAEF]] in the European Theater of Operations. The unit operated over Alsace, the Saar, and the western Rhine during the closing months of WWII. ^[extracted]

The squadron is the unit credited in the [[references/foo-fighters-shaef-1944-1945|SHAEF Air/TS.37153/A-2 file]] with **introducing the term "foofighter" into official military intelligence correspondence**. The 30 January 1945 indorsement signed by **Capt F. B. Ringwald, A.C., Intelligence Officer**, ends with the explicit footnote: *"Foofighters is the name given these phenomenon by combat crews of this Squadron."* ^[extracted]

## Reports of record

Capt Ringwald's 30 January 1945 indorsement consolidated extracts from **ten dated sortie reports** filed between 14 December 1944 and 29–30 January 1945. The sectors named include Erstein, Breisach, Sarrebourg–Strasbourg highway, Hagenau, Worms, Luneville, Saverne, Weissembourg–Landau, Ingweiller. Each sortie ends with the same null result on GCI radar cross-check: when the pilot called Control to ask whether a Bogey aircraft was in the area, "he received a negative answer". ^[extracted]

Notable sortie extracts:

| Night | Sector | Brief observation |
|---|---|---|
| 14–15 Dec 1944 | Erstein (V-9381) | Large red light at 2000 ft going east at 18:40, ~200 mph |
| 16–17 Dec 1944 | 20 mi N of Breisach (W-0173) | 5–6 flashing red/green lights in "T" shape, paced and turned with the aircraft |
| 22–23 Dec 1944 | Sarrebourg–Strasbourg | Two ground-up lights levelled off at the aircraft's altitude, peeled off, "appeared to be under perfect control" |
| 26–27 Dec 1944 | Q-9050 / R-1556 | "Distinct lines somewhat like arrows" off port |
| 26–27 Dec 1944 | V-7050 | Airborne white lights, "staggered evenly vertically", 1–4 visible at once |
| 26–27 Dec 1944 | Worms | Light came within 100 ft and followed for 5 minutes |
| 27–28 Dec 1944 | NE of Luneville | Three sets of three (red/white) lights, port and starboard, closed in to Angels 8 |
| 1–2 Jan 1945 | N of Strasbourg / N of Saverne | Saw "Foofighters" |
| 14–15 Jan 1945 | Ingweiller | Large orange glow ~5 ft diameter at 6000 ft, 20:00 |
| 29–30 Jan 1945 | Weissembourg–Landau | Two amber lights, ~30 sec, "about a foot in diameter", followed and disappeared on hard turn |

(See [[references/foo-fighters-shaef-1944-1945]] for the full set of extracts.)

## Reporting chain

The squadron's S-2 intelligence section reported up through:

1. **A.C. of S. A-2, XII Tactical Air Command (Adv)** — Lt Col Leavitt Corning, Jr.; later Lt Col John E. Wooley
2. **First Tactical Air Force (Provisional), A/C of S A-2** — Maj S. V. Boykin (Executive Officer); Col C. A. Young
3. **Air Staff, [[entities/shaef-air-staff|SHAEF]]** — A/Cmdr [[entities/c-m-grierson|C. M. Grierson]], A.C. of S. A-2
4. **Air Ministry, [[entities/uk-air-ministry-ddi2|DDI2]]** (and A.D.I.(K), and SHAEF Scientific Investigation Division) ^[extracted]

## Place in the larger UAP picture

The 415th NFS report bundle is the **earliest known unit-level institutional collection of unexplained-aerial-phenomena reports** in the wiki. Its structure — anonymous extracts, behavioural detail, GCI cross-check, escalation up the chain of command — prefigures the 1948 [[concepts/flying-disc-reporting-protocol|10-element Flying Disc reporting template]] by ~3 years. ^[inferred]
