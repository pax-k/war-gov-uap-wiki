# Albert K. Bender

**Albert K. Bender** (active ~1952-1958+) was a **Bridgeport, Connecticut**-based civilian saucer-research figure who founded the **[[entities/international-flying-saucer-bureau|International Flying Saucer Bureau (IFSB)]]** in **1952**. ^[extracted] Bender served as **President / Editor** of the IFSB and edited its quarterly *Space Review* magazine (Vol II, No. 1, January 1953 issue retained at FBI HQ as 62-HQ-83894 serial 326 — see [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-7]]). ^[extracted] In 1953 Bender allegedly announced he had solved the saucer mystery (*"I know what the saucers are"*) and was **silenced by 'three men in black suits'** — the founding incident of the **[[concepts/three-men-in-black|MIB myth]]**. ^[extracted from secondary attestations]

## What the FBI corpus actually contains on Bender

The wiki's primary-source position on Bender is **fragmentary and indirect**. ^[inferred] Section 9 of [[entities/fbi-hq-62-83894-file|62-HQ-83894]] contains **no Bender-side filing** — no SAC Bridgeport report, no FBI Bender-residence interview, no Bender direct correspondence. ^[inferred] What the file does contain is **third-party restatements** of the silencing narrative:

| Date | Source | Restatement of the Bender silencing |
|---|---|---|
| 7 Dec 1958 | Claude H. Marck Jr. → FBI (serial 397 background) | *"In 1953 Bender stated bluntly, 'I know what the saucers are.' Then, 'three men in black suits' silenced him."* |
| 19 Dec 1958 | Phoenix SAC interview of Marck (serial 398) | *"Bender formed the International Flying Saucer Bureau in Connecticut in 1952 to look into the flying saucer mystery. In 1953 Bender allegedly stated he knew what the saucers are; then, according to Marck, 'Three men in black suits' silenced Bender to the extent that even today Bender will not discuss the matter of his 'hush-up' with anyone."* |
| 15 Oct 1958 | *Saucerian Bulletin* Vol 3 #4 (ed. [[entities/gray-barker]]) — clipped per serial 396 | *"You have often wondered where the 3 men came from that silenced Bender. … Adamski tells me that the 3 men were from the FBI, Air Force Intelligence, and the Central Intelligence Agency."* |
| 1956 | Barker, *They Knew Too Much About Flying Saucers* (jacket only — see [[references/barker-1956-they-knew-too-much]]) | book structured around Bender silencing |

The **FBI never approaches Bender** in any section-9 filing. ^[inferred] The Bureau's posture is **passive accumulation** of materials that name Bender — never **investigative engagement** with Bender himself. ^[inferred]

## What we infer about Bender

From the IFSB *Space Review* masthead retained at HQ (62-83894 serial 326, Jan 1953 issue): ^[extracted]

- **Role**: President / Editor of IFSB
- **Mailing address**: Post Office Box 241, Bridgeport 2, Connecticut, U.S.A.
- **Family**: a **Fred J. Bender** is listed as IFSB Historian — almost certainly a relative. ^[inferred]
- **Magazine cadence**: quarterly *Space Review*; the FBI retained the entire Jan 1953 issue.

We do **not** know from the FBI corpus: ^[inferred]

- Bender's date of birth, age, profession, marital status
- Whether the alleged 1953 silencing involved any actual federal contact
- What Bender's *"I know what the saucers are"* answer was supposed to be
- When Bender ceased IFSB operations (the IFSB became defunct sometime in 1953 per Marck's retelling)
- Whether Bender wrote his own account of the silencing (he later did, in *Flying Saucers and the Three Men*, 1962 — but the wiki has no FBI-corpus retention of that book) ^[inferred]

## Position vs. the MIB myth

Bender is the **single primary case** at the root of the broader MIB rumor. ^[inferred] The FBI file **does not corroborate** the silencing happened, **does not deny** it happened, and **does not investigate**. The Bureau's posture is **structurally consistent with both** truth and falsity of the rumor — see [[concepts/three-men-in-black]] for the analytical write-up.

The fact that the wiki's primary-source coverage of Bender is **only via Marck (a Phoenix-area civilian) and via Maney (a 13-year-old in Oklahoma City)** — i.e., not via Bridgeport SAC reporting — is a **structural feature of the file** worth noting. ^[inferred] If the FBI had ever investigated Bender, that filing would presumably appear under FBI New Haven (the SAC office covering Bridgeport CT in 1953-1958), not under Phoenix or Oklahoma City. ^[inferred] No such New Haven filing is in the wiki corpus to date. ^[open]
