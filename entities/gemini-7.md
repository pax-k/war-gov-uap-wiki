# Gemini 7

NASA Project Gemini mission **GT-7**, flown by **[[entities/frank-borman|Cdr Frank Borman]]** (Command Pilot) and **[[entities/jim-lovell|Pilot James A. Lovell Jr.]]** from **4 December 1965** through **18 December 1965**. GT-7 served as the **long-duration objective** (~14 days, the longest manned spaceflight to that date) and as the **passive rendezvous target** for the GT-6A mission flown by Schirra and Stafford on 15 Dec 1965 — the first orbital rendezvous of two manned spacecraft. The combined mission designation **"GT-7/6"** (visible on page 0 of [[references/nasa-pao-t-00763-r1b-gemini-7-1965|T-00763(R1b)]]) refers to this paired flight.

In the UAP corpus, GT-7 is the **mission frame** for the wiki's first orbital UAP-adjacent record: **[[references/sighting-gemini-7-bogey-1965-12-04|the 4 Dec 1965 "bogey at ten o'clock high" call]]** at approximately **04 hr 24 min mission elapsed time** (~ 2354Z 4 Dec 1965). ^[inferred]

## Mission profile (relevant facts only)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Launch | 4 Dec 1965 ~ 19:30:03 UTC, KSC LC-19, Titan-II GLV |
| Crew | Borman (CDR), Lovell (Pilot) |
| Spacecraft | Gemini 7 (Spacecraft No. 7) |
| Orbit | ~ 161 × 326 km, 28.9° inclination, 89.4 min period ^[inferred] |
| Duration | ~ 13 d 18 h 35 m |
| Recovery | 18 Dec 1965 |
| Co-mission | Passive target for GT-6A rendezvous (Schirra / Stafford), 15 Dec 1965 |

These facts are not extracted from the source artifact (which contains only the air-to-ground commentary) but are supplied as background for cross-checking the MET-to-UTC conversion. ^[inferred]

## At ~04 hr 24 min MET

The `04h24m` PAO sign-off (page 0) puts the bogey-particle-booster exchange near the **end of GT-7 day 1 / start of revolution 3** ^[inferred] — well within the window where the spent **Titan-II second stage** would still be in close visual range of the spacecraft. This is consistent with Borman and Lovell each independently acquiring the booster on opposite-side bearings (Borman *"ten o'clock high"* initially, Lovell *"two o'clock"*).

The cross-track 90° particle stream Borman describes 3–4 mi off the left is **harder to attribute to GT-7's own launch** because debris co-launched with GT-7 would track approximately along, not perpendicular to, the spacecraft's orbital plane. ^[inferred] No source-side disambiguation.

## Why the GT-7/6 mission frame matters for UAP corpus

- **Mission Control / PAO release channel**: this is the wiki's first record where the originating-agency intake apparatus is **NASA Mission Control + NASA PAO**. Every other 1944–1994 corpus channel runs through DoD-classified or DoS-classified routing. ^[inferred] See [[entities/nasa-pao]].
- **Voice-loop / radio-instrument primary record**: this is the wiki's first record where the **primary instrument** is a real-time voice-loop recording, not a written report or cable. ^[extracted]
- **Trained-observer pedigree at the highest extant tier**: NASA Group 2 test pilots, on-orbit, structurally familiar with their own spacecraft systems and with the visual classes of orbital debris.
