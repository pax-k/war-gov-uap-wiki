# NASA

The US **National Aeronautics and Space Administration** — the civilian space agency established by the **National Aeronautics and Space Act of 1958**, with operational lineage from NACA. In the wiki's UAP corpus, NASA enters as the **originating agency** of the **[[references/nasa-pao-t-00763-r1b-gemini-7-1965|GT-7 PAO Tape T-00763(R1b)]]** — the **4 Dec 1965 air-to-ground voice-loop transmission** in which **[[entities/frank-borman|Borman]]** calls *"a bogey at ten o'clock high"* on **[[entities/gemini-7|Gemini 7]]** day 1.

This is the wiki's first **NASA-originated** UAP-adjacent record. It is institutionally distinct from every prior 1944–1994 corpus record on three structural axes:

1. **Civilian, not military.** NASA is a Title-51 civilian agency under EOP delegation; intake-and-release apparatus is governed by the 1958 Space Act's *"contributions to the expansion of human knowledge of phenomena... shall be made available for the broadest practicable and appropriate dissemination."* This sets a **release-by-default** posture absent from the USAF / Army / Navy / FBI / DOS channels. ^[inferred]
2. **Mission-control real-time intake.** The intake instrument is a **CAPCOM-mediated air-to-ground voice loop** captured on a master tape at MCC Houston, dubbed and released to the press the same news cycle. ^[extracted] No written form, no investigative file, no cable.
3. **PAO release channel.** The output instrument is a **NASA Public Affairs Office** tape replay with brief narrator commentary — see [[entities/nasa-pao]]. The closest functional analogue in the corpus is the **modern AARO press-deck briefing** ([[entities/aaro]]), but AARO post-dates NASA PAO by 60+ years and operates under different statutory authority. ^[inferred]

The 1965 entry inverts the disclosure default. The 1973 entries are **different NASA channels** — internal investigative/training (technical debrief) and internal scientific (science debrief), neither press-facing — and demonstrate that **NASA is not a uniformly release-by-default agency** across its sub-offices. The MSC PAO (1965), MSC Training Office (1973 technical), and MSC Science Requirements Branch (1973 science) sit on a multi-point disclosure-posture gradient inside the same center: PAO at the release-by-default end, Training Office at the CONFIDENTIAL-with-auto-declass-but-not-released end, Science Requirements Branch at an apparently-unclassified-but-also-not-released middle position. ^[inferred] The d5 / d6 pair further establishes that **a single Apollo mission produced parallel documentation streams** routed to different sub-offices of the same center within days of each other.

## Sub-entities (not yet stub-paged)

- **Mission Control Center (Houston)** — at the time of GT-7, MCC-Houston was the operational locus, with MCC-Cape (Florida) carrying a launch role only. The Borman / Lovell air-to-ground loop was Houston-centric. ^[inferred]
- **Manned Spacecraft Center (MSC)** — predecessor name to Johnson Space Center (renamed Feb 1973). The 1965 PAO tape would have been generated by MSC PAO under the MSC-era organizational chart, and the **[[references/nasa-apollo-17-technical-crew-debriefing-1973|Jan 1973 MSC-07631 Apollo 17 Technical Crew Debriefing]]** was prepared by the **MSC Training Office, Crew Training and Simulation Division** in the final weeks before the MSC → Johnson Space Center rename. ^[inferred]
- **Training Office, Crew Training and Simulation Division (MSC)** — the **investigative / training-oriented internal** sub-office that produced the 1973 Apollo 17 Technical Crew Debriefing. Distinct from MSC PAO (release-by-default, press-facing) — same parent center, different intake-and-output instrument. The **auto-declassification clause** (90 days from publication) on the MSC-07631 cover is a structural feature of this sub-office's standard cover sheet, contrasting with the PAO sub-office's release-by-default press-tape posture. ^[inferred]
- **Science Requirements Branch, Planetary and Earth Sciences Division (MSC)** — the **scientific-investigator-facing internal** sub-office that produced the 1973 Apollo 17 Crew Debriefing for Science (MSC-07632, 8 Jan 1973). Distinct from both MSC PAO (release-by-default press tape) and MSC Training Office (CONFIDENTIAL technical debrief). The d5 cover sheet shows **no classification stamp and no auto-declass clause** in the OCR — suggesting a less-restricted distribution than the d6 technical debrief, though paper-original verification is open. ^[inferred] On the page-119–120 excerpt, the speaker is **HENRY** — a principal investigator delivering UV-astronomy results from the Apollo 17 SIM-bay instruments — not a member of the flight crew. ^[inferred]
- [[entities/nasa-pao]] — Public Affairs Office; the GT-7 artifact's release instrument; first explicit corpus subentity.

## Statutory posture (relevant only)

- **1958 Space Act §102** establishes the *"broadest practicable and appropriate dissemination"* presumption.
- **Title 14 CFR Part 1213** (NASA News Release authority) institutionalizes the PAO release channel.
- These authorities do **not** preempt USAF / DoD classified handling of in-flight observations that DoD chooses to claim. They establish NASA's **default** disposition of mission data, not an absolute rule. ^[inferred]

## Second corpus role: NARA RG 255 archival home

The 1999 [[references/cometa-report-1999|COMETA Report]] — a private French non-government UAP report — is also archived in NARA RG 255 (Records of NASA). This is a **second, structurally different role** for NASA in the corpus: not as originating agency, but as **receiving-and-archiving agency** for an externally-produced policy document hand-delivered by [[entities/carol-rosin|Carol Rosin]] in 2001 and addressed to **"Dan"** at NASA — almost certainly **Daniel Goldin**, NASA Administrator from April 1992 to November 2001. ^[inferred]

The chain:

```
COMETA Committee (private, France)
    → published as VSD magazine special issue (1999)
    → Carol Rosin (private, US, undocumented sub-route)
    → hand-delivered to NASA Administrator Daniel Goldin (May 2001) ^[inferred]
    → archived in NARA RG 255
```

This is the corpus's first **non-governmental → US-civilian-agency** routing path for a UAP policy document. NASA functioned here as the **receiving institution** willing to accept and file an unsolicited policy report from a private foreign body — without (visibly in the source) generating any internal response document. ^[inferred]

The function NASA performs in this routing is therefore **archival accessibility**: a private French document made findable to US researchers via NARA's RG 255 finding aid. Without this routing, COMETA might never have entered a US archive at all. ^[inferred]
