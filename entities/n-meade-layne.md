# N. Meade Layne

**N. Meade Layne** (San Diego, 3615 Alexia Place ^[extracted]) — civilian writer, mimeograph publisher, and **founder of Borderland Sciences Research Associates** (1945, San Diego ^[inferred]) — the **earliest civilian-paranormal-saucer-theorist captured in the FBI corpus**. ^[inferred]

## Why he matters

Layne authors the **8 July 1947 Round Robin / Flying Roll 9-point memorandum** — a mimeographed manifesto distributed to *"certain scientists of distinction, to important aeronautical and military authorities, to a number of public officials, and to a few publications"* — see [[references/round-robin-flying-roll-1947]] for the full text. ^[extracted] The memo is captured at p. 160 of section 1 of [[entities/fbi-hq-62-83894-file|file 62-HQ-83894]]. ^[extracted]

The memo's **9 propositions** establish core saucer-paranormal frames that recur for decades: ^[inferred]

- inter-dimensional craft from an "etheric planet" interpenetrating earth (Tibetan-Buddhist Loka cosmology applied to saucers);
- materialization/dematerialization mechanics (anticipates 1960s+ abduction-phenomenon language);
- peaceful intent + remote-control + crewed mixed disposition;
- disintegration-ray armament + radar (not radio) detectability;
- humanoid-but-larger occupants.

## Self-described credentials

In the memo's preamble, Layne states: ^[extracted]

> The present writer has several university degrees and was formerly a university department head.

This is **the only direct credential claim** in the corpus's view of Layne. ^[extracted] The credentials are **not separately verified** in the FBI corpus. ^[inferred]

## Position in corpus

Layne is the **earliest example** in the wiki corpus of a structural pattern: a **civilian writer who claims partial-academic legitimacy** and uses it to anchor an explicitly **non-prosaic** saucer hypothesis. ^[inferred] The pattern recurs through:

- [[entities/gray-barker]] (1956) — saucer-popular-publishing
- [[entities/gabriel-green]] (1966) — AFSCA / Flying Saucers International
- [[entities/jaime-maussan]] (modern) — Mexican saucer-broadcasting

But Layne is structurally distinct from later authors in **explicitly invoking esoteric / Theosophical / Tibetan-Buddhist cosmology** ("Lokas or Talas"). ^[extracted] The framing is closer to **Theosophical Society / esoteric tradition** than to the later **contactee tradition** (Adamski-Bethurum-Williamson late 1940s/50s). ^[inferred]

## How the FBI processes him

The FBI's processing of Layne is **passive accumulation** — file the memo, no acknowledgment, no investigation, no forwarding. ^[inferred] This is **distinct** from how the Bureau treats civilian writers like Reibold-Omaha (acknowledged, forwarded to War Dept) or Pluskat-Brooklyn (acknowledged, "previous correspondent" note). The contrast suggests Layne's **letterhead-and-mimeograph apparatus** ("The Round Robin / The Flying Roll" header) marks him as fringe/paranormal and the standard acknowledgment-forward pattern is suspended. ^[inferred]

## Position in saucer historiography

Outside the FBI corpus, Layne is documented as the founder of **Borderland Sciences Research Associates / BSRA** (1945, San Diego), one of the earliest dedicated paranormal-saucer-research orgs in the US. ^[inferred] His Round Robin bulletins (predating July 1947) had been engaging Forteana, mediumship, and "etheric matter" topics; the 8 July 1947 memo marks his pivot to flying-saucer specialization in the days after [[references/sighting-arnold-mt-rainier-1947-06-24|Kenneth Arnold's]] sighting. ^[inferred]
