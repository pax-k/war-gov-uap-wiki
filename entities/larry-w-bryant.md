# Larry W. Bryant

**Larry W. Bryant** (b. **23 Feb 1938 in Shenandoah, Virginia** ^[extracted]) was a **Department of the Army civilian** (specifically, an **editorial clerk at the U.S. Army Transportation School, Fort Eustis, Virginia** as of 1963) and a long-running **civilian UFO-disclosure correspondent / critic** whose engagement with the FBI on saucer questions spans **at least 1959 → 1963** (and likely later, beyond the [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-9|section 9]] cutoff). ^[extracted]

Section 9 captures Bryant across **four serials**: 402 (1 Feb 1959 first letter), 430 (Sep 1963 follow-up + Hoover refusal), 431 (20 Sep 1963 internal Director-to-Norfolk-SAC airtel), and 439 (1965 *"Let's Challenge the UFO Censors"* article enclosed in section 9). ^[extracted]

## Biographical detail

- **Born**: 23 Feb 1938, Shenandoah, Virginia. ^[extracted]
- **1959 address**: 1002 Arnold Street, Newport News, Virginia. ^[extracted]
- **1963 address**: 5603 Madison Avenue, Newport News, Virginia 23605. ^[extracted]
- **Employment**: Editorial clerk, U.S. Army Transportation School, Fort Eustis, VA (per FBI Norfolk note in serial 430 NOTE, 27 Sep 1963). ^[extracted]
- **Hobby/avocation**: Civilian critic of *"the Federal Government's role in UFO research"* — by his own 1965 article description, *"writing for the past six years unofficial critiques of the Federal Government's role in UFO research"*. ^[extracted from 1965 article retained in serial 439]

## What Bryant asked the FBI in 1959 (serial 402)

Letter dated **1 February 1959**, addressed *"Director of Field Investigations, Unidentified Flying Objects, The Federal Bureau of Investigation"* — note Bryant assumes the FBI has such a position. ^[extracted] His three questions: ^[extracted]

1. *"How many UFO sighting reports involving alleged photographs or films of unconventional aerial objects has your organization collected, evaluated, or analyzed since January 1, 1952 to present?"*
2. *"Has the FBI ever been furnished with an analysis of maneuvers of UFO's?"*
3. *"I understand that congressional closed-door hearings are being held on the UFO subject. Has any FBI representative ever given testimony in these hearings?"*

The FBI sent a form-letter reply ([[concepts/saucer-as-hoax-default|standard policy disclaimer]] — *"we have no authority to investigate reports on unidentified flying objects"*). ^[extracted]

## What Bryant did in September 1963 (serials 430-431)

Bryant wrote again on **17 September 1963**; Hoover responded via Helen W. Gandy on **27 September 1963**: *"He instructed me to advise you that the confidential nature of our files will preclude complying with your request."* ^[extracted]

Internally, Hoover's response triggered a **counterintelligence-adjacent escalation**: ^[inferred]

- **20 Sep 1963 (serial 431)** — Director's airtel to **SAC Norfolk** instructs the field office to *"submit a summary of information in your files pertaining to Larry W. Bryant"* — including *"an account of his interview with Special Agent John S. Castles"*. ^[extracted] The phrasing of *"his interview"* implies Bryant had **already been formally interviewed** by an FBI Special Agent prior to 1963 — but no record of that interview appears in section 9. ^[inferred]
- **Note on serial 430**: *"The G-2 Section of Second Army, Fort Meade, Maryland is conducting a background check of Bryant because he has directed voluminous correspondence to military officials in the area and because he is employed by the Army."* ^[extracted]

The G-2 background check is a substantive **military counterintelligence inquiry** — the Army's Intelligence section pursuing background on its own civilian employee for **excess UFO correspondence with military officials**. ^[inferred] Bryant's status as an Army civilian made his UFO advocacy a **subject of inter-agency concern** that purely civilian advocates (Marck, Maney, Mastroberte, Powell) did not generate. ^[inferred]

## Bryant's 1965 article — *"Let's Challenge the UFO Censors"*

Filed at FBI HQ as **62-HQ-83894 serial 439**, dated **5 February 1942** [almost certainly **1965** — the *"WSP 2/5/42"* OCR is degraded and contradicts the article's substance, which references Bryant's *"past six years"* of UFO writing relative to a 1959 start]. ^[ambiguous] The article opens: ^[extracted]

> *"Before you see this article in print, it will have been submitted to appropriate Defense Department authorities for security review and clearance. Why? Because, under U. S. Army regulations, its author is in duty bound to have its contents examined for adherence to accuracy, propriety, and security."*

Bryant's frame is structurally novel within the FBI corpus: ^[inferred]

- He **publicly admits** he is subject to DoD security review for his civilian UFO writing.
- He explicitly poses the censorship question (*"Someone must challenge the UFO censors, assuming they exist"*) — i.e., he treats *"UFO censors"* as a **hypothesis**, not a foregone conclusion.
- He cites *"at least two of my previously cleared manuscripts and by one that failed to hurdle the review-clearance obstacle"* — i.e., evidence that DoD review **is** sometimes blocking his content. ^[extracted]

This is a **rhetorical position more sophisticated** than the contemporaneous Adamski/Van Tassel/Stranges [[concepts/contactee-movement-1952-1954|contactee scene]], and structurally distinct from the **MIB myth** ([[concepts/three-men-in-black]]) — Bryant is **operating inside the system** (as a DoD-cleared civilian) and using the system's own review process as evidence for the censorship hypothesis. ^[inferred] His method is **whistleblower-adjacent**, not contactee. ^[inferred]

## Position in the wiki's rhetoric corpus

Bryant's writing is the wiki's **earliest primary-source instance** of **civilian UFO disclosure activism using internal-government-evidence** as its lever. ^[inferred] Connects to:

- [[concepts/figurative-ufo-rhetoric]] — discourse-analysis frame
- [[concepts/three-men-in-black]] — the Bender/Barker MIB myth that Bryant's *"UFO censors"* hypothesis structurally inherits but reframes more analytically

Bryant's later career (post-1966) goes beyond section 9; he became active with **NICAP** ([[entities/nicap]]) and later filed multiple FOIA suits. ^[inferred — based on standard UAP-historiography knowledge] None of that activity is in the section-9 corpus.

## 1974 records-request — section 10 update (serial 481)

On **31 March 1974** Bryant (now at Apartment 2, 2904 South 13th Road, Arlington VA 22204 — i.e., relocated from Newport News VA to the DC suburbs by 1974) writes the FBI Director invoking President Nixon's recent privacy-assurance framing ^[extracted]:

> *"On September 17, 1963, Mr. John S. Castles, an FBI agent of the Newport News, Va. contingent, visited me at my place of employment (Ft. Eustis, Va.) to interview me as to my interest in the subject of unidentified flying objects (UFO's). I understand that Mr. Castles prepared a formal report of that interview.*
> *Since President Nixon recently has expressed his intention to assure that the privacy and civil liberties of individual citizens receive the protection they deserve under our form of government, it is in the spirit of that assurance that I respectfully request that you send me a copy of Mr. Castles' report along with a copy of all other documents in your files pertaining to my activities in the UFO-research field."*

Bryant cc's the **Director, Washington DC Office, American Civil Liberties Union** ^[extracted].

[[entities/clarence-m-kelley|Director Kelley]]'s 15 Apr 1974 reply ^[extracted]:

> *"In response to your request for information from FBI files regarding your activities in the unidentified flying objects research field, please be advised that we have no such information in our files except copies of prior correspondence with you and a record of your September 17, 1963, interview. Therefore, we have no documents to send to you and cannot further comply with your request."*

The internal NOTE confirms key facts ^[extracted]:

- **Castles interview date: 17 September 1963** — resolving the **section-9 open question** ([[entities/larry-w-bryant#Open Questions|"What was the date of Bryant's interview with SA John S. Castles?"]]) ^[inferred]
- **Originating Norfolk Field Office airtel: 9/24/63** — the formal FBI report of the interview is at file 62-115530 (FOI-REPLIES bufile).
- **SA Steve Boyle of the Norfolk Office was contacted** during the FBI's 1974 review.
- **Reason for the 1963 interview**: *"the interview of Bryant in 1963 was initiated because of voluminous correspondence which Bryant had sent to Federal and military as well as law enforcement agencies."* — confirming the section-9 inferred reading that Bryant's UFO advocacy triggered counterintelligence-adjacent scrutiny because of his Army-civilian status.

The 1974 filing serial is **62-HQ-83894-481** ^[extracted]; the formal FOI reply is bufile **62-115530** ^[extracted]. This is the FBI corpus's **first acknowledged-on-record release of historical FBI correspondence and interview documentation in response to a citizen records request** ^[inferred] — pre-dating the major 1974 FOIA amendments by ~10 months.

Bryant's 1974 letter is structurally the **first FBI-corpus citizen FOIA-style records request invoking Presidential-policy framing as warrant** ^[inferred] — Bryant cites Nixon's privacy assurance (this is months before Nixon's Aug 1974 resignation), pre-figuring the 1974 FOIA-amendment expansion of citizen records access.
