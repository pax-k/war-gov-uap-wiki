# Buzz Aldrin

**Edwin Eugene "Buzz" Aldrin Jr.** (b. 1930) — **US Air Force colonel**, **MIT PhD** in orbital mechanics (1963 thesis: *Line-of-Sight Guidance Techniques for Manned Orbital Rendezvous*), **NASA Astronaut Group 3** ("the Fourteen", 1963 — same group as **[[entities/alan-bean|Bean]]**, **[[entities/dick-gordon|Gordon]]**, **[[entities/gene-cernan|Cernan]]**), Lunar Module Pilot of [[entities/gemini-12|Gemini 12]] (Nov 1966) and Lunar Module Pilot of **[[entities/apollo-11|Apollo 11]]** (16–24 Jul 1969), **the second person to walk on the Moon** (20 Jul 1969). ^[inferred]

In the wiki's UAP corpus, Aldrin is the **single most-quoted astronaut witness** to date, contributing **four substantively-distinct observations** to the **[[references/nasa-uap-d4-apollo-11-technical-crew-debriefing-1969|d4 Apollo 11 Technical Crew Debriefing]]**. He is the **primary observer** on the Day-3 cylinder observation, the **primary characterizer** of the in-cabin cosmic-ray flashes (3.5 years before ALFMED), and the **author of one of the corpus's clearest astronaut-self-correction sequences** (the lunar-orbit "laser → lake" reattribution).

## Apollo 11 d4 testimony — four distinct observations

### 1. Day-3 cylinder observation (Vol I §6 pp 6-33 to 6-36) — sub-class E primary witness

> **ALDRIN**: *"The first unusual thing that we saw I guess was 1 day out or something pretty close to the moon. It had a sizeable dimension to it, so we put the monocular on it."* ^[extracted]

> **ALDRIN**: *"Yes, and we weren't sure but what it might be the S-IVB. We called the ground and were told the S-IVB was 6000 miles away. We had a problem with the high gain about this time, didn't we?"* ^[extracted]

> **ALDRIN**: *"Of course, we were seeing all sorts of little objects going by at the various dumps and then we happened to see this one brighter object going by. We couldn't think of anything else it could be other than the S-IVB. We looked at it through the monocular and it seemed to have a bit of an L shape to it."* ^[extracted]

> **ALDRIN**: *"We were in PTC at the time so each one of us had a chance to take a look at this and it certainly seemed to be within our vicinity and of a very sizeable dimension."* ^[extracted]

> **ALDRIN**: *"So then I got down in the LEB and started looking for it in the optics. We were grossly mislead because with the sextant off focus what we saw appeared to be a cylinder."* ^[extracted]

> **ALDRIN**: *"I guess there's not too much more to say about it other than it wasn't a cylinder."* ^[extracted]

Aldrin is the **primary observer**: he is the first crewman to call attention to the object (*"The first unusual thing that we saw"*), the one who **moves to the LEB to look through the sextant**, and the one who **identifies the cylinder reading as a focus artifact** (*"with the sextant off focus what we saw appeared to be a cylinder"*). ^[extracted] His **sextant-as-orbital-mechanics-instrument** background — Aldrin's MIT thesis was on rendezvous guidance, and he was widely known on the astronaut corps as the foremost expert on orbital optics — gives his **focus-artifact analysis** unusual interpretive weight. ^[inferred]

His **closing line** (*"it wasn't a cylinder"*) settles the morphology question in favor of the **in-focus** open-suitcase / L-shape / two-ring readings rather than the off-focus cylinder reading. ^[inferred]

### 2. In-cabin cosmic-ray flashes (Vol I §6 pp 6-37 to 6-39) — first-principles characterizer

> **ALDRIN**: *"The other observation that I made accumulated gradually. I don't know whether I saw it the first night, but I'm sure I saw it the second night. I was trying to go to sleep with all the lights out. I observed what I thought were little flashes inside the cabin, spaced a couple of minutes apart..."* ^[extracted]

> **ALDRIN**: *"During transearth coast, we had more time and I devoted more opportunity to investigating what this could have been. It was at that point that I was able to observe on two different occasions that, instead of observing just one flash, I could see double flashes, at points separated by maybe a foot."* ^[extracted]

> **ALDRIN**: *"...the only thing that comes to my mind is that this is some sort of penetration. At least that's my guess, without much to support it; some penetration of some object into the spacecraft that causes an emission as it enters the cabin itself. Sometimes it was one flash on entering. Possibly departing from an entirely different part of the cabin, outside the field of view. The double flashes appeared to have an entry and then impact on something such as the struts."* ^[extracted]

> **ALDRIN**: *"For a while, I thought it might have been some static electricity because I was also able, in moving my hand up and down the sleep restraint, to generate very small sparks of static electricity. But there was a definite difference between the two as I observed it more and more."* ^[extracted]

> **ALDRIN**: *"Sometimes a minute or two would go by and then you'd see the two within the space of 10 seconds. On an average, I'd say just as a guess it was maybe something like one a minute. Certainly more than enough to convince you that it wasn't an optical illusion. It did give you a rather funny feeling to contemplate that something was zapping through the cabin. There wasn't anything you could do about it."* ^[extracted]

Aldrin is the **first astronaut to systematically characterize** the [[concepts/cosmic-ray-light-flashes|cosmic-ray retinal flash phenomenon]]: he **measures rate** (*"about one a minute"*), **distinguishes single from double flashes**, **rules out static electricity** through controlled comparison, **identifies sun-direction correlation**, and **proposes the correct physical model** (penetration of charged particles through the spacecraft and presumably the eye). ^[inferred] **His framing predates Schmitt's d6 ALFMED testimony by 3.5 years** and predates the [[entities/apollo-17|Apollo 17]] ALFMED experiment by 3.5 years.

The **"entry and then impact on something such as the struts"** framing is **structurally correct** at the single-particle level even though the location framing is wrong: the flashes are in Aldrin's eye, not on the struts of the spacecraft cabin. But the **single-particle-two-events model** Aldrin extracts from the double-flash phenomenology is the same model the modern physical understanding uses. ^[inferred]

### 3. Lunar-orbit "laser" → "lake" reattribution (Vol II §21.5)

> **ALDRIN**: *"In lunar orbit, following ascent, we did note and mention to the ground that approaching CDH when the Earth came up above the lunar horizon, I observed what appeared to be a fairly bright light source which we tentatively ascribed to a possible laser. That seemed to be the best possible explanation until we were coming back in the command module approaching the Earth and were able to observe something that gave about the same appearance. When putting the monocular on the light source, it appeared as though it was the reflection of the Sun from a relatively smooth body of water such as a lake."* ^[extracted]

> **ALDRIN**: *"I think we've revised our initial conclusion as to what the source of that light was that we saw coming from the Earth. If no one owns up to having beamed the laser toward the Moon at that time, it was more probably a reflection off a lake. I still think it's an unusual phenomenon, at that distance, to see so bright a source of light."* ^[extracted]

This is the corpus's **clearest astronaut-self-correction sequence**: ^[inferred]

1. **Real-time call to ground** during lunar orbit identifies a candidate **laser** beamed at the spacecraft.
2. **Post-flight self-reattribution** — during transearth coast, Aldrin observes a similar phenomenon through monocular and identifies it as **specular sunlight off a smooth body of water (a lake)**.
3. **Aldrin still flags the brightness as anomalous** (*"I still think it's an unusual phenomenon, at that distance, to see so bright a source of light"*) — he does **not** fully naturalize the observation; he revises the candidate explanation while preserving the residual anomaly.

This is **astronaut-class observational discipline**: a willingness to **revise** rather than **defend** a real-time explanation, and a willingness to **preserve a residual anomaly** rather than fully naturalize the observation. ^[inferred]

### 4. Mars optical illusion (Vol II §21.4)

> **ALDRIN**: *"There was only one minor observation returning from the Moon. Looking back at it, at a time after Mars had passed behind the Moon, there was one time period where I imagined that the image of Mars was coming from a region where it couldn't come from, because it was in a dark portion of the Moon. This obviously was an optical illusion of some sort."* ^[extracted]

This is **astronaut-self-attributed as an optical illusion** — Armstrong offers a candidate explanation (Mars *"immediately adjacent to the horizon"*). **No UAP content.** ^[inferred] But the inclusion is structurally significant: Aldrin's transcript shows **a willingness to record minor observational anomalies even when the resolution is "obviously an optical illusion"** — i.e., his testimony is not pre-filtered by significance. ^[inferred]

## What Aldrin's d4 testimony establishes

- **Aldrin is the primary observer on the Day-3 cylinder observation** — first to call attention to the object, sextant operator in the LEB, and focus-artifact analyst. ^[extracted]
- **Aldrin characterizes the in-cabin flash phenomenon on first principles** — rate, structure, sun-correlation, static-rule-out, and penetration-model — 3.5 years before ALFMED. ^[extracted]
- **Aldrin demonstrates astronaut-class observational discipline** — willing to (a) self-correct ("laser" → "lake"); (b) preserve residual anomaly after self-correction (*"I still think it's an unusual phenomenon"*); (c) record minor anomalies even when self-attributing to optical illusion. ^[inferred]
- **Aldrin's MIT orbital-mechanics PhD background** is structurally relevant to his sextant-focus-artifact analysis — he is the foremost orbital-optics expert on the corps at the time. ^[inferred]

## What Aldrin's d4 testimony does *not* establish

- **No "ice in the urine line" framing** appears in the d4 excerpt's Vol I §6 or Vol II §21.4–§21.5 sections, contrary to popular UFO-literature claims that Aldrin's Apollo 11 mission account uses that phrase. ^[ambiguous] The phrase may exist elsewhere in the parent debriefing or in Aldrin's later autobiographies.
- **No assertion of extraterrestrial origin** for the Day-3 cylinder. Aldrin offers no candidate alternative to the eliminated S-IVB / urine dump / jettison panel / antenna fragment hypotheses; the deliberately-inconclusive crew conclusion is **collective**, not Aldrin-specific. ^[extracted]
- **No real-time public-press statement** about either observation during the immediate post-flight press cycle (the d4 debriefing was CONFIDENTIAL at the time). ^[inferred]

## Comparator with other corpus astronaut witnesses

Aldrin is the corpus's **fifth named astronaut witness**, after [[entities/frank-borman|Borman]] (GT-7, 1965), [[entities/alan-bean|Bean]] ([[entities/apollo-12|Apollo 12]] d1, 1969), [[entities/harrison-schmitt|Schmitt]] (Apollo 17 d6, 1972), and [[entities/neil-armstrong|Armstrong]] (Apollo 11 d4, 1969). ^[inferred]

By **observation count**, Aldrin is the **most-quoted** astronaut in the corpus — four distinct observations on a single document, three of which are substantively analyzed (the Day-3 cylinder, the in-cabin flashes, the laser/lake reattribution) and one of which is a self-attributed optical illusion (Mars).

By **role distinction**:

- **Aldrin is the only corpus astronaut who is primary witness on both a sub-class E (strong-positive) UAP-adjacent observation AND a cosmic-ray-flash characterization** within the same mission. The structural significance is that **the same observer's two reports separate cleanly into "genuine unknown" and "conventionally-resolvable anomaly"** — a willingness-to-self-correct data point that strengthens the cylinder observation by demonstrating Aldrin's observational discipline on a parallel case. ^[inferred]
