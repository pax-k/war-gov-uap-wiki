# Gerald P. Carr

**Gerald Paul "Jerry" Carr** (1932–2020) — **US Marine Corps naval aviator** and **NASA Astronaut Group 5** ("the Original Nineteen", 1966 — same group as **[[entities/jack-lousma|Jack Lousma]]** and **[[entities/paul-weitz|Paul Weitz]]**), Commander of **[[entities/skylab|Skylab 4]]** (16 Nov 1973 – 8 Feb 1974), the **longest** of the three Skylab visits at **84 days** — and at the time the **longest crewed spaceflight**. ^[inferred]

In the wiki's UAP corpus, Carr enters as the **primary speaker** on the **[[references/nasa-uap-d7-skylab-technical-crew-debriefing-1973|d7 Skylab Technical Crew Debriefing]]** SL-4 §7 *Visual Sightings / Unusual Events* discussion — providing the corpus's **second sub-class D entry** (astronaut-spontaneous on-orbit ephemeral-light observation with astronaut-supplied conventional explanation in the same transmission). The SL-4 d7 testimony parallels [[entities/alan-bean|Alan Bean]]'s [[references/nasa-uap-d1-apollo-12-transcript-1969|Apollo 12 d1 AOT particle-flash observation]] from 4 years earlier — extending sub-class D from one to two corpus instances.

## SL-4 d7 §7 testimony

Carr's contribution:

> **CARR**: *"One other area of unusual events that we reported on the dump tapes was that on occasion we saw some lights flashing outside with very a definite motion relative to ours. We presumed that they were other pieces of Skylab, or possibly other satellites. We reported our two or three sightings of that kind as soon as they occurred. We have no special comments concerning them, but we did find it very interesting to be able to see other objects up there with us. The fact that one or two of them appeared to be tumbling was apparently due to the oscillation of the light flashes that we were getting from them."* ^[extracted]

The substantive content for the corpus:

1. **Astronaut-spontaneous observation** — Carr volunteers the report as *"one other area of unusual events"*. ^[extracted] No prompt from a debriefer; Carr opens the topic.
2. **Multi-event** — *"two or three sightings of that kind"* over the SL-4 mission's 84-day duration. ^[extracted] This is the corpus's first **multi-event** sub-class D observation (Bean's Apollo 12 d1 was one event).
3. **Real-time reporting** — *"reported our two or three sightings of that kind as soon as they occurred"* (on the dump tapes, the post-pass voice loop). ^[extracted] **Real-time reporting protocol** was followed; the d7 §7 testimony is the post-mission debrief framing of those real-time reports.
4. **Astronaut-self-attributed-conventional in the same transmission** — *"We presumed that they were other pieces of Skylab, or possibly other satellites."* ^[extracted] **Same-transmission self-attribution** is the structural feature that places this observation in sub-class D. The crew offered the conventional explanation themselves.
5. **Tumbling-from-light-oscillation inference** — *"one or two of them appeared to be tumbling was apparently due to the oscillation of the light flashes"* — Carr is **explicitly aware** that the apparent tumbling could be interpretive (a light oscillation read as a rotating body) rather than directly observed. ^[extracted] This is **observational-discipline data**: Carr distinguishes between **direct observation of rotation** and **inference of rotation from brightness modulation**. ^[inferred]
6. **Astronaut-class non-overinterpretation** — *"we have no special comments concerning them"* — Carr explicitly declines to escalate the observation, while still flagging it as *"interesting"*. ^[extracted] The framing is **mature astronaut-class observational discipline** — the same posture [[entities/buzz-aldrin|Aldrin]] showed on the d4 Mars-illusion observation and the laser-to-lake reattribution.

The structural significance for the corpus:

- **Second sub-class D instance** — Bean's Apollo 12 d1 AOT particle-flash + Carr's SL-4 d7 §7 flashing-lights = sub-class D goes from one to two instances in the [[concepts/orbital-uap-sighting]] typology.
- **First sub-class D instance with real-time-reporting + multi-event characteristics** — the Bean d1 observation was a single one-time AOT exchange with a single CAPCOM "Roger"; Carr's was 2–3 events with structured real-time reporting.
- **First Skylab-station-debris sub-class D self-attribution** — the conventional candidate is **Skylab's own debris field** (or other satellites), not water-boiler effluent (Bean d1) or lunar outgassing (Bean d1 alternative). Different physical mechanism; same structural pattern.

## SL-4 §7 — non-UAP context

Carr's d7 §7 page also includes **non-UAP** mission-systems observations (preserved here for completeness only):
- **Ammonia odor in the SL-4 head**, traced to a blower unit, not the charcoal canister (page 7-8). Mission-systems issue.
- **OWS Heat Exchanger lint accumulation** discussed by [[entities/william-pogue|Pogue]] (page 7-9). Design issue.
- **EVA water leaks** discussed by [[entities/edward-gibson|Gibson]] (page 7-9). Operational issue.

Carr is the speaker who **bridges from these mission-systems-anomaly contexts to the UAP-adjacent flashing-lights observation** within the same §7 *Unusual Events* section. The bridging is structural: NASA's debriefing framework treats *unusual events* as a single category covering both internal-systems anomalies and external-observation anomalies. ^[inferred]

## Witness pedigree

Carr is the corpus's **second USMC astronaut witness** (after [[entities/jack-lousma|Lousma]]) and the corpus's **second NASA Group 5 commanding officer** in a corpus mission frame (after Lousma's SL-3 PLT role). The Group 5 cohort now occupies **3 of the 6 SL-2/3/4 crew positions** in the corpus's astronaut layer (Weitz SL-2 PLT; Lousma SL-3 PLT; Carr SL-4 CDR).

His radio language on the d7 SL-4 §7 page is **technical-precise** (*"reported on the dump tapes ... very a definite motion relative to ours ... due to the oscillation of the light flashes"*) — consistent with USMC aviation convention but more verbose than the Apollo-era voice-loop conventions. ^[inferred]
