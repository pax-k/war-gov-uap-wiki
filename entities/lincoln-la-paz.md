# Lincoln La Paz

American astronomer and Director of the **Institute of Meteoritics, University of New Mexico**, who in December 1948 became the first professional astronomer in the wiki's corpus to **investigate, witness, and analytically assess** a Project SIGN incident cluster — the [[concepts/green-fireballs-new-mexico|New Mexico green fireball wave]] of 5–13 December 1948. ^[extracted]

## Role in Project SIGN Incident 227

On the night of **12 December 1948 at approximately 2100**, La Paz was a passenger in a car near Bernal, NM, with four named companions:

- **Maj C.L. Phillips** — AF-CAP Liaison Officer, Kirtland Field
- **Lt Allan Clark** — Intelligence Officer, Eastern New Mexico Wing, Civil Air Patrol
- **Inspectors Jeffers and McGuigan** — ARC Security Service ^[extracted]

La Paz observed (initially by **indirect vision**, then by direct sighting) a "very bright green ball" of apparent angular diameter 5' moving from east to west "very low on the horizon" at an estimated altitude of 8–10 miles. Visibility was 2.1–2.3 seconds. He observed "almost exact horizontal flight until a 1/10th second before disappearance when a slight but definite curvature downward was noted." His estimated speed: 8–12 miles or 3–6 miles per second depending on the duration estimate. ^[extracted]

He shouted "Look!" to his companions, all of whom corroborated the sighting. ^[extracted]

## La Paz's analytical memo

The second sheet of Project SIGN Incident 227 preserves a memo titled **"Significant Differences between the Fireballs Observed in the Interval Dec 5–13 and Typical Meteors"**. La Paz wrote it as a real-time professional-astronomy assessment of the December 1948 New Mexico wave. The core arguments: ^[extracted]

1. The Dec 1948 fireballs **did not emanate from the Geminid radiant** that was active during the same week — Geminid trajectories have a distinctive radiant geometry with steep entry angles, and "the path of the bright green fireball seen from near Starvation Peak was very nearly, if not exactly, parallel to the horizon."
2. The wave **temporally coincides** with documented meteor showers (Geminid for Dec 1948, Perseid for the August 1948 North Powder OR event, Quadrantid for the January 1948 Abilene TX event). This temporal correspondence is **suggestive** of a meteoric component but the **horizontal-trajectory geometry breaks the otherwise-clean meteor identification**.
3. If the December 1948 fireballs are extraterrestrial in origin, they would imply a **new (non-Geminid) meteoric radiant** — but the horizontal-trajectory geometry is **inconsistent** with such an interpretation.

La Paz's memo is the **earliest preserved professional-astronomy assessment** of an unidentified-aerial-phenomena cluster in the wiki's corpus, and the only one in 1948. ^[inferred]

## Analytical posture

Three features distinguish La Paz's role in the wiki:

1. **Domain expertise.** As Director of the Institute of Meteoritics at UNM, La Paz had the disciplinary qualification to make the meteor-vs-not-meteor judgment that Project SIGN's USAF intelligence officers could not. ^[inferred]
2. **First-person observation.** Unlike most USAF intelligence officers in the corpus, La Paz personally witnessed the phenomenon he was asked to analyze. The Inc 227 sheet is therefore both a **witness statement** and an **analytical memo** in one document. ^[inferred]
3. **Civilian academic anchor.** La Paz's institutional anchor (UNM, civilian university) put him outside the AFOIR-CO / Project SIGN intake apparatus. His memo's incorporation as Inc 227's second sheet is a Project SIGN bookkeeping decision — pulling civilian academic expertise into a classified sighting-evaluation chain. ^[inferred]

## Why La Paz matters for the wiki

- **The first civilian academic expert** in the corpus to be brought inside Project SIGN's evaluation loop. ^[inferred]
- **The proximate cause** for Project Twinkle (1949–51, Holloman AFB) — a follow-on USAF observational network specifically targeting the green fireball phenomenon, in which La Paz remained a central technical advisor. ^[inferred] *(This is general historical background; not directly attested in the 173–233 bundle.)*
- **The corpus's first instance of trained-civilian-witness-as-investigator-as-analyst** — a role configuration that would later become institutionally normalized via [[entities/aaro|AARO]] and the [[references/cometa-report-1999|COMETA]] technical-advisory model. ^[inferred]
