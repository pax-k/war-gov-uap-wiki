# H. B. Fletcher

A **senior FBI HQ Inspector** active in **December 1948 - March 1949** as the **operational driver of the FBI's nonlinear post-[[references/fbi-bureau-bulletin-57-1947|BB-57]] saucer policy**. ^[inferred] Fletcher's two documented section-4 actions structurally connect:

1. **Active dispatch** on a possible disc recovery in [[references/sighting-noack-helendale-1948-12|Noack Helendale CA]] (28 Dec 1948), and
2. **Drafting** the [[references/fbi-sac-letter-38-1949|SAC Letter No. 38, Series 1949]] partial-reactivation directive (~14 Mar 1949).

Both actions occur **~14-17 months after BB-57's nominal termination** of FBI saucer investigation. ^[inferred]

## Why Fletcher matters

1. **Operational selectivity in BB-57's enforcement**: Fletcher's 28 Dec 1948 dispatch on the Noack case — including the explicit *"sufficient Agents to ... guard the disc"* instruction — is the **only documented active-FBI dispatch on a possible disc recovery in the post-BB-57 wiki corpus**. ^[inferred] He had institutional authority to coordinate Houston SAC + LA SAC + FBI Lab on a disc recovery without re-clearing BB-57.
2. **Drafter of SAC Letter No. 38**: Fletcher's 14 Mar 1949 memo is the **drafting document** for the SAC Letter that all FBI field offices received 11 days later. Fletcher's substantive proposal — *"although no active investigation will be conducted by the Bureau, it is believed that the captioned matter is of sufficient importance to the internal security of the country that our field offices should secure as much information as possible from complainants in order to assist the Department of the Air Force"* (page 117) — is the operational language reproduced in the SAC Letter. ^[extracted]
3. **Bridge between Noack-style dispatch and SAC Letter 38**: the two events are **~14 weeks apart** and **structurally connected**. Fletcher had operationally treated saucer-recovery as a Bureau function in late December 1948, and **13-15 weeks later** he formalized that posture for all SACs via the SAC Letter. ^[inferred] The Noack case is plausibly **part of the institutional context** that motivated the SAC Letter 38.
4. **Gasser-channel relay**: Fletcher's 14 Mar 1949 memo is the **earliest wiki documentation** of [[entities/c-d-gasser|Col C. D. Gasser]]'s "Russian missile / northerly trajectory" framing being relayed to FBI HQ leadership. ^[inferred] Fletcher is therefore the **internal FBI champion** of the Gasser hypothesis — the person who carries it from Gasser-Reynolds liaison up to Ladd and onward into the SAC Letter.

## Position in the FBI HQ command chain (1948-49)

Fletcher's title is given as "Inspector" (page 81: *"Inspector Howard Fletcher"*) — in 1948-49 FBI HQ, an Inspector was a senior position **below Assistant Director** but **above Section Chief**. The HQ Bureau routing list on the 25 Mar 1949 SAC Letter No. 38 places "FLETCHER, H. B." in the alphabetic stack of HQ recipients — consistent with a senior section-supervisor or division-head role. ^[inferred]

The 14 Mar 1949 memo is **routed to Ladd** (Assistant Director) — i.e. Fletcher reports to Ladd on saucer matters, and Ladd routes to Hoover for sign-off. The institutional path: **Liaison Section (Reynolds) → Ladd's office → Fletcher → Ladd → Director (Hoover)**. ^[inferred]

## What Fletcher was NOT

- **Not** an SAC (Special Agent in Charge) — Fletcher was at HQ, not in a field office. ^[extracted]
- **Not** the Liaison Section principal — that role is [[entities/s-w-reynolds|S. W. Reynolds]]. Fletcher receives Reynolds' liaison output and converts it to operational instructions. ^[inferred]
- **Not** the FBI's senior intelligence officer — that role is [[entities/d-m-ladd|D. M. Ladd]] (Asst Dir). Fletcher operates one level below Ladd. ^[inferred]
- **Not** related to the Stratemeyer-AF cooperation reaffirmation issue — that was handled by V. P. Keay → Ladd → Reynolds. Fletcher's involvement is purely on the saucer-policy / saucer-tip-routing dimension. ^[inferred]
