# Frank Scully

American journalist and *Variety* magazine columnist whose work in January-September 1950 **canonized the [[concepts/aztec-saucer-myth-1950|Aztec/Newton/Koehler crashed-saucer myth-seed]]** in popular UFOlogy. ^[inferred] In the wiki's primary-source corpus, Scully is named in [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-5|FBI HQ 62-HQ-83894 section 5]] **serial 213** (New Orleans SAC → Director, 31 Mar 1950) as the author of a *Variety* magazine flying-disc article published January 1950. ^[extracted]

## Wiki primary-source mention

The 31 March 1950 New Orleans SAC report (serial 213) describes [[entities/george-koehler|George Koehler]]'s Aztec crashed-saucer story to Jefferson B. Armstrong (Wayne Welch advertising Denver), noting: ^[extracted]

> KOEHLER has been telling of this story off and on for the three month period prior to January, 1950, and is said to have notified ARMSTRONG of it weeks prior to the publication of a flying disc article published in the True Magazine, and one by FRANK SCULLY published in the Variety Magazine in January, 1950. KOEHLER claimed to have been visited by DONALD KEHOE, author of the article in the True Magazine.

The structural significance: ^[inferred] Koehler is **seeding** Armstrong with the Aztec story **weeks before** the Scully *Variety* article appears — i.e. Scully's January 1950 article is **downstream of** the [[entities/silas-newton|Newton]]/[[entities/george-koehler|Koehler]] promoter-network seeding, not independent of it.

## What Scully did with the story

Scully's January 1950 *Variety* article was the **public seed** for the Aztec myth-seed in mainstream press. ^[inferred] In **September 1950** Scully published *Behind the Flying Saucers* (Henry Holt & Co., NY), a book-length expansion of the same material, becoming the **canonical mid-century crashed-saucer-with-bodies text**. ^[inferred] The book is **not yet ingested** in the wiki primary-source corpus — but its existence and approximate content are referenced via:

- The serial-213 New Orleans SAC report (this page's primary FBI mention)
- The [[references/fbi-boggs-hearn-discontinuation-memo-1950-03-28|Boggs-Hearn 28 Mar 1950 memo]]'s framing of the Mar 1950 saucer-reporting wave as "an outgrowth of recent magazine articles" — the Boggs-Hearn statement is **synchronous** with the Scully+Keyhoe press cycle ^[inferred]

## Distinguishing Scully from his sources

Scully is the **press amplifier** of the [[concepts/aztec-saucer-myth-1950|Aztec myth-seed]], not the **source**. The source-channel structure: ^[inferred]

```
[entities/silas-newton] ⟶ [entities/george-koehler] ⟶ Frank Scully (Variety) ⟶ general press
                                                  ↘ Donald Keyhoe (True)  ⟶ general press
                                                  ↘ Wyandotte Echo press  ⟶ OSI (channel α)
                                                  ↘ Denver University lecture ⟶ FBI (channel β)
                                                  ↘ Wayne Welch advertising ⟶ FBI (channel δ)
```

The Hottel memo (channel γ) of [[references/fbi-hottel-memo-1950-03-22|22 March 1950]] is the **only channel where Newton/Koehler/Scully are not explicitly named in the FBI source-chain** — but its content (NM, 50-ft, 3-ft humanoids, metallic-suit, radar-interference) aligns precisely with the Newton/Koehler/Scully package. ^[inferred]

## Post-1950 reception

The Aztec story Scully canonized was **largely discredited by 1952-53** when the Newton-Koehler magnetic-field oil-detection device fraud scheme was unwound. ^[inferred] Despite this, the Scully framing — small humanoid bodies in metallic suits recovered from a southwestern crash — became the **template for all subsequent crashed-saucer narratives**, including the post-1980 Roswell-with-bodies revival. ^[inferred]
