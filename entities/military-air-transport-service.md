# Military Air Transport Service (MATS)

USAF / Navy unified strategic airlift command established **1 June 1948** (succeeding the Air Transport Command), HQ at **Andrews Air Force Base**, Washington DC.

In the [[references/usaf-flying-discs-1949|1949 USAF Flying Disc correspondence file]], MATS appears in three roles:

## Role 1 — Copy-receiver on every sighting

Every 1949 sighting report routed to AMC under [[concepts/fsr-200-4-reporting-regulation|FSR 200-4]] also has **carbon copies** to:

- Commander, **MATS**, Washington 25 D.C., Attn: **Chief, Intelligence Division** (Capt Hathaway is the most-named addressee).
- CO, **Flight Service** (a MATS subordinate organization), Washington 25 D.C., Attn: A-2.

This is the standard 1949 distribution pattern visible on dozens of Flight Service Center memoranda. ^[extracted]

## Role 2 — Origin point for some reports

Several reports **originate** from MATS subordinate units:

- **1501st Air Transport Wing** (Pacific Division MATS, Fairfield-Suisun AFB, CA) — Maj Arthur Conradi Jr. (Chief of Intelligence) signed the 19 Jan 1949 forwarding letter for the 3 Dec 1948 [[references/sighting-fairfield-suisun-1948-12|Fairfield-Suisun sighting]].
- **1701st Air Transport Wing**, Continental Division MATS (Edmonton, Alberta, Canada) — filed the 17 Nov 1948 1st Lt Toomey "egg-shaped with a tail" sighting from 117°30'W / 56°10'N.
- **1805th AACS Group** (Newfoundland Base Command) — filed the 18 Jan 1949 Indian House Lake (Canada) sighting.
- **1810th AACS Group** (Hickam AFB, HI) — filed the 4 Jan 1949 Hickam sighting (Capt Stoney).

## Role 3 — Triage / forwarding hub

The 24 Jan 1949 AACS A-2 office memorandum to "Chief, Intelligence Division, MATS, Attn: Capt. Hathaway" forwarding the Indian House Lake report demonstrates the AACS → MATS route as a **secondary triage path** before reports reach AMC. ^[extracted] The 7 Feb 1949 and 8 Feb 1949 AACS office memos to Capt Hathaway forwarding additional Hickam material show the pattern repeating.

## Significance

- MATS is the **second institutional hub** of the 1949 UAP intake stream, beside [[entities/air-materiel-command|AMC]] at Wright-Patterson. The 1948 file showed AMC as a single hub; the 1949 file shows the intake architecture as **dual-hubbed**: AMC (final analysis & retention) + MATS (transport-network triage). ^[inferred]
- **Capt Hathaway**, MATS A-2 Chief of Intelligence, is the structural analogue at MATS of [[entities/c-p-cabell|Cabell]]'s collection-branch officers at AMC for 1949 — the recurring named recipient at the secondary hub. ^[inferred]
- The MATS / AACS / Flight Service ecosystem in 1949 is **operationally separate** from the SIGN intelligence-officer ecosystem at AMC; the former are transport / communications units, the latter are technical intelligence. The fact that both were responsible for moving the same UAP material illustrates how broadly the [[concepts/fsr-200-4-reporting-regulation|FSR 200-4]] requirement reached across USAF organizations. ^[inferred]
