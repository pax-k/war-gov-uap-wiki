# Russian Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MFA)

The Russian Ministry of Foreign Affairs (Russian: *MID* — *Министерство иностранных дел*) — the foreign-policy executive department of the Russian Federation. ^[inferred] Counterparty department to the [[entities/state-department|US Department of State]] in the [[references/dos-cable-russia-georgia-2001-10|30 October 2001 MOSCOW 13169]] cable on Russian airspace violations of Georgia.

## Why this page exists

The Russian MFA is the **source of the cable's "UFO" trope** — the rhetorical / sarcastic device that drove the 2001 Moscow cable's release into the FOIA UAP-keyword tranche. The cable records **two MFA officials** denying Russian airspace violations of Georgia on 28–29 October 2001:

1. **Russian Deputy Foreign Minister Mamedov** — quoted as having "categorically denied that Russian planes were involved in any incidents in Georgian airspace," citing the **Russian Ministry of Defense**'s denial. ^[extracted] Likely **Georgiy Mamedov** (Russian Deputy Foreign Minister 1996–2003). ^[ambiguous]

2. **MFA Georgia desk chief Tereoken** — echoed Mamedov's denial and **deployed the UFO trope**: "credible reports of Abkhaz helicopters bombing the areas 'where the terrorists were' but ... reports of planes in the area might as well have been about 'UFOs.'" ^[extracted] Tereoken added that "Moscow does not have the technical capability to determine whether there were foreign planes in the region" — and, in formally non-accusatory terms, that "any side" might have sent planes over Kodori. ^[extracted] OCR rendering of the Russian-language name as "TEREOKEN" is unverified — the name is plausibly an OCR variant of a longer Russian-language toponymic surname. ^[ambiguous]

## Why the MFA matters for the corpus

- **First foreign-government foreign-ministry counterparty** in the corpus. ^[inferred] Prior [[concepts/diplomatic-channel-uap-reporting|diplomatic-channel cables]] involve a foreign **intelligence service** ([[entities/png-national-intelligence-organization|PNG NIO]] in 1985) and **foreign / US-citizen aviators** ([[entities/tajik-air|Tajik Air]] crew in 1994); the 2001 Moscow cable adds a **foreign-ministry-to-foreign-ministry** counterparty layer.
- **First instance** in the corpus where a **foreign government's representative** deploys "UFO" as a **rhetorical denial device** in conversation with a US ambassador. The trope's role is denial-by-sarcasm — invoking the absurd to deflect a serious charge. ^[inferred] See [[concepts/figurative-ufo-rhetoric]] for the consolidated typology.
- **Foreign / Russian-MoD echo**: Mamedov's and Tereoken's denials cite the Russian Ministry of Defense as the underlying authority; the MFA acts as the diplomatic conduit for the MoD's denial. The cable thus has **two foreign-government layers** behind the UFO trope (MoD → MFA → US embassy), neither of which contains UAP narrative. ^[inferred]
- **No corpus claim about Russian MFA UAP-handling apparatus**: nothing in the cable establishes whether the Russian MFA has a UAP intake function. The trope is presented as a **rhetorical device by an individual desk officer**, not as institutional posture. The wiki should not infer a Russian-MFA UAP-handling stance from this artifact alone. ^[inferred]
