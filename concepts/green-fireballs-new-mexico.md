# Green Fireballs of New Mexico (Dec 1948)

A working concept page for the **Dec 1948 New Mexico green fireball wave** as it appears in [[references/project-sign-incident-summaries-173-233|the Project SIGN 173–233 incident summaries bundle]]. The wave is a tightly-clustered set of high-luminosity green-colored fast-moving aerial events observed across the Albuquerque / Las Vegas (NM) / Sandia / Vaughn corridor between 5 and 13 December 1948, and analyzed in real time by **[[entities/lincoln-la-paz|Dr Lincoln La Paz]]** (Director, Institute of Meteoritics, University of New Mexico) — whose analytical memo on these events is preserved as part of Project SIGN Incident 227.

This is the corpus's **first multi-event single-region UAP cluster** in the 1948–49 window — although it has **demonstrable precursors** in the [[references/project-sign-incident-summaries-101-172|101–172 bundle]] (see "1948 precursors" below).

## Operational definition (working)

A 1948 New Mexico event is included in the green-fireball set when the witness account describes:

1. A **high-intensity green** (or bright blue-green) light source — explicitly *not* the orange / silver / metallic body color predominant in disc-class reports.
2. A **fast horizontal trajectory** at fairly high altitude (5–25 km), typically several seconds in duration, sometimes with a brief end-curvature.
3. **No sound** at the observer's position (despite proximity that would suggest a sonic component for a conventional meteor).
4. (Frequently) a **flat near-horizontal trajectory** without the steep entry angle characteristic of meteors. ^[inferred]

The Bernal NM event (Inc 227) is the most analytically rich: La Paz observed it with four independent corroborating witnesses (a USAF major, a CAP intelligence officer, and two ARC Security Service inspectors) from a moving car, and used the **direction relative to the constellation Cygnus** (15° west of γ-Cygni) to anchor a precise sky-position record. ^[extracted]

## La Paz's analytical conclusion

La Paz's memo (Inc 227, second sheet — titled **"Significant Differences between the Fireballs Observed in the Interval Dec 5–13 and Typical Meteors"**) explicitly compares the December 1948 NM events to the **Geminid meteor shower** (active in mid-December) and to other ~1948 documented meteor showers. His conclusion: the green fireballs **did not emanate from the Geminid radiant** — the trajectory of the bright green fireball seen from near Starvation Peak was "very nearly, if not exactly, parallel to the horizon," in **sharp contrast** to the steep-radiant trajectories typical of the Geminid stream. ^[extracted]

La Paz also catalogues the **temporal correspondence** between the December 1948 events and the Geminid shower interval, suggesting that **if** the fireballs were extraterrestrial in origin, they would represent a **new (non-Geminid) meteoric radiant** — but he flags the **horizontal trajectory** as **inconsistent** with that hypothesis. The August 1948 North Powder OR event (Inc 230) coincided with the Perseid shower; the January 1948 Abilene event (Inc 231) coincided with the Quadrantid shower; the December events fell within the Geminid interval. The temporal correlation is **suggestive of a meteoric component**, but the **horizontal-trajectory geometry** breaks the otherwise-clean meteor identification. ^[extracted]

The memo is the **earliest documented professional astronomical assessment** of an unidentified-aerial-phenomena cluster in the wiki's corpus. ^[inferred]

## Cross-event signatures

The Dec 1948 NM events share several diagnostic features that distinguish them from both:

- **Conventional aircraft / Project SIGN disc reports** of the period — discs are typically silver/aluminum/white, often metallic, often with sustained-flight signatures of minutes to hours; the green fireballs are colored, brief (2–3 sec), and trail-bearing.
- **Standard meteor entry events** — meteors enter at steep radiant-anchored angles; the NM events show **near-horizontal trajectories** with sometimes a final-tenth-second downward arc. ^[extracted]

The diagnostic features the events share with each other:

- **Bright green color** (sometimes blue-green); rarely a "fluorescent" qualifier.
- **2–3 second visibility** with explicit termination (extinguishing rather than horizon-set).
- **Near-horizontal motion** with no consistent radiant direction.
- **No reported sound** despite low-altitude estimates.
- **Trailing fragment behavior** — the most physically diagnostic case is Inc 224 (Stahl/Neff) where "a trail of glowing fragments reddish orange in color was observed falling toward the ground" for "less than a second before disappearing." ^[extracted]

## Investigator action

The 173–233 bundle preserves an explicit investigator note attached to Inc 223:

> "Inquiry of local airfields both commercial and governmental unearthed no applicable explanation of this phenomena. Holloman AF Base performs no research work at night and on Sundays which involves the use of any lighting device or flares such as described."

This rules out the most obvious conventional candidate (Holloman / White Sands experimental flares or rockets fired during the relevant windows). ^[extracted]

The 31 Dec 1948 Inc 211 telecon (a separate Bellfontaine OH event resolved as a Very-pistol flare from the ground) is sometimes confused with the NM cluster but is geographically and morphologically distinct. ^[inferred]

## 1948 precursors (NEW)

The [[references/project-sign-incident-summaries-101-172|101–172 bundle]] preserves three earlier 1948 events whose morphology overlaps the green-fireball signature but which were filed *before* the December 1948 wave clustered the phenomenon:

- **Inc 149 (19 Jan 1948 0730 North Jutland Denmark)** — three "ball-saucer" rocket-like objects, **trail of green light**, "suddenly stopped, exploded and disappeared", E→W. Multi-vantage Scandinavian; ghost-rocket-era routing. ^[extracted]
- **Inc 150 (~1 Mar 1948 0930 Swedish Coast)** — single projectile at 20,000 ft, **bluish-green flame**, no trail, 4-sec visibility, Norwegian pilot witness, with explicit institutional flag tying similar Norwegian observations to **heavy-water production at Rjuken**. ^[extracted]
- **Inc 121 (9 Jan 1948 2330 Cartersville GA)** — **intense blue flame** circular pot-shaped object, 3000 ft, 400 mph, 175°M, EAL pilot. ^[extracted] Color profile is "light sky-blue" with intense blue flame on path crossing — close to but not identical to the Dec 1948 NM "fluorescent green" signature.
- **Inc 146 (17 Jul 1948 1650 San Acacia Dam NM, 65 mi S Albuquerque)** — **7 metallic circular objects in formation** at ~1500 mph if at 20k ft, NW; same Kirtland-AFB witness pool ([[entities/air-materiel-command|428th AFBU]]) that surfaces in [[references/project-sign-incident-summaries-173-233|the Dec 1948 wave]]. **NM-formation precursor** to the December cluster — same airspace, same NCO pool, 5 months earlier. ^[extracted]

The Scandinavian cases (149 + 150) extend the green/blue-flame morphology back to **early 1948**, with explicit institutional language tying them to ghost-rocket / heavy-water-facility surveillance concerns. The NM precursor (146) is the single strongest candidate for a "phase-zero" event of the December 1948 wave — same airspace, same institutional witness pool, 5 months earlier. ^[inferred]

The 11 April 1948 La Paz letter (Inc 101n) preserved in the same 101–172 bundle shows La Paz had already been hedging the "or-not-meteorites" pattern across a 4-event sequence (29 Nov 1945, Four Corners, Norcatur, Ussuri) **8 months before** the December 1948 NM wave. La Paz's December 1948 SIGN-orbit analysis was therefore not his first SIGN-orbit engagement but the first cluster he could investigate **with first-person field access** (UNM is in Albuquerque). ^[inferred] See [[references/sighting-norcatur-ks-1948-02|the Norcatur dedicated page]] and [[entities/lincoln-la-paz|the La Paz entity page]].

## Relation to broader UAP history

The December 1948 New Mexico green-fireball wave is the **proximate cause** for several subsequent USAF intelligence developments not documented in this bundle but well-attested in the broader literature: ^[inferred]

- **Project Twinkle** (1949–51, Holloman AFB) — a follow-on USAF observational network specifically aimed at instrumented detection of the green fireballs. La Paz remained a central technical advisor.
- **The transition from Project SIGN to Project GRUDGE** in early 1950 — partially driven by SIGN's inability to operationally explain the green-fireball wave.
- **The Kirtland / Sandia Base sensitive-installation focus** that runs through later USAF UFO program documentation — anchored to events like Inc 226/26 (Sandia / Toulouse) and Inc 224 (Stahl/Neff Las Vegas NM, near Sandia operational area).

These developments are not documented in the 173–233 file itself. The bundle captures the wave **at its operational onset**, with La Paz's analysis still in real-time and Project SIGN's institutional response not yet codified. ^[inferred]

## Open hypotheses

The 1948 sheet evaluators did not endorse any candidate identification. Standard candidates include: ^[inferred]

- **Soviet missile / experimental-airframe activity** — the Cold War context (1948 Berlin airlift, Soviet H-bomb concerns) made this the leading early hypothesis. The horizontal-trajectory geometry is conceivably consistent with cruise-missile-class flight.
- **Geophysical / atmospheric anomaly** — unusual airglow or upper-atmosphere chemistry. La Paz's professional astronomy framework does not cover this.
- **Bolide variant** — particularly low-entry-angle "earth-grazing" bolides could match the horizontal-trajectory descriptor; but the **repeated wave structure** over a 9-day period is hard to attribute to bolide statistics.
- **Genuine unknown** — held open in the contemporary investigators' notes.

## Provenance notes

- The Inc 227 second sheet (La Paz memo) is OCR-degraded in places (chunked sentences, missing words) but the analytical core is preserved intact. ^[extracted]
- The Inc 226 / Inc 26 cross-filing of the Sandia / Toulouse 6 Dec 1948 event is a SIGN-internal bookkeeping artifact, not a duplicate event. ^[inferred]
- The December 1948 wave is real-time-documented in this bundle in a way that almost no other UAP "wave" episode in 1944–50 is. The 173–233 bundle is therefore the **primary-source anchor** for any future synthesis on the green-fireball phenomenon. ^[inferred]
