# Horten Flying Wing

A family of German tailless flying-wing aircraft designed by the [[entities/horten-brothers]] (Reimar and Walter Horten) during World War II. In 1948, USAF intelligence treated the Horten lineage as the leading conventional explanation for "Flying Disc" sightings — and worried that the Soviet Union was building a postwar bomber force on the same blueprint.

## Why the Horten thesis attracted intelligence attention

A 24 September 1947 USAF letter ("Flying Disc", to CG Hq AAF) requested that recipients flag the following passages of the Royal Aircraft Establishment's *Tech Note AERO 1703* as "considered significant in relating the Horten brothers' perspective thinking and accomplishments toward the alleged 'Flying Saucer' case":

- p. 5 ¶4 & ¶6
- p. 6 ¶1 & ¶7
- p. 26 ¶3 & ¶10
- p. 27 ¶12
- p. 28 ¶1–5
- p. 54 — photograph of the Horten "Parabola"
- p. 69 — the Horten VIII
- pp. 71–72 — photographs of the Horten IX versions
- p. 74 — drawing of the "Parabola"

The same memo references a parallel T-2 report, **F-SU-1110-HD — "German Flying Wings Designed by Horten Brothers"**. ^[extracted]

The implication: USAF analysts believed the Horten airframes' visual silhouette (low-aspect, no fuselage, no tail) could plausibly be reported by ground observers as a "disc" — particularly at altitude and against bright sky. ^[inferred]

## The Soviet bomber concern

The same 1947 letter reports a 9 June 1947 attaché cable from Moscow stating:

> "1800 aircraft, directly or indirectly based on the Horten VIII design (six engine pusher, having a wing spread of 131 feet and a gross weight of approximately thirty-three thousand pounds) are being built for bomber squadron use. The Russian version, however, is jet propelled."

Reference number cited: V-32291-8. ^[extracted]

The 1800-aircraft figure is striking and almost certainly inflated — but the perception that the USSR was scaling a Horten-derived strategic bomber would have shaped how seriously USAF intelligence took every Western Hemisphere sighting in 1948. ^[ambiguous]

## Connection to the US response

The US drew up its own disc-shaped concept, the [[references/loedding-flying-disc|Loedding Flying Disc]] (drawing LD-2), housed at [[entities/air-materiel-command|AMC]] and treated as patent-sensitive. The juxtaposition — a German wing lineage feared in Soviet hands, a US disc concept under patent control, and civilian sightings being routed up the same chain — frames the entire intelligence picture of 1948. ^[inferred]

## Wartime precursor — the Me 262 attribution in the foo-fighter file

In the [[references/foo-fighters-shaef-1944-1945|1944–45 SHAEF foo-fighter file]] (DDI2 reply, 13 March 1945), the **Messerschmitt Me 262** jet — operationally distinct from but contemporaneous with the Horten airframes — was named as one of two leading conventional explanations for the WWII night-phenomena reports: *"A few of the alleged aircraft may have been Me. 262 and for the rest, flak rockets are suggested as the most likely explanation."* ^[extracted]

This makes the Horten thesis of 1948 the **direct successor** of a wartime conventional-explanation discipline that had already produced the Me 262 attribution and the [[concepts/flak-bombe-hypothesis|Flak Bombe]] candidate. ^[inferred] See [[synthesis/foo-fighters-to-flying-discs]] for the cross-cutting analysis.
