# FBI Saucer-Policy Stasis (1958-1962 Annual Reviews)

A **near-verbatim sequence of four internal FBI memos** issued from October 1958 through October 1962, each restating that **no update to the Bureau's saucer/UFO policy is needed**. ^[inferred] Filed at FBI HQ within [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-9|62-HQ-83894 section 9]] as **serials 392, 422, 425, 427**. ^[extracted] The pattern is a clean primary-source instance of **bureaucratic policy stasis as a documented stance** — i.e., the absence of policy change is **affirmatively memorialized** as policy itself. ^[inferred]

## The four reviews

| Date | Author → Recipient | Serial | Review interval |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13 Oct 1958 | E. S. Sanders → S. B. Donahoe | 392 | (initial — first canonical statement) |
| 30 Sep 1960 | S. B. Donahoe → A. H. Belmont | 422 | reviews period since 1958 |
| 2 Oct 1961 | S. B. Donahoe → W. C. Sullivan | 425 | reviews FY 1961 |
| 8 Oct 1962 | W. R. Wannall → W. C. Sullivan | 427 | reviews FY 1962 |

The reviews occur **on or near October 1** of each year, and each closes with the standing instruction to **review again on or about 10/1/[next year]**. ^[extracted] After 1962 the chain disappears from section 9. ^[inferred] (The 1963 review may exist in a missing serial or may have been silently abandoned after the increasing complaint-rate made the *"only one complaint"* claim untenable.) ^[ambiguous]

## The canonical text

Sanders's 13 Oct 1958 memo (serial 392) compiled the **canonical policy history**, which subsequent reviews replay near-verbatim: ^[extracted]

> *"Reports of 'flying saucers' became prevalent in 1947 and at request of Army Air Forces Intelligence, Bureau agreed to cooperate in investigation of flying discs. By Bureau Bulletin Number 42, 7-30-47, field instructed to investigate each instance to determine if bona fide sighting, a prank, or hallucination. Coordination with Army through liaison channels in field and at SOG.*
>
> *By letter 9-3-47 from Headquarters Air Defense Command addressed to Commanding Generals of the various Air Forces it was noted original intent of Air Force was that Air Force would interview responsible observers and Bureau would investigate incidents of so-called discs found on ground. Letter stated services of the FBI were enlisted in order to relieve the numbered Air Forces of the task of tracking down all the many instances which turned out to be 'trash can covers, toilet seats and what not.' As result, Bureau Bulletin Number 57 of 10/1/47 instructed 'all future reports connected with flying discs should be referred to the Air Forces and no investigative action should be taken by Bureau Agents.' Letter of 9-27-47 advised Air Force that personnel and time of FBI could not be dissipated in this manner (62-83894-121).*
>
> *SAC Letter Number 38, 3-25-49, advised that from reliable source it had been determined the USSR had been experimenting for four years on unknown type of flying disc. SAC Letter listed for field data desired by Air Force in connection with reports of sighting of flying saucers. Letter again called attention to fact no investigation should be conducted by Bureau but if reports made, information should be taken and furnished to Air Force.*
>
> *SAC Letter Number 83, 8-29-52, referred to [the same policy]. … Air Force has on several occasions assured that Bureau will be advised of any developments of interest to us. Last such assurance given 11-12-57 (62-83894-387)."* ^[extracted]

The 1960, 1961, 1962 memos shrink this history to a **one-paragraph compressed restatement** that lists the same four anchor documents (BB-57, SAC Letter 38, SAC Letter 83, 11-12-57 AF assurance). ^[extracted] The compressed paragraph is **identical across the three reviews** — strongly suggesting a **template-copy-paste workflow**. ^[inferred]

## The "only one complaint" claim

The 1960, 1961, 1962 reviews each state: ^[extracted]

> *"During the past 12 months only one complaint regarding flying saucers has been received."* (1960, 1961)
> *"During the past 12 months only one complaint regarding flying saucers has been received. The facts in this complaint were immediately referred to the Air Force."* (1962)

This claim is **demonstrably inconsistent** with section 9's own contents. ^[inferred] In FY 1961-62 the file shows **multiple inbound civilian-correspondence filings** to which Hoover replied, plus the Stringfield serial 426 filing, plus various OSI referrals. The *"only one complaint"* count appears to be measuring something narrower — likely **complaints requiring investigative consideration** (not citizen letters routed to OSI). ^[inferred] Or possibly the count is simply inaccurate and reflects template inertia. ^[ambiguous]

## Structural features of the stasis

Three observations: ^[inferred]

1. **The reviews are addressed up the chain.** Sanders → Donahoe → Belmont → Sullivan; the recipient is always one of the FBI's **Domestic Intelligence Division** assistant directors. ^[inferred] The reviews are therefore **for the AD level's information** — i.e., a **box-checking institutional ritual**, not a working-level policy document. ^[inferred]
2. **The action is always 'For your information'**. None of the reviews recommends a change; none requires a sign-off; none initiates a new investigation. ^[extracted] The function of the document is to **record the absence of change** — a structurally necessary act for the bureaucratic accounting of policy. ^[inferred]
3. **The review template is content-stable across personnel changes**. Sanders (1958) → Donahoe (1960, 1961) → Wannall (1962) — three different authors using the **same canonical paragraph**. ^[inferred] This is template inheritance through bureaucratic socialization. ^[inferred]

## The political economy of stasis

The reviews implicitly answer two **standing questions** the AD level might ask: ^[inferred]

- *"Has anything happened recently that should change our policy?"* — Answer: No.
- *"Has the Air Force walked back its 11-12-57 assurance?"* — Answer: No.

By memorializing the *no-change* answer in writing, the reviews **insulate the FBI from later accusations** of having missed a policy-relevant event. ^[inferred] If a 1963 saucer-related event had emerged that required policy action, the **annual-review chain provides documentary evidence** that the question had been formally considered each year. ^[inferred]

The chain therefore functions as **legal/political insurance** — the kind of paper trail that institutional actors maintain to demonstrate due diligence in low-priority but non-zero-political-risk subject areas. ^[inferred]

## What ends the stasis chain

After the **8 Oct 1962 Wannall memo (serial 427)**, the annual-review chain disappears from section 9. ^[inferred] The next substantive section-9 filings are: ^[extracted]

- **June-July 1963** — Hoover form-letter responses (Mastroberte 428, Black 429)
- **Sep 1963** — Larry W. Bryant background investigation (430-432)
- **Apr-May 1964** — Socorro NM Zamora landing case (435, 436, 438)

The Socorro case is the **most plausible explanation for why the annual review stops** — the Albuquerque SAC Byrnes report (8 May 1964, serial 438) is a **substantive FBI-authored field investigation** of the kind that BB-57 nominally forbids, and conducted **at the direct instruction of Hoover** ("Submit letterhead memorandum"). ^[inferred] After Socorro, the annual *"only one complaint, no policy change needed"* boilerplate would have been **structurally unsustainable**. ^[inferred]

This is a useful **negative finding**: the absence of a 1963 review memo is **itself informative** — it suggests the file's bureaucratic rhythm broke under the pressure of the file's own most kinematically anomalous case. ^[inferred]

## What this concept replaces or extends

This concept extends — but does not replace — [[concepts/saucer-as-hoax-default]]. ^[inferred] The hoax-default concept describes the **operating presumption** that any given saucer report is a fabrication. The policy-stasis concept describes the **institutional reflex** for memorializing that presumption as standing policy across time. ^[inferred] Both concepts are wholly compatible; the stasis concept is the **temporal-institutional dimension** of the hoax default.

## Open questions

- **Where is the 1963 annual review?** ^[open] Either it was filed under a different serial number that we have not yet ingested, or the chain ends with serial 427.
- **Did the policy chain resume after Socorro?** ^[open] If a 1965 or 1966 review exists, it would be in a later section. ^[inferred]
- **Did any field office push back on the stasis?** ^[open] No section-9 evidence of internal disagreement, but the OPR and external-agency files might contain dissent.
- **What was AF's 1958-1962 internal policy on FBI engagement?** ^[open] AF/ATIC contemporary internal records would clarify whether the *"11-12-57 assurance"* was a substantive AF commitment or a Bureau-side liaison-pleasantry that the Bureau then over-relied on.
