# Saturn V S-IVB (third stage)

The **S-IVB** ("S-four-bee") was the **third stage of the Saturn V launch vehicle** used on every crewed Apollo lunar mission (and also flew as the Saturn IB second stage on early Apollo CSM-only missions). ^[inferred] Built by **Douglas Aircraft / McDonnell Douglas**, single **Rocketdyne J-2 engine** (LH2/LOX), ~17.85 m long × 6.6 m diameter. ^[inferred]

In the wiki's UAP corpus, the S-IVB is structurally relevant because it is the **recurring conventional-explanation candidate** for early-translunar-coast astronaut UAP-adjacent observations. The S-IVB is the **largest, brightest, most-easily-tracked man-made object** in the immediate vicinity of the Apollo CSM/LM stack during the first ~24 hours after Trans-Lunar Injection — and is therefore the **default first hypothesis** for any unidentified body the crew sights during that period.

## Mission profile (post-TLI)

The S-IVB's standard Apollo lunar-mission profile after TLI:

1. **TLI burn** — second J-2 burn of the mission (the first having been at Earth-orbit insertion); injects the spacecraft stack onto a translunar trajectory. ^[inferred]
2. **CSM/LM separation** — about 30 min after TLI, the CSM separates, turns 180°, and docks with the LM in the S-IVB's forward adapter. After docking, the joined CSM/LM withdraws from the spent S-IVB. This is the **transposition, docking, and extraction (TD&E)** maneuver. ^[inferred]
3. **S-IVB slingshot maneuver** — propellant venting (LH2 / LOX residuals) and a small APS (auxiliary propulsion system) burn deflect the S-IVB onto a **lunar-flyby + heliocentric (solar-orbit) trajectory** to ensure it clears the spacecraft's path and the lunar surface. ^[inferred] On later missions (Apollo 13–17), the S-IVB was instead **deliberately impacted onto the lunar surface** as a seismic source for the ALSEP seismometers. ^[inferred]
4. **By Day 2–3 post-TLI**, the S-IVB on the slingshot trajectory is **thousands of miles away** from the spacecraft on a diverging path. ^[inferred]

## Corpus relevance

| Observation | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **GT-7 1965 Borman "bogey at ten o'clock high"** | S-IVB-equivalent (Titan-II spent stage) is **not ruled out** by source record; Houston offers booster-or-natural-sighting as the dichotomy and Borman declines to choose. ^[ambiguous] [[references/sighting-gemini-7-bogey-1965-12-04]] |
| **Apollo 11 d4 §6 Day-3 cylinder** (~18 Jul 1969) | **S-IVB explicitly ruled out** by Mission Control range data: *"the S-IVB was 6000 miles away"*. ^[extracted] First ranging-data-eliminated conventional candidate in the corpus. [[references/sighting-apollo-11-cylinder-1969-07]] |
| **Apollo 12 d1 Bean AOT particle-flash** (~20 Nov 1969) | S-IVB not raised by crew as candidate (the AOT phenomenology — particles, not a discrete object — does not match S-IVB morphology). [[references/nasa-uap-d1-apollo-12-transcript-1969]] |

The **Apollo 11 d4 ranging-data elimination** is structurally important: ^[inferred]

- At **6,000 miles range**, the S-IVB's ~17 m length subtends only ~0.1 arcseconds — well below the **human eye's ~60-arcsecond resolution limit** and below the LM monocular's likely magnification capability (~7–10×). ^[inferred]
- A spacecraft observer therefore **cannot resolve "L shape" / "two rings" / "tumbling" morphology on an object at 6,000 miles** through unaided eye + 7× monocular. ^[inferred]
- The **S-IVB elimination is robust at this range** — the inverse-square-law geometry alone closes the candidate, independent of what the bright object actually was.

This is the corpus's **first instance of independent ranging data closing a conventional candidate** rather than relying on witness self-attribution or absence of follow-up. ^[inferred]

## Why the S-IVB is the recurring default candidate

- **Brightness**: The S-IVB's spent body is highly reflective (gold-anodized insulation panels) and is the **brightest** man-made object in solar illumination near the spacecraft. ^[inferred]
- **Co-trajectory phase**: For ~24 h post-TLI, the S-IVB is on a **near-identical trajectory** to the spacecraft, so it is **frequently visible** from the cabin windows. ^[inferred]
- **Astronaut familiarity**: Crews are **briefed on the S-IVB's expected position** at every phase of the early translunar coast. ^[inferred]
- **Mission Control tracking**: NASA Goddard / Manned Space Flight Network tracks the S-IVB independently of the spacecraft, so **range data is available on demand** to the crew via the air-to-ground voice loop. ^[extracted] (This is the mechanism Apollo 11 d4 §6 uses to close the S-IVB hypothesis.)

These four properties combine to make the S-IVB the **canonical first hypothesis** for any unidentified body in early-translunar-coast astronaut sightings — and the canonical first elimination once Mission Control reports its range. ^[inferred]
