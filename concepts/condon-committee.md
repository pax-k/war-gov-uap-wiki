# Condon Committee (University of Colorado UFO Study, 1966-1968)

The **University of Colorado UFO study** — formally titled *"Scientific Study of Unidentified Flying Objects"* — conducted **November 1966 through late 1968** under **USAF contract F44620-67-C-0035** (~$300,000 budget) ^[extracted from FBI-filed press coverage at 62-HQ-83894 serials 451, 455 + 1977 Fact Sheet at serial 483], directed by **[[entities/edward-u-condon|Dr. Edward U. Condon]]** (former director of the National Bureau of Standards, then Physics Professor at the University of Colorado, Boulder). ^[extracted]

The study's January 1969 final report (3 volumes, 1,465 pages, 68 plates) — known colloquially as the *"Condon Report"* — was the **proximate institutional warrant for the [[concepts/project-blue-book-termination-1969|17 December 1969 termination of Project Blue Book]]**. ^[inferred] The Air Force commissioned the study explicitly to provide an external, peer-reviewable basis for closing or continuing its UFO program. ^[inferred]

## The contract award (12 October 1966) — Newsweek coverage filed 62-HQ-83894-451

The Newsweek issue of **31 October 1966** (cover date) was clipped and filed at **FBI HQ 62-HQ-83894 serial 451** as a [[entities/fbi|press-echo]] datapoint ^[extracted]:

> *"The Air Force last week took definite steps to make itself feel better. Fed up with investigating reports of glowing, flying, zooming, winking, awesome, blinking, streaking, hovering things, the Air Force last week decided to bail itself out of all responsibility for future conclusions concerning UFOs. The Air Force announced that it had commissioned the University of Colorado to conduct an 'in-depth' study of flying saucers for the next 18 months. The director of the study (expected to cost $300,000) would be Dr. Edward U. Condon, former director of the National Bureau of Standards, and now a physics professor at Colorado."* ^[extracted]

Three structural features of the contract per Newsweek and later coverage ^[extracted]:

1. **Director**: [[entities/edward-u-condon|Edward U. Condon]] — physicist, former NBS Director, by 1966 at U Colorado Physics Dept.
2. **Multi-university participation**: *"To satisfy the skeptics, and insure the study's objectivity, Colorado would select several other universities to take part in the independent investigation, with about 100 scientists participating."* ^[extracted]
3. **NAS overlay**: *"And in case that wasn't enough, the National Academy of Sciences also agreed to appoint a panel to review the Colorado report."* ^[extracted]

The **NAS-panel review overlay** is doctrinally distinctive ^[inferred] — the AF built **independent peer review by an external scientific body into the contract structure from the outset**, ensuring the eventual termination decision could be defended as scientifically validated, not merely AF-internal. ^[inferred]

## The study-launch press wave (Jan 1967) — 62-HQ-83894-455

The Newsweek issue of **23 January 1967** filed at serial 455 covers the study's substantive launch and provides the FBI's primary-source view of the Condon Committee's institutional posture ^[extracted]:

- **Frank Edwards's *Flying Saucers: Serious Business*** (1966) is invoked as documenting *"that 1965 was the year of the greatest number of UFO sightings"* — the immediate precipitating-pressure context for the AF commissioning Condon. ^[extracted]
- **Klass / NICAP rhetorical poles** are foregrounded: Philip Klass (Aviation Week avionics editor) proposed *"ball lightning"* generated by high-tension power lines as the explanation; **[[entities/donald-keyhoe|Major Donald Keyhoe]]** of NICAP rejected this, accusing the AF of conducting *"a long-term whitewash"*. ^[extracted]
- **Hopkinsville KY 1955 / Hillsdale MI 1966 / Hillsdale 1966 / Toledo OH / Valdese NC** narrative-bizarre cases are foregrounded as the kind of material the Condon Committee will need to address. ^[extracted]
- **Gerald Ford's Congressional inquiry posture**: *"Michigan's Gerald Ford, the House Republican leader, who suggested it would be 'a very wholesome thing' for Congress to look into unidentified flying objects."* ^[extracted]

The press coverage thus frames the Condon Committee as the **AF's response to civilian-saucer-advocate pressure** ([[entities/donald-keyhoe|Keyhoe]], [[entities/nicap|NICAP]]) and **Congressional pressure** (Ford) ^[inferred] — not as a primary-research scientific inquiry. ^[inferred] This frame is **structurally consistent with the AF's eventual use of the report to terminate Blue Book**: ^[inferred] the contract was political-cover-as-research from the outset, and the report's negative findings completed the cover by enabling closure. ^[inferred]

## The landing-prediction letter (1967) — 62-HQ-83894-456

The LA Times wire from **24 January 1967** filed at serial 456 captures a structurally novel datapoint ^[extracted]:

> *"BOULDER, COLO. -- DR. EDWARD CONDON, HEAD OF A GOVERNMENT-FINANCED SCIENTIFIC STUDY OF UNIDENTIFIED FLYING OBJECTS (UFO), SAID TODAY HE HAD RECEIVED A LETTER TELLING HIM WHEN AND WHERE A FLYING SAUCER WOULD LAND ON EARTH. … 'HE GAVE ME THE EXACT DATE AND THE EXACT LOCATION THE SAUCER WOULD LAND … SAID IT WOULD BE MANNED BY PERSONS FROM ANOTHER PLANET,' CONDON SAID. … 'HE SUGGESTED WE CONTACT THE SPACE TRAVELERS AND MAKE FRIENDS WITH THEM.'"* ^[extracted]

Condon publicly engages — *"I'm going to answer the letter to try and find out what the basis is for the man's prediction"* — without disclosing the predictor's name, the predicted date, or the predicted location. ^[extracted] This is a structural example of how the Condon Committee functioned as an **inbound-channel for civilian saucer-claims** in 1967, attracting [[concepts/contactee-movement-1952-1954|contactee-movement]] correspondence on top of the formal sighting cases. ^[inferred]

## NICAP's institutional embrace of the Colorado Project

By **November 1966**, [[entities/nicap|NICAP]] is operating as the Condon Committee's **primary civilian-research partner** ^[extracted]. The 20 Nov 1967 letter from **Robert G. Edwards** (Texas Unit #2 NICAP subcommittee chairman, Arlington TX) — filed at FBI HQ 62-HQ-83894 serial 465 (Jan 1968) — describes the institutional arrangement explicitly ^[extracted]:

> *"NICAP is currently working closely with the Colorado Project, a comprehensive study of UFO's begun in November 1966, by a group of distinguished scientists at the University of Colorado under a U. S. Air Force contract."*

NICAP's structure at the time ^[extracted]:

- **National HQ**: Washington DC, directed by [[entities/donald-keyhoe|Major Donald E. Keyhoe (USMC ret.)]]
- **30 subcommittees nationwide** (Edwards's letter)
- **~14,000 associate members** in 50 states (Edwards's letter)
- **Mission**: *"scientific investigation of Unidentified Flying Objects … not interested in the alleged psychic, occult, mystical, or religious aspects of UFO phenomena"* — the explicit positivist boundary against [[concepts/contactee-movement-1952-1954|contactee-movement]] organizations.

The Edwards letter is the **earliest FBI-corpus primary-source documentation of NICAP's national organizational structure** ^[inferred].

## What the FBI did NOT receive

The FBI's 62-HQ-83894 file contains **no copy of the Condon Report itself**. ^[extracted] The 1977 [[references/fbi-blue-book-termination-fact-sheet-1977|UFO Fact Sheet]] (62-HQ-83894 serial 483 enclosure) lists the report as orderable from NTIS at $6/volume ($18 set) under accession numbers AD 680:975, AD 680:976, AD 680:977 — i.e., the report is in the Bureau's awareness but not in its file. ^[extracted] The Bureau's posture during the entire Condon period (1966-1968) and its aftermath (1969-1976) is **passive press-clipping**, not substantive analytical engagement. ^[inferred]

## Position in the institutional-history corpus

The Condon Committee is the **bridge** between the [[concepts/fbi-saucer-policy-stasis-1958-1962|1958-1962 FBI policy stasis]] and the [[concepts/project-blue-book-termination-1969|17 Dec 1969 Blue Book termination]] ^[inferred]:

- **Stasis** (1958-1962): annual reviews memorialize *no policy change*.
- **Reactivation** (1965-1966): [[entities/donald-keyhoe|Keyhoe]] / [[entities/nicap|NICAP]] / [[entities/larry-w-bryant|Bryant]] / Frank Edwards's *Flying Saucers: Serious Business* + Hillsdale/Hopkinsville/Michigan press wave create civilian-pressure spike.
- **Commissioning** (Oct 1966): AF responds with the Condon contract — political-cover-as-research overlay.
- **Study** (Nov 1966 → late 1968): 100 scientists, multi-university, NAS-panel-reviewed.
- **Final report** (Jan 1969): 3 volumes, 1,465 pp, recommends closure.
- **NAS panel review** (1969): 6-page review; concurs with Condon's recommendations. ^[extracted from Fact Sheet description]
- **Termination** (17 Dec 1969): Blue Book closes per the Condon recommendation chain.
