# Transparent UAP

A working concept page for the **partial-transparency** witness signature in UAP reporting — objects visible enough to be drawn or photographed yet not opaque enough to fully occlude the background sky, plus the related "beam-blocking" effect where a light source appears to terminate prematurely on no visible surface.

## Operational definition (working)

A UAP report exhibits the *transparency* signature when one or more of:

1. **Stars or background features remain visible through the object**, possibly attenuated.
2. **A spotlight or active illumination beam appears to stop in mid-air** without an opaque surface returning a normal-bright reflection — and the same beam aimed elsewhere projects normally.
3. **NVG-only or bare-eye-only visibility** — the object is detectable in one channel but not the other, suggesting a non-uniform optical cross-section across wavelengths or intensifier behavior. ^[inferred]
4. **Selective witness visibility** — multiple co-located witnesses do not all see the object even though their lines of sight are equivalent.

## Primary instance currently in the wiki

The [[references/sighting-transparent-kite-western-us|"Transparent Kite"]] sighting (Western U.S., pre-dawn) instantiates **all four**:

- USPER5 (NVGs): "vaguely see a bright star or two in the distance through the object" — partial optical transparency.
- USPER5 (spotlight): the beam "went from shining far into the distance to stopping about 50 yards away on nothing in particular," then projected normally a moment later — beam-blocking with no persistent occluder.
- USPER5 saw it in NVGs; USPER6 saw it bare-eye; USPER7 did not see it at all — channel and witness selectivity.

## Adjacent observations from the 1948 corpus

The 1948 USAF "[[references/usaf-flying-discs-1948|Flying Disc]]" file does not contain a clear analogue: the [[references/sighting-hobson-ohio-1948-05|Hobson, OH sighting]] describes a **phosphorescent** disc — i.e., self-luminous and visible against the dark sky — not a transparent one. Earlier sources record **disappearance**, not measurable transparency. ^[inferred]

## Hypotheses (not endorsed)

- **Refractive-index gradient**: an object whose envelope has near-air refractive index would produce subtle distortion rather than opacity, marginally consistent with witness reports. ^[inferred]
- **Active electro-optical countermeasure**: a system actively shaping its visible-band signature; pre-dawn light conditions and NVG observation behavior partially fit. ^[inferred]
- **Witness-induced visual artifact / shared misperception**: the selective-witness pattern (USPER7 vs. USPER5/6) is consistent with this hypothesis, but USPER5 and USPER6 corroborated each other across distinct sensing channels (NVG and bare eye), which weakens it. ^[inferred]

## Why the beam-blocking sub-signature matters

The "spotlight beam stops on nothing" detail is unusually **falsifiable-looking** for a UAP report — it implies a bounded volume of space that absorbs or scatters visible light without normal reflective return. If reproducible with instrumentation, it would be one of the more diagnostic signatures in the literature. ^[inferred]
