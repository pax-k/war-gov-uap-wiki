# Engine-Stall Phenomenon

A recurring UAP-adjacent **physical-effect signature**: vehicle engines, vehicle electrical systems, or other internal-combustion / electrical equipment **stop functioning** coincident with a UAP observation, **then resume normal operation** when the object departs or its acoustic signature stops. The witness records the engine effect as something separable from the visual observation, often as the *first* cue (engine fails before the object is sighted).

## Witnessing structure

The pattern is typically: ^[inferred]

1. **Triggering proximity** — the UAP enters the witness's vicinity, often unseen at first.
2. **Acoustic / atmospheric cue** — high-pitched whine, generator-like hum, or pressure change. (Not always present.)
3. **Engine / electrical failure** — internal-combustion engine stalls; electrical equipment goes off.
4. **Hold period** — the engine **cannot be restarted** while the cue persists. Operators report this as "the driver did not attempt to start the engine until the noise stopped."
5. **Recovery** — once the UAP departs or the cue stops, the equipment **starts normally on the first try**, with no apparent damage.

The engine failure is therefore **selective in time** (coincides with the proximity) and **non-destructive** (no permanent damage), distinguishing the pattern from ordinary mechanical failures.

## Why the pattern matters as evidence

- **Falsifiable physical claim.** A witness saying "the engine stalled and would not restart until the object left" is a claim that can be checked against vehicle maintenance / testing logs (when accessible). The 2023 gate anomaly is the corpus's first case where this claim is made in a *facility-instrumented context* (the gate had a PLC log, in principle). ^[inferred]
- **Selective in time.** The selectivity (engine stalls **only** during the observation) is harder to attribute to ordinary mechanical failure than a one-off engine stall would be.
- **Coupled to acoustic cue (when present).** When the witness records a high-pitched whine simultaneous with the stall, the bundle becomes harder to attribute to a perception artifact. ^[inferred]
- **Non-destructive.** The equipment recovers, suggesting an external **influence** rather than damage. The Krasuski tractor and the 2023 gate both returned to normal operation immediately afterward.

## What the pattern does not establish

- The **mechanism** is unidentified. "Electromagnetic interference" is the standard frame in UAP literature ^[inferred], but the witness records in this corpus do not include in-situ EM measurements that would confirm an EM mechanism over (say) a thermal, acoustic, or RF mechanism.
- **Witness-recall artifacts**: the 1944 case is delayed-13-years recall, and the 1957 Levelland cluster is multi-vehicle but each vehicle's stall is short-duration. The 2023 case's strength is that the witness **immediately recorded a no-prior-no-after baseline** with the responsible facility maintenance role.

## Krasuski's 1957 trigger to report — the Levelland connection

[[entities/wladyslaw-krasuski|Krasuski]]'s [[references/fbi-detroit-100-26505-krasuski-1957|letter to Cutler]] (4 Nov 1957) explicitly cites a **"news report of mysterious vehicle in Texas causing engines to stall"** he had heard on the **Polish radio program** as the trigger for reporting his 1944 observation. The Texas case is almost certainly the **2–3 November 1957 Levelland TX cluster**. ^[inferred]

This makes Krasuski's account a **pre-Levelland-witness post-Levelland-report**: the 1944 observation pre-dates Levelland by 13 years, but the *report* is shaped by Levelland-era public consciousness of the engine-stall pattern. The phenomenology match is what triggered Krasuski's 13-year-delayed report, which is itself useful corpus evidence — the phenomenology is recognizable enough across two contexts (Polish forced-labor 1944, Texas civilian highway 1957) that an immigrant-DP witness recognized his own 1944 experience in the 1957 reports.
