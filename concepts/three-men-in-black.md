# Three Men in Black (MIB) — FBI-Era Origin

The **"Three Men in Black"** (MIB) myth — the persistent civilian-research claim that **federal agents in dark suits silenced civilian saucer researchers** in the early 1950s — originated in a specific 1953 silencing of **[[entities/albert-k-bender|Albert K. Bender]]** of the **[[entities/international-flying-saucer-bureau|International Flying Saucer Bureau]]** (Bridgeport CT) and was propagated by **[[entities/gray-barker|Gray Barker]]** through his 1956 book *[[references/barker-1956-they-knew-too-much|They Knew Too Much About Flying Saucers]]* and his magazine *The Saucerian*. ^[inferred]

This page tracks the myth's appearance **inside the FBI's own files** — specifically [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-9|FBI HQ 62-HQ-83894 section 9]] (serials 396-403, 437) — where the **rumor that names the FBI as a perpetrator is filed by the FBI itself, without rebuttal**. ^[inferred]

## The 1953 silencing event

Per the Bender narrative as restated in section-9 filings (Marck letter 7 Dec 1958, Phoenix interview 19 Dec 1958, Maney/Saucerian Bulletin Vol 3 #4 of Oct 1958): ^[extracted]

1. **1952** — Bender founds the **International Flying Saucer Bureau (IFSB)** in Bridgeport, Connecticut, to investigate the flying saucer mystery. ^[extracted]
2. **~1953** — Bender announces he has solved the saucer mystery: *"I know what the saucers are."* ^[extracted]
3. **Subsequently** — *"three men in black suits"* visit Bender and silence him. ^[extracted from Marck letter] *"Even today Bender will not discuss the matter of his 'hush-up' with anyone."* ^[extracted]
4. **1956** — Gray Barker publishes *They Knew Too Much About Flying Saucers* (University Books, NYC), structured around the Bender silencing. ^[extracted]
5. **15 Oct 1958** — Barker's *Saucerian Bulletin* Vol 3 #4 attributes the three men to *"the FBI, Air Force Intelligence, and the Central Intelligence Agency"* per **George Adamski's** account, and additionally claims *"3 men tried to silence George Adamski"*: *"It was the man from the CIA who made the threats that were designed to shut Adamski up."* ^[extracted]

The myth is therefore **dual-attributed in 1958**: the Bender hush-up rumor (single primary case) is **expanded** by Barker via Adamski to encompass federal silencing of multiple researchers. ^[inferred]

## How the FBI files filed the rumor

Section 9 ingests the MIB rumor through **four distinct intake events**: ^[inferred]

| Date | Serial | Event |
|---|---|---|
| 7 Dec 1958 | 397 (background) | **Claude Harold Marck Jr.** (Scottsdale AZ) writes the FBI directly: *"the FBI is involved in this affair"* |
| 19 Dec 1958 | 398 | Phoenix SAC interviews Marck, captures the *"3 men in black suits"* narrative + Barker's book reference |
| 12 Feb 1959 | 403 | Chicago SAC mails the FBI HQ a copy of Barker's book, captioned *"Nationalities Intelligence"* |
| 9 Dec 1958 | 396 | Oklahoma City SAC reports **James Maney**, age 13, who showed a Bureau Agent the *Saucerian Bulletin* Vol 3 #4 issue containing the Adamski 3-men passage |
| Jan 1964 | 437 | **Ray Montello** (Wickliffe OH) directly asks the FBI Office of Public Information whether *"agents of the intelligence bureaus (FBI and CIA) have, at times, pressured private citizens to keep quiet about the 'mysterious objects' that they saw"* |

In **none** of these intake events does the FBI file contain a **rebuttal** of the rumor. ^[inferred] The Bureau's response in each case is **standard form-letter handling**: file the inbound material, route inquiries to OSI, do not investigate. ^[extracted]

The FBI's file therefore exhibits a **reflexive structure**: the rumor that names the FBI as silencing agent is **collected and preserved by the FBI itself**, becoming part of the very file ([[entities/fbi-hq-62-83894-file|62-HQ-83894]]) that the rumor implicitly points to. ^[inferred]

## What the file does not say

Three things the file deliberately or incidentally does not address: ^[inferred]

1. **Whether Bender was actually visited by federal agents.** ^[ambiguous] The file contains no reference to any FBI Bender-investigation, no SAC report on Bender's residence, no Bender-side filing. The Bureau acquires Marck's restatement of the rumor and Barker's book — but never approaches Bender. The Bureau's silence on this point is consistent with both *"the rumor is groundless and we don't dignify it"* and *"we maintain a tacit non-engagement policy on a real action by another agency"*. ^[ambiguous]
2. **Whether the FBI considered the Adamski silencing claim.** Marck's letter is captioned *"Nationalities Intelligence"* — implying counterintelligence-adjacent disposition — but no Adamski-specific file appears in section 9. ^[inferred]
3. **Whether the FBI traced the rumor's circulation pattern.** The Saucerian Bulletin Vol 3 #4 was the **operational vehicle** by which the rumor reached the Maney/Marck readership; the FBI clipped the Bulletin into the file but did not commission a circulation-and-influence analysis. ^[inferred]

## The MIB as rhetoric

The MIB rumor combines three rhetorical primitives that recur across the [[projects/uap/uap|UAP archives corpus]]: ^[inferred]

- **Federal silencing as default explanation** — "X knows the truth and was silenced" is a structural inverse of the FBI's actual posture, which is "X has nothing actionable, refer to AF, no investigation". The myth **attributes meaningful action** to an agency whose actual behavior is bureaucratic disengagement. ^[inferred] See also [[concepts/saucer-as-hoax-default]] and [[concepts/observer-suppression-behavior]].
- **Sartorial signature** — *"three men in black suits"* / *"three men in dark suits"* is **physically specific** in a way that the federal-bureaucracy generic image is not. The detail is durable across re-tellings, suggesting it operates as a **mnemonic anchor** rather than a literal description. ^[inferred]
- **The number three** — explicitly **three** men, not two or four. The number is invariant across retellings. ^[inferred] Possibly a triadic symmetry adapted from earlier supernatural-visitor folklore (the three witches, three wise men, three knocks) reused into a Cold War institutional skin. ^[inferred]

The myth has the structural shape of **state-folklore** — a rumor about state action that **circulates in civilian space without state confirmation or denial** and that, by virtue of its circulation, becomes a pseudo-fact that the state must then either acknowledge or pointedly ignore. ^[inferred] The FBI's choice in section 9 is to ignore — but to **file**.

## What about Adamski / Van Tassel / Stranges?

Section 9 carries multiple traces of the broader 1958-1960 contactee scene that the MIB myth grew from: ^[extracted]

- **George Adamski** — named in the Saucerian Bulletin Vol 3 #4 as a 3-men silencing victim (per Barker's editorial). No separate Adamski file in section 9; cross-referenced from [[concepts/contactee-movement-1952-1954]].
- **George W. Van Tassel** — Denver SA Kelly attended his Phipps Auditorium talk 4-17-60 (serial 418); Van Tassel's "Giant Rock" CA address is *"not located by Denver in the Postal Guide or available maps"*. ^[extracted]
- **Frank E. Stranges** — Vice President, American Evangelistic Association; Vice President, Baltimore Bible College; subject of Seattle SAC inquiry serials 423-424 (Oct 1960); had been corresponding with FBI since Oct 1957 and had been *"given permission to reprint two of my previously published articles"* by Hoover. ^[extracted]

These figures **post-date the Bender 1953 silencing** but are **structurally adjacent** to the MIB rumor — each one builds the broader *"government-knows-but-suppresses"* civilian frame that gives the MIB myth its discursive home. ^[inferred]

## Open Questions

- **Was Albert K. Bender ever actually approached by federal agents?** ^[ambiguous] The wiki's primary-source position is **non-committal** — see [[entities/albert-k-bender]].
- **What's in Barker's 1956 book that the FBI considered noteworthy?** ^[open] The wiki holds the **publisher's jacket copy** ([[references/barker-1956-they-knew-too-much]]) but does not yet hold the body of the book, even though Chicago SAC mailed a copy to HQ on 12 Feb 1959. The actual Bureau-retained book copy may exist in a separate FBI file class.
- **Why is the file's ingest-channel for the MIB rumor through Marck and Maney specifically?** ^[open] Both are minor figures — Marck moved to Denver after writing in; Maney was a 13-year-old. The fact that these are the file's best primary-source captures of the rumor suggests the **rumor's primary circulation was juvenile/grassroots**, not journalistic or political. ^[inferred]
