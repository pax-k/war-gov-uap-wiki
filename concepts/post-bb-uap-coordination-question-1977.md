# Post-Blue-Book UAP Coordination Question (Carter White House, June 1977)

The **first post-[[concepts/project-blue-book-termination-1969|Blue-Book-termination]] Executive-Branch institutional-coordination question** in the wiki's UAP corpus. ^[inferred] On **14 June 1977**, **Stanley Schneider** (Assistant to the Director, **Office of Science and Technology Policy**, Executive Office of the President) telephoned **Jay Cochran Jr.** (FBI Assistant Director, Technical Services Division) to ask *"as to the Bureau's procedure for handling information received from the public or otherwise, relating to sightings of UFOs"*. ^[extracted from FBI HQ 62-HQ-83894 serial 486]

The question's origin and framing are documented verbatim in Cochran's same-day internal memo to Mr. McDermott ^[extracted]:

> *"He advised that Jody Powell of the President's staff has raised the question as to whether or not there is any coordination within the Executive Branch relating to information concerning this subject. He stated that the U.S. Air Force had closed their investigation of these matters several years ago and currently does nothing but refer such information to appropriate local authorities."*

This is the first FBI-corpus primary-source documentation of:

1. **A White House Press Secretary** ([[entities/jody-powell|Jody Powell]]) raising a UFO question. ^[extracted]
2. **A formal OSTP-to-FBI inquiry on UAP institutional coordination**. ^[inferred]
3. **An explicit Executive-Branch acknowledgement that no AF UFO-investigation function still exists**. ^[extracted] The question being asked is structurally aware that **Project Blue Book closed in 1969 and has not been replaced**.

## The institutional setup

- **Stanley Schneider** — Assistant to the Director, OSTP, EOP. Office of Science and Technology Policy was created by the National Science and Technology Policy, Organization, and Priorities Act of 1976; OSTP succeeded Eisenhower-era OSAP and Ford-era OSTP-precursors. ^[inferred]
- **[[entities/jody-powell|Jody Powell]]** — Press Secretary to President Jimmy Carter (Jan 1977 → Jan 1981). ^[inferred]
- **Jay Cochran Jr.** — FBI Assistant Director, Technical Services Division, 1977. ^[extracted]
- **Mr. McDermott** — recipient of Cochran's same-day internal memo (the FBI-internal reporting chain). ^[extracted]

The chain of routing — **Carter Press Secretary → OSTP Asst Director → FBI Tech Services Asst Director → FBI internal memo to McDermott + cc all Asst Directors** — places the question at the **highest non-Director level** of the FBI's institutional response apparatus, while simultaneously declining to escalate it to the FBI Director (Clarence M. Kelley at the time, soon to be replaced by William H. Webster). ^[inferred]

## Cochran's response

Cochran's recorded response to Schneider is the **clearest 1977 statement of the FBI's post-Blue-Book UAP posture** ^[extracted]:

> *"I advised him that as far as the FBI is concerned there appears to be no conceivable jurisdiction for us to conduct any inquiries upon receipt of information relating to a UFO sighting and, in the absence of some investigative jurisdiction based upon the information furnished, that information would be referred to the Department of the Air Force without any action being taken by the Bureau."*

Two structural observations: ^[inferred]

1. **The disclaimer-without-mechanism is openly stated.** Cochran says the FBI would refer such information to the AF — but Cochran is speaking to a White House staffer who has just told Cochran that the AF *"closed their investigation of these matters several years ago and currently does nothing but refer such information to appropriate local authorities."* ^[extracted] Both parties on the call therefore **knowingly affirm** the FBI's standard-policy disclaimer **without challenging the fact that the AF program the disclaimer references no longer functions**. ^[inferred]
2. **No coordination exists, by Cochran's account.** Cochran does not name any Executive-Branch coordination body for UFO information. The implied answer to Powell's question — *"is there any coordination within the Executive Branch relating to information concerning this subject"* — is **No, there is none**. ^[inferred]

## The 15 June 1977 follow-up letter

The next day, **15 June 1977**, Cochran sends Schneider a sealed enclosure ^[extracted]:

> *"Pursuant to our conversation today, there is enclosed a copy of the FBI Law Enforcement Bulletin for the month of February, 1975. Beginning on page 16 there is an article covering the handling of UFO sightings which may be of interest to you in connection with your current inquiry."*

The reference to a **February 1975 FBI Law Enforcement Bulletin UFO article** is the only FBI-corpus reference to internal UAP guidance for **state and local law enforcement** ^[inferred] — a structurally different intake channel than the FBI HQ 62-HQ-83894 file's federal-correspondence pattern. The article itself is **not retained in section 10**; only the Cochran-to-Schneider transmittal is. ^[extracted]

## Why the question matters institutionally

The Carter White House inquiry is **structurally novel** for three reasons ^[inferred]:

1. **It originates from the Press Secretary's office.** Jody Powell's role is communications, not science policy. ^[inferred] The inquiry is therefore likely a **response to a press-ready question** — i.e., either a journalist had asked Powell about UFO coordination, or Powell wanted to be prepared for such a question. The Carter administration was historically open about UFO interest (Carter's 1969 Leary GA sighting), so the question's origin in the Press Secretary's office is consistent with that public posture. ^[inferred]
2. **It surfaces the post-Blue-Book coordination gap.** No agency held statutory or regulatory responsibility for UFO investigation after 17 Dec 1969. ^[inferred] Powell's question is the **first documented Executive-Branch acknowledgment** in the FBI corpus that the gap exists.
3. **It produces the FBI's clearest statement of "no jurisdiction, no mechanism".** The Cochran formulation *"no conceivable jurisdiction for us to conduct any inquiries"* is **stronger than any prior FBI corpus statement** ^[inferred] — earlier disclaimers (e.g., the standard Hoover form-letter) used *"not within our investigative jurisdiction"*; Cochran intensifies to *"no conceivable jurisdiction"*.

## What did NOT happen after the question

The FBI corpus's section 10 — and the file at large, pending section 6 — contains **no follow-up coordination action** ^[inferred]:

- No inter-agency meeting is documented.
- No FBI policy revision is documented.
- No White House follow-up call is documented after Schneider's *"if any further contact was necessary he would call back."* ^[extracted]
- No executive order on UFO coordination is issued (and the historical record shows Carter never produced one). ^[inferred]

The question is therefore **answered and shelved** — Powell wanted to know whether coordination existed, the answer was no, and the matter was closed. ^[inferred]
