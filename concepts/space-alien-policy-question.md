# Space Alien Policy Question

A specific framing established in the [[references/hunter-1963-space-alien-race-memo|1963 NASC memo]] by [[entities/maxwell-w-hunter|Maxwell W. Hunter II]]: the question of what US policy *should be* if first contact with an alien intelligence occurred — handled at White House staff level, addressed to State Department International Scientific Affairs. Hunter does not assert contact has happened or is imminent; he formalizes the conditional.

## The four-part conditional structure

The memo decomposes the question into nested conditionals:

1. **Is there intelligent life elsewhere in the galaxy at all?** Hunter's 1963 read: scientifically, "quite probable among the stars."
2. **Could it be in our solar system?** Hunter's read: "quite unlikely" within the solar system, but with explicit acknowledgement that interpretation runs differently than it did "a few decades ago" — a reversal driven by changed planetary-formation theory.
3. **Could solar-system intelligent life be Mars-derived?** Hunter walks through it as a thought experiment: Mars escape speed (16,500 fps) and lunar braking (<10,000 fps) make a Martian space program *physically easier* than ours, and even chemical rockets could carry "almost 10% gross weight in payload" Mars→Moon at favorable windows. Treats it as plausible-given-the-conditional, not as an active claim.
4. **What if visitors are interstellar?** Two sub-cases: relativistic but Einsteinian (the [[concepts/three-class-alien-taxonomy|"nuclear class"]]), and superluminal/post-Einstein. Each implies a different US posture.

## Hunter's policy recommendations

- **Whatever the contact case, "a policy of the immediate burying of all Terrestrial hatchets would likely be in order."** This is the single clearest *prescriptive* sentence in the memo.
- **Triage the contact rapidly to determine which class of civilization it is** ([[concepts/three-class-alien-taxonomy]]); diplomatic posture depends sharply on whether you face "tame chemical Martians," nuclear-class peers, or post-Einstein superiors.
- **No advance preparation is realistically possible**: "no one of consequence is going to take this rubbish seriously unless it happens. At that point, our policy will be determined in the traditional manner of grand panic."

The third point reads as deliberately self-undermining — Hunter writes the memo *and* concedes the memo will not change US preparedness. ^[inferred]

## Why this framing matters

- It is the wiki's earliest archived **EOP staff-level treatment** of UAP/contact as a foreseeable policy question rather than a fringe topic. ^[inferred]
- It is **explicitly diplomatic in routing** (NASC → State/OISA), pre-figuring the modern interagency handling of UAP coordination. ^[inferred]
- It reframes "flying saucers" without endorsing them: Hunter brackets the saucer-advocate position ("nonsense" in the strict scientific view) while also bracketing the strict scientific dismissal ("disturbing"). The result is a posture of **prepared skepticism**, not advocacy. See [[concepts/scientific-vs-saucer-advocate-frame]].
- The memo identifies a structural problem the wiki keeps re-encountering across decades: **the institutional incentive structure does not reward preparing for contact** until contact is unambiguous, at which point preparation is too late. ^[inferred]
