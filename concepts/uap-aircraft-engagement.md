# UAP–Aircraft Engagement

A working concept page for **active UAP behavior toward crewed aircraft** — close approach, apparent tracking, breaking-off-to-pursue patterns. Distinct from the more common "passive hover and depart" report class catalogued under [[concepts/orb-phenomenon]].

## Operational definition (working)

A UAP report involves *aircraft engagement* when the object:

1. **Closes** on a crewed aircraft to within a small fraction of typical separation (e.g. ≤30 m / ≤100 ft).
2. **Co-locates with the aircraft's vicinity** for non-trivial time — including stationkeeping above, below, or alongside.
3. **Breaks off from one aircraft to track or position above another** — i.e. exhibits *target-selection* behavior. ^[inferred]
4. (Optionally) **Times appearance to phases of flight** the aircraft cannot influence — e.g. flaring up over the aircraft as it descends to land. ^[inferred]

The bar for #3 is high: cross-aircraft target selection by a UAP is a strong claim and should only be marked when the source explicitly says the object disengaged from one platform and went to another.

## Source observations

In the 2025 redacted statement [[references/usper-statement-2025]] / [[references/sighting-helicopter-orb-encounter-2025]]:

- An orb **came within ~10 feet** of helicopter `[CALL SIGN 1]` (LP/OP report at ~22:18).
- After helicopter `[CALL SIGN 1]` "broke off pursuit" because it could not match speed, a co-pilot saw "**something emerge from the two objects**" and travel away on a different vector at high speed.
- The witness explicitly states: **"the orbs appeared to break off from `[CALL SIGN 1]` and pursue the `[MILITARY AIRCRAFT]`."**
- Multiple flare-formation events at ~22:28 and ~22:57 occurred **directly above the `[MILITARY AIRCRAFT]`** — once during normal flight, once during descent-to-land. Spatial correlation is suggestive of selection, but the source does not establish causation. ^[inferred]

## Why this gets its own page (vs. living on orb-phenomenon)

- Most public UAP reports describe **either** a stationary hover **or** a high-speed departure. The 2025 statement adds a third behavioral class: **active proximity to crewed aircraft, with apparent target switching**.
- Engagement-class behavior carries qualitatively different implications than passive hover behavior — for the underlying phenomenon's intent (or for the conventional explanation set, e.g. drone tasking).
- It anchors a **future synthesis page** comparing engagement vs. surveillance vs. passive behaviors.

## Comparison to the broader corpus

- The 1948 [[references/usaf-flying-discs-1948|USAF "Flying Disc" file]] does **not** document UAP-aircraft engagement of this kind; sightings are largely passive, stationary, or unidirectional. ^[inferred]
- The 2026 [[references/western-us-event-2026|Western U.S. Event]] involves **ground-based federal LE witnesses**, not aircraft. The orb-class behaviors there (launching, hovering, briefly transparent) do not include cross-platform engagement.
- This 2025 statement therefore sits in a category of its own within the wiki so far, anchored by its multi-sensor (FLIR + NVG + naked eye) corroboration. ^[inferred]

## Open hypotheses (not endorsed)

- **Tasked surveillance drones**: cross-platform behavior is consistent with a swarm tasked to follow whichever aerial target is most "interesting." Inconsistent with the **flare-up / flare-down formation** behavior on the same night ([[concepts/orb-flare-formation]]). ^[inferred]
- **Range/perspective artifact**: at the ~10 ft separation report, witness could be misjudging range as in the AARO-corrected 2026 [[references/sighting-fiery-orb-western-us|Fiery Orb]] event. But the LP/OP team's FLIR observation of the close-approach gives a second sensor channel.
- **Genuine target-selecting unknown**: held open; this is what makes the source SECRET//NOFORN-classified. ^[inferred]

## Open questions
