# Project Blue Book Termination (17 December 1969)

The **Secretary of the Air Force's 17 December 1969 announcement** terminating **Project Blue Book** — the USAF's UFO investigation program operating since 1952. ^[extracted] The closure rescinded **Air Force Regulation 80-17** (which had established and controlled the UFO investigation program), transferred all Blue Book documentation to the **National Archives Modern Military Branch** at 8th and Pennsylvania Avenue NW Washington DC 20408, and ended the AF's institutional engagement with civilian UFO complaints. ^[extracted]

For the FBI corpus this matters because **it removes the institutional anchor** that the FBI's own saucer policy had been pegged to since [[references/fbi-bureau-bulletin-57-1947|Bureau Bulletin 57 of 1 Oct 1947]] — the standing policy *"all reports … should be referred to the Air Force"* still operates after Dec 1969, but the AF the FBI is referring complaints to **no longer investigates them**. ^[inferred]

## The decision basis (per the 1977 FBI-filed Fact Sheet)

The 1977 [[references/fbi-blue-book-termination-fact-sheet-1977|UFO Fact Sheet]] enclosed in FBI HQ 62-HQ-83894 serial 483 preserves the AF's stated decision basis verbatim ^[extracted]:

> *"The decision to discontinue UFO investigations was based on an evaluation of a report prepared by the University of Colorado entitled, 'Scientific Study of Unidentified Flying Objects;' a review of the University of Colorado's report by the National Academy of Sciences; past UFO studies; and Air Force experience investigating UFO reports during the past two decades."*

The four-element decision basis is structurally informative ^[inferred]:

1. **The [[concepts/condon-committee|University of Colorado study]]** (1966-1968, $300K AF contract, dir. [[entities/edward-u-condon|Dr. Edward U. Condon]]) — the **proximate institutional warrant** for closure.
2. **The National Academy of Sciences review panel** of the Colorado study — the **independent-validation overlay** the AF had built into the contract from the start. ^[inferred — the NAS panel was specified at contract award per Newsweek 31 Oct 1966 coverage filed at FBI HQ 62-HQ-83894 serial 451]
3. **"Past UFO studies"** — the [[concepts/robertson-panel-runup-1952|Robertson Panel of 1953]] and earlier internal reviews. ^[inferred]
4. **AF experience investigating UFO reports during the past two decades** — the cumulative 1947-1969 sighting record (the Fact Sheet attaches a year-by-year totals chart showing 12,618 total sightings, 701 unidentified). ^[extracted]

## The three Blue Book conclusions (per the 1977 Fact Sheet)

The Fact Sheet preserves the AF's three "Blue Book conclusions" verbatim ^[extracted]:

> *"(1) no UFO reported, investigated, and evaluated by the Air Force has ever given any indication of threat to our national security;*
> *(2) there has been no evidence submitted to or discovered by the Air Force that sightings categorized as 'unidentified' represent technological developments or principles beyond the range of present day scientific knowledge; and*
> *(3) there has been no evidence indicating that sightings categorized as 'unidentified' are extraterrestrial vehicles."*

These three findings are the **canonical institutional-end statement** of the AF saucer regime ^[inferred] — they answer (in the negative) the three questions the UAP discourse had foregrounded since 1947: ^[inferred]

- **Threat?** — No.
- **Anomalous-technology indication?** — No.
- **Extraterrestrial indication?** — No.

The findings are **negative across all three axes**, and they are **internally consistent with the [[concepts/saucer-as-hoax-default|saucer-as-hoax default]] regime that has governed institutional saucer-handling since BB-57**. ^[inferred] In structural terms, Blue Book's termination is an **affirmative continuation of BB-57's hoax-default reasoning** (no investigative justification → no investigation), not a paradigmatic break from it. ^[inferred]

## What the closure rescinded and transferred

Per the Fact Sheet ^[extracted]:

- **AFR 80-17 was rescinded.** This was the AF regulation establishing and controlling the program for investigating and analyzing UFOs.
- **All Blue Book documentation transferred to NARA Modern Military Branch.** The records become FOIA-accessible at 8th and Pennsylvania Avenue NW Washington DC 20408 *"and is available for public review and analysis"*.
- **No successor program.** *"Since the termination of Project Blue Book, no evidence has been presented to indicate that further investigation of UFOs by the Air Force is warranted. In view of the considerable Air Force commitment of resources in the past, and the extreme pressure on Air Force funds at this time, there is no likelihood of renewed Air Force involvement in this area."* ^[extracted]

The Fact Sheet's 1977 *"extreme pressure on Air Force funds at this time"* framing ties closure not just to the Condon Committee's analytical conclusions but to the **post-Vietnam AF budget environment**. ^[inferred]

## How the FBI files the closure (1977 — 7 years later)

The FBI's institutional acknowledgment of Blue Book closure is **delayed by 7 years and 1 month**. ^[inferred] It enters the 62-HQ-83894 file via the **McClanahan-Moore correspondence pair** (serials 483 + 484, Jan 1977):

- **17 Jan 1977** — **Lt Col H. A. McClanahan** (USAF Chief, Civil Branch, Community Relations Division, Office of Information) sends the UFO Fact Sheet to **Mr. Malmfeldt** (FBI Room 7825), prompted by an inquirer the AF had received who *"recently received a public inquiry regarding UFOs, referred to us from the Bureau"*. ^[extracted]
- **24 Jan 1977** — **Donald W. Moore Jr.** (FBI Asst Director, External Affairs) replies for Malmfeldt, *"thanking [McClanahan] for sending us a copy of your UFO Fact Sheet and for apprising us of the termination of your project"*. ^[extracted]

The phrasing *"apprising us of the termination of your project"* — written by an FBI Asst Director in 1977 about a 1969 event — is structurally significant ^[inferred]. It implies the FBI had not been formally apprised of the Blue Book termination through institutional channels at the time of closure (Dec 1969). ^[inferred] The Bureau's standard policy of **referring UFO reports to the AF** had therefore been operating for 7+ years against an addressee that no longer existed as a UFO-investigation program — and the formal acknowledgment that the addressee had ceased to exist arrives only **at the AF's prompting in 1977**, not at the FBI's prompting. ^[inferred]

## Why the closure didn't stop the FBI's standard-policy disclaimer

After 17 Dec 1969, the FBI continues to use the same form-letter disclaimer it had been using since BB-57 ^[extracted]:

- **5 Jul 1973** form-letter pattern (Kelley): *"the investigation of Unidentified Flying Objects is not and never has been a matter that is within the investigative jurisdiction of the FBI."* ^[extracted]
- **15 Apr 1974** Bryant reply (Kelley): same disclaimer. ^[extracted]
- **14 Jun 1977** Cochran to Schneider: *"there appears to be no conceivable jurisdiction for us to conduct any inquiries upon receipt of information relating to a UFO sighting and, in the absence of some investigative jurisdiction based upon the information furnished, that information would be referred to the Department of the Air Force without any action being taken by the Bureau."* ^[extracted]

The Cochran 1977 framing is the **clearest instance of the disclaimer-without-mechanism** problem ^[inferred] — the Bureau says it would *"refer such information to the Department of the Air Force"*, but **the AF program for handling such referrals has been closed for 7.5 years**. ^[inferred] In structural terms, the FBI's saucer policy has **outlived the institution it was anchored to**. ^[inferred]

## Position in the institutional-history corpus

Blue Book's termination sits at the **end of the post-1947 institutional saucer regime**:

- **Origin** (1947): [[references/fbi-bureau-bulletin-57-1947|BB-57]] establishes the FBI-AF jurisdictional split.
- **Continuation** (1948-1957): [[concepts/project-sign|Project Sign]] → [[concepts/project-grudge|Project Grudge]] → Project Blue Book — the AF's UFO investigation program, with the FBI in passive-referral mode.
- **Stasis** (1958-1962): [[concepts/fbi-saucer-policy-stasis-1958-1962|annual-review chain]] memorializes the no-change policy.
- **Reactivation framings** (1957, 1965-1966): [[concepts/sputnik-saucer-reactivation-1957|Sputnik-era spike]] and the [[concepts/condon-committee|1966 Condon Committee commissioning]].
- **Termination** (Dec 1969): **THIS PAGE** — Blue Book closes; AFR 80-17 rescinded; documentation transferred to NARA.
- **Post-termination question** (Jun 1977): [[concepts/post-bb-uap-coordination-question-1977|Carter White House coordination inquiry]] — the question of whether *anyone* in the Executive Branch coordinates UFO information now that BB is closed.

## Open questions

- **Did the FBI receive any direct AF notification of the 17 Dec 1969 closure at the time?** ^[ambiguous] The 1977 McClanahan letter's framing *"apprising us of the termination of your project"* suggests not — but section 10 covers only the HQ-level filing, and a field-office letter notification is possible. ^[inferred]
- **What was the precise date the AF's referral function became inoperative?** ^[open] Was it 17 Dec 1969 (announcement) or some later date (program close-out, document transfer)?
- **What was the AF's institutional successor for civilian UFO inquiries?** ^[open] The 1977 Fact Sheet effectively says "none — write the AAAS" but doesn't name a successor function.
