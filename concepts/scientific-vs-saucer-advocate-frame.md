# Scientific vs. Saucer-Advocate Frame

A rhetorical posture explicitly inhabited by [[entities/maxwell-w-hunter|Maxwell W. Hunter II]] in the [[references/hunter-1963-space-alien-race-memo|1963 NASC memo]]: the writer brackets both the *flying-saucer-advocate* community and the *strict-scientific-impossibility* community, declines to fully join either, and writes from the resulting middle position.

Hunter's exact words: *"In my own mind, I find it difficult to side with the flying saucer advocates, but the almost total impossibility envisioned by most scientists also is disturbing. Therefore, I present the problem in current perspective, as I see it."*

## The two poles

| Pole | What it asserts | What Hunter says about it |
|---|---|---|
| **Flying-saucer advocate** | Overwhelming evidence of alien beings already visiting; Mars mining the Moon; Phobos/Deimos as artificial space stations | "nonsense" in the strict scientific view; quoted with mild irony but not engaged with |
| **Strict scientific dismissal** | Intelligent life within the solar system: negligible. Interstellar visitors: travel-time-impossible. Reports: misperception, hoax, classified test programs | "the almost total impossibility envisioned by most scientists also is disturbing" |
| **Hunter's middle** | Neither posture handles the conditional well; therefore *write the policy paper anyway*, bracketing the empirical claim, and treat contact as a **conditional planning problem**. | The memo itself. |

## What this posture does

- **Permits writing policy on a conditional that cannot yet be confirmed empirically.** Hunter doesn't have to settle whether saucer reports are real to articulate "if contact, then…" ^[inferred]
- **Allows quotation of saucer-advocate hypotheses as foils** without endorsing them — Hunter cites the "Martians mining the Moon" claim and the "Phobos and Deimos are artificial" claim, then uses each to anchor a numerical thought experiment. ^[inferred]
- **Inoculates the document against dismissal** — Hunter explicitly distances from the saucer community before offering the analytical content. ^[inferred]
- **Concedes its own limits** — the memo's final paragraph admits no one of consequence will take the question seriously until contact happens.

## Where this posture shows up elsewhere in the wiki

- The 1948 USAF [[concepts/project-sign|Project SIGN]] material works similarly at the operational level: it collects reports, brackets the "phenomenon is real" question, and pursues conventional explanations ([[concepts/horten-flying-wing|Horten flying wings]], [[references/loedding-flying-disc|domestic disc concepts]]) while documenting witness testimony without endorsing it. ^[inferred]
- The 1999 [[references/cometa-report-1999|COMETA Report]] foreword by [[entities/andre-lebeau|André Lebeau]] (former CNES chairman) inhabits this posture explicitly — *"stripping the UFO phenomenon of its irrational layer"*; *"a scientist's belief is not important to the results of his research nor does it have any effect on those results if he is rigorous."* But the report's *committee* takes a different step than Hunter did 36 years earlier: after grounding itself in the foreword's prepared-skepticism posture, it commits to the extraterrestrial hypothesis as "the best scientific hypothesis." This is the posture used as a *launchpad to assertion*, not as a final position. ^[inferred] See [[synthesis/policy-papers-uap-1963-vs-1999]].
- Modern [[entities/aaro|AARO]]-handled material ([[references/western-us-event-2026]]) takes a structurally similar posture: collects sensor + multi-witness testimony, applies corrections (e.g. range/size underestimation), and resists asserting either "definitely UAP" or "definitely conventional."
- The 2025 [[references/usper-statement-2025|intel-witness statement]] is interesting precisely **because** the witness — by virtue of being a senior intel official, not a saucer advocate — partially escapes this frame. The document's institutional weight comes from its narrator's pre-statement credibility. ^[inferred]

## A name for the posture

Within the wiki we'll call this **"prepared skepticism"**: the writer does not assert the phenomenon is real, does not assert it is not real, and writes the policy paper anyway. ^[inferred — coinage internal to the wiki]
