# Flak Bombe Hypothesis

The conventional-explanation candidate offered on **17 February 1945** by **W/Cdr S. D. Felkin** of **A.D.I.(K)** (Air Intelligence Directorate (K), the UK air-prisoner-interrogation branch of the [[entities/uk-air-ministry-ddi2|Air Ministry]]) for at least some of the WWII "night phenomena" reports forwarded by [[entities/shaef-air-staff|SHAEF Air Staff]]. ^[extracted]

In a one-paragraph reply (SHAEF reference SDF/GJ 2365 4350) responding to Grierson's 14 February 1945 routing memo with the attached CX report, Felkin wrote: ^[extracted]

> *"this probably refers to 'Flak Bombe' mentioned in A.D.I.(K) Report No. 562/1944, paragraphs 128 and 129."*

That is the entire substantive content of the A.D.I.(K) reply in the file — a pointer to a specific intelligence report, not a free-standing explanation.

## What is "Flak Bombe"?

A.D.I.(K) Report No. 562/1944 ¶128–129 is **not in the [[references/foo-fighters-shaef-1944-1945|SHAEF file]]**, so the hypothesis can only be reconstructed from external context: ^[inferred]

- The German term *Flak-Bombe* (anti-aircraft bomb) refers, in WWII air-defence usage, to upward-propelled or air-burst weapons designed to threaten Allied bomber formations from below or alongside. ^[inferred]
- A.D.I.(K) Reports were intelligence summaries derived primarily from **Luftwaffe POW interrogations** and captured documents. ¶128–129 of report 562/1944 was therefore most likely a description of a German weapon system disclosed by aircrew or technical prisoners. ^[inferred]
- Felkin's identification is offered with hedging — "probably refers to" — rather than as a definitive match. ^[extracted]

The wiki retains this page as a placeholder; the actual A.D.I.(K) Report 562/1944 should be ingested if a declassified copy can be located. ^[open]

## Where it fits in the candidate-explanations menu

The [[references/foo-fighters-shaef-1944-1945|SHAEF file]] surfaces three distinct British conventional-explanation candidates for the WWII night-phenomena reports:

| Candidate | Source memo | Likely targets |
|---|---|---|
| Flak Bombe | A.D.I.(K) Felkin, 17 Feb 1945 | The cylindrical-object class — see [[references/sighting-cylindrical-object-vicinity-f5710-1945-03-01]] ^[inferred] |
| Me 262 (a few cases) | DDI2 Hopkins, 13 Mar 1945 | The faster, more aircraft-like reports ^[inferred] |
| Flak rockets (most cases) | DDI2 Hopkins, 13 Mar 1945 | The light-and-trail reports ^[inferred] |

DDI2's later reply does **not** mention Flak Bombe — implying either Felkin's hypothesis was reframed under "flak rockets" or DDI2 considered it covered only a sub-class of reports. ^[inferred]

## Significance

The Flak Bombe hypothesis is the **first explicit attempt by Allied air intelligence to anchor unexplained-aerial-phenomena reports to a named, documented enemy weapon system**. Its structural role is identical to the [[concepts/horten-flying-wing|Horten flying wing]] thesis in 1948: ^[inferred]

- A specific catalogued enemy capability is held up as the most plausible conventional explanation.
- The cited document is *retained* by intelligence rather than declassified to the field unit.
- The candidate explanation is offered with hedging, not certainty.
- It does not actually settle the question.
