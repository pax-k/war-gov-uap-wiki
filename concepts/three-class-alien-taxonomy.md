# Three-Class Alien Taxonomy (Hunter, 1963)

The classification scheme proposed in the [[references/hunter-1963-space-alien-race-memo|1963 NASC memo]] for any first-contact event. Hunter argues that the **first** thing US policy must determine on contact is which of three classes the alien race belongs to, because policy implications differ enormously across the three.

## The three classes

| Class | Defining capability | Hunter's example | Implied US posture |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chemical-rocket** | Mastery of chemical propulsion only; no nuclear-energy use | Hypothetical "Martians" who could have colonized our Moon using chemical rockets and a refueling base | "Tame"; "current national policy would be made to order" — manageable. |
| **Nuclear-class** | Nuclear-propelled interstellar capability; bound by Einstein | Hypothetical race expanding from the galactic center at half lightspeed, hopping every 10 light-years | Far more capable; "would not be nearly as tame," but **distance still throttles their force projection** — they cannot bring all forces to bear. |
| **Einstein-violating** | Superluminal travel; mastery of fundamental physics beyond current US understanding | Posited as "scientifically abhorrent" but explicitly held open as a third case | "Negotiate fast" — sharply asymmetric situation; their understanding of fundamental forces would be obvious. |

## Source quotes

- *On the chemical class:* "Should the Martians have colonized the moon without discovering nuclear energy, then they represent no real problem, and our current national policy would be made to order for the situation."
- *On the nuclear class:* "Of course, if we were to run across representatives of this kind of interstellar race, they would not be nearly as tame as the previously hypothesized chemical Martians, and our policy would need to be revised accordingly. Fortunately, travel time restrictions would inhibit their ability to bring all forces to bear, in case we should develop differences of viewpoint."
- *On the Einstein-violating class:* "If we were to meet such a race, our policy had better be to negotiate fast, because the implications of their far better understanding and control of the fundamental forces of nature would be obvious."

## Embedded numerical arguments

Hunter justifies the classes with specific numbers extracted from the memo:

- **Chemical Martian feasibility:** Mars escape speed **16,500 fps**; lunar braking **<10,000 fps**; "normal high energy chemical rocket" carries "almost 10% of its gross weight in payload" Mars→Moon at favorable times, and "almost 50% payload" on the return. The lunar refueling base is described as a "painfully established" precondition.
- **Nuclear-class galactic colonization timing:** at half lightspeed, with twenty-year stops every 10 light-years, "they would only take two hundred thousand years, starting at the center of the galaxy, to spread throughout the whole system." Set against early-human remains "approximately one million seven-hundred thousand years."
- **Einstein-violating class** is *not* numerically modeled — Hunter treats it as the limit case where modeling itself fails, which is the point.

## Why this taxonomy is interesting

- It anchors classification on the **physics the visitor demonstrably commands**, not on origin (Mars vs. exoplanet) or on morphology (saucer vs. orb). ^[inferred] This makes it a different axis from sighting-morphology classifications like [[concepts/orb-phenomenon]] or [[concepts/transparent-uap]].
- It acknowledges asymmetry as the policy-relevant variable: the chemical class is "no real problem"; the Einstein-violating class collapses every diplomatic option to "negotiate fast." ^[inferred]
- It treats the **superluminal case as policy-relevant rather than dismissible** — a notable inclusion for a 1963 EOP staff paper. ^[inferred]
- It implicitly frames a **technology-asymmetry-driven diplomatic posture** that prefigures later thinking on contact protocols. ^[inferred]

## How this connects to the rest of the wiki

- The 1948 USAF [[concepts/project-sign|Project SIGN]] file did **not** classify by physics command — it classified by report type / morphology / explanation candidate (Horten thesis, US disc, hoax, misperception). Hunter's taxonomy is doing different work: not "what did the witness see?" but "if a contact event were confirmed, what policy class are we facing?" ^[inferred]
- The modern [[entities/aaro|AARO]]-handled material ([[references/western-us-event-2026]], [[references/usper-statement-2025]]) collects sensor and witness data; it does not classify by physics command either. The Hunter taxonomy is therefore **orthogonal to all current operational classifications in the wiki** — a *policy* axis, not a *sighting* axis. ^[inferred]
- The 1999 [[references/cometa-report-1999|COMETA Report]] adds a **second policy-axis taxonomy** that is also orthogonal to Hunter's: [[concepts/uap-state-knowledge-classes]] classifies *states* (a/b/c/d) by knowledge and capacity. Hunter classifies *aliens*. The two taxonomies are therefore **orthogonal to each other and jointly orthogonal to operational morphology** — three independent classification axes for UAP-policy thinking. ^[inferred]
- The COMETA Report's [[concepts/mhd-propulsion-uap|MHD propulsion model]] places a substantial fraction of observed UAP characteristics inside Hunter's **nuclear class** (Einstein-respecting, post-current-art) — i.e., the COMETA work is implicitly populating Hunter's middle category with a candidate physics. ^[inferred]
