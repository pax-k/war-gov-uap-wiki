# Flight Service Regulation 200-4 (1948)

**Flight Service Regulation 200-4**, dated **2 November 1948**, subject **"Unidentified Flying Objects"** — the **standing order** under which all USAF Flight Service Centers were required to report sightings of unidentified flying objects to **Commanding General, [[entities/air-materiel-command|Air Materiel Command]], Wright-Patterson AFB, Attn: MCIAXO-3**. ^[extracted]

## Status in the documentary record

FSR 200-4 is **referenced by name in nearly every 1949 sighting report** filed in [[references/usaf-flying-discs-1949|NARA box 186 file 319.1]]. The standard opening sentence of the 1949 reports is some variant of:

> "In compliance with FS Regulation 200-4 dated 2 November 1948 and letter, Headquarters USAF, Subject: 'Reporting of Information on Flying Discs', dated 6 February 1948, the following information is submitted." ^[extracted]

The full text of FSR 200-4 itself is **not preserved** in the 1949 file — only its file number, date, and subject are quoted. ^[extracted]

## Relationship to other 1948 instruments

FSR 200-4 sits at the apex of a three-instrument 1948 stack:

1. **Hq USAF Letter, 6 February 1948**, "Reporting of Information on 'Flying Discs'", signed [[entities/c-p-cabell|Brig Gen C. P. Cabell]] — the foundational charter directing routing to AMC, Attn: TSDIN, and listing the [[concepts/flying-disc-reporting-protocol|10-element observational template]]. (This letter is preserved in full in the 1949 file as inclosure to a 26 Oct 1948 TWX.)
2. **26 Oct 1948 Hq USAF TWX** (signed Maj James F. Rhodes for Maj Gen Kuter) — modifies the Cabell letter to extend reporting to **all sightings worldwide** (not just ZI + Alaska) and shifts the attention line to **MCIAXO-3**. ^[extracted]
3. **FSR 200-4, 2 November 1948** — codifies the resulting requirement as a standing **Flight Service** order. The reason for a separate Flight Service Regulation is institutional: Flight Service Centers are operationally an Air Transport Command / [[entities/military-air-transport-service|MATS]] component, not directly under the Cabell directive's chain of command. FSR 200-4 imports the Cabell requirement into Flight Service's own regulatory hierarchy. ^[inferred]

## Rescission

A handwritten note on the bottom of the page-129 / page-130 inclosure of the 26 Oct 1948 TWX in the 1949 file reads:

> **"Rescinded per Ltr Hq USA dtd 11 Jan 50"** ^[extracted]

This rescission **occurs on the same day** as transmission of the [[references/sighting-kansas-city-1950-01|6 Jan 1950 Kansas City]] report up the chain. The rescission affects the **26 Oct 1948 TWX** (the worldwide-reporting modification); whether FSR 200-4 itself was rescinded simultaneously, replaced, or rolled into Project GRUDGE's intake regime is **not specified** in this file. ^[ambiguous] The 11 Jan 1950 date roughly aligns with the formal stand-up of Project GRUDGE.

## Significance

- **Operational backbone** of the 1949 sighting-intake stream. Without FSR 200-4 the Cabell directive would have remained an HQ-USAF letter; FSR 200-4 propagated the requirement to every Flight Service Center commander as a regulatory obligation rather than a one-time directive.
- **Continuity instrument** linking the [[concepts/project-sign|Project SIGN]] charter (Dec 1947 / Feb 1948) to the early-1950 transition to Project GRUDGE. The exact transitional language is in the 11 Jan 1950 rescinding letter, which is **not in the corpus**. Open thread to locate. ^[inferred]
