# Project Twinkle (1950-51)

A **USAF-contracted scientific observation network** for instrumentally documenting the **green-fireball / disc phenomena** in New Mexico, established mid-1950 at **Vaughn, New Mexico** (and later Holloman AFB) under contract from **Air Materiel Command's Geophysical Research Division (Cambridge MA)** to **Land-Air, Inc., Alamogordo, New Mexico**. ^[extracted] Project Engineer: **Dr. Anthony O. Mirarchi**. ^[extracted] The project enters the FBI corpus through [[references/fbi-hq-62-83894-section-6|FBI HQ 62-HQ-83894 section 6]] serials 246 + 250 (Aug-Oct 1950) ^[extracted], and is the corpus's **first formal AF-contractor critical-facility scientific-study program** for UAP / unconventional-aerial-phenomena. ^[inferred]

## Founding and operational scope

The project's institutional origin is documented verbatim in the **21 August 1950 Belmont → Ladd memo** *"SUMMARY OF AERIAL PHENOMENA IN NEW MEXICO"* (serial 246): ^[extracted]

> Observations of aerial phenomena occurring within the vicinity of sensitive installations have been recorded by the Air Force since December, 1948 … approximately 150 observations of aerial phenomena referred to above have been recorded in the vicinity of installations in New Mexico … OSI has contracted with Land-Air Inc., Alamogordo, New Mexico, to make scientific study of the unexplained phenomena.

The phenomena are explicitly classified into **three types** ^[extracted]:

1. **Green fireballs** — objects moving at high speed in shapes resembling half moons, circles and discs emitting green light.
2. **Discs** — round flat-shaped objects or phenomena moving at fast velocity, emitting brilliant white light or reflected light.
3. **Meteors** — aerial phenomena resembling meteoric material moving at high velocity, varying in color.

The Belmont memo continues ^[extracted]:

> The Air Force together with Land-Air, Incorporated, have established a number of observation posts in the vicinity of Vaughn, New Mexico, for the purpose of photographing and determining the speed, height and nature of the unusual phenomena referred to as green fireballs and discs. On May 24, 1950, personnel of Land-Air, Incorporated, sighted 8 to 10 objects of aerial phenomena. A 24-hour day watch is being maintained and has been designated "Project Twinkle."

The operational architecture: **24-hour day watch** at **multiple observation posts** instrumented for **photography + speed/height/nature determination**. ^[extracted] The contract was let by **AMC Geophysical Research Division, Cambridge MA** ^[extracted] — structurally distinct from AMC Wright-Patterson T-2 (which had inherited the green-fireball portfolio at the [[concepts/project-grudge|Project GRUDGE]] 16 Feb 1949 Los Alamos conference, per [[references/fbi-san-antonio-vital-installations-1949|the SAC San Antonio memo]]). ^[inferred]

## Scientific predicate — LaPaz's recommendations

Project Twinkle is the **operational implementation of [[references/lapaz-seventh-report-1950-05-23|Dr Lincoln LaPaz's 23 May 1950 Seventh Report]]** recommendations to **Lt Col Doyle Rees** (CO 17th District OSI). ^[inferred] LaPaz's report concluded that the green fireballs **could not be ordinary meteors** (per his 11-point taxonomy of differences) and proposed **dual-station optical instrumentation** at Roswell + Sacramento NM under Dr. L. A. Manning of Stanford, **radar investigation** under Dr. Millman, and **ground search / dust collection** under Dr. William Crozier (New Mexico School of Mines). ^[extracted] LaPaz declined the $40/day consultant rate and offered **voluntary service**. ^[extracted]

The 24 May 1950 8-10-object event at Vaughn — recorded by Land-Air personnel **on the project's first operational day** ^[inferred] — is the first observational success entry in the FBI-corpus record of the project. ^[extracted] No further specific Twinkle observational successes are preserved in section 6.

## Institutional posture and the FBI's role

The FBI's role in Twinkle is documented as **passive liaison** ^[extracted]:

> The Albuquerque Office, in a letter dated August 10, 1950, advised that there have been no new developments in connection with the efforts to ascertain the identity of the strange aerial phenomena referred to as green fireballs and discs. The Albuquerque Office advised that Dr. Anthony O. Mirarchi, Project Engineer, had been informed of the Bureau's jurisdiction relative to espionage and sabotage and arrangements have been made so that the Bureau will be promptly advised in the event additional information relative to this project indicates any jurisdiction on the part of the Bureau.

This establishes the **AF-contractor-to-FBI-Albuquerque liaison channel** at the scientific-investigation tier ^[inferred] — a structurally novel inter-agency framework. The Bureau is **not** a participant; it is plugged into Mirarchi's reporting chain for espionage/sabotage-jurisdiction triggers only. ^[inferred] *"ACTION: None. The above is for your information."* ^[extracted]

The **9 October 1950 Ladd → Director update** (serial 250) reports ^[extracted]:

> To date the Air Force has not advised us of any new developments in connection with this project … According to Bureau files, an average of approximately three or four complaints have been received per month from June through September. These complaints were brought to the attention of OSI. A review of Bureau files does not indicate that there has been any increase in the sightings of these phenomena during or as a result of the war in Korea.

And, most consequentially: *"Bureau liaison determined on the morning of October 9, 1950 from OSI headquarters that the investigations of these aerial phenomena are being handled by OSI, Wright Field, Ohio. Their investigation of these phenomena fails to indicate that the sightings involved space ships or missiles from any other planet or country. According to OSI, the complaints received by them have failed to indicate any definite pattern of activity."* ^[extracted]

**Institutional consequence**: by 9 Oct 1950, **6 weeks into the project**, OSI Wright Field has concluded **(a) no extraterrestrial-or-foreign-state attribution** and **(b) no definite activity pattern** — a posture that **flatly contradicts** LaPaz's Urals-missile hypothesis (which had a strong temporal pattern: maximum frequency at 2030 local time, coinciding with 0700-1300 Urals time). ^[inferred] The structural read: **AF and FBI are aligned on prosaic-residual conclusions while the contractor and the academic consultant (LaPaz) are aligned on anomalous-pattern findings**. ^[inferred] The project thus runs through a **two-track institutional posture** from its founding.

## Distinct from Project GRUDGE / SIGN / Blue Book

Project Twinkle is **operationally distinct** from the contemporary USAF UFO investigation programs ^[inferred]:

| Feature | Project SIGN (1948-49) | [[concepts/project-grudge\|Project GRUDGE]] (1949-51) | **Project Twinkle (1950-51)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sponsor office** | AMC Wright-Patterson T-2 | AMC Wright-Patterson T-2 | **AMC Geophysical Research Division, Cambridge MA** |
| **Subject scope** | Unconventional aircraft generally | Unconventional aircraft generally | **Green fireballs and discs in NM specifically** |
| **Methodology** | Sighting-report analysis | Sighting-report analysis | **Active observational instrumentation** |
| **Contractor** | None | None | **Land-Air, Inc., Alamogordo NM** |
| **Geographic focus** | National | National | **Vaughn / Holloman AFB / NM critical-facility ring** |
| **Termination** | 1949 (transferred to GRUDGE) | Dec 1949 (formally per [[references/fbi-boggs-hearn-discontinuation-memo-1950-03-28\|Boggs-Hearn]]) | **Late 1951** ^[inferred] |

Twinkle is the **first US programmatic UAP-instrumentation project** in the wiki corpus ^[inferred] — predating later AF/contractor scientific instrumentation efforts by decades.
