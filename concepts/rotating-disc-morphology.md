# Rotating-Disc Morphology

A specific UAP morphology characterized by:

1. **Round / circular / discus** body shape (not elongated, not winged, not faceted).
2. **Slowly rotating outer surface** about an axis approximately perpendicular to the disc plane (commonly the vertical axis at takeoff).
3. **Stationary inner light points** that do *not* rotate with the outer surface — typically two, placed near the top of the disc inboard from the rim.
4. **Constant flying attitude** — the disc plane stays in the same orientation through ascent and cruise (Ryan's witnesses described it as *"like a discus in flight"*). ^[extracted]
5. **Rotation direction**: where reported, **clockwise / to the right** as viewed from a fixed external observer.
6. **No protrusions, sticks, or bulges** on the disc body itself.

The class is distinct from the **luminous-orb** ([[concepts/orb-phenomenon]]) class (no internal differentiation between rotating shell and stationary lights) and the **transparent-UAP** ([[concepts/transparent-uap]]) class (optical-transparency signature). It is also distinct from the **delta-wing / Horten flying-wing** ([[concepts/horten-flying-wing]]) class (winged geometry, no rotation).

## The 1955 Trans-Caucasus instance

**Source:** [[references/sighting-trans-caucasus-1955-10-04|4 Oct 1955 sighting]] / [[references/iaf-ir-193-55-russell-1955|IR 193-55]] page 3–4, witnesses [[entities/richard-russell|Russell]] / [[entities/e-u-hathaway|Hathaway]] / [[entities/ruben-efron|Efron]].

The witnesses' raw notes (numbered in the IR):

> 4. *"The whole object whirred and then levelled and seemed off at very high speed."* (Questioning revealed that the **outer surface of the object revolved slowly in a clockwise direction or to the right**, with the rate of ascent being relatively slow in comparison to the high speed which the object took off at when it reached altitude.)
>
> 12. *"There were **two lights towards the inside of the disc** which **remained stationary** as the outer surface went around."* (Both agreed firmly on this point.) *"The lights sat near the top of the disc."* (If a line representing the diameter of the disc were divided into three segments, the lights would have been located at the two points of division between the middle segment and the two outside segments.)
>
> 13. *"The aircraft was circular." "The aircraft was round." "It resembled a flying saucer." "It seemed to be the shape of a discus, round and circular—revolving clockwise or to the right."*
>
> 17. *"Outer part revolved slowly to right."* (There seemed to be a different impression of the disc's movement while climbing and after assuming level flight, with the terms **"revolving slowly" used for the climb** and the term **"whirring" for the cruising flight**.)
>
> 18. *"The disc rose in the same position as it was in when it sped away."* (The flying attitude of the flying disc was said to resemble a **discus in flight**.) ^[extracted]

The Trans-Caucasus instance establishes the **6-feature joint signature** above. Two distinct craft were observed one minute apart, both matching the same signature.

## Aviation-engineering implications

If taken at face value, the rotating-shell + stationary-inner-points configuration implies:

- **A mechanical or electromagnetic decoupling** between the outer body and the light-bearing inner structure. ^[inferred] Two natural readings: (a) the lights are projected from a non-rotating internal source through transparent windows that scan past as the shell rotates, but the lights stay aligned because the projection direction is fixed; or (b) the inner structure is on a counter-rotating bearing.
- **Lift mechanism is not the rotating shell.** The rotation is too slow for a lift-producing rotor (witnesses describe it as observable as rotation, not as a blur), and the post-apex high-speed cruise continues the same rotation rate without aerodynamic disruption — implying lift comes from elsewhere (ducted, antigravitic, plasma-thrust, or any non-classical source). ^[inferred]
- **Attitude control independent of rotation.** The disc plane stays horizontal through ascent (slow vertical climb), apex, and high-speed level cruise — so attitude is not coupled to rotation rate. ^[inferred]
- **No 1955-vintage Soviet aviation candidate matches.** The morphology is not on the menu of public Soviet 1955 fixed-wing, rotary-wing, or VTOL prototype hardware. ^[inferred]

## Cross-pattern relationships

- **Distinct from [[concepts/orb-phenomenon]]**: orbs are luminous, often spherical, and rarely show internal differentiation between a rotating shell and stationary inner points.
- **Distinct from [[concepts/transparent-uap]]**: rotating discs in the corpus are opaque; transparency is not part of the joint signature.
- **Distinct from [[concepts/horten-flying-wing]]**: tailless wings have wing geometry, not disc geometry, and do not rotate as part of normal aerodynamic flight.
- **Compatible with [[concepts/uap-aircraft-engagement]]**: a rotating-disc-class object could in principle exhibit the engagement / pacing behavior recorded in 2025 and 1949 cases, though the specific 1955 Trans-Caucasus instance did not — the discs ascended and departed without engaging the witness rail or any aircraft.
- **Compatible with [[concepts/observer-suppression-behavior]]**: the same 1955 instance carries both signatures.

## What rotation reportedly looks like in cruise

The Trans-Caucasus witnesses' "**revolving slowly during ascent / whirring during cruise**" distinction is interpretively important. ^[extracted] Most parsimoniously, the rotation rate stays constant; what changes is the **observability**:

- During slow ascent, individual rotational features can be tracked → "revolving slowly".
- At high cruise speed, those features blur into a continuous shimmer → "whirring".

This is consistent with a **constant-mechanism rotation** decoupled from translational velocity, rather than two separate rotation regimes. ^[inferred]
