# Project SIGN

The first official US Air Force investigative program for "Flying Discs". Project SIGN was the in-house code name for the [[entities/air-materiel-command]] (AMC) effort, established by USAF letter dated **30 December 1947**, that consolidated all incoming sighting reports at [[entities/wright-patterson-afb]].

## Charter

By the [[references/usaf-flying-discs-1948|February 1948 Cabell directive]] (AFOIR-CO-5, signed [[entities/c-p-cabell|Maj Gen C. P. Cabell]]):

> "It is Air Force policy not to ignore reports of sightings and phenomena in the atmosphere, but to recognize that part of its mission is to collect, collate, evaluate, and act on information of this nature."

The four verbs — **collect, collate, evaluate, distribute** — recur verbatim across multiple memoranda in the 1948 file and define SIGN's operational mandate. ^[extracted]

## Operational structure

- **Hub**: [[entities/air-materiel-command|AMC]], Wright-Patterson AFB, Dayton, OH (attention line: MCI; later TSDIN).
- **Chain**: All Air Force installations (ZI + Alaska) report direct to AMC; Department of the Army installations are *requested* to do the same; AMC has authorization to contact installations directly when developing follow-up information. ^[extracted]
- **Reporting standard**: the [[concepts/flying-disc-reporting-protocol|10-element observational template]].
- **Indexing**: routed under file numbers `000.9 Phenomena` / `000.9 Flying Discs` and project file `380 Project`. ^[extracted]

## The continuous-alert proposal and its rejection

In early 1948, AMC proposed that every Air Force installation in the ZI keep at least one fighter or night-interceptor on continuous alert with gun cameras and armament, dedicated to investigating and photographing flying discs. ^[extracted]

The Director of Plans and Operations (Maj Gen S. E. Anderson) and Director of Intelligence (Maj Gen George C. McDonald) jointly killed the proposal in March 1948 on three grounds:

1. The aircraft/personnel outlay was disproportionate to expected results.
2. Without complete radar coverage (which the Air Force could not provide) interception was "not possible, except by accident".
3. Civilian-source reports were unlikely to be actionable on fighter-scramble timescales. ^[extracted]

This early "no-scramble" decision foreshadows the later decoupling of UFO investigation from operational interception — a pattern that persists into modern UAP collection regimes. ^[inferred]

## Working hypotheses inside SIGN

The 1948 correspondence shows SIGN's intelligence officers actively pursuing a conventional explanation: the [[concepts/horten-flying-wing|Horten flying wing]] thesis. Documents request retention of the T-2 report "German Flying Wings Designed by Horten Brothers" (F-SU-1110-HD) and the RAE Technical Note AERO 1703, and link to the [[references/loedding-flying-disc|Loedding Flying Disc]] internal drawing. ^[extracted]

See [[synthesis/horten-thesis-vs-disc-sightings]] for the cross-cutting analysis.

## Successor programs

Project SIGN was succeeded by Project GRUDGE (1949) and then Project BLUE BOOK (1952–1969). ^[inferred] *(The 1948 file does not name successors; this is general historical background.)*

## Wartime precursor

SIGN was not Allied air intelligence's first attempt to consolidate unexplained-aerial-phenomena reports. Three years earlier, the [[references/foo-fighters-shaef-1944-1945|SHAEF Air Staff "Night Phenomena" / "Balls of Fire — Red" file]] (1944–45) ran a structurally similar collection-and-evaluation loop — [[entities/415th-night-fighter-squadron|415th NFS]] sortie reports → XII TAC A-2 → SHAEF A-2 ([[entities/c-m-grierson|Grierson]]) → [[entities/uk-air-ministry-ddi2|Air Ministry DDI2]] / A.D.I.(K) — with the same hedged, retained-document candidate-explanation pattern (Me 262, flak rockets, [[concepts/flak-bombe-hypothesis|Flak Bombe]]). ^[inferred] See [[synthesis/foo-fighters-to-flying-discs]].
