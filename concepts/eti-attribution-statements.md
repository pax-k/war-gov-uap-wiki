# ETI Attribution Statements

A working concept page for tracking **explicit extraterrestrial-origin (ETI) attributions** in primary-source UAP records — i.e., cases where a document preserves a verbatim statement that the observed phenomenon originates from an **intelligence outside the earth**.

The motivation: the corpus contains many sources that *describe* unexplained-aerial-phenomenon morphologies and behaviors, but only a small subset preserve **explicit ETI attributions** in the document of record. Tracking those statements (and their *class*) separately from morphology / behavior data lets the wiki distinguish "what was observed" from "how the observation was institutionally interpreted."

## Two anchor cases

The wiki currently holds two anchor cases, ~46 years apart, that are **structurally distinct** classes of ETI attribution:

| Class | Anchor case | Document of record | Date | Speaker | Hedging |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Institutional / foreign-liaison** | [[entities/swedish-air-intelligence-service\|Swedish AIS]] "outside the earth" position, relayed via [[references/usafe-tt1524-1948-11\|USAFE TT 1524 Item 14]] to [[entities/c-p-cabell\|Cabell]] | TS USAF dispatch | 4 Nov 1948 | Foreign-allied national intelligence service | "**Some reliable and fully technically qualified people have reached the conclusion that...**" — i.e., the statement is *attributed to qualified personnel within the service*, not signed by the service collectively. ^[extracted] |
| **Witness-side** | Capt **Ed Rhodes** (Tajik Air, ex-PanAm) attribution, preserved in [[references/dos-cable-kazakhstan-1994-01\|DUSHANBE 00259]] | UNCLAS DOS cable | 31 Jan 1994 | Civilian commercial airline pilot (US citizen) | "**Rhodes expressed the opinion, which his crew seemed to support, that the object was extraterrestrial and under intelligent control.**" Followed by the embassy's flat *"WE HAVE NO OPINION AND REPORT THE ABOVE FOR WHAT IT MAY BE WORTH."* ^[extracted] |

The two cases differ on **every operational dimension**: speaker class, document classification, document direction (inbound foreign-liaison vs outbound diplomatic), and hedging structure. Yet both share the structural feature that the **document preserves the ETI attribution as a verbatim statement** while keeping the issuing US authority (HQ USAF in 1948, AMEMBASSY Dushanbe in 1994) institutionally non-committal.

## Why the class distinction matters

- **A foreign-liaison institutional statement** like Swedish AIS in 1948 is a **state-level** datum — it tells the wiki that a foreign-allied intelligence service held an internal position on the phenomenon's origin. It does not require a specific witness to have endorsed the position; the witness here ([[references/sighting-swedish-lake-crash-1948|the unnamed Swedish technical expert who saw the lake crash]]) is *evidence* the AIS used to support its position, not the *source* of the position itself. ^[inferred]
- **A witness-side attribution** like Capt Rhodes in 1994 is a **personal** datum — the witness explicitly states his interpretation, and the document of record (here a State cable) preserves it verbatim despite the embassy's own non-endorsement. ^[inferred]
- The two classes have **different evidentiary value**. An institutional statement tells you about a state's posture; a witness statement tells you about a witness's interpretation of what they saw. They are not interchangeable.

## Where each class fits in the corpus

### Institutional / foreign-liaison ETI attributions

- **1948 Swedish AIS** — the only archived case in the wiki to date. **Earliest** institutional ETI-adjacent attribution in the corpus.

### Witness-side ETI attributions

- **1994 Capt Ed Rhodes (Tajik Air)** — the only archived case in the wiki to date. **Earliest** witness-side ETI attribution in the corpus.

## Rhetorical-structure observations

- Both anchor documents **bracket the ETI attribution with explicit non-endorsement**: USAFE TT 1524 closes Item 14 with *"Although accepting this theory of the origin of these objects poses a whole new group of questions and puts much of our thinking in a changed light, we are inclined not to discredit entirely this somewhat spectacular theory, meantime keeping an open mind on the subject. What are your reactions?"* ^[extracted] DUSHANBE 00259 closes with *"WE HAVE NO OPINION AND REPORT THE ABOVE FOR WHAT IT MAY BE WORTH."* ^[extracted] In both cases the ETI attribution is preserved *verbatim* and the issuing US authority *forwards-without-endorsing*.
- This bracketed-but-preserved pattern is itself a **corpus signal** — the institutional posture for handling ETI attributions in primary documents is **forward-and-bracket**, not **suppress** and not **endorse**. ^[inferred]
- The pattern aligns with the broader [[concepts/scientific-vs-saucer-advocate-frame|"prepared skepticism"]] rhetorical posture documented at the 1963 NASC layer ([[references/hunter-1963-space-alien-race-memo]]) and at modern [[entities/aaro|AARO]] briefings — the issuing authority writes from the middle ground between strict-scientific dismissal and saucer-advocate endorsement. ^[inferred]

## Adjacent material that is **not** an ETI attribution

For clarity, several corpus items contain ETI-flavored *language* but do **not** count as ETI attribution statements under this concept:

- The 1944–45 [[concepts/foo-fighter-phenomenon|foo-fighter file]] candidate explanations cycle through Me 262 / flak rockets / [[concepts/flak-bombe-hypothesis|Flak Bombe]] — all conventional. No ETI attribution. ^[extracted]
- The 1948 [[references/usaf-flying-discs-1948|AFOIR file]] and 1948–50 [[references/usaf-flying-discs-1949|continuation file]] cycle through [[concepts/horten-flying-wing|Horten flying-wing]] / Soviet-derivative / [[references/loedding-flying-disc|Loedding LD-2]] — all conventional. No ETI attribution. ^[extracted]
- The 1963 [[references/hunter-1963-space-alien-race-memo|NASC alien-policy memo]] is a *policy contingency* memo (*"if* contact were confirmed, *what would US policy be?"*) — see [[concepts/space-alien-policy-question]]. It is structurally **conditional**, not an attribution. ^[inferred]
- The 2025 [[references/usper-statement-2025|intel-official witness statement]] describes morphologies and behaviors but **does not preserve** an ETI attribution. ^[extracted]
- The 2026 [[references/western-us-event-2026|Western US AARO slides]] and the 2023 [[references/fbi-fd302-drone-pilot-redacted-2023|FBI FD-302 pair]] preserve detailed witness narratives but **do not preserve** ETI attributions. ^[extracted]

The narrowness of the set — two attributions across the corpus's ~80-year span — is itself a corpus signal: explicit ETI attributions in primary records are rare enough to be individually trackable. ^[inferred]
