## Key Takeaways

- **NEW — Earliest UAP-adjacent operational record in the corpus is now the WWII SHAEF file** (Dec 1944 – Mar 1945). It documents (a) the first appearance of "foofighter" in an official military intelligence document, (b) DDI2's formal admission "the whole affair is still something of a mystery," and (c) a parallel cylindrical-object engagement at F-5710 with photographic evidence lost.
- **NEW — Allied UAP-reporting institutional template is at least as old as WWII**, not a postwar phenomenon. Same 5-step loop (witness → unit S-2 → tactical A-2 → senior A-2 → tech-intel + science adviser → file closes without resolution) runs in 1944–45 SHAEF, 1948 USAF SIGN, 2026 AARO. ^[inferred]
- **First policy-staff-layer artifact in the corpus**: the 1963 NASC memo is the wiki's first archived EOP-staff-level treatment of the alien-contact question — addressed to State/OISA, not DoD, framing contact as a diplomatic problem. Its core recommendation, in any contact case: "the immediate burying of all Terrestrial hatchets."
- **Hunter concedes the memo's own limit**: "no one of consequence is going to take this rubbish seriously unless it happens. At that point, our policy will be determined in the traditional manner of grand panic." A self-aware staff paper.
- **Project SIGN's operational hub** in 1948 was Air Materiel Command at Wright-Patterson AFB; all USAF installations were ordered to route disc reports there directly via Maj Gen Cabell's 27 Feb 1948 directive (AFOIR-CO-5).
- **The 10-element 1948 sighting template** is the prototype for every later UAP report schema, including the structured intake AARO inherits today. ^[inferred]
- **Multi-sensor cross-channel corroboration is the strongest evidence class so far in the wiki**: the 2025 helicopter encounter has FLIR + NVG + naked eye all aligning on time-stamped events.
- **Federal LE multi-team multi-vantage corroboration** raises the credibility floor — but trained observers still systematically underestimate range/size (AARO's Fiery Orb correction).
